What to look for in a rehab clinic

Star ratings are a starting point, not an answer. Before you book anywhere in Sarasota, Bradenton or Venice, ask a few plain questions:

  • Who actually treats you? In many clinics the licensed clinician evaluates you and an aide runs the exercises. Ask how much time you will spend with the person who made the plan.
  • How long is the first visit? A 20-minute evaluation cannot look at your hips, spine and feet together. Problems that have lasted months need an hour or more to sort out.
  • Does the plan include hands-on work and exercise? Releasing a guarded muscle without changing the pattern that loaded it feels good for a day. Exercise without release means pushing through a muscle that is still protecting itself.
  • Will they tell you when they are not the right fit? A clinic that never refers out is not screening carefully.

When to get medical care first

  • Chest pain, shortness of breath, or pain spreading into the jaw or left arm: call 911.
  • New loss of bladder or bowel control, or numbness in the groin or inner thighs, needs emergency care the same day.
  • Progressive weakness in an arm or leg, or a foot that drags, should be seen by a physician promptly.
  • Unexplained weight loss, fever or night sweats alongside pain is a reason to see your doctor before starting rehab.
  • Pain after a fall that may involve a fracture needs imaging first.
