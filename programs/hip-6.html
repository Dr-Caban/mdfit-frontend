6-Week Hip Protocol

★★★★★ 5.0 · 89 Google reviews

Build tolerance, improve control, reduce irritation. Build strong, stable, pain-free hips through controlled movement, glute engagement and core stability. Daily exercises for all 6 weeks with weekly progressions.

  • Level: beginner
  • Where: at home
  • Type: Rehabilitation

Week by week

  1. Week 1. Learn movement and control
  2. Week 2. Improve control and stability
  3. Week 3. Increase reps and hold time
  4. Week 4. Increase range and endurance
  5. Week 5. Build strength and stability
  6. Week 6. Max control, full range, confidence

Daily basics

  • Posture: Shoulders down, chest tall, core lightly braced.
  • Glute engagement: Keep glutes lightly engaged at all times.
  • Core bracing: Brace before every rep (exhale, tighten core as if bracing for a tap).
  • Breathing: Exhale during effort. Inhale through the nose, exhale through the mouth.
  • Quality over quantity: Better to do less with perfect control than more with tension or pain.

Guidelines

  • Add 1-2 reps or seconds each week if pain-free.
  • Increase range of motion gradually.
  • Keep movement slow, and controlled.
  • If pain increases, regress and return to basics.

What to expect

  • Improved hip stability and strength
  • Reduced pain and irritation
  • Better movement and daily function
  • Less radiating pain over time
  • Stronger glutes and core connection

Pain is a signal, not a goal. Listen to your body. Stay consistent. Stronger hips. Better movement. For life.

The rules of this program

  • No pain rule. Stay within a pain-free range.
  • Slow & controlled. Quality and control over quantity.
  • Consistency. Daily work builds lasting results.
  • Glute engagement. Glutes on, shoulders down, engage core.
  • Frequency. Daily. Do all routines EVERY DAY

Week 1 preview: Learn movement and control

Hip Dip (Eccentric Control) (2-3 sets, 6-10 each side reps, tempo 3-5 sec down, 1-2 sec up., Daily, [object Object])

  1. Stand on a yoga block with the painful side (stabilizing side).
  2. Keep both legs locked (straight) and hips level (no twisting or bending at the hips).
  3. Slowly lower the opposite leg by letting the pelvis drop straight down.
  4. Tap the foot lightly on the floor.
  5. Use a support (wall, chair, counter) to bring the leg back up.
  6. Both legs stay perfectly vertical the entire time.
  7. Do not lean, twist, or hike the hip.
  8. START: Stand tall on block (painful side).
  9. LOWER (3-5 SEC): Lower opposite leg straight down.

Focus: Hips stay level and square.; Move slowly and controlled (eccentric lower).; Feel the work in the standing hip and glute..

Tip: Control is the goal. Don't rush the lowering phase.

Side Plank (Modified) (1-2 sets, 3-5 each side reps, hold 3-5 seconds, Daily, [object Object])

  1. Lie on your side, elbow under shoulder.
  2. Knees bent and stacked, feet together.
  3. Engage glutes and core.
  4. Lift hips off the ground into a mild side plank.
  5. Exhale and push your air out while holding.
  6. Hold for a few seconds, then lower down with control.
  7. LIFT & HOLD (EXHALE)
  8. LOWER (INHALE)

Focus: Hips forward (no rolling back).; Glutes and core stay engaged.; Neck neutral, gaxe forward..

Tip: Quality holds build stability. Stop if form breaks.

Knee Drive (For Radiating Pain Only) (1-2 sets, 5-10 reps, Use only when in pain, [object Object]) When to use it: Use only if you have radiating pain down the lateral hip, thigh, leg or even into the calf/foot.

  1. Stand tall with feet hip-width.
  2. Keep both feet planted.
  3. Engage core and glutes.
  4. Turn (rotate) the knee of the affected side inward and gently drive it toward the opposite knee. (Do not move the foot.)
  5. Keep hips and torso facing forward.
  6. Hold for a moment, then relax.
  7. Repeat as needed.
  8. START: Feet hip-width, knees forward.
  9. DRIVE KNEE IN: Turn knee inward toward opposite knee.

Focus: Short, controlled movement.; Do not lift or move the foot.; Relief is the goal..

Tip: Use gently. This is a relief tool, not a strength exercise.

Remember: Small daily habits create big results. Stay consistent, trust the process, and protect today so you can perform tomorrow.

Stop and get checked if

  • An exercise causes sharp, shooting or worsening pain, or pain that lasts into the next day.
  • You have new numbness, tingling or weakness.
  • You are recovering from surgery and your surgeon has not cleared you for this kind of exercise.
  • You have chest pain, shortness of breath or a sudden severe headache: call 911.

Stop it. Every MD Fit program works inside a pain-free range; discomfort from effort is fine, sharp or worsening pain is not. Go back to the previous week's version or contact Dr. Jared.
