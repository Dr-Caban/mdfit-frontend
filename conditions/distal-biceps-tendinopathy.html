Pain in the crease of the elbow

The tendon runs from the biceps muscle across the front of the elbow and attaches to a bump on the forearm bone. Pain sits deep in the crease, often a little toward the thumb side, and people usually describe it as an ache with a sharp edge on certain movements.

  • Pain lifting with the palm up, carrying a bag, or picking up a toddler or a pet.
  • Pain turning the palm upward against resistance: a screwdriver, a jar lid, a wrench.
  • Tenderness deep in the elbow crease that you have to press firmly to find.
  • An ache that lingers in the front of the elbow after a session of pulling, rowing or curls.
  • Sometimes mild swelling or a thickened feel at the front of the joint.

It builds with repetition rather than appearing overnight. Rowing, chin-ups, heavy curls, carrying, and trade work with repeated forearm rotation are the typical histories.

Not the same as shoulder biceps tendon pain

MD Fit has a separate page for biceps tendinopathy, and that one is about the long head at the shoulder, which runs in a groove at the front of the upper arm. The two ends of the same muscle produce quite different problems.

  • Shoulder end: pain at the front of the shoulder, worse reaching overhead or behind, commonly alongside rotator cuff problems.
  • Elbow end: pain in the elbow crease, worse with palm-up lifting and forearm rotation, with the shoulder usually comfortable.

It also needs separating from the two common elbow tendinopathies. Tennis elbow is on the outside of the elbow and hurts with gripping and lifting palm down. Golfer's elbow is on the inside bony point. Distal biceps pain is in the middle, at the front, deeper than both.

Muscles around the problem

  • Biceps long head and short head both funnel into this single tendon, so tension anywhere in the muscle loads the attachment.
  • Brachioradialis runs alongside as an elbow flexor and refers pain to the front of the elbow and into the web of the thumb, which can mimic the tendon closely.
  • Supinator shares the job of turning the palm upward. When the biceps tendon is irritated, supinator usually takes over and becomes tender in its own right, deep in the forearm.
  • Triceps on the opposite side influences how freely the elbow straightens, which changes the load through the front.

Dr. Jared checks all of these, because treating only the tendon and leaving a loaded supinator or brachioradialis tends to leave the pain half-resolved.

What you can do now

  1. Carry things palm-down or hugged to the chest rather than palm-up, which takes the direct pull off the tendon.
  2. Pause heavy curls, chin-ups and rowing while it is sore; press and overhead work are usually fine.
  3. Use a wider grip on jars, doors and tools, or a gripping aid, to reduce how hard you must rotate the forearm.
  4. Try an isometric: hold a light weight with the elbow at a right angle, palm up, and simply hold still for thirty seconds. Easing afterwards points to the tendon.

Muscles behind this pain

  • Biceps long head: Front of the shoulder over the biceps groove and down the front of the arm toward the elbow crease.
  • Biceps short head: Front of the shoulder near the collarbone tip, the inner front of the arm and the elbow crease.
  • Brachioradialis: Outside of the elbow and the web of the thumb.
  • Supinator: Outer elbow and the thumb web; common in tennis elbow that won't settle.
  • Triceps long head: Back of the shoulder and arm, up toward the base of the neck, and down the back of the forearm.

When to get medical care first

  • A sudden pop in the front of the elbow while lifting, with bruising and a biceps muscle that bunches up toward the shoulder: possible tendon rupture, see a physician urgently.
  • Marked weakness turning the palm upward or bending the elbow against light resistance.
  • A hot, red, swollen elbow with fever or feeling unwell.
  • Numbness, tingling or weakness spreading into the hand or fingers.
  • Elbow pain following a fall with deformity or inability to move the joint.

Frequently asked questions

How is this different from tennis elbow?

Tennis elbow sits on the outer bony point and hurts with gripping and lifting palm down. Distal biceps pain is deep in the front crease of the elbow and hurts most with palm-up lifting and turning.

Does a popping sound mean I tore it?

A sudden pop while lifting, with bruising and the muscle bunching up toward the shoulder, can indicate a rupture, which is a surgical question and needs a physician promptly. Gradual pain without a single moment is far more often tendinopathy.

Can I keep training?

Usually yes, with palm-up pulling reduced and pressing continued. Dr. Jared sets what to keep and what to pause based on what the elbow tolerates without soreness the next day.

Why is my forearm sore as well?

Supinator and brachioradialis take over the work the tendon is avoiding, and both become tender in their own right. Treating them alongside the tendon usually resolves the lingering ache.
