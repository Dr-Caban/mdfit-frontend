What genuine carpal tunnel looks like

  • Numbness and tingling in the thumb, index, middle and half the ring finger. The little finger is spared, because a different nerve supplies it.
  • Worst at night, waking you, and relieved by shaking the hand.
  • Worse holding a phone, a steering wheel or a book, with the wrist bent.
  • In longer-standing cases, dropping things and losing grip, with visible thinning of the muscle at the base of the thumb.

If the little finger is involved, that is the ulnar nerve and a different problem. If all five fingers are numb, suspect the neck or a more general cause.

Checking the whole route

A nerve already irritated higher up tolerates less compression lower down. So the evaluation follows the whole path:

  • The neck. Nerve root irritation can produce hand symptoms that look like carpal tunnel.
  • The scalenes and pectoralis minor. The nerve passes beneath both. Tightness here produces numbness that is worse with the arms overhead. See thoracic outlet symptoms.
  • The forearm. The wrist flexors and the pronators can compress the nerve before it reaches the wrist.
  • The tunnel itself. Where swelling, thickened tissue or sustained wrist flexion raise the pressure.

Addressing only the wrist when the real restriction is at the chest or neck is a common reason people do not improve.

What helps right away

  1. A neutral wrist splint at night keeps the wrist from curling, which is when most people's symptoms peak.
  2. Raise your keyboard and mouse so the wrist is not extended; a vertical mouse helps many people.
  3. Take hands off the wheel and off the phone at intervals during long drives and long reading.
  4. Avoid sleeping with the arm overhead or curled under the pillow.

When conservative care is not enough

Mild and moderate cases often respond well to the approach above. Cases where the thumb muscle has visibly wasted, where numbness is constant rather than intermittent, or where symptoms have been present a long time, are more likely to need a surgical opinion, and the nerve is better released before lasting damage sets in.

Dr. Jared will say plainly when the findings point that way rather than continuing indefinitely. Nerve conduction testing, when it is needed, is arranged through your physician.

Wrist pain that is not nerve at all

Plenty of wrist pain has nothing to do with the median nerve. Pain at the thumb side with gripping and lifting points toward the tendons there; see de Quervain's. Pain on the back of the wrist with weight-bearing, aching at the base of the thumb with pinching, and catching or locking of a finger are all different problems with different answers. The evaluation sorts out which one you have before anything is treated.

Muscles behind this pain

  • Wrist flexors: Inside of the elbow (golfer's elbow) and the palm side of the wrist.
  • Finger flexors: Palm and the front of the fingers, sometimes a 'trigger finger' catching feeling.
  • Scalenes: Chest, front and back of the upper arm, inner shoulder blade and down the forearm to the thumb and index finger.
  • Pectoralis minor: Front of the shoulder and chest, down the inner arm to the last three fingers.
  • Brachioradialis: Outside of the elbow and the web of the thumb.
  • Subclavius: Under the collarbone and down the front of the arm and forearm into the thumb, index and middle fingers.

When to get medical care first

  • Constant numbness rather than intermittent, or visible wasting of the muscle at the base of the thumb, which needs prompt medical assessment.
  • Sudden weakness or numbness affecting the whole arm, the face, or one side of the body: call 911.
  • Hand symptoms after a fall or wrist injury, with swelling or deformity.
  • Numbness in all five fingers, or in both hands, which suggests a cause beyond the wrist.
  • A hot, red, swollen wrist with fever, which needs urgent medical assessment.

Frequently asked questions

Do I need surgery?

Many mild to moderate cases improve without it. Severe or long-standing cases may still need it, and that decision is made with your doctor.

Should I wear a wrist brace at night?

A neutral wrist splint at night helps many people while the rest of the plan takes hold.

What tests do I need?

A clinical exam often shows what is happening. Nerve conduction testing is used for severe or atypical cases, arranged through your doctor.

Why do my symptoms involve my shoulder too?

The nerve passes under the collarbone and through the chest muscles on its way to the hand. Tightness there can produce hand symptoms and is worth treating alongside the wrist.
