Recognizing hip osteoarthritis

The pain is usually in the groin or the front of the hip, sometimes wrapping to the outer hip or buttock, and in a surprising number of people it is felt mostly in the thigh or knee. Mornings are stiff. The first steps after sitting are the worst, then it eases, then it aches again after a long walk. Rotating the hip, crossing the legs, reaching the foot to tie a shoe, and getting in and out of the car become harder over time. Walking distance shrinks, a limp appears, and the leg may start to turn outward.

Like the knee, the amount of arthritis on an X-ray does not predict how much a hip hurts. Dr. Jared treats people with significant imaging who move well and others with mild changes who are in constant pain because the hip flexors and glutes have locked the joint down.

The muscles around a stiff hip

When a joint loses motion, the muscles around it shorten to match, and then they become a pain source of their own.

  • Iliacus and psoas are almost always short and tender in an arthritic hip. They refer a deep groin and front-pocket ache, pull the trunk forward so standing tall is hard, and make the first steps after sitting painful.
  • Gluteus medius and gluteus minimus weaken as walking changes. They refer along the belt line and down the outside of the leg, cause the lurching limp, and make lying on the side painful at night.
  • Tensor fasciae latae overworks when the glutes do not, producing front-outer hip pain down the thigh that is often labeled bursitis.
  • Adductor longus tightens with the loss of rotation and broadens the groin ache down the inner thigh.

Releasing these does not change the cartilage, but it changes how far the hip moves and how much it hurts, which is what most people actually want.

What you can do today

  • Stand up and move every thirty to forty-five minutes. Hip arthritis hates long sitting more than it hates walking.
  • Lie flat on your back for a few minutes daily, or on your stomach if comfortable, to let the front of the hip lengthen.
  • Keep walking on flat surfaces within comfort; shorter, more frequent walks are kinder to an irritable hip.
  • Sleep with a pillow between the knees if lying on your side hurts.

Problems that mimic hip arthritis

Outer hip pain with tenderness over the bony point is more often trochanteric bursitis or gluteal tendon pain. A deep pinch with sitting in younger adults suggests a labral tear. Buttock pain running down the leg is more likely sciatica or piriformis. The lumbar spine and SI joint refer to the hip region constantly. Inflammatory arthritis, hernias, hip stress fractures and, rarely, bone disease need a physician's evaluation when the pattern does not fit.

Hip arthritis and the Sarasota lifestyle

Golf asks the lead hip to rotate inward, which is the motion arthritis takes first. Boating involves stepping over gunwales and balancing on one leg. Both are easier with a hip that has been released and glutes that are strong, and many patients here keep playing for years with the right maintenance program.

Muscles behind this pain

  • Iliacus: Groin, front of the thigh, and the back of the pelvis high in the glute.
  • Psoas: Vertical band along the low back, plus the front of the thigh and groin.
  • Gluteus medius: Along the belt line, over the SI joint, the buttock and the top of the hip.
  • Gluteus minimus: Outer hip down the side of the thigh and leg to the ankle, or down the back of the thigh and calf.
  • Tensor fasciae latae: Front-outer hip down the outside of the thigh, usually stopping at the knee.
  • Adductor longus & brevis: Deep groin, the inner thigh and down to the inner knee and top of the shin.

What to expect

Hip osteoarthritis is long-term, but stiffness and groin pain commonly improve over several weeks of release and strengthening, and walking tolerance tends to grow steadily when the glute work is kept up.

When to get medical care first

  • Hip or groin pain after a fall with inability to bear weight: possible fracture, seek emergency care.
  • A hot, swollen joint with fever or feeling unwell: possible infection, see a physician the same day.
  • Sudden severe hip pain in someone on long-term steroids or with a history of heavy alcohol use (possible bone collapse).
  • Night pain that does not change with position, unexplained weight loss, or a history of cancer.
  • Groin pain with a bulge that increases when coughing.

Frequently asked questions

Why does my knee hurt if the problem is my hip?

The hip joint and the muscles around it share nerve pathways with the front of the thigh and knee, so hip arthritis frequently refers pain downward. A hip exam usually sorts it out.

Can I avoid a hip replacement?

Many people delay or avoid surgery by restoring motion and strengthening the glutes and trunk. When symptoms progress despite that, an orthopedic surgeon helps decide on timing.

Is walking good or bad for hip arthritis?

Walking within comfort is one of the best things for an arthritic hip. Problems come from long periods of sitting and from sudden big increases in activity.

Does hands-on release help hip arthritis?

It cannot change the joint surface, but releasing the hip flexors, TFL and glutes often improves rotation and reduces the groin ache noticeably.
