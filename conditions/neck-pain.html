Why neck muscles cause headaches

The top three nerve roots of the neck share a pathway with the nerve that supplies the face and front of the head. The nervous system cannot always tell which is which, so tension at the base of the skull arrives in consciousness as a headache behind the eye or across the forehead.

That is why neck treatment often resolves headaches that have been managed as migraines for years. The specific culprits:

  • Suboccipitals, four small muscles under the skull, refer a deep ache that wraps to behind the eye.
  • Upper trapezius refers up the side of the neck to the temple.
  • Levator scapulae produces the stiff, cannot-turn-my-head pain at the neck and shoulder junction.
  • Sternocleidomastoid refers into the forehead, ear and jaw, and can bring dizziness with it.

See cervicogenic headache and tension headache.

When neck pain goes down the arm

Arm symptoms change the question. The scalenes refer convincingly into the shoulder, upper arm and even the thumb, and so does infraspinatus. Genuine nerve root irritation from the neck usually brings weakness or true numbness in a specific distribution, and often worsens when you look up or tip your head toward the painful side.

If the arm symptoms come with weakness that is getting worse, that belongs with a physician. See thoracic outlet symptoms.

What happens at your evaluation

Dr. Jared measures how far your neck actually turns and tips, then tests whether the deep neck flexors can hold the head steady, because they are usually the ones that quit. He palpates the suboccipitals, levator, scalenes and upper traps to see which reproduce your exact pain or headache, and screens for anything that needs a physician.

He does not manipulate or crack necks. Treatment is soft-tissue work, graded mobility and strengthening.

How MD Fit treats it

Hands-on release of the suboccipitals and levator usually gives immediate relief and often eases a headache within the session. Correction then addresses why they were loaded: the forward-head position, a desk set too low, a phone held in the lap, a pillow that is wrong for how you sleep.

Strengthening targets the deep neck flexors and the mid-back, because a neck is only as steady as the thorax underneath it. A rounded upper back forces the head forward no matter how often the neck is stretched. Daily routines come through the free MD Fit app.

What you can change this week

  1. Raise your screen so the top of it is at eye level. This single change removes more load than most exercises add.
  2. Sleep with one supportive pillow that fills the gap between your head and the mattress, not two stacked.
  3. Every 30 minutes, tuck your chin gently and hold for a few seconds. Resetting beats stretching.
  4. Carry bags on alternating sides, or use a backpack.

Desk work, phones and the Sarasota reality

A lot of neck pain here arrives from laptops at kitchen counters and tablets in recliners. Neither is a bad habit so much as a bad geometry, and geometry can be fixed. See desk-related neck pain and desk work and sedentary occupations for the setup specifics.

Muscles behind this pain

  • Suboccipitals: A band of headache from the base of the skull wrapping to behind the eye and the forehead.
  • Upper trapezius: Up the side of the neck, behind the ear and into the temple (a question-mark shape).
  • Levator scapulae: The angle where the neck meets the shoulder, down the inner edge of the shoulder blade.
  • Scalenes: Chest, front and back of the upper arm, inner shoulder blade and down the forearm to the thumb and index finger.
  • Sternocleidomastoid: Forehead, around and behind the eye, cheek, ear and top of the head.
  • Splenius capitis & cervicis: Pain on the top of the head, and deep ache inside the head behind the eye.

When to get medical care first

  • A sudden, severe headache unlike any you have had, or neck pain with a stiff neck and fever: call 911 or go to an emergency room.
  • Neck pain after a car accident or fall, especially with numbness, weakness or a change in how you walk.
  • Arm or hand weakness that is getting worse, or dropping things.
  • Dizziness, double vision, slurred speech or trouble swallowing with neck movement.
  • Neck pain with unexplained weight loss, night sweats, or a history of cancer.

Frequently asked questions

Can neck muscles cause headaches?

Yes. Muscles at the base of the skull and along the neck commonly refer pain into the head and behind the eyes, which is why treating the neck often settles the headache.

Will you crack my neck?

No. Treatment uses hands-on soft-tissue work, gentle mobility and strengthening.

What can I do at home?

You get a short daily routine in the MD Fit app, including posture resets and stretches you can do at your desk.

My MRI shows degeneration. Does that explain my pain?

Often not on its own. Age-related changes in the neck are extremely common and appear in people with no symptoms. The exam matters more than the report.
