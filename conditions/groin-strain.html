What a groin strain feels like

A sharp pull high on the inner thigh during a lunge, a lateral push-off on the court, a slip on a wet dock, or a kick. The pain sits near the pubic bone or a few inches down the inner thigh and is reproduced by squeezing the knees together, bringing the leg across the body, sprinting or getting out of a car. Bruising on the inner thigh may show a day or two later in a more significant tear.

Milder, nagging groin pain tends to build over weeks in people who have ramped up pickleball, soccer, skating or tennis. It is stiff in the morning, warms up with activity, and aches afterward. That slow version is frequently a tendon problem at the adductor attachment rather than a fresh muscle tear.

Muscles involved

Dr. Jared checks each of these, because treating the obvious one alone often leaves the pain behind:

  • Adductor longus and brevis are the most frequently strained. They attach near the pubic bone and refer pain deep in the groin and down the inner thigh toward the inner knee and top of the shin. Kicking, cutting and sprinting bring it on.
  • Adductor magnus is the large inner-thigh muscle. It produces a deep, hard-to-pinpoint ache in the groin or pelvis and hurts with wide stances, deep squats and lunges.
  • Iliacus and psoas sit right next door and refer to the same groin and front-of-thigh area. A tight hip flexor changes how the pelvis sits and keeps the adductors under tension.
  • Rectus femoris attaches just above the hip socket and is often strained along with the adductors in a kicking injury.

The MD Fit approach

Groin pain is one of the areas where a careful assessment matters most, because hernias, hip joint problems and pelvic conditions share the territory. Dr. Jared, as a physician, screens for those first. Once it is clearly muscular, the plan is hands-on release of the adductors, hip flexors and surrounding tissue to reduce guarding, correction of the pelvic position and the way you load a lateral step, homework from the free MD Fit app with videos like Standing Hip Abduction/Adduction, 90/90 Hip Switch and Cossack Squat scaled to pain-free ranges, and progressive strengthening of the adductors, glutes and trunk. Gentle isometric squeezes early on tend to calm the tendon; later the adductors are loaded through full range and finally in the fast, lateral patterns that caused the injury.

What to do in the first few days

  • Walk normally within comfort and avoid the wide-stance movements and sprinting that provoke it.
  • Gentle, pain-free squeezing of a pillow between the knees for a few seconds at a time can ease the tendon; stop if it sharpens.
  • Do not stretch hard into the groin in the first week. Pulling on a torn adductor is a reliable way to make it last longer.
  • Sit with knees roughly hip-width and avoid long periods with the legs crossed or splayed.

What else causes groin pain

Hip labral tears and femoroacetabular impingement cause a deep pinch in the front of the hip with sitting and deep bending. Hip osteoarthritis produces groin ache with morning stiffness and reduced rotation. Sports hernia (athletic pubalgia) sits low near the pubic bone and hurts with sit-ups and coughing; an inguinal hernia produces a bulge. Osteitis pubis is inflammation at the pubic symphysis itself. Pelvic floor dysfunction, hip stress fractures in runners, and referred pain from the lumbar spine or SI joint round out the list, and a physician should rule out testicular, gynecologic and urinary causes when anything does not fit a simple strain.

Groin strains on Sarasota courts

The lateral shuffle and sudden stop in pickleball and tennis are classic adductor loaders, and the humid heat makes fatigue arrive sooner. Building lateral strength with Cossack squats and side steps, and warming up the hips before the first game, prevents a lot of these.

Muscles behind this pain

  • Adductor longus & brevis: Deep groin, the inner thigh and down to the inner knee and top of the shin.
  • Adductor magnus: Deep inside the pelvis and groin, and the inner thigh from the groin toward the knee.
  • Iliacus: Groin, front of the thigh, and the back of the pelvis high in the glute.
  • Psoas: Vertical band along the low back, plus the front of the thigh and groin.
  • Rectus femoris: A deep ache around and behind the kneecap, and the front of the hip bone.

What to expect

Minor adductor strains often settle in a couple of weeks; larger tears and longstanding adductor tendon pain can take several weeks to a few months of progressive loading before fast lateral movement feels safe.

When to get medical care first

  • A bulge in the groin that enlarges when you cough or strain, or a bulge that becomes hard, painful and cannot be pushed back: possible hernia, seek medical care urgently.
  • Groin pain with fever, painful urination, testicular pain or swelling, or abnormal bleeding: see a physician promptly.
  • A loud pop with rapid bruising and inability to bring the leg inward: possible tendon avulsion.
  • Hip pain that makes you limp and wakes you at night, or groin pain after a fall with inability to bear weight.
  • Numbness on the inner thigh with weakness.

Frequently asked questions

How do I know if it is a groin strain or a hernia?

A strain hurts when you squeeze the legs together or stretch the inner thigh; a hernia often produces a bulge that increases with coughing. Dr. Jared screens for hernia and hip joint problems before treating it as a muscle issue.

Is heat or ice better for a groin pull?

In the first day or two many people prefer ice for comfort; after that, warmth before gentle movement is often more useful. Neither replaces progressive loading.

Why does my groin strain keep coming back?

Usually because the adductors were never strengthened through full range and the hip flexors and glutes were not addressed. Returning to lateral sport before that work is done invites a repeat.

Can I play pickleball with a mild groin strain?

Playing through it typically prolongs recovery. Short, controlled drills without lunging are a better bridge until squeezing and lateral steps are pain-free.
