Tears are more common than symptoms

Cuff tendons change with age the way hair greys. Imaging studies of people with no shoulder pain at all find tears with some regularity, and the proportion rises steadily through the decades. Many people have a tear and full function.

So the question at your evaluation is not only whether there is a tear, but whether the tear explains your symptoms, and what the shoulder can do despite it. A shoulder that still raises the arm, holds it against resistance and works through the day is a shoulder with options.

Which tendon, and what it changes

  • Supraspinatus. The most commonly involved. Pain reaching out and up, weakness lifting the arm to the side, trouble lying on it at night.
  • Infraspinatus and teres minor. Weakness turning the arm outward, pain behind the shoulder.
  • Subscapularis. Weakness tucking the hand behind the back, pain at the front of the shoulder.

A full-thickness tear with genuine loss of function behaves differently from a partial tear that is irritable but strong, and the plans diverge accordingly.

Why loading rather than resting

Tendons adapt to load. Rest reduces pain temporarily and reduces tendon capacity at the same time, so the pain returns the moment normal life resumes. The useful approach is graded loading: enough to drive adaptation, not so much that the tendon is irritated into a flare.

Alongside that, the shoulder blade has to rotate properly or the remaining healthy tendon gets compressed on every repetition. That means building serratus anterior and lower trapezius deliberately rather than hoping they come along for the ride.

What to do while it settles

  1. Stop sleeping on that shoulder, and support the arm on a pillow if you sleep on your back.
  2. Keep the arm moving within comfort every day. A guarded shoulder stiffens fast and stiffness is harder to fix than weakness.
  3. Lower overhead work rather than abandoning training entirely.
  4. Carry bags on the other side while the tendon is irritable.

When surgery is the better path

Surgery tends to be considered for an acute traumatic tear in a younger or very active person, for a tear with genuine and persistent loss of function, or when a solid course of loading has not changed things. Those are surgeon decisions, and Dr. Jared will tell you when it is time to ask rather than continue. Going into a repair with as much surrounding strength as possible makes the recovery afterward shorter.

Muscles behind this pain

  • Supraspinatus: Deep ache at the top and side of the shoulder that can run down the outside of the arm to the elbow.
  • Infraspinatus: Deep in the front of the shoulder joint, down the front and outside of the arm.
  • Subscapularis: Back of the shoulder, down the back of the arm, sometimes a band around the wrist.
  • Teres minor: A localized deep ache at the back of the shoulder.
  • Serratus anterior: Side of the rib cage and the bottom tip of the shoulder blade; a side "stitch" that hurts with deep breaths.
  • Lower trapezius: Up the back of the neck to the base of the skull, the top of the shoulder, and along the inner shoulder blade.

When to get medical care first

  • Shoulder or arm pain with chest pressure, shortness of breath, sweating or nausea: call 911.
  • Sudden inability to lift the arm at all after a fall or a lifting injury, which needs prompt assessment.
  • Visible deformity, or a bulge in the upper arm after a sudden pop.
  • A hot, red, swollen shoulder with fever, which needs urgent medical assessment.
  • Numbness or progressive weakness in the hand rather than shoulder pain alone.

Frequently asked questions

Do all rotator cuff tears need surgery?

No. Many partial and even full-thickness tears do well with the right loading plan. Your evaluation and your surgeon's advice together decide the path.

Can I still lift weights?

Yes, with a plan. You keep training around the shoulder, then progress loads back as strength returns.

Why does it hurt so much at night?

Lying down removes the support gravity gives the joint during the day, and side-lying compresses the tendon directly. Night pain is one of the most common complaints with cuff problems.

Does a tear get bigger if I use the arm?

Sensible, graded loading is not what enlarges tears, and avoiding all use weakens the shoulder further. Your plan sets what is appropriate for your specific tear.
