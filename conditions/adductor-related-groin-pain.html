The squeeze test and what it tells you

Adductor-related groin pain is tender along the inner thigh tendons where they attach to the pubic bone, and it is reproduced by resisting the legs squeezing together. That single test separates it from most of the alternatives.

  • Pain pressing along the inner thigh near the groin crease, often one specific tender band.
  • Pain squeezing a ball or a fist between the knees.
  • Worse with changing direction, lunging, kicking, or pushing off sideways.
  • Stiffness and soreness getting out of a car or rolling over in bed.
  • An ache spreading down the inner thigh rather than deep inside the hip.

It is frequently longstanding by the time people seek help, having been managed with stretching for months. That matters, because stretching a tendon that is already irritated at its attachment often keeps it going.

Strain or longstanding tendon problem

MD Fit has a separate page for groin strain, and the difference is the story rather than the site.

  • A strain has a moment. A lunge, a slip, a sudden stretch, immediate pain, sometimes bruising a few days later. It is an acute muscle tear and follows a healing timeline.
  • Adductor-related groin pain builds over weeks or months without a clear moment, centres on the tendon attachment rather than the muscle belly, and persists well past when a strain should have settled.

A strain that was never fully rehabilitated very often becomes the longstanding version, which is why the two pages link to each other.

The other things that cause groin pain

This region is crowded, and getting the source right changes the treatment entirely.

  • Hip labral tear and hip impingement produce a deeper pinch felt inside the joint, often with a C-shaped grip of the hand around the hip, worse with deep bending and sitting low. Resisted squeezing is usually comfortable.
  • Osteitis pubis is bone irritation right at the midline pubic joint. It is tender exactly on the midline rather than off to one side, and it often follows the adductor problem rather than preceding it.
  • Inguinal or sports hernia sits higher, nearer the crease, and is worse with coughing, sneezing or sitting up. A bulge that increases with straining needs a physician.
  • Hip osteoarthritis gives groin ache with morning stiffness and loss of hip rotation.
  • Referred pain from the lower back or from the psoas can be felt in the groin with nothing tender locally.

Hip joint problems and adductor problems also coexist regularly, which is why Dr. Jared tests the hip even when the squeeze test is clearly positive.

Muscles that drive it

  • Adductor longus is involved most often. Its tendon attachment on the pubic bone is the classic tender point, and it refers pain down the inner thigh toward the knee.
  • Adductor magnus is the largest of the group and also extends the hip, so it is loaded in sprinting and lunging as well as in squeezing.
  • Iliacus and psoas sit alongside and refer into the same area; tight hip flexors change pelvic position and load the adductor attachments further.
  • External oblique attaches to the front of the pelvis from above and pulls in the opposite direction. Where the abdominal wall is not holding its side of the balance, the adductors take more strain, and that imbalance is central to this problem.

What you can do this week

  1. Stop stretching the groin into pain. Lengthening an irritated tendon attachment usually prolongs this.
  2. Pause lateral sports and sudden direction changes while it settles. Straight-line walking and cycling are often fine.
  3. Try an isometric squeeze: lie on your back, knees bent, a rolled towel or ball between the knees, and squeeze at a moderate effort for thirty seconds. Comfort afterwards is a useful sign.
  4. Get in and out of the car by swinging both legs together rather than splitting them.

Sarasota notes

Pickleball and tennis are the frequent culprits locally, because the sideways lunge for a wide ball is precisely the movement that loads these tendons at length. Golf loads them through the hip turn. In every case the pattern is the same: the adductors have been asked to control more than they have been trained to handle, and the answer is loading rather than stretching.

Muscles behind this pain

  • Adductor longus & brevis: Deep groin, the inner thigh and down to the inner knee and top of the shin.
  • Adductor magnus: Deep inside the pelvis and groin, and the inner thigh from the groin toward the knee.
  • Iliacus: Groin, front of the thigh, and the back of the pelvis high in the glute.
  • Psoas: Vertical band along the low back, plus the front of the thigh and groin.
  • External oblique: Abdominal wall, the side of the belly and down into the groin; can feel like heartburn or a side stitch.

What to expect

Longstanding adductor problems typically respond over a number of weeks to a few months of progressive loading, and tend to settle more slowly than a simple strain. Dr. Jared uses the squeeze test as the practical marker, progressing load as it becomes less provocative.

When to get medical care first

  • A bulge in the groin that increases with coughing or straining: possible hernia, see a physician.
  • Groin pain with fever, feeling unwell, or a hot swollen hip joint.
  • Groin pain after a fall with inability to bear weight: possible fracture, seek urgent care.
  • Night pain that does not change with position, unexplained weight loss, or a history of cancer.
  • Groin pain in a child or adolescent, which always needs medical evaluation and imaging.

Frequently asked questions

Why does stretching make it worse?

The problem is usually at the tendon attachment on the pubic bone. Stretching pulls on that attachment, which is already irritated. Loading the muscle through controlled strength work tends to settle it where stretching does not.

Is this a sports hernia?

They can feel similar and sometimes occur together. A sports hernia is typically higher, nearer the crease, and worse with coughing or sitting up, and it is a physician's diagnosis. Dr. Jared will refer where the picture suggests it.

Can I keep playing pickleball?

Often at reduced intensity, avoiding the wide lunge, if it does not leave you sore the next morning. Lateral push-off is usually the last thing to return.

Why does my abdominal strength matter?

The abdominal wall and the adductors pull on opposite sides of the same pelvic attachment. If one side is weak, the other takes more strain, which is a common reason this problem keeps returning.
