OCCIPITALS • SUBOCCIPITALS • LEVATOR SCAPULAE

Referred pain patterns

  • Occipitalis (Scalp Pain)

    Headache at the back of the head, often felt as scalp tenderness and tension.

  • Suboccipital Muscles (Deep Headache & Eye Pain)

    Deep ache at the base of the skull, can radiate to the top of the head. Can refer pain to the forehead, behind the eye, and temple (often mimics migraine). Can cause pain behind the ear, jaw tension, and upper cervical stiffness.

  • Levator Scapulae (Neck, Shoulder & Scapular Pain)

    Pain at the side of the neck, into the top of the shoulder, and medial border of the scapula. Can refer to the base of the skull and cause tension headaches.

  • Combined Patterns

    Overlap can cause headaches, neck pain, shoulder pain, and scapular pain. Accurate assessment helps identify the primary driver.

  • Arm Radiation (Levator Scapulae)

    Can refer pain down the shoulder, lateral arm, forearm and into the hand (often C5-C6 distribution), commonly felt with scapular tightness or nerve irritation.

Anatomy & attachments

Occipitalis

Origin
Superior nuchal line (of occipital bone)
Insertion
Epicranial aponeurosis (galea)
Action
Retracts scalp
Nerve
Facial nerve (CN VII)
Blood supply
Occipital artery (branch of ECA)

Rectus Capitis Posterior Major

Origin
C2 spinous process
Insertion
Inferior nuchal line (occiput)
Action
Extension, ipsilateral rotation
Nerve
Suboccipital nerve (C1, dorsal ramus)
Blood supply
Vertebral artery (muscular branches)

Rectus Capitis Posterior Minor

Origin
Posterior tubercle of C1
Insertion
Inferior nuchal line (occiput)
Action
Extension, stabilization
Nerve
Suboccipital nerve (C1)
Blood supply
Vertebral artery

Obliquus Capitis Superior

Origin
Transverse process of C1
Insertion
Inferior nuchal line (occiput)
Action
Ipsilateral rotation, lateral flexion
Nerve
Suboccipital nerve (C1)
Blood supply
Vertebral artery

Obliquus Capitis Inferior

Origin
Spinous process of C2
Insertion
Transverse process of C1
Action
Ipsilateral rotation, stabilization
Nerve
Suboccipital nerve (C1)
Blood supply
Vertebral artery

Levator Scapulae

Origin
Posterior tubercles of C1-C4 transverse processes
Insertion
Medial border of scapula (superior angle to spine)
Action
Elevates scapula, downward rotation, ipsilateral lateral flexion of neck
Nerve
Dorsal scapular nerve (C5) + C3-C4 (direct branches)
Blood supply
Dorsal scapular artery (from subclavian artery)

Key Clinical Pearls

  • Suboccipital tightness often contributes to headaches, dizziness, and visual disturbances.
  • The greater occipital nerve (C2) is a common source of occipital headaches.
  • Levator scapulae can mimic cervical radiculopathy (C5-C6).
  • Assess for nerve involvement, vascular compression, and posture.
  • Treat the source — not just the symptoms.
  • Manual therapy, mobility, and postural correction can restore function.

Common Conditions

  • Tension headaches
  • Occipital neuralgia
  • Cervicogenic headache
  • Thoracic outlet syndrome (scalene/levator involvement)
  • Neck and shoulder pain
  • Scapular dyskinesis
  • Cervical radiculopathy (C5-C6)
  • Dizziness and visual disturbances (upper cervical)
