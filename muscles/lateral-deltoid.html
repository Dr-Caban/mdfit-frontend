Where the lateral deltoid sends pain

Outside of the shoulder cap and the side of the upper arm toward the deltoid's insertion. People usually come in describing pain at the outside of the shoulder or upper arm or top of the shoulder.

What the lateral deltoid does

Lifts the arm out to the side (abduction) from about 15 to 90 degrees; holds the arm up for overhead or shoulder-height work.

  • Starts at: Outer edge of the acromion (the bony shelf at the top of the shoulder).
  • Attaches to: Deltoid tuberosity on the outside of the upper arm bone.
  • Nerve supply: Axillary nerve (C5, C6).

Symptoms people describe

  • Ache over the outside of the shoulder cap and down the side of the upper arm.
  • Pain lifting the arm out to the side or holding it at shoulder height.
  • Soreness lying on that shoulder.
  • Fatigue and ache after carrying bags or working with the arms raised.
  • Tender spots in the middle of the shoulder cap.

What makes it worse

Reaching overhead · Gripping, lifting or typing · Lying on it at night. If two or more of those match your day, this muscle moves up the list.

Quick self-check

Education, not a diagnosis. The more of these that ring true, the more likely the lateral deltoid is involved:

  • Is the pain on the outside of the shoulder cap, not the front or back?
  • Does lifting your arm out to the side hurt, especially holding it at shoulder height?
  • Does it ache after carrying heavy bags or working with your arms up?
  • Is the tender spot on the muscle itself, a few finger-widths below the top of the shoulder?
  • Does it hurt to lie on that side at night?

Resisted side raise. Lift your arm out to the side to 45 degrees, palm down, and hold while your other hand presses down at the elbow for 5 seconds. Suggests this muscle if: Ache on the outside of the shoulder cap or obvious weakness compared with the other side.

What it gets mistaken for

  • Supraspinatus tendinopathy
  • Subacromial bursitis
  • Shoulder impingement
  • Cervical radiculopathy (C5)

Common causes

  • Repeated lateral raises or overhead work without rest.
  • Carrying heavy bags with the arm held away from the body.
  • Sleeping on one side every night.
  • A weak supraspinatus forcing the lateral delt to start every lift.
  • Upper back stiffness that limits how the shoulder blade moves.

Dr. Jared's 3-phase relief plan

Work through the phases in order and move on when the current one feels easy and pain-free. Every video is in the free MD Fit app.

Phase 1 · Calm it down

  1. Side-delt pressure release · 60 sec, twice daily
    Lean the outside of the shoulder into a ball on a wall and hold gentle pressure on the tender spot while breathing slowly.
  2. Scapular CARs (video) · 2 x 5 circles each way, daily
    Slow shoulder-blade circles to restore smooth motion without load.
  3. Shoulder Drop Hold (video) · 3 x 20 sec, daily
    Relax the shoulder down away from the ear to unload the deltoid.

Phase 2 · Restore movement

  1. Rotator Cuff 90 Band Vibration (video) · 3 x 20 sec, daily
    Wake up the cuff so it centers the ball before the deltoid lifts.
  2. Thoracic Extension Over Chair (video) · 2 x 8, daily
    Improve upper-back extension so the shoulder blade can rotate up as you lift.

Phase 3 · Strengthen

  1. Lateral Raise (video) · 3 x 12 light, every other day
    Thumbs slightly up, lift to shoulder height only, lower over 3 seconds.
  2. Abducted External Rotation (video) · 3 x 10, every other day
    Arm out to the side at 90 degrees, rotate up slowly to build cuff endurance.
  3. Farmer Carry (video) · 3 x 30 m, twice weekly
    Walk tall with weights at your sides, shoulders level and slightly back.

Habits that help

  • Switch carrying sides and use a backpack for heavy loads.
  • Take breaks every 20 minutes when working with the arms raised.
  • Sleep on your back or the other side with a pillow hugged in front.

What to expect

Most people feel easier side-lifting within 1 to 2 weeks; endurance for carrying and overhead work builds over 4 to 6 weeks.

When to see a professional

Book an evaluation if lifting the arm stays painful after 3 weeks, there is a painful arc between 60 and 120 degrees, or weakness is getting worse. Get urgent care for any of these:

  • Sudden inability to lift the arm after a fall.
  • Numbness over the outside of the shoulder with weakness (possible axillary nerve injury).
  • A hot, red, swollen shoulder or fever.
  • Pain that wakes you every night regardless of position.

Frequently asked questions

Can a tight lateral deltoid cause pain at the outside of the shoulder or upper arm?

Often, yes. Outside of the shoulder cap and the side of the upper arm toward the deltoid's insertion. That referred pain is why it gets blamed on the joint or the spot that hurts instead of the muscle.

How long does lateral deltoid pain take to settle?

Most people feel easier side-lifting within 1 to 2 weeks; endurance for carrying and overhead work builds over 4 to 6 weeks.

When should I see someone about it?

What else can feel like lateral deltoid pain?
