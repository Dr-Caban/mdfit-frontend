Where the external oblique sends pain

Abdominal wall, the side of the belly and down into the groin; can feel like heartburn or a side stitch. People usually come in describing pain at the belly or side of the abdomen.

What the external oblique does

Rotates the trunk to the opposite side, bends it sideways and compresses the abdomen.

  • Starts at: Outer surfaces of the lower eight ribs.
  • Attaches to: Iliac crest, the abdominal aponeurosis to the midline (linea alba) and the pubic bone.
  • Nerve supply: Thoracoabdominal nerves (T7–T11) and subcostal nerve (T12).

Symptoms people describe

  • Ache or burning in the abdominal wall or the side of the belly.
  • Pain that can feel like heartburn, a hernia or a side stitch.
  • Pain into the groin on the same side.
  • Pain twisting, coughing or getting out of bed.

What makes it worse

Turning your head or body · Deep breaths or coughing · Running or sports. If two or more of those match your day, this muscle moves up the list.

Quick self-check

Education, not a diagnosis. The more of these that ring true, the more likely the external oblique is involved:

  • Does twisting your trunk reproduce the pain?
  • Does tensing your stomach (like a crunch) make a tender spot in the abdominal wall hurt more?
  • Does it ache into the groin on the same side?
  • Did it start after rotational sport, heavy lifting or coughing?
  • Is it unrelated to eating?

Head-lift test (Carnett sign). Lie on your back, find the most tender spot, then lift your head and shoulders to tense the abdomen while pressing the spot. Suggests this muscle if: The spot hurts the same or more when the abdomen is tensed, which points to the wall muscle rather than an organ.

What it gets mistaken for

  • Heartburn or reflux
  • Inguinal hernia
  • Gallbladder or appendix problems (right side)
  • Rib dysfunction

Common causes

  • Rotational sports: golf, tennis, throwing.
  • Heavy lifting with twisting.
  • Chronic coughing.
  • Stiff upper back forcing the trunk to rotate at the abdomen.

Phase 1 · Calm it down

  1. Thread The Needle (video) · 2 x 8 per side, daily

Phase 3 · Strengthen

  1. Dead Bug (video) · 3 x 8 per side, every other day
    Anti-rotation core control with the low back gently down.
  2. Farmer Carry (video) · 3 x 30 m, twice weekly
    Walk tall; one-arm carries train anti-rotation.

Habits that help

  • Turn the whole body instead of twisting at the waist when lifting.
  • Treat a lingering cough.
  • Warm up rotation before sport.

What to expect

Abdominal wall pain from the obliques usually eases in 1 to 3 weeks.

When to see a professional

Abdominal pain needs a doctor first if it comes with fever, vomiting, a bulge or changes with eating. Book with Dr. Jared once organ causes are ruled out. Get urgent care for any of these:

  • Abdominal pain with fever, vomiting, or a rigid belly: seek urgent care.
  • A bulge in the groin or abdomen.
  • Pain related to eating, with blood in stool or black stools.
