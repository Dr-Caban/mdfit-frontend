Where the wrist extensors sends pain

Outside of the elbow (tennis elbow) and the back of the wrist and hand on the thumb side. People usually come in describing pain at the back of the hand or wrist, forearm or thumb and index finger. It is also a common hidden driver behind Tennis Elbow (Lateral Epicondylitis).

What the wrist extensors does

Lifts the wrist back and steadies it whenever you grip.

  • Starts at: Lateral epicondyle and the ridge above it (outside of the elbow).
  • Attaches to: Bases of the second and third metacarpals on the back of the hand.
  • Nerve supply: Radial nerve and its deep branch (C6, C7).

Symptoms people describe

  • Pain at the outside of the elbow (tennis elbow).
  • Ache down the back of the forearm to the back of the hand near the thumb side.
  • Pain gripping, shaking hands, lifting a cup or turning a key.
  • Weaker grip.

What makes it worse

Gripping, lifting or typing · Desk, phone or screens. If two or more of those match your day, this muscle moves up the list.

Quick self-check

Education, not a diagnosis. The more of these that ring true, the more likely the wrist extensors is involved:

  • Does gripping or lifting a cup with the palm down hurt at the outside of the elbow?
  • Is there a tender spot just below the bony bump on the outside of the elbow?
  • Does lifting your wrist back against resistance hurt?
  • Do you type, use a mouse or use tools a lot?
  • Does it ache down the back of the forearm toward the thumb side of the hand?

Resisted wrist extension. With the elbow straight and palm down, lift the hand back against resistance from your other hand. Suggests this muscle if: Pain at the outside of the elbow.

What it gets mistaken for

  • Lateral epicondylitis (tendinopathy)
  • Radial tunnel syndrome
  • Cervical radiculopathy (C6–C7)
  • Triceps lateral head or supinator trigger points

Common causes

  • Repetitive gripping, mouse use and typing with the wrist bent back.
  • Racquet sports and tool use.
  • Sudden increase in lifting or yard work.
  • Neck and shoulder tension adding load.

Dr. Jared's 3-phase relief plan

Work through the phases in order and move on when the current one feels easy and pain-free. Every video is in the free MD Fit app.

Phase 1 · Calm it down

  1. Forearm extensor release · 60 sec, twice daily
    Press with the thumb or a ball on the tender spots of the upper outer forearm while slowly bending the wrist up and down.
  2. Weighted Wrist Circles (video) · 2 x 10 each way, no weight to start, daily
    Gentle circles to keep the wrist moving.

Phase 2 · Restore movement

  1. Wrist extensor stretch · 3 x 30 sec, twice daily
    Elbow straight, palm down, gently bend the wrist down with the other hand.

Phase 3 · Strengthen

  1. Eccentric Wrist Drops (video) · 3 x 15, daily
    Lift with the other hand, lower slowly over 3–4 seconds. Mild discomfort is acceptable; sharp pain is not.
  2. Farmer Carry (video) · 3 x 30 m, twice weekly (from week 4)
    Light grip endurance once lowering is comfortable.

Suggested library videos for this muscle: Eccentric Wrist Drops, Weighted Wrist Circles.

Habits that help

  • Keep the wrist straight on the keyboard and mouse.
  • Lift with the palm up when you can.
  • Use a thicker grip on tools and racquets.

What to expect

Tendon-driven elbow pain takes 6 to 12 weeks of consistent loading; muscle tenderness eases sooner.

When to see a professional

Book an evaluation if pain persists past 4 weeks, wakes you at night, or there is any weakness or numbness. Get urgent care for any of these:

  • Elbow swelling with redness or fever.
  • Wrist drop or weak finger straightening.
  • Pain after a fall with inability to straighten the elbow.

Frequently asked questions

Can a tight wrist extensors cause pain at the back of the hand or wrist?

Often, yes. Outside of the elbow (tennis elbow) and the back of the wrist and hand on the thumb side. That referred pain is why it gets blamed on the joint or the spot that hurts instead of the muscle.

How long does wrist extensors pain take to settle?

Tendon-driven elbow pain takes 6 to 12 weeks of consistent loading; muscle tenderness eases sooner.

When should I see someone about it?

Book an evaluation if pain persists past 4 weeks, wakes you at night, or there is any weakness or numbness.

What else can feel like wrist extensors pain?

Lateral epicondylitis (tendinopathy), Radial tunnel syndrome, Cervical radiculopathy (C6–C7). An evaluation separates muscle pain from these, and anything that needs a physician is referred on.
