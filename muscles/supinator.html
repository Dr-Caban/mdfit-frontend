Where the supinator sends pain

Outer elbow and the thumb web; common in tennis elbow that won't settle. People usually come in describing pain at the thumb and index finger. It is also a common hidden driver behind Tennis Elbow (Lateral Epicondylitis).

What the supinator does

Turns the forearm so the palm faces up (supination). Key movements include turning a doorknob, using a screwdriver, or accepting change in your hand.

  • Starts at: The outer elbow bone (lateral epicondyle of the humerus) and nearby forearm bone (ulna).
  • Attaches to: The upper third of the other forearm bone (radius).
  • Nerve supply: Radial nerve (from neck roots C6, C7).

Symptoms people describe

  • A deep, aching pain at the outer elbow, often mistaken for 'tennis elbow'.
  • Pain that may radiate down the back of the forearm to the web of the thumb.
  • Weakness or sharp pain when trying to turn your palm up against resistance.
  • Aching at rest, especially after a day of heavy hand use.
  • Tenderness when you press deeply into the muscles on the top of your forearm.
  • Pain that persists even when you are not actively gripping anything.

What makes it worse

Gripping, lifting or typing. If two or more of those match your day, this muscle moves up the list.

Quick self-check

Education, not a diagnosis. The more of these that ring true, the more likely the supinator is involved:

  • Do you feel a deep ache on the outside of your elbow, even when you're not gripping anything?
  • Is it painful to turn a doorknob or use a screwdriver, specifically the motion of turning your palm upwards?
  • Have you been treated for 'tennis elbow' but the pain just won't go away?
  • Does pressing deeply into the muscle on the top of your forearm, a few inches below the elbow, reproduce your specific pain?
  • Is the pain a constant, deep ache rather than a sharp pain that only happens when you lift?

Resisted Supination Test. Rest your elbow on a table, bent to 90 degrees with your palm facing down. Use your other hand to resist as you try to turn your palm up towards the ceiling. Suggests this muscle if: Pain deep in the outer elbow or forearm.

What it gets mistaken for

  • Lateral epicondylitis ('Tennis Elbow')
  • Radial tunnel syndrome (nerve compression)
  • C6 or C7 cervical radiculopathy (pinched nerve in the neck)
  • Elbow joint arthritis

Common causes

  • Repetitive turning motions, such as using tools or playing racquet sports with a lot of spin.
  • Carrying heavy items with bent elbows and palms up, like a waiter's tray.
  • A fall onto an outstretched hand.
  • Prolonged typing on a non-ergonomic keyboard that forces forearm rotation.
  • Forcefully tightening screws or jar lids.

Dr. Jared's 3-phase relief plan

Work through the phases in order and move on when the current one feels easy and pain-free. Every video is in the free MD Fit app.

Phase 1 · Calm it down

  1. Supinator Pressure Release · 3 x 30-60 sec, daily
    Rest your forearm on a table. Use the fingers or thumb of your other hand to find and press on tender spots deep in the muscle on the top of your forearm, just below the elbow.
  2. Gentle Palm Up/Palm Down · 2 x 15 reps, twice daily
    Sit with your elbow bent at 90 degrees and resting at your side. Slowly rotate your forearm to turn your palm up, then slowly turn it back down. Stay in a pain-free range.
  3. Box Breathing (video) · 5 minutes, 1-2 times daily
    Follow the video cues to inhale for 4 seconds, hold for 4, exhale for 4, and hold for 4. This helps calm the nervous system to reduce muscle tension.

Phase 2 · Restore movement

  1. Pronator Stretch · 3 x 30 sec, daily
    Hold your arm out, palm up. Gently use your other hand to bend your wrist down, feeling a stretch on the top/inside of your forearm. Keep your elbow straight.
  2. Eccentric Wrist Pronation (video: Eccentric Wrist Pronator) · 3 x 15, every other day
    Rest your forearm on your thigh, palm up, holding a light weight. Use your other hand to help turn your palm down, then slowly control the motion as the weight turns your palm back up.
  3. Radial Nerve Glide (video: Neck Neuroglide Seated) · 1 x 10 reps, daily
    Perform the radial nerve glide as shown in the video. This helps the nerve that passes through the supinator muscle to move more freely.

Phase 3 · Strengthen

  1. Weighted Supination · 3 x 10-12, every other day
    Hold a light dumbbell or hammer handle vertically. Rest your forearm on a table. Slowly rotate the weight so your palm turns up, then control it back to the start.
  2. Bicep Curl (video) · 3 x 10-12, every other day
    Perform standard bicep curls, focusing on the final part of the movement where you fully turn your palm up at the top. This strengthens the biceps, a primary supinator.

Suggested library videos for this muscle: Eccentric Wrist Pronator, Weighted Wrist Circles.

Habits that help

  • Use power tools instead of manual screwdrivers for repetitive tasks.
  • Keep your wrists in a neutral (handshake) position when typing or using a mouse.
  • Open tight jars by using a gripper or tapping the lid to break the seal, rather than using maximum force.
  • Carry objects with palms facing in or down when possible.

What to expect

Pain relief often begins within 1-2 weeks of consistent release and stretching. Building strength to prevent recurrence can take 4-6 weeks.

When to see a professional

If you have persistent pain despite 2-3 weeks of this program, or if you have significant weakness or numbness, schedule an appointment for a full evaluation. Get urgent care for any of these:

  • Inability to turn your palm up or down.
  • Constant, severe pins and needles or numbness in the hand.
  • Visible swelling or deformity around the elbow after an injury.
  • Pain that wakes you from sleep and is not relieved by changing position.

Frequently asked questions

Can a tight supinator cause pain at the thumb and index finger?

Often, yes. Outer elbow and the thumb web; common in tennis elbow that won't settle. That referred pain is why it gets blamed on the joint or the spot that hurts instead of the muscle.

How long does supinator pain take to settle?

Pain relief often begins within 1-2 weeks of consistent release and stretching. Building strength to prevent recurrence can take 4-6 weeks.

When should I see someone about it?

If you have persistent pain despite 2-3 weeks of this program, or if you have significant weakness or numbness, schedule an appointment for a full evaluation.

What else can feel like supinator pain?

Lateral epicondylitis ('Tennis Elbow'), Radial tunnel syndrome (nerve compression), C6 or C7 cervical radiculopathy (pinched nerve in the neck). An evaluation separates muscle pain from these, and anything that needs a physician is referred on.
