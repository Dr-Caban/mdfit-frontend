Where the latissimus dorsi sends pain

Bottom tip of the shoulder blade and down the back of the arm to the ring and little fingers. People usually come in describing pain at the bottom tip of the shoulder blade, side of the ribs, ring and little finger, palm side of the wrist and hand or back of the upper arm.

What the latissimus dorsi does

Pulls the arm down, toward the body, and rotates it inward. It's a powerful muscle for pulling movements like pull-ups, swimming, or rowing.

  • Starts at: Spine (from mid-back to the sacrum), lower ribs, and top of the pelvis.
  • Attaches to: The front of the upper arm bone (humerus).
  • Nerve supply: Thoracodorsal nerve (from cervical roots C6, C7, and C8).

Symptoms people describe

  • A persistent, deep ache at the bottom angle of the shoulder blade.
  • Pain that travels down the back of the arm and forearm, sometimes reaching the ring and little fingers.
  • A feeling of stiffness or restriction when reaching overhead.
  • A dull ache in the mid-back, especially after pulling activities.
  • Difficulty sleeping on either side due to pressure on the muscle.
  • A 'pinching' sensation in the front of the shoulder when lifting the arm.

What makes it worse

Reaching overhead · Gripping, lifting or typing. If two or more of those match your day, this muscle moves up the list.

Quick self-check

Education, not a diagnosis. The more of these that ring true, the more likely the latissimus dorsi is involved:

  • Does the pain feel like a deep ache under your shoulder blade that refers down the back of your arm?
  • Do you feel a 'catch' or stiffness when you try to reach for something on a high shelf?
  • Have you noticed numbness or tingling in your ring and little finger along with this shoulder and back pain?
  • Does your mid-back ache after activities like swimming, rowing, or carrying a heavy backpack?
  • Is it difficult to bring your arm fully overhead without arching your lower back?

Overhead Reach Test. Stand with your back flat against a wall, feet slightly away from the wall. Try to raise both arms straight overhead to touch the wall with your thumbs, without arching your lower back or bending your elbows. Suggests this muscle if: You feel a strong pull in your mid-back or the back of your armpit, or you are unable to touch the wall without your lower back arching away from it.

What it gets mistaken for

  • Rotator cuff tear or tendinopathy
  • Cervical radiculopathy (pinched nerve in the neck)
  • Thoracic outlet syndrome
  • Shoulder impingement syndrome
  • Teres major muscle strain

Common causes

  • Overuse from pulling exercises like pull-ups, rowing, or swimming.
  • Carrying heavy bags or backpacks consistently on one shoulder.
  • Prolonged sitting with poor posture, especially reaching forward for a computer mouse.
  • Vigorous activities like chopping wood or playing tennis.
  • Sleeping with an arm stretched far overhead.

Dr. Jared's 3-phase relief plan

Work through the phases in order and move on when the current one feels easy and pain-free. Every video is in the free MD Fit app.

Phase 1 · Calm it down

  1. Child's Pose Lat Stretch (video: Dynamic Childs Pose) · 2 x 30-60 seconds, once daily
    From a kneeling position, fold forward and walk your hands out in front, then to one side to feel a stretch along the opposite side of your back. Breathe into the stretch.
  2. Foam Roller Release · 1-2 minutes per side, once daily
    Lie on your side with a foam roller placed under the fleshy part of your back, just behind your armpit. Gently roll up and down, pausing on tender spots.
  3. Doorway Hang · 2 x 20-30 seconds, twice daily
    Grasp the top of a sturdy door frame. Let your knees bend and allow your body weight to gently stretch your lats and shoulders. Keep your core engaged.

Phase 2 · Restore movement

  1. Quadruped Thoracic Rotation (video: Thread The Needle) · 2 x 10 reps per side, once daily
    On hands and knees, reach one arm under your body, then open it up toward the ceiling. Follow your hand with your eyes to encourage rotation through your mid-back.
  2. Dynamic Childs Pose (video) · 2 x 12-15 reps, every other day
    Sit back toward your heels with arms reaching forward, then walk the hands to one side to lengthen the opposite lat. Breathe into the side ribs.

Phase 3 · Strengthen

  1. Dumbbell Row (video) · 3 x 8-12 reps per side, every other day
    Hinge at your hips with a flat back. Pull the dumbbell up towards your hip, squeezing your shoulder blade towards your spine. Control the weight on the way down.
  2. Lat Pulldown (video) · 3 x 8-12 reps, every other day
    Using a machine or band, pull the bar down to your upper chest. Focus on driving your elbows down and back, feeling the contraction in your back muscles.

Suggested library videos for this muscle: Overhead Straight Arm Pulldown, Dynamic Childs Pose, Thread The Needle.

Habits that help

  • Adjust your desk setup so your mouse is close, preventing a constant forward reach.
  • When carrying bags, switch sides frequently or use a backpack with both straps.
  • Avoid sleeping with your arm stretched far overhead. Try hugging a pillow instead.
  • Warm up properly with dynamic stretches before swimming, climbing, or lifting weights.

What to expect

You can expect relief from the sharpest pain within 1-2 weeks of stretching and release work. Building strength and resilience can take 4-8 weeks of consistent exercise.

When to see a professional

Schedule a visit if your overhead reach doesn't improve, if you have persistent numbness or tingling in your fingers, or if the pain significantly limits your daily activities after 2 weeks. Get urgent care for any of these:

  • Sudden, severe back pain that is not related to movement.
  • Pain accompanied by weakness in the legs or changes in bowel/bladder control (seek immediate emergency care).
  • Numbness or tingling that is constant, severe, and doesn't change with position.
  • Pain associated with fever, night sweats, or unexplained weight loss.

Frequently asked questions

Can a tight latissimus dorsi cause pain at the bottom tip of the shoulder blade?

Often, yes. Bottom tip of the shoulder blade and down the back of the arm to the ring and little fingers. That referred pain is why it gets blamed on the joint or the spot that hurts instead of the muscle.

How long does latissimus dorsi pain take to settle?

You can expect relief from the sharpest pain within 1-2 weeks of stretching and release work. Building strength and resilience can take 4-8 weeks of consistent exercise.

When should I see someone about it?

What else can feel like latissimus dorsi pain?

Rotator cuff tear or tendinopathy, Cervical radiculopathy (pinched nerve in the neck), Thoracic outlet syndrome. An evaluation separates muscle pain from these, and anything that needs a physician is referred on.
