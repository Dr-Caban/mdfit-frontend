Where the lower trapezius sends pain

Up the back of the neck to the base of the skull, the top of the shoulder, and along the inner shoulder blade. A hidden driver of neck tension. People usually come in describing pain at the inner edge of the shoulder blade, where the neck meets the shoulder, base of the skull, on the shoulder blade, top of the shoulder or back of the neck.

What the lower trapezius does

Pulls the shoulder blade down and rotates it upward so the arm can reach overhead.

  • Starts at: Spinous processes of the middle and lower thoracic vertebrae (about T6 to T12).
  • Attaches to: Inner end of the spine of the shoulder blade.
  • Nerve supply: Spinal accessory nerve (cranial nerve XI) with sensory fibers from C3, C4.

Symptoms people describe

  • Ache up the back of the neck toward the base of the skull and behind the ear.
  • Ache across the top of the shoulder.
  • Ache along the inner edge of the shoulder blade.
  • Neck tension that keeps coming back even after neck stretches.

What makes it worse

Desk, phone or screens · Reaching overhead. If two or more of those match your day, this muscle moves up the list.

Quick self-check

Education, not a diagnosis. The more of these that ring true, the more likely the lower trapezius is involved:

  • Do you get neck or base-of-skull tension that never fully goes away with neck stretches?
  • Is there a tender spot low on the inner edge of the shoulder blade?
  • Do your shoulders creep up toward your ears when you reach overhead?
  • Do you sit slumped for long periods?
  • Does reaching overhead feel weak or tiring?

Prone Y lift. Lie face down with arms overhead in a Y, thumbs up, and lift the arms a few inches for 10 seconds. Suggests this muscle if: The arms feel weak and shaky, or you feel your familiar neck or shoulder-blade ache.

What it gets mistaken for

  • Upper trapezius or levator trigger points
  • Cervicogenic headache
  • Thoracic facet irritation
  • Shoulder impingement

Common causes

  • Slumped sitting that stretches and weakens the lower trap.
  • Shoulders shrugging during overhead work.
  • Tight chest and lats pulling the shoulder blade forward.
  • Little or no pulling exercise.

Dr. Jared's 3-phase relief plan

Work through the phases in order and move on when the current one feels easy and pain-free. Every video is in the free MD Fit app.

Phase 1 · Calm it down

  1. Lower shoulder-blade ball release · 60–90 sec, twice daily
    Place a ball against a wall low on the inner edge of the shoulder blade and hold gentle pressure on tender spots.
  2. Thoracic Extension Over Chair (video) · 2 x 8, daily
    Extend the upper back gently to undo slumping.

Phase 2 · Restore movement

  1. Scapular CARs (video) · 2 x 5 each way, daily
    Emphasize the down-and-back part of each circle.
  2. Baseball Cable Stretch (video) · 2 x 30 sec, daily
    Free the chest so the shoulder blade can sit back.

Phase 3 · Strengthen

  1. Floor Slide Scapular Retract (video) · 3 x 10, daily
    Slide the arms up while keeping the shoulders away from the ears.
  2. Face Pull (video) · 3 x 12, every other day
    Finish each rep with the shoulder blades down and back.

Suggested library videos for this muscle: Floor Slide Scapular Retract, Scapular CARs, Face Pull.

Habits that help

  • Sit back in the chair with support at mid-back.
  • Think 'shoulders in back pockets' during overhead tasks.

What to expect

Neck tension often eases within 2 weeks; lower-trap strength takes 4 to 6 weeks to build.

When to see a professional

Book an evaluation if neck or shoulder pain persists past 3 weeks or there are arm symptoms. Get urgent care for any of these:

  • Neck pain with arm weakness, numbness or loss of coordination.
  • Sudden severe headache.
  • Pain with fever or unexplained weight loss.
