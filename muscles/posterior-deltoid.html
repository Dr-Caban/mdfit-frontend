Where the posterior deltoid sends pain

Back of the shoulder and the back of the upper arm. People usually come in describing pain at the back of the shoulder.

What the posterior deltoid does

Pulls the arm back (extension) and turns it outward; works in rows, pulling and reaching behind you.

  • Starts at: Lower edge of the spine of the shoulder blade.
  • Attaches to: Deltoid tuberosity on the outside of the upper arm bone.
  • Nerve supply: Axillary nerve (C5, C6).

Symptoms people describe

  • Ache at the back of the shoulder and the back of the upper arm.
  • Pain reaching behind you, such as into a back pocket or the back seat.
  • Soreness after rowing, pulling or throwing.
  • Tender spots on the back of the shoulder cap.
  • Sometimes pain lying on the back of that shoulder.

What makes it worse

Reaching behind your back · Gripping, lifting or typing. If two or more of those match your day, this muscle moves up the list.

Quick self-check

Education, not a diagnosis. The more of these that ring true, the more likely the posterior deltoid is involved:

  • Is the pain at the back of the shoulder, behind the shoulder cap?
  • Does reaching behind you (back pocket, seat belt, back seat) bring it on?
  • Did it start after rowing, pulling or throwing?
  • Can you press on a tender spot at the back of the shoulder cap that matches your pain?
  • Does the pain stay in the shoulder and upper arm without tingling?

Resisted backward reach. Stand with the arm straight at your side and push it backward against a wall or your other hand for 5 seconds. Suggests this muscle if: Ache at the back of the shoulder or weakness compared with the other side.

What it gets mistaken for

  • Infraspinatus or teres minor trigger points
  • Posterior rotator cuff tendinopathy
  • Posterior shoulder capsule tightness
  • Cervical radiculopathy (C5–C6)

Common causes

  • High-volume rowing, pulling or throwing.
  • Sudden backward reach, such as grabbing something from the back seat.
  • Weak rotator cuff (infraspinatus, teres minor) leaving the rear delt to stabilize.
  • Rounded upper back keeping the rear delt on constant stretch.
  • Sleeping with the arm behind you.

Dr. Jared's 3-phase relief plan

Work through the phases in order and move on when the current one feels easy and pain-free. Every video is in the free MD Fit app.

Phase 1 · Calm it down

  1. Rear-delt ball release · 60–90 sec, twice daily
    Lie on your back with a ball under the back of the shoulder cap; let your weight sink in gently and breathe.
  2. Scapular CARs (video) · 2 x 5 circles each way, daily
    Smooth, pain-free shoulder-blade circles.
  3. Thread The Needle (video) · 2 x 8 per side, daily
    Rotate gently through the upper back to ease the back of the shoulder.

Phase 2 · Restore movement

  1. Cross-body stretch · 2 x 30 sec, daily
    Bring the arm across the chest and hold just above the elbow with the other hand. Keep the shoulder blade down.
  2. Floor Slide Scapular Retract (video) · 2 x 10, daily
    Slide the arms with the shoulder blades drawn back and down to balance the back of the shoulder.

Phase 3 · Strengthen

  1. Face Pull (video) · 3 x 12, every other day
    Light band, elbows high, pull apart at face height and pause.
  2. Rotator Cuff External Rotation (video) · 3 x 15, every other day
    Elbow tucked, rotate out slowly against a light band.
  3. Dumbbell Row (video) · 3 x 10, twice weekly
    Pull the elbow toward the hip, shoulder blade back first, lower slowly.

Suggested library videos for this muscle: Face Pull, Rotator Cuff External Rotation, Floor Slide Scapular Retract.

Habits that help

  • Turn your body instead of reaching straight back for objects.
  • Keep pulling volume increases to about 10 percent a week.
  • Sleep with the arm in front of you, not behind.

What to expect

Back-of-shoulder ache from the rear delt usually settles in 1 to 3 weeks; pulling strength returns over 4 to 6 weeks.

When to see a professional

Book an evaluation if reaching behind stays painful past 3 weeks or the shoulder feels unstable or clicks painfully. Get urgent care for any of these:

  • Shoulder that feels like it slips out the back.
  • Sudden weakness after a fall onto the outstretched arm.
  • Numbness over the outside of the shoulder.
  • Fever or a hot, swollen joint.

Frequently asked questions

Can a tight posterior deltoid cause pain at the back of the shoulder?

Often, yes. Back of the shoulder and the back of the upper arm. That referred pain is why it gets blamed on the joint or the spot that hurts instead of the muscle.

How long does posterior deltoid pain take to settle?

Back-of-shoulder ache from the rear delt usually settles in 1 to 3 weeks; pulling strength returns over 4 to 6 weeks.

When should I see someone about it?

Book an evaluation if reaching behind stays painful past 3 weeks or the shoulder feels unstable or clicks painfully.

What else can feel like posterior deltoid pain?

Infraspinatus or teres minor trigger points, Posterior rotator cuff tendinopathy, Posterior shoulder capsule tightness. An evaluation separates muscle pain from these, and anything that needs a physician is referred on.
