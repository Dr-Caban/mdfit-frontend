Where the flexor hallucis longus sends pain

The ball of the foot under the big toe, and along the inner ankle behind the bone; the dancer's tendon. People usually come in describing pain at the inner ankle and arch or sole of the foot.

What the flexor hallucis longus does

Flexes the big toe and helps point the foot downward. It provides the final push-off in walking, running and rising onto the toes.

  • Starts at: The lower two thirds of the posterior surface of the fibula and the interosseous membrane.
  • Attaches to: The base of the distal phalanx of the great toe, after running through a groove behind the inner ankle bone.
  • Nerve supply: Tibial nerve.

Symptoms people describe

  • Pain under the ball of the foot at the big toe.
  • Aching along the inner ankle, behind the bony bump.
  • Pain when pushing off, rising onto the toes or climbing stairs.
  • A creaking or catching sensation behind the inner ankle.
  • Stiffness bending the big toe upward.
  • Discomfort in dancers, runners and anyone doing repeated calf raises.

What makes it worse

Running or sports · Standing or walking · Stairs, squats or hills. If two or more of those match your day, this muscle moves up the list.

Quick self-check

Education, not a diagnosis. The more of these that ring true, the more likely the flexor hallucis longus is involved:

  • Does it hurt under the ball of your foot at the big toe when you push off?
  • Is there pain along the inner ankle behind the bony bump?
  • Is rising onto your toes or climbing stairs painful?
  • Do you feel catching or creaking behind the inner ankle?
  • Do you dance, run, or do a lot of calf raises?

Resisted big toe flexion. Try to curl the big toe downward against resistance from a finger under the toe. Suggests this muscle if: Pain behind the inner ankle or under the ball of the foot.

Inner ankle tendon palpation. Press just behind and below the inner ankle bone while slowly curling and straightening the big toe. Suggests this muscle if: Tenderness, or a creaking sensation felt under the fingers as the tendon moves.

What it gets mistaken for

  • Tibialis posterior tendinopathy, which runs in a neighbouring groove.
  • Tarsal tunnel syndrome.
  • Posterior ankle impingement.
  • Turf toe or big toe joint arthritis.
  • Achilles problems.

Common causes

  • Dance, particularly repeated rising onto the toes.
  • A sudden increase in running or hill work.
  • Repeated calf raise training.
  • Flat or over-pronating feet, which load the tendon behind the inner ankle.
  • Footwear with poor support.

Dr. Jared's 3-phase relief plan

Work through the phases in order and move on when the current one feels easy and pain-free. Every video is in the free MD Fit app.

Phase 1 · Calm it down

  1. Reduce push-off load · Two weeks
    Pause calf raises, hill running and dance work that loads the toes.
  2. Big toe mobility · Ten repetitions, twice daily
    Gently bend and straighten the big toe by hand through its comfortable range.
  3. Calf lengthening (video: Eccentric Calf Drops) · Ten slow repetitions, light
    Gentle controlled lowering, keeping it pain free.

Phase 2 · Restore movement

  1. Ankle mobility (video: Ankle Circles) · Ten each way, daily
    Restores motion at the ankle so the tendon is not working through a restricted range.
  2. Tendon gliding (video: Ankle Neuroglide) · Ten slow repetitions
    Gentle movement to keep the tendon sliding in its groove.
  3. Foot intrinsic work (video: Toe Walk) · Two sets of 20 metres
    Shares the push-off load across the whole foot.

Phase 3 · Strengthen

  1. Progressive calf loading (video: Eccentric Calf Drops) · Three sets of twelve, twice weekly
    Build strength through the whole calf and tendon complex.
  2. Big toe strength · Ten repetitions, twice daily
    Press the big toe into the floor and hold briefly, building gradually.
  3. Single leg hop progression (video: Single Leg Farmer Hop) · Three sets of ten
    Return to impact loading once the slower work is pain free.

Suggested library videos for this muscle: Eccentric Calf Drops, Toe Walk, Ankle Neuroglide.

Habits that help

  • Build calf and push-off loading gradually.
  • Choose shoes with adequate support for your foot type.
  • Warm up the ankle and big toe before dance or running.
  • Progress hill and speed work in stages.

What to expect

Tendon problems here commonly take six to twelve weeks because tendons adapt slowly. Early load reduction followed by graded strengthening gives the best results.

When to see a professional

Book an evaluation if inner ankle or big toe pain persists beyond three weeks, or sooner for sudden loss of push-off, inability to bear weight, or any foot problem in someone with diabetes. Get urgent care for any of these:

  • Inability to bear weight after an injury.
  • Sudden loss of the ability to push off, which may indicate a tendon rupture.
  • Ankle swelling with redness, warmth and fever.
  • Numbness in the sole of the foot.
  • Foot problems in someone with diabetes.

What else can feel like flexor hallucis longus pain?
