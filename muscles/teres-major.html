Where the teres major sends pain

Back of the shoulder and the back of the upper arm, sometimes the back of the forearm. Hurts reaching up and forward. People usually come in describing pain at the back of the upper arm or back of the shoulder.

What the teres major does

Pulls the arm down and back and turns it inward; works with the latissimus dorsi in pulling and climbing.

  • Starts at: Lower outer corner of the back of the shoulder blade.
  • Attaches to: Front of the upper arm bone (medial lip of the bicipital groove), next to the lat.
  • Nerve supply: Lower subscapular nerve (C5–C7).

Symptoms people describe

  • Ache at the back of the shoulder and down the back of the upper arm.
  • Pain reaching up and forward, such as to a high shelf or swimming.
  • Sometimes ache in the back of the forearm.
  • Tender thick band of muscle at the back of the armpit.
  • Stiffness lifting the arm all the way overhead.

What makes it worse

Reaching overhead · Gripping, lifting or typing. If two or more of those match your day, this muscle moves up the list.

Quick self-check

Education, not a diagnosis. The more of these that ring true, the more likely the teres major is involved:

  • Is there a tender band of muscle at the back of your armpit, at the bottom corner of the shoulder blade?
  • Does reaching high overhead or forward pull and hurt at the back of the shoulder?
  • Does the ache run down the back of the upper arm?
  • Did it start after pull-ups, rowing, swimming or climbing?
  • Is it worse at the end of the overhead reach rather than at the start?

Overhead reach on the wall. Stand with your back flat on a wall and raise the straight arm overhead, trying to touch the wall with your thumb without arching the low back. Suggests this muscle if: You cannot reach the wall and feel pulling or ache at the back of the armpit and shoulder.

What it gets mistaken for

  • Latissimus dorsi strain
  • Triceps (long head) trigger points
  • Posterior rotator cuff tendinopathy
  • Thoracic outlet syndrome

Common causes

  • Heavy pulling: pull-ups, rows, lat pulldowns, climbing.
  • Swimming and throwing.
  • Prolonged work with the arm raised.
  • Stiff upper back limiting overhead motion.
  • Weak lower trapezius so the shoulder blade does not rotate up.

Dr. Jared's 3-phase relief plan

Work through the phases in order and move on when the current one feels easy and pain-free. Every video is in the free MD Fit app.

Phase 1 · Calm it down

  1. Armpit ball release · 60–90 sec, twice daily
    Lie on your side with a ball under the back of the armpit, at the corner of the shoulder blade. Breathe and let it soften.
  2. Dynamic Childs Pose (video) · 2 x 6 slow reps, daily
    Sit back with arms reaching forward and breathe into the side ribs.

Phase 2 · Restore movement

  1. Thread The Needle (video) · 2 x 8 per side, daily
    Rotate and reach to lengthen the back of the shoulder.
  2. Thoracic Extension Over Chair (video) · 2 x 8, daily
    Extend the upper back so the arm can go overhead without the teres major pulling.
  3. Floor Slide Scapular Retract (video) · 2 x 10, daily
    Slide the arms up with the shoulder blades rotating smoothly.

Phase 3 · Strengthen

  1. Lat Pulldown (video) · 3 x 10 light, twice weekly
    Full overhead reach at the top, pull elbows to ribs, lower slowly.
  2. Dumbbell Row (video) · 3 x 10, twice weekly
    Controlled row with a pause at the top.

Suggested library videos for this muscle: Dynamic Childs Pose, Thread The Needle, Lat Pulldown.

Habits that help

  • Add pulling volume gradually.
  • Stretch the side of the body after swimming or climbing sessions.
  • Break up long overhead tasks every 15 minutes.

What to expect

Overhead reach usually improves within 2 weeks; full pulling tolerance in 4 to 6 weeks.

When to see a professional

Book an evaluation if you cannot raise the arm fully after 3 weeks or there is armpit swelling or nerve symptoms. Get urgent care for any of these:

  • A pop in the armpit with bruising after a heavy pull.
  • Swelling or a lump in the armpit.
  • Numbness or tingling down the inner arm.
  • Fever or unexplained weight loss.

Frequently asked questions

Can a tight teres major cause pain at the back of the upper arm?

Often, yes. Back of the shoulder and the back of the upper arm, sometimes the back of the forearm. Hurts reaching up and forward. That referred pain is why it gets blamed on the joint or the spot that hurts instead of the muscle.

How long does teres major pain take to settle?

Overhead reach usually improves within 2 weeks; full pulling tolerance in 4 to 6 weeks.

When should I see someone about it?

Book an evaluation if you cannot raise the arm fully after 3 weeks or there is armpit swelling or nerve symptoms.

What else can feel like teres major pain?

Latissimus dorsi strain, Triceps (long head) trigger points, Posterior rotator cuff tendinopathy. An evaluation separates muscle pain from these, and anything that needs a physician is referred on.
