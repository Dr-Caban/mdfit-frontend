Where the wrist flexors sends pain

Inside of the elbow (golfer's elbow) and the palm side of the wrist. People usually come in describing pain at the palm side of the wrist and hand or forearm. It is also a common hidden driver behind Golfer's Elbow (Medial Epicondylitis).

What the wrist flexors does

Bends the wrist forward and steadies it in pulling, lifting and throwing.

  • Starts at: Medial epicondyle (inside of the elbow) by the common flexor tendon.
  • Attaches to: Bases of the second metacarpal (FCR) and the pisiform and fifth metacarpal (FCU).
  • Nerve supply: Median nerve (FCR, C6, C7) and ulnar nerve (FCU, C7–T1).

Symptoms people describe

  • Pain at the inside of the elbow (golfer's elbow).
  • Ache on the palm side of the forearm and wrist.
  • Pain gripping, lifting or throwing.
  • Weaker grip.

What makes it worse

Gripping, lifting or typing. If two or more of those match your day, this muscle moves up the list.

Quick self-check

Education, not a diagnosis. The more of these that ring true, the more likely the wrist flexors is involved:

  • Is the pain at or just below the bony bump on the inside of the elbow?
  • Does bending the wrist forward against resistance hurt?
  • Does gripping, golf, throwing or lifting with the palm up bring it on?
  • Does it ache down the palm side of the forearm?
  • Do you ever get tingling in the ring and little fingers?

Resisted wrist flexion. With the elbow straight and palm up, bend the wrist forward against resistance from your other hand. Suggests this muscle if: Pain at the inside of the elbow.

What it gets mistaken for

  • Medial epicondylitis
  • Ulnar nerve irritation (cubital tunnel)
  • Pronator teres syndrome
  • Cervical radiculopathy (C7–C8)

Common causes

  • Golf, throwing and racquet sports.
  • Heavy gripping and lifting with the palms up.
  • Climbing and pull-ups.
  • Sudden increase in manual work.

Dr. Jared's 3-phase relief plan

Work through the phases in order and move on when the current one feels easy and pain-free. Every video is in the free MD Fit app.

Phase 1 · Calm it down

  1. Forearm flexor release · 60 sec, twice daily
    Roll a ball along the upper palm-side forearm while slowly bending the wrist.
  2. Weighted Wrist Circles (video) · 2 x 10 each way, no weight, daily
    Gentle motion.

Phase 2 · Restore movement

  1. Wrist flexor stretch · 3 x 30 sec, twice daily
    Elbow straight, palm up, gently pull the fingers and wrist back.

Phase 3 · Strengthen

  1. Eccentric Wrist Pronator (video) · 3 x 15, daily
    Slow, controlled rotation to load the inner-elbow tendons.
  2. Eccentric wrist curl · 3 x 15, daily
    Palm up on a table, lift a light weight with both hands and lower slowly with one.

Suggested library videos for this muscle: Eccentric Wrist Pronator, Weighted Wrist Circles.

Habits that help

  • Check golf and throwing technique.
  • Use gloves or thicker grips for heavy holding.

What to expect

Tendon-related inner-elbow pain usually needs 6 to 12 weeks of consistent loading.

When to see a professional

Book an evaluation if pain persists past 4 weeks or finger numbness appears. Get urgent care for any of these:

  • Numbness and weakness in the ring and little fingers.
  • Elbow instability after a throwing injury.
  • Swelling with redness or fever.

Frequently asked questions

Can a tight wrist flexors cause pain at the palm side of the wrist and hand?

Often, yes. Inside of the elbow (golfer's elbow) and the palm side of the wrist. That referred pain is why it gets blamed on the joint or the spot that hurts instead of the muscle.

How long does wrist flexors pain take to settle?

Tendon-related inner-elbow pain usually needs 6 to 12 weeks of consistent loading.

When should I see someone about it?

Book an evaluation if pain persists past 4 weeks or finger numbness appears.

What else can feel like wrist flexors pain?

Medial epicondylitis, Ulnar nerve irritation (cubital tunnel), Pronator teres syndrome. An evaluation separates muscle pain from these, and anything that needs a physician is referred on.
