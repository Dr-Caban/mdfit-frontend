Where the coracobrachialis sends pain

Front of the shoulder, the back of the upper arm and forearm, and the back of the hand toward the middle finger. People usually come in describing pain at the front of the shoulder, back of the hand or wrist or back of the upper arm.

What the coracobrachialis does

Pulls the arm forward and toward the body; helps hold the ball of the shoulder in place.

  • Starts at: Coracoid process (the bony hook at the front of the shoulder blade).
  • Attaches to: Inner side of the middle of the upper arm bone.
  • Nerve supply: Musculocutaneous nerve (C5–C7), which passes through the muscle.

Symptoms people describe

  • Deep ache at the front of the shoulder.
  • Pain on the back of the upper arm and forearm, sometimes to the back of the hand and middle finger.
  • Pain reaching behind your back or lifting the arm overhead.
  • Tenderness high on the inner upper arm near the armpit.

What makes it worse

Reaching overhead · Reaching behind your back. If two or more of those match your day, this muscle moves up the list.

Quick self-check

Education, not a diagnosis. The more of these that ring true, the more likely the coracobrachialis is involved:

  • Is there a tender spot high on the inner upper arm, just below the front of the armpit?
  • Does pain jump from the front of the shoulder to the back of the arm or hand?
  • Does reaching behind your back (tucking in a shirt) bring it on?
  • Did it start after push-ups, dips or bench pressing?
  • Does the outside of your forearm ever feel numb?

Hand-behind-back reach. Reach the hand behind your back and slide it up the spine; compare with the other side. Suggests this muscle if: Front-of-shoulder ache and a lower reach than the other side.

What it gets mistaken for

  • Biceps (short head) tendinopathy
  • Subscapularis trigger points
  • Cervical radiculopathy (C7)
  • Musculocutaneous nerve irritation

Common causes

  • Push-ups, dips and pressing with the elbows flared.
  • Carrying heavy items in front of the body.
  • Rock climbing and gymnastics.
  • Prolonged reaching forward and across the body.

Dr. Jared's 3-phase relief plan

Work through the phases in order and move on when the current one feels easy and pain-free. Every video is in the free MD Fit app.

Phase 1 · Calm it down

  1. Inner-arm pressure release · 60 sec, twice daily
    Press gently with the thumb high on the inner upper arm just below the armpit, holding until it eases. Stop if you feel tingling.
  2. Shoulder Drop Hold (video) · 3 x 20 sec, daily
    Let the shoulder settle down and back.

Phase 2 · Restore movement

  1. Baseball Cable Stretch (video) · 2 x 30 sec per side, daily
    Arm out and slightly behind the body to lengthen the front of the shoulder.
  2. Rotator Cuff Internal Rotation (video) · 2 x 12 light, daily
    Gentle cuff work to share the load of centering the shoulder.

Phase 3 · Strengthen

  1. Wall Push Up (video) · 3 x 12, every other day
    Elbows at 45 degrees, not flared.
  2. Face Pull (video) · 3 x 12, every other day
    Balance pushing with pulling.

Suggested library videos for this muscle: Baseball Cable Stretch, Shoulder Drop Hold.

Habits that help

  • Keep elbows tucked to about 45 degrees on push-ups and presses.
  • Carry loads close to the body.

What to expect

Usually settles within 2 to 3 weeks with reduced pressing and gentle stretching.

When to see a professional

Book an evaluation if forearm numbness or elbow weakness appears, or pain persists past 3 weeks. Get urgent care for any of these:

  • Numbness over the outside of the forearm with weak elbow bending.
  • A lump or swelling in the armpit.
  • Chest pain with shortness of breath: call 911.

Frequently asked questions

Can a tight coracobrachialis cause pain at the front of the shoulder?

Often, yes. Front of the shoulder, the back of the upper arm and forearm, and the back of the hand toward the middle finger. That referred pain is why it gets blamed on the joint or the spot that hurts instead of the muscle.

How long does coracobrachialis pain take to settle?

Usually settles within 2 to 3 weeks with reduced pressing and gentle stretching.

When should I see someone about it?

Book an evaluation if forearm numbness or elbow weakness appears, or pain persists past 3 weeks.

What else can feel like coracobrachialis pain?

Biceps (short head) tendinopathy, Subscapularis trigger points, Cervical radiculopathy (C7). An evaluation separates muscle pain from these, and anything that needs a physician is referred on.
