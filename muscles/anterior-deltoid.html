Where the anterior deltoid sends pain

Front of the shoulder and down the front of the upper arm, close to the muscle itself. Often blamed on the joint. People usually come in describing pain at the front of the shoulder.

What the anterior deltoid does

Raises the arm forward (flexion), turns it inward and pulls it across the body; drives pressing and front raises.

  • Starts at: Front edge of the outer third of the collarbone.
  • Attaches to: Deltoid tuberosity on the outside of the upper arm bone (humerus).
  • Nerve supply: Axillary nerve (C5, C6).

Symptoms people describe

  • Ache at the front of the shoulder, right over the muscle, that people often assume is the joint.
  • Pain lifting the arm forward, pressing overhead or pushing a door open.
  • Tenderness you can press on just below the front of the collarbone tip.
  • Pain after bench pressing, push-ups or long periods holding the arms forward (steering, typing).
  • Usually no numbness or tingling.

What makes it worse

Reaching overhead · Gripping, lifting or typing. If two or more of those match your day, this muscle moves up the list.

Quick self-check

Education, not a diagnosis. The more of these that ring true, the more likely the anterior deltoid is involved:

  • Is the pain at the very front of the shoulder, just below the bony tip of the collarbone?
  • Does lifting your straight arm forward to shoulder height bring it on?
  • Did it start after pressing, push-ups or a lot of reaching forward?
  • Can you press on a tender spot in the front of the shoulder that recreates your ache?
  • Does it stay around the shoulder rather than travel past the elbow?

Resisted forward raise. Stand with your arm straight at your side, thumb up. Raise it forward to shoulder height while your other hand pushes down on the wrist. Suggests this muscle if: The front of the shoulder aches or the arm feels weak compared with the other side.

Front-delt press test. With the arm relaxed, press firmly into the front of the shoulder just below the collarbone tip for 10 seconds. Suggests this muscle if: It reproduces your familiar ache at the front of the shoulder.

What it gets mistaken for

  • Biceps tendinopathy (long head)
  • Rotator cuff tendinopathy or tear
  • Shoulder impingement
  • AC joint sprain
  • Referred pain from the infraspinatus

Common causes

  • Heavy or frequent pressing (bench, push-ups, overhead press) without enough pulling work.
  • Rounded shoulders and a forward head posture.
  • Long periods with the arms held forward: driving, typing, carrying.
  • Sudden overload such as catching a fall or lifting a heavy box to a high shelf.
  • Weak rear delts and rotator cuff leaving the front delt to do too much.

Dr. Jared's 3-phase relief plan

Work through the phases in order and move on when the current one feels easy and pain-free. Every video is in the free MD Fit app.

Phase 1 · Calm it down

  1. Front-delt pressure release · 60–90 sec, twice daily
    Lean the front of your shoulder into a ball against a door frame. Find the tender spot and hold gentle pressure while breathing slowly until it eases.
  2. Scapular CARs (video) · 2 x 5 slow circles each way, daily
    Make slow, controlled circles with the shoulder blade, staying in a pain-free range.
  3. Shoulder Drop Hold (video) · 3 x 20 sec, daily
    Let the shoulder settle down and back away from the ear and hold, keeping the neck relaxed.

Phase 2 · Restore movement

  1. Baseball Cable Stretch (video) · 2 x 30 sec per side, daily
    Open the front of the shoulder and chest gently; stop at a stretch, not pain.
  2. Thoracic Open Book (video) · 2 x 8 per side, daily
    Rotate through the upper back so the shoulder can sit back without straining the front delt.
  3. Face Pull (video) · 2 x 12, every other day
    Light band, elbows high, pull toward your face and squeeze the shoulder blades to balance pressing.

Phase 3 · Strengthen

  1. Wall Push Up (video) · 3 x 12, every other day
    Slow 3-second lowering, shoulder blades gliding around the ribs, no front-shoulder pinch.
  2. Strict Press (video) · 3 x 8 light, twice weekly
    Light dumbbells, ribs down, press in the slightly-forward 'scapular plane'. Progress only if pain-free next day.
  3. Rotator Cuff External Rotation (video) · 3 x 15, every other day
    Build the cuff that keeps the ball centered so the front delt is not overworked.

Suggested library videos for this muscle: Scapular CARs, Shoulder Drop Hold, Wall Push Up.

Habits that help

  • Balance every set of pressing with at least one set of pulling.
  • Bring the keyboard and steering hands lower and closer so the arms are not held forward.
  • Carry bags close to the body or on alternating sides.
  • Warm up the shoulder with circles before pressing.

What to expect

Muscle-driven front-shoulder pain usually eases within 1 to 3 weeks of release and load management; full pressing strength returns over 4 to 6 weeks.

When to see a professional

Book an evaluation if pain persists beyond 3 weeks, you cannot raise the arm, or the shoulder catches, clicks painfully or feels unstable. Get urgent care for any of these:

  • A pop followed by bruising or a bulge in the arm.
  • Inability to lift the arm at all after a fall or injury.
  • Shoulder pain with chest pressure, shortness of breath or sweating.
  • Fever, redness or a hot, swollen shoulder.

Frequently asked questions

Can a tight anterior deltoid cause pain at the front of the shoulder?

Often, yes. Front of the shoulder and down the front of the upper arm, close to the muscle itself. Often blamed on the joint. That referred pain is why it gets blamed on the joint or the spot that hurts instead of the muscle.

How long does anterior deltoid pain take to settle?

Muscle-driven front-shoulder pain usually eases within 1 to 3 weeks of release and load management; full pressing strength returns over 4 to 6 weeks.

When should I see someone about it?

Book an evaluation if pain persists beyond 3 weeks, you cannot raise the arm, or the shoulder catches, clicks painfully or feels unstable.

What else can feel like anterior deltoid pain?

Biceps tendinopathy (long head), Rotator cuff tendinopathy or tear, Shoulder impingement. An evaluation separates muscle pain from these, and anything that needs a physician is referred on.
