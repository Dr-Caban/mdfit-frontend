Where the finger flexors sends pain

Palm and the front of the fingers, sometimes a 'trigger finger' catching feeling. People usually come in describing pain at the palm side of the wrist and hand. It is also a common hidden driver behind Golfer's Elbow (Medial Epicondylitis).

What the finger flexors does

Closes the fingers and thumb into a grip.

  • Starts at: Medial epicondyle, upper forearm bones and the membrane between them.
  • Attaches to: Middle and end segments of the fingers and thumb (palm side).
  • Nerve supply: Median nerve and, for the ring and little fingers, the ulnar nerve (C7–T1).

Symptoms people describe

  • Ache in the palm and the front of the fingers.
  • A catching or locking finger when you open the hand.
  • Forearm fatigue and ache after gripping.
  • Stiff fingers in the morning.

What makes it worse

Gripping, lifting or typing. If two or more of those match your day, this muscle moves up the list.

Quick self-check

Education, not a diagnosis. The more of these that ring true, the more likely the finger flexors is involved:

  • Do you feel ache in the palm or along the front of the fingers when gripping?
  • Does a finger catch or click when you open your hand?
  • Does long gripping (tools, phone, steering wheel, climbing) bring it on?
  • Is the forearm on the palm side tender when you squeeze it?
  • Are the fingers stiff first thing in the morning?

Grip and release. Make a tight fist for 10 seconds, then open the hand fully and spread the fingers. Suggests this muscle if: Ache in the palm or forearm, or a finger that catches as it opens.

What it gets mistaken for

  • Trigger finger (stenosing tenosynovitis)
  • Carpal tunnel syndrome
  • Hand arthritis
  • Dupuytren's contracture

Common causes

  • Long gripping: tools, phone, steering, climbing.
  • Heavy carrying with a narrow handle.
  • Repetitive pinching and clicking.
  • Diabetes and inflammatory conditions (increase trigger-finger risk).

Dr. Jared's 3-phase relief plan

Work through the phases in order and move on when the current one feels easy and pain-free. Every video is in the free MD Fit app.

Phase 1 · Calm it down

  1. Palm and forearm release · 60 sec, twice daily
    Roll a ball in the palm and along the palm-side forearm while opening and closing the hand.
  2. Tendon glides · 10 reps, 3 times daily
    Move slowly through straight hand, hook fist, full fist, tabletop and straight fist.

Phase 2 · Restore movement

  1. Finger flexor stretch · 3 x 30 sec, twice daily
    Elbow straight, palm up, gently pull the fingers back.
  2. Weighted Wrist Circles (video) · 2 x 10 each way, daily
    Light, smooth wrist motion.

Phase 3 · Strengthen

  1. Eccentric Wrist Pronator (video) · 3 x 12, every other day
    Build forearm control.
  2. Farmer Carry (video) · 3 x 30 m, twice weekly
    Thick handles if available; stop before grip fails.

Suggested library videos for this muscle: Eccentric Wrist Pronator, Weighted Wrist Circles.

Habits that help

  • Use thicker grips and gloves.
  • Switch hands and take breaks during long gripping.
  • Open and spread the hands every 30 minutes.

What to expect

Muscle ache eases within 2 weeks; catching fingers may take 4 to 8 weeks.

When to see a professional

Book an evaluation if a finger locks, numbness appears, or symptoms persist past 4 weeks. Get urgent care for any of these:

  • A finger locked closed that will not open.
  • Red, hot, swollen finger (possible infection).
  • Numbness in the thumb and first fingers that wakes you at night.

Frequently asked questions

Can a tight finger flexors cause pain at the palm side of the wrist and hand?

Often, yes. Palm and the front of the fingers, sometimes a 'trigger finger' catching feeling. That referred pain is why it gets blamed on the joint or the spot that hurts instead of the muscle.

How long does finger flexors pain take to settle?

Muscle ache eases within 2 weeks; catching fingers may take 4 to 8 weeks.

When should I see someone about it?

Book an evaluation if a finger locks, numbness appears, or symptoms persist past 4 weeks.

What else can feel like finger flexors pain?

Trigger finger (stenosing tenosynovitis), Carpal tunnel syndrome, Hand arthritis. An evaluation separates muscle pain from these, and anything that needs a physician is referred on.
