Where the intercostals sends pain

Sharp or aching pain along the rib, worse with deep breaths, coughing or twisting. People usually come in describing pain at the along one rib, side of the ribs or belly or side of the abdomen.

What the intercostals does

Elevate and depress the ribs to expand and contract the chest cavity, allowing you to breathe.

  • Starts at: The lower border of one rib.
  • Attaches to: The upper border of the rib below.
  • Nerve supply: Intercostal nerves (from thoracic spinal nerves T1-T11).

Symptoms people describe

  • A sharp, stabbing, or aching pain located directly between or along a single rib.
  • Pain that gets significantly worse with a deep breath, cough, sneeze, or laugh.
  • Tenderness to the touch in the space between two ribs.
  • A feeling of tightness or restriction around your chest.
  • Pain that is very localized to one specific spot, rather than being widespread.
  • Difficulty twisting or bending your torso without sharp pain.

What makes it worse

Deep breaths or coughing · Turning your head or body. If two or more of those match your day, this muscle moves up the list.

Quick self-check

Education, not a diagnosis. The more of these that ring true, the more likely the intercostals is involved:

  • Is your pain sharp and located in a specific spot on your rib cage that you can point to with one finger?
  • Does taking a deep breath, coughing, or sneezing make the pain dramatically worse?
  • Can you press on a spot between your ribs and reproduce the exact pain?
  • Did the pain start after a bout of heavy coughing, a sudden twisting motion, or a direct impact?
  • Does twisting your upper body trigger the pain?

Palpation Test. Gently press with one or two fingers in the spaces between your ribs in the painful area. Compare the sensation to the same area on the opposite, non-painful side. Suggests this muscle if: Pressing on a specific spot between the ribs reproduces your sharp pain.

What it gets mistaken for

  • Rib fracture
  • Serratus anterior muscle strain
  • Pleurisy or other lung conditions
  • Shingles (herpes zoster), which often follows a rib line
  • Costochondritis (inflammation of cartilage connecting ribs to the breastbone)

Common causes

  • Forceful and repetitive coughing or sneezing.
  • A sudden, sharp twisting of the torso, such as in golf, tennis, or reaching into the back seat of a car.
  • A direct blow or impact to the chest or ribs.
  • Over-stretching the torso, such as reaching for something far to the side.
  • Lifting an object that is too heavy with poor technique.

Dr. Jared's 3-phase relief plan

Work through the phases in order and move on when the current one feels easy and pain-free. Every video is in the free MD Fit app.

Phase 1 · Calm it down

  1. Gentle Diaphragmatic Breathing (video: 90/90 Breathing) · 5 minutes, 2-3 times daily
    Lie comfortably on your back. Breathe in gently through your nose, letting your belly rise, but only to the point before pain begins. Exhale slowly and fully.
  2. Supported Side Stretch (video: Serratus Stretch Breathing) · 2 x 10 gentle breaths, once daily
    Lie on your non-painful side with a pillow or rolled towel under your ribs. Let your body relax over the pillow to create a gentle opening stretch in the painful area as you breathe.

Phase 2 · Restore movement

  1. Cat-Cow (video: Cat Cow Desk Variation) · 2 x 10 cycles, once daily
    On your hands and knees (or seated), gently round your spine up towards the ceiling, then slowly arch it down. Move slowly and within a comfortable, pain-free range of motion.
  2. Lying Open Book (video) · 2 x 8 reps per side, once daily
    Lie on your side with knees bent and arms extended in front of you. Keeping your hips still, rotate your top arm and torso open, as if opening a book. Only go as far as is comfortable.

Phase 3 · Strengthen

  1. Core Brace (video: Core Brace Intro Seated) · 3 x 10 second holds, several times a day
    Gently tighten your abdominal muscles as if you are about to be poked in the stomach. Hold this gentle brace while breathing normally to help stabilize the rib cage.
  2. Dead Bug (video) · 3 x 8 reps per side, every other day
    Lie on your back with knees bent at 90 degrees. Slowly lower one opposite arm and leg towards the floor while keeping your core braced and low back flat. Start with small movements.

Suggested library videos for this muscle: 90/90 Breathing, Serratus Stretch Breathing, Lying Open Book.

Habits that help

  • When you need to cough or sneeze, hug a pillow against your chest to brace the area and reduce painful movement.
  • Avoid sudden twisting motions. When turning, move your feet and let your whole body turn together.
  • Use proper lifting technique: bend at the knees and hips, and keep objects close to your body.
  • Sleep on your back or the unaffected side to avoid putting pressure on the strained area.

What to expect

Intercostal strains can be very painful. Expect the sharpest pain to subside in 1-3 weeks, but a dull ache may linger for 4-6 weeks or more. It is important to avoid re-injury during this time.

When to see a professional

See a professional if the pain is severe and unmanageable, if you have any red flags, or if there is no improvement after 2 weeks of gentle care. Get urgent care for any of these:

  • Severe difficulty breathing or shortness of breath (call 911).
  • Pain accompanied by fever, chills, or a cough that produces colored phlegm.
  • Pain that occurred after a major trauma or fall, with risk of fracture or internal injury.
  • A visible deformity or a 'crunching' sound in the rib cage.

Frequently asked questions

Can a tight intercostals cause pain at the along one rib?

Often, yes. Sharp or aching pain along the rib, worse with deep breaths, coughing or twisting. That referred pain is why it gets blamed on the joint or the spot that hurts instead of the muscle.

How long does intercostals pain take to settle?

Intercostal strains can be very painful. Expect the sharpest pain to subside in 1-3 weeks, but a dull ache may linger for 4-6 weeks or more. It is important to avoid re-injury during this time.

When should I see someone about it?

What else can feel like intercostals pain?

Rib fracture, Serratus anterior muscle strain, Pleurisy or other lung conditions. An evaluation separates muscle pain from these, and anything that needs a physician is referred on.
