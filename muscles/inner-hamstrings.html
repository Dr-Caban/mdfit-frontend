Where the semitendinosus & semimembranosus sends pain

The buttock crease, back of the thigh and the inner back of the knee, sometimes the inner calf. People usually come in describing pain at the buttock crease, back of the thigh, inside of the knee or back of the knee.

What the semitendinosus & semimembranosus does

Bend the knee, extend the hip and turn the lower leg inward.

  • Starts at: Sitting bone (ischial tuberosity).
  • Attaches to: Inner upper shin (semitendinosus via the pes anserinus; semimembranosus at the back-inner tibia).
  • Nerve supply: Tibial part of the sciatic nerve (L5–S2).

Symptoms people describe

  • Ache at the buttock crease and sitting bone.
  • Pain down the back of the thigh.
  • Ache at the inner back of the knee, sometimes into the inner calf.
  • Pain sitting on hard chairs.

What makes it worse

Sitting · Running or sports. If two or more of those match your day, this muscle moves up the list.

Quick self-check

Education, not a diagnosis. The more of these that ring true, the more likely the semitendinosus & semimembranosus is involved:

  • Is the pain at the inner back of the knee or the buttock crease?
  • Does sitting on a hard chair hurt at the sitting bone?
  • Does bending forward with straight legs pull at the inner back of the thigh?
  • Did it start after running hills, sprinting or deadlifts?
  • Does it ache into the inner calf?

Straight-leg raise with inward turn. Lie on your back and raise the straight leg with the foot turned slightly out, compared with the other side. Suggests this muscle if: Pulling or ache along the inner back of the thigh before the other side.

What it gets mistaken for

  • Proximal hamstring tendinopathy
  • Pes anserine bursitis
  • Baker's cyst
  • Lumbar radiculopathy

Common causes

  • Long sitting on hard surfaces.
  • Hill running and sprinting.
  • Heavy deadlifts without progression.
  • Weak glutes.

Habits that help

  • Use a cushion and stand up every 30 minutes.
  • Build hill running gradually.

What to expect

Usually improves in 2 to 4 weeks; tendon pain at the sitting bone can take 8 to 12 weeks.

When to see a professional

Book an evaluation if sitting pain persists past 3 weeks, or after any pop with bruising. Get urgent care for any of these:

  • A pop and bruising at the buttock crease.
  • Calf swelling, warmth or redness (possible clot).
  • Numbness in the sole of the foot with weakness.
