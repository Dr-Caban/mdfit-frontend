Where the finger extensors sends pain

Back of the forearm and back of the hand into the middle or ring finger; a 'stiff fingers' feeling. People usually come in describing pain at the forearm or back of the hand or wrist. It is also a common hidden driver behind Tennis Elbow (Lateral Epicondylitis).

What the finger extensors does

Straightens the fingers and helps lift the wrist.

  • Starts at: Lateral epicondyle (outside of the elbow) by the common extensor tendon.
  • Attaches to: Backs of the fingers (extensor hood of fingers 2 to 5).
  • Nerve supply: Posterior interosseous nerve, a branch of the radial nerve (C7, C8).

Symptoms people describe

  • Ache in the back of the forearm.
  • Pain on the back of the hand into the middle or ring finger.
  • Stiff, achy fingers after typing or gaming.
  • Outer-elbow ache with finger use more than wrist use.

What makes it worse

Gripping, lifting or typing · Desk, phone or screens. If two or more of those match your day, this muscle moves up the list.

Quick self-check

Education, not a diagnosis. The more of these that ring true, the more likely the finger extensors is involved:

  • Does straightening your middle finger against resistance cause outer-elbow or forearm pain?
  • Does pain run down the back of the hand toward the middle or ring finger?
  • Do typing, gaming or playing an instrument bring it on?
  • Do your fingers feel stiff after heavy use?
  • Is the tender spot in the upper back of the forearm, not right on the elbow bump?

Resisted middle-finger extension. With the elbow straight and palm down, press down on the middle finger while trying to straighten it. Suggests this muscle if: Pain in the back of the forearm or outside of the elbow.

What it gets mistaken for

  • Tennis elbow
  • Radial tunnel / posterior interosseous nerve irritation
  • Hand arthritis
  • Cervical radiculopathy (C7)

Common causes

  • Long typing, mouse, phone or gaming sessions.
  • Playing piano or guitar.
  • Gripping and releasing tools repeatedly.
  • Wrist bent back while typing.

Dr. Jared's 3-phase relief plan

Work through the phases in order and move on when the current one feels easy and pain-free. Every video is in the free MD Fit app.

Phase 1 · Calm it down

  1. Back-of-forearm release · 60 sec, twice daily
    Roll a ball along the upper back of the forearm while opening and closing the fingers slowly.
  2. Weighted Wrist Circles (video) · 2 x 10 each way, no weight, daily
    Keep the forearm and fingers moving.

Phase 2 · Restore movement

  1. Finger and wrist flexion stretch · 3 x 30 sec, twice daily
    Make a gentle fist, then bend the wrist down with the elbow straight until a mild stretch.

Phase 3 · Strengthen

  1. Eccentric Wrist Drops (video) · 3 x 15, daily
    Slow lowering to build tendon tolerance.
  2. Rubber band finger opening · 3 x 20, daily
    Place a rubber band around the fingertips and open the fingers against it slowly.

Suggested library videos for this muscle: Eccentric Wrist Drops, Weighted Wrist Circles.

Habits that help

  • Take a 1-minute break every 30 minutes of typing or gaming.
  • Keep wrists neutral on the keyboard.

What to expect

Usually improves in 2 to 6 weeks with load changes.

When to see a professional

Book an evaluation if fingers become weak or numb, or pain persists past 4 weeks. Get urgent care for any of these:

  • Inability to straighten the fingers (finger drop).
  • Swollen, hot finger joints.
  • Numbness on the back of the hand.

Frequently asked questions

Can a tight finger extensors cause pain at the forearm?

Often, yes. Back of the forearm and back of the hand into the middle or ring finger; a 'stiff fingers' feeling. That referred pain is why it gets blamed on the joint or the spot that hurts instead of the muscle.

How long does finger extensors pain take to settle?

Usually improves in 2 to 6 weeks with load changes.

When should I see someone about it?

Book an evaluation if fingers become weak or numb, or pain persists past 4 weeks.

What else can feel like finger extensors pain?

Tennis elbow, Radial tunnel / posterior interosseous nerve irritation, Hand arthritis. An evaluation separates muscle pain from these, and anything that needs a physician is referred on.
