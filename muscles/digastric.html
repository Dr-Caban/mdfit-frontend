Where the digastric sends pain

Under the chin and into the floor of the mouth, the front lower teeth, and behind the angle of the jaw toward the ear. People usually come in describing pain at the front of the neck.

What the digastric does

Opens the jaw against resistance and raises the hyoid bone during swallowing and speech.

  • Starts at: The posterior belly arises from the mastoid notch of the temporal bone; the anterior belly from the digastric fossa on the inner surface of the lower jaw.
  • Attaches to: Both bellies join at an intermediate tendon anchored to the hyoid bone by a fibrous sling.
  • Nerve supply: Anterior belly: trigeminal nerve (mandibular division). Posterior belly: facial nerve (cranial nerve VII).

Symptoms people describe

  • Ache under the chin or in the floor of the mouth.
  • Pain in the four lower front teeth with no dental cause.
  • Soreness behind the angle of the jaw, near the top of the neck.
  • A sense of a lump or tightness in the throat when swallowing.
  • Discomfort when the head is held forward for long periods.

What makes it worse

Clenching or chewing · Stress · Desk, phone or screens. If two or more of those match your day, this muscle moves up the list.

Quick self-check

Education, not a diagnosis. The more of these that ring true, the more likely the digastric is involved:

  • Do you have an ache under your chin or in the floor of your mouth?
  • Do your lower front teeth ache when the dentist has found nothing?
  • Does it feel like something is in your throat when you swallow?
  • Do you spend long periods with your head jutted forward at a screen?
  • Do you clench your teeth?

Under-chin palpation. With the jaw relaxed, press gently upward into the soft area just behind the point of the chin, then work backwards toward the angle of the jaw. Suggests this muscle if: Tenderness reproduces the under-chin ache or the front tooth pain.

Resisted opening. Place a fist under your chin and try to open your mouth gently against it. Suggests this muscle if: Pain under the chin rather than in the joint.

What it gets mistaken for

  • Dental problems in the lower incisors.
  • Salivary gland inflammation or a stone.
  • Swollen lymph nodes under the jaw.
  • Thyroid or throat problems.
  • Temporomandibular joint dysfunction.

Common causes

  • Sustained forward head posture at a desk or phone.
  • Mouth breathing, which changes the resting position of the tongue and hyoid.
  • Clenching and grinding.
  • Whiplash, which loads the front of the neck.
  • Prolonged dental work.

Dr. Jared's 3-phase relief plan

Work through the phases in order and move on when the current one feels easy and pain-free. Every video is in the free MD Fit app.

Phase 1 · Calm it down

  1. Tongue and jaw rest position · Throughout the day
    Tongue on the roof of the mouth, teeth apart, lips closed, breathing through the nose. This unloads the muscles that lift the hyoid.
  2. Gentle front-of-neck release (video: SCM Release Seated) · Two minutes per side
    Work the neighbouring front-of-neck muscles first; this area responds better to light, slow pressure than to deep work.
  3. Warmth and quiet breathing (video: Box Breathing) · 5 minutes
    Calm breathing through the nose settles the swallowing muscles.

Phase 2 · Restore movement

  1. Chin tuck without force (video: Chin Glide Seated) · Two sets of ten, several times daily
    Glide, do not jam. The aim is to bring the head back over the shoulders so the front of the neck is not holding on.
  2. Swallow retraining · Ten swallows, twice daily
    Swallow with the tongue tip on the roof of the mouth and the jaw relaxed, rather than bracing the jaw shut.
  3. Thoracic opening (video: Thoracic Extension Over Chair) · Ten repetitions daily
    A rounded upper back forces the head forward, which keeps this muscle working.

Phase 3 · Strengthen

  1. Deep neck flexor endurance (video: Deep Cervical Flexor Nods Seated) · Ten second holds, five repetitions, daily
    The deep flexors should hold the head, rather than the swallowing muscles doing it.
  2. Postural resets (video: Standing Posture Reset) · Several times daily
    Short, frequent corrections through the day.
  3. Trunk stability (video: Dead Bug) · Three sets of ten each side
    Gives the neck a stable base.

Suggested library videos for this muscle: Chin Glide Seated, SCM Release Seated, Deep Cervical Flexor Nods Seated.

Habits that help

  • Raise your screen so the head is not carried forward.
  • Breathe through the nose where you can.
  • Avoid chewing gum while symptoms are active.
  • Have persistent lumps or swelling under the jaw checked rather than treated as muscular.

What to expect

Symptoms from this muscle usually ease within two to four weeks once head position and tongue rest position change. Throat tightness often settles before the tooth ache does.

When to see a professional

Book an evaluation once a physician or dentist has excluded infection, gland and dental causes. Any lump, swelling or swallowing difficulty needs a physician first. Get urgent care for any of these:

  • A hard or growing lump under the jaw or in the neck.
  • Swelling in the floor of the mouth, which can obstruct breathing.
  • Fever with swelling under the jaw.
  • Persistent hoarseness or difficulty swallowing solids.
  • Unexplained weight loss alongside throat symptoms.
