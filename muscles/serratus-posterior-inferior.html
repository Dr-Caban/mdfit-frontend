Where the serratus posterior inferior sends pain

A nagging ache across the lower ribs at the back, just above the waist, that is often mistaken for kidney or low back pain. People usually come in describing pain at the flank or side of the low back or side of the ribs.

What the serratus posterior inferior does

Draws the lower ribs downward and backward, steadying them against the pull of the diaphragm during breathing. It is thin and often overlooked.

  • Starts at: The spinous processes of T11 through L3 and the thoracolumbar fascia.
  • Attaches to: The lower borders of ribs nine through twelve, near their angles.
  • Nerve supply: Intercostal nerves T9 to T12.

Symptoms people describe

  • A nagging ache across the lower ribs at the back, just above the waist.
  • Discomfort that does not move and is not helped by back stretches.
  • Pain that is worse with deep breathing or a prolonged cough.
  • A sense of restriction on one side when twisting.
  • Pain often described as kidney pain, in the flank area.
  • Discomfort after a period of heavy lifting or a respiratory illness.

What makes it worse

Deep breaths or coughing · Turning your head or body · Standing or walking · Bending or lifting. If two or more of those match your day, this muscle moves up the list.

Quick self-check

Education, not a diagnosis. The more of these that ring true, the more likely the serratus posterior inferior is involved:

  • Is the ache across your lower ribs at the back, just above your waist?
  • Has it been blamed on your kidneys or low back without anything being found?
  • Is it worse with a deep breath or a cough?
  • Did it start after a chest infection or a bout of coughing?
  • Does it stay in one spot rather than spreading?

Lower rib palpation. Press along the lower ribs at the back, between the bottom of the shoulder blade and the top of the pelvis, with the trunk relaxed. Suggests this muscle if: Tenderness directly over the lower ribs reproduces the ache.

Breathing out test. Breathe all the way out and press into the same area at the end of the breath. Suggests this muscle if: Tenderness increases at the end of a full breath out.

What it gets mistaken for

  • Kidney infection or stones, which must be excluded.
  • Rib fracture or joint irritation.
  • Quadratus lumborum or thoracolumbar back pain.
  • Shingles before the rash appears.
  • Gallbladder problems on the right side.

Common causes

  • A prolonged cough or chest infection.
  • Heavy lifting, especially overhead.
  • A breathing pattern that does not use the lower ribs.
  • Sustained rotation at work or in sport.
  • A fall or direct blow to the lower ribs.

Dr. Jared's 3-phase relief plan

Work through the phases in order and move on when the current one feels easy and pain-free. Every video is in the free MD Fit app.

Phase 1 · Calm it down

  1. Lower rib breathing (video: 90/90 Breathing) · 5 minutes daily
    Breathe wide into the lower ribs. This muscle and the diaphragm work as a pair.
  2. Support while coughing · As needed
    Hug a pillow to the lower ribs when coughing to reduce the strain.
  3. Gentle unloading (video: Dynamic Childs Pose) · Ten slow breaths
    Takes the lower ribs out of their working position for a few minutes.

Phase 2 · Restore movement

  1. Side-bend and rotate (video: Sit Turn Lean) · Ten each way, daily
    Restores motion through the lower ribs rather than the low back.
  2. Thoracic rotation (video: Thoracic Rotation Seated) · Ten each side
    Gives rotation back to the trunk so the lower ribs are not forced.
  3. Open book (video: Lying Open Book) · Ten each side
    Opens the rib cage with the spine supported.

Phase 3 · Strengthen

  1. Trunk anti-rotation (video: Pallof Press) · Three holds of 20 seconds each side
    Builds steadiness through the lower trunk.
  2. Loaded carries (video: Farmer Carry) · Three carries of 30 seconds
    Trains the rib cage and trunk to hold under load.
  3. Hip hinge (video: Dowel Hip Hinge) · Three sets of ten
    Loads the back correctly so the lower ribs are not taking the strain.

Suggested library videos for this muscle: 90/90 Breathing, Sit Turn Lean, Dynamic Childs Pose.

Habits that help

  • Breathe into the lower ribs rather than the upper chest.
  • Support the ribs when coughing during an illness.
  • Build lifting load gradually after a respiratory illness.
  • Have persistent flank pain checked before assuming it is muscular.

What to expect

Lower rib muscle pain usually settles within two to three weeks, but takes longer after a prolonged cough. Progress is often faster once breathing mechanics are addressed.

When to see a professional

Flank pain needs a physician first if there is fever, urinary symptoms or blood in the urine. Book an evaluation once those have been excluded. Get urgent care for any of these:

  • Flank pain with fever, chills or urinary symptoms, which suggests a kidney infection.
  • Blood in the urine.
  • Rib pain after a significant fall, particularly with thinning bones.
  • Shortness of breath or coughing up blood.
  • A band of rash, which may be shingles.
