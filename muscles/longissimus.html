Where the longissimus sends pain

Low in the buttock and over the SI joint, often well below where the muscle actually is. People usually come in describing pain at the si joint and back of the pelvis, right beside the spine (low back) or middle of the buttock.

What the longissimus does

Extends the spine and holds upright posture.

  • Starts at: Sacrum, back of the pelvis and lower vertebrae (common tendon).
  • Attaches to: Transverse processes of the vertebrae and the lower ribs, up to the skull.
  • Nerve supply: Dorsal rami of the spinal nerves (segmental).

Symptoms people describe

  • Pain low in the buttock and over the SI joint, often well below the muscle.
  • Ache beside the spine when standing for long periods.
  • Stiffness in the morning that eases with movement.
  • Pain arching back or straightening up.

What makes it worse

Standing or walking · Sitting · Bending or lifting. If two or more of those match your day, this muscle moves up the list.

Quick self-check

Education, not a diagnosis. The more of these that ring true, the more likely the longissimus is involved:

  • Is your pain low in the buttock or over the SI joint, but tender spots are higher up beside the spine?
  • Does long standing or walking upright bring it on?
  • Does arching backward hurt?
  • Is it stiff in the morning and better once you move?
  • Does sitting slumped relieve it a little?

Standing back-bend. Stand with hands on hips and gently arch backward. Suggests this muscle if: Your familiar ache beside the spine or low in the buttock.

What it gets mistaken for

  • SI joint dysfunction
  • Lumbar facet joint pain
  • Piriformis trigger points
  • Lumbar radiculopathy

Common causes

  • Long standing with a sway-back posture.
  • Weak abs and glutes.
  • Heavy lifting with an arched back.
  • Running on hard surfaces.

Phase 1 · Calm it down

Habits that help

  • Stand with weight even and knees soft.
  • Shift positions every 20 to 30 minutes when standing.

What to expect

Usually improves within 1 to 3 weeks.

When to see a professional

Book an evaluation if pain spreads down the leg, there are nerve symptoms, or no improvement in 2 to 3 weeks. Get urgent care for any of these:

  • Saddle numbness or loss of bladder or bowel control: emergency.
  • Leg weakness or foot drop.
  • Night pain with fever or weight loss.

Frequently asked questions

Can a tight longissimus cause pain at the si joint and back of the pelvis?

Often, yes. Low in the buttock and over the SI joint, often well below where the muscle actually is. That referred pain is why it gets blamed on the joint or the spot that hurts instead of the muscle.

How long does longissimus pain take to settle?

Usually improves within 1 to 3 weeks.

When should I see someone about it?

Book an evaluation if pain spreads down the leg, there are nerve symptoms, or no improvement in 2 to 3 weeks.

What else can feel like longissimus pain?

SI joint dysfunction, Lumbar facet joint pain, Piriformis trigger points. An evaluation separates muscle pain from these, and anything that needs a physician is referred on.
