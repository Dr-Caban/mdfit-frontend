Where the iliocostalis sends pain

Low back and buttock from the lumbar part; the lower shoulder blade and even the belly from the thoracic part. People usually come in describing pain at the flank or side of the low back, bottom tip of the shoulder blade, upper buttock at the belt line or between the shoulder blades. It is also a common hidden driver behind Low Back Pain.

What the iliocostalis does

Extends the spine and bends it to the side; the outer column of the back extensors.

  • Starts at: Back of the pelvis (iliac crest), sacrum and the back of the ribs.
  • Attaches to: Angles of the ribs and the transverse processes of the lower neck vertebrae.
  • Nerve supply: Dorsal rami of the spinal nerves (segmental).

Symptoms people describe

  • Low-back ache that spreads into the buttock (lumbar part).
  • Ache at the lower shoulder blade, sometimes wrapping toward the belly (thoracic part).
  • Stiffness getting up after sitting.
  • Pain bending to the side or standing up from bending.

What makes it worse

Standing or walking · Sitting · Bending or lifting. If two or more of those match your day, this muscle moves up the list.

Quick self-check

Education, not a diagnosis. The more of these that ring true, the more likely the iliocostalis is involved:

  • Is the tender spot a few finger-widths out from the spine, not right beside it?
  • Does the low-back ache spread down into the buttock?
  • Does side-bending away from the painful side pull and hurt?
  • Is it worst getting up from a chair or straightening from bending?
  • Do you sometimes feel ache wrapping toward the belly or under the shoulder blade?

Side-bend test. Stand tall and slide one hand down the outside of the leg, then the other. Suggests this muscle if: Pulling or your familiar ache on the side opposite the bend, a few inches from the spine.

What it gets mistaken for

  • Quadratus lumborum trigger points
  • Lumbar facet irritation
  • Kidney pain (flank)
  • SI joint dysfunction

Common causes

  • Long sitting followed by sudden bending or lifting.
  • Lifting and twisting at the same time.
  • Weak glutes and core leaving the back to do the work.
  • Standing in one position for long periods.

Phase 1 · Calm it down

  1. Back extensor ball release · 60–90 sec per side, twice daily
    Lie on a ball placed a few inches out from the spine at the sore level and let your weight sink in gently.
  2. Cat Cow Desk Variation (video) · 2 x 10, twice daily

Phase 2 · Restore movement

  1. Sit Turn Lean (video) · 2 x 6 per side, daily
    Side-bend and rotate gently to lengthen the outer back.
  2. Dynamic Childs Pose (video) · 2 x 6, daily
    Walk the hands to the side to stretch the opposite side of the back.

Phase 3 · Strengthen

  1. Dowel Hip Hinge (video) · 3 x 10, every other day
    Hinge from the hips with a neutral spine so the glutes share the load.
  2. Dead Bug (video) · 3 x 8 per side, every other day
    Keep the low back gently pressed down as the limbs move.

Habits that help

  • Stand up every 30 minutes.
  • Hinge from the hips when lifting.
  • Avoid lifting and twisting at the same time.

What to expect

Muscle-driven low-back pain usually improves in 1 to 3 weeks.

When to see a professional

Book an evaluation if pain spreads below the knee, there is numbness or weakness, or no improvement in 2 to 3 weeks. Get urgent care for any of these:

  • Loss of bladder or bowel control, or numbness in the saddle area: emergency.
  • Flank pain with fever or painful urination.
  • Pain after a fall, with osteoporosis, or with weight loss.

Frequently asked questions

Can a tight iliocostalis cause pain at the flank or side of the low back?

Often, yes. Low back and buttock from the lumbar part; the lower shoulder blade and even the belly from the thoracic part. That referred pain is why it gets blamed on the joint or the spot that hurts instead of the muscle.

How long does iliocostalis pain take to settle?

Muscle-driven low-back pain usually improves in 1 to 3 weeks.

When should I see someone about it?

Book an evaluation if pain spreads below the knee, there is numbness or weakness, or no improvement in 2 to 3 weeks.

What else can feel like iliocostalis pain?

Quadratus lumborum trigger points, Lumbar facet irritation, Kidney pain (flank). An evaluation separates muscle pain from these, and anything that needs a physician is referred on.
