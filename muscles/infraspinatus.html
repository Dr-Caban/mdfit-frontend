Where the infraspinatus sends pain

Deep in the front of the shoulder joint, down the front and outside of the arm. Hard to reach behind your back or sleep on that side. People usually come in describing pain at the front of the shoulder, deep inside the shoulder joint, on the shoulder blade, outside of the shoulder or upper arm, forearm or back of the shoulder.

What the infraspinatus does

Primarily rotates your arm outward (external rotation), like when you're winding up to throw a ball. It also helps stabilize the shoulder joint.

  • Starts at: Infraspinous fossa of the scapula (the main flat part on the back of your shoulder blade).
  • Attaches to: Greater tubercle of the humerus (the top, outer part of your upper arm bone).
  • Nerve supply: Suprascapular nerve (C5, C6).

Symptoms people describe

  • A deep, aching pain felt in the front of the shoulder.
  • Pain that travels down the front and outside of the arm, sometimes to the hand.
  • Difficulty reaching behind your back, like to fasten a bra or grab a wallet.
  • Inability to sleep on the affected shoulder due to pain.
  • A feeling of weakness when trying to lift or rotate your arm outward.

What makes it worse

Reaching overhead · Lying on it at night · Reaching behind your back. If two or more of those match your day, this muscle moves up the list.

Quick self-check

Education, not a diagnosis. The more of these that ring true, the more likely the infraspinatus is involved:

  • Is it painful to sleep on your affected shoulder?
  • Do you feel a deep ache in the FRONT of your shoulder, even though the muscle is on the back?
  • Is it difficult to reach behind your back, like tucking in a shirt or fastening a bra?
  • Does the pain travel down the outside or front of your arm?
  • Do you have trouble combing your hair or reaching for something on a high shelf?

External Rotation Resistance Test. Bend your affected arm's elbow to 90 degrees, keeping it tucked into your side. Use your other hand to gently resist as you try to rotate your forearm outward. Suggests this muscle if: Pain or noticeable weakness in the back of your shoulder compared to the other side.

Hand-to-Back Reach. Try to reach your affected arm behind your back and up toward your opposite shoulder blade. Compare how high you can reach with your good arm. Suggests this muscle if: Significant pain or a major difference in how high you can reach.

What it gets mistaken for

  • Shoulder bursitis
  • Biceps tendonitis
  • Cervical radiculopathy (pinched nerve in the neck)
  • Shoulder joint arthritis
  • Supraspinatus tear

Common causes

  • Repetitive overhead activities (e.g., throwing, swimming, painting).
  • Sudden trauma, like a fall onto an outstretched arm.
  • Poor posture, with rounded shoulders and a forward head position.
  • Sleeping on the same side every night, compressing the shoulder.
  • Weakness in the surrounding back and core muscles, which overloads the shoulder.

Dr. Jared's 3-phase relief plan

Work through the phases in order and move on when the current one feels easy and pain-free. Every video is in the free MD Fit app.

Phase 1 · Calm it down

  1. Lacrosse Ball Release · 30-60 seconds, 3-5 times daily
    Lie on your back and place a lacrosse ball or tennis ball on the muscle belly on your shoulder blade. Gently roll to find a tender spot and hold pressure until the tenderness eases.
  2. Pendulum Swings · 2 x 10 circles each way, twice daily
    Lean over, supporting your good arm on a table. Let the affected arm hang down and gently swing it in small circles, using your body's momentum, not your shoulder muscles.
  3. 90/90 Breathing (video) · 5 minutes, once or twice daily
    Lie on your back with your feet on a chair or wall, knees and hips at 90 degrees. Focus on deep belly breaths to relax the nervous system and reduce muscle guarding.

Phase 2 · Restore movement

  1. Sleeper Stretch · 2 x 30 seconds, once daily
    Lie on your affected side with your arm bent at 90 degrees in front of you. Use your other hand to gently press your forearm down toward the floor until you feel a stretch in the back of the shoulder.
  2. Lying Open Book (video) · 2 x 8 reps per side, once daily
    Lie on your side with knees bent and arms straight out in front. Keeping your hips still, rotate your top arm and upper body open toward the ceiling to improve thoracic mobility.
  3. Isometric External Rotation · 3 x 10-second holds, twice daily
    Stand in a doorway with your elbow bent to 90 degrees, tucked at your side. Press the back of your hand into the door frame as if trying to rotate outward, but don't let it move.

Phase 3 · Strengthen

  1. Rotator Cuff External Rotation (video) · 3 x 15 reps, every other day
    Use a light resistance band. Keep your elbow tucked into your side at a 90-degree angle and rotate your forearm outward against the band's resistance. Control the movement back.
  2. Abducted External Rotation (video) · 3 x 10 reps, every other day
    Raise your arm to the side, parallel to the floor, with your elbow bent at 90 degrees. Rotate your hand upward toward the ceiling, keeping your shoulder blade stable.
  3. Dumbbell Row (video) · 3 x 10-12 reps, every other day
    Support one knee and hand on a bench. Pull a dumbbell up toward your chest, squeezing your shoulder blade. This strengthens the back muscles that support the rotator cuff.

Suggested library videos for this muscle: Rotator Cuff External Rotation, Abducted External Rotation, Lying Open Book.

Habits that help

  • Avoid sleeping directly on the painful shoulder. Try sleeping on your back or other side with a pillow to hug.
  • When sitting, ensure your shoulders are relaxed and back, not rounded forward. Use our 'Seated Desk Posture Reset' video.
  • Take frequent micro-breaks from computer work to roll your shoulders back and down.
  • When lifting, keep your elbows close to your body to reduce strain on the shoulder joint.

What to expect

With consistent adherence to the protocol, most people feel a significant reduction in pain within 2-4 weeks. Full strength and function may take 6-8 weeks to restore.

When to see a professional

If pain is severe, doesn't improve after 2 weeks of self-care, or if you suspect a tear from an injury, book an appointment for a full diagnostic evaluation. Get urgent care for any of these:

  • Sudden, severe pain with an inability to lift your arm at all (possible full-thickness tear).
  • Pain accompanied by fever, chills, or unexplained weight loss.
  • Visible deformity of the shoulder joint after an injury.
  • Numbness or tingling that is constant and doesn't change with position.

Frequently asked questions

Can a tight infraspinatus cause pain at the front of the shoulder?

Often, yes. Deep in the front of the shoulder joint, down the front and outside of the arm. Hard to reach behind your back or sleep on that side. That referred pain is why it gets blamed on the joint or the spot that hurts instead of the muscle.

How long does infraspinatus pain take to settle?

With consistent adherence to the protocol, most people feel a significant reduction in pain within 2-4 weeks. Full strength and function may take 6-8 weeks to restore.

When should I see someone about it?

If pain is severe, doesn't improve after 2 weeks of self-care, or if you suspect a tear from an injury, book an appointment for a full diagnostic evaluation.

What else can feel like infraspinatus pain?

Shoulder bursitis, Biceps tendonitis, Cervical radiculopathy (pinched nerve in the neck). An evaluation separates muscle pain from these, and anything that needs a physician is referred on.
