Where the spinalis sends pain

A narrow strip of pain close to the spine in the mid and upper back, between and just below the shoulder blades. People usually come in describing pain at the right beside the spine (low back) or between the shoulder blades.

What the spinalis does

Extends the spine and holds the mid and upper back upright. It is the innermost of the three erector spinae columns, sitting closest to the spinous processes.

  • Starts at: The spinous processes of the upper lumbar and lower thoracic vertebrae, around T11 to L2.
  • Attaches to: The spinous processes of the upper thoracic vertebrae, and in its cervical portion the vertebrae of the neck.
  • Nerve supply: Posterior rami of the thoracic and cervical spinal nerves.

Symptoms people describe

  • A narrow strip of aching right beside the spine in the mid back.
  • Pain that builds through a day of sitting rather than arriving suddenly.
  • Discomfort between and just below the shoulder blades.
  • A feeling of needing to arch or stretch backwards for relief.
  • Pain that eases on lying down and returns on standing up.
  • Tenderness very close to the spine rather than out to the side.

What makes it worse

Sitting · Standing or walking · Desk, phone or screens · Bending or lifting. If two or more of those match your day, this muscle moves up the list.

Quick self-check

Education, not a diagnosis. The more of these that ring true, the more likely the spinalis is involved:

  • Is your pain in a narrow strip right beside your spine rather than out to the side?
  • Does it build through a day of sitting?
  • Do you get relief from arching backwards or lying flat?
  • Is it between or just below your shoulder blades?
  • Do you work at a screen for long periods?

Paraspinal palpation. Press firmly in the groove immediately beside the spinous processes in the mid back, then compare with pressing two inches out to the side. Suggests this muscle if: Tenderness is clearly worse close to the spine than further out.

Sustained sitting test. Sit unsupported in your usual posture for five minutes. Suggests this muscle if: The familiar strip of pain builds beside the spine during the hold.

What it gets mistaken for

  • Thoracic facet or rib joint irritation.
  • Vertebral compression fracture, particularly in older adults.
  • Referred pain from the chest or abdomen.
  • Rhomboid or middle trapezius pain, which sits further from the spine.
  • Inflammatory back conditions in younger adults.

Common causes

  • Prolonged unsupported sitting.
  • A rounded upper back from screen work.
  • Heavy or repeated lifting.
  • Sustained forward bending at work.
  • Deconditioning of the back extensors.

Work through the phases in order and move on when the current one feels easy and pain-free. Every video is in the free MD Fit app.

Phase 1 · Calm it down

  1. Support the back while sitting (video: Desk Setup Tutorial) · Once, then review
    A supported lumbar curve takes the sustained load off the whole extensor group.
  2. Regular position change (video: Walk Break Protocol Seated) · Every 30 minutes
    These muscles fatigue with static holding; movement is the fastest relief.
  3. Gentle spinal movement (video: Cat Cow Desk Variation) · Ten slow repetitions
    Small, pain-free flexion and extension to reduce guarding.

Phase 2 · Restore movement

  1. Thoracic extension (video: Thoracic Extension Over Chair) · Ten repetitions daily
    Restores the extension range that sitting takes away.
  2. Rotation (video: Thoracic Rotation Seated) · Ten each side
    A mobile mid back is less likely to be held stiffly by these muscles.
  3. Open book (video: Lying Open Book) · Ten each side
    Opens the chest and the mid back together.

Phase 3 · Strengthen

  1. Scapular retraction endurance (video: Floor Slide Scapular Retract) · Two sets of ten, daily
    Shares the postural load with the shoulder blade muscles.
  2. Row strength (video: Dumbbell Row) · Three sets of ten, twice weekly
    Builds upper back strength to hold position.
  3. Hip hinge (video: Dowel Hip Hinge) · Three sets of ten
    Teaches the back to work with the hips rather than instead of them.

Habits that help

  • Set the screen at eye level and support the low back when sitting.
  • Stand and move at least every half hour.
  • Build lifting load gradually.
  • Strengthen the upper back rather than only stretching it.

What to expect

Postural extensor pain usually eases within two to three weeks once sitting setup and movement breaks change, provided strengthening follows the stretching.

When to see a professional

Book an evaluation if mid back pain persists beyond three weeks. Back pain after a fall, with fever, or with leg symptoms needs a physician first. Get urgent care for any of these:

  • Mid back pain after a fall, especially with thinning bones, which may be a compression fracture.
  • Back pain with fever or unexplained weight loss.
  • Pain that is worse at night and not relieved by position change.
  • Band-like chest pain with numbness or weakness in the legs.
  • Pain with chest pressure or shortness of breath, which is a 911 call.
