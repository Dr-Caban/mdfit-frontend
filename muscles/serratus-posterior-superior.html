Where the serratus posterior superior sends pain

Deep ache underneath the shoulder blade that you cannot reach, spreading down the back of the arm to the little finger side. People usually come in describing pain at the inner edge of the shoulder blade or between the shoulder blades.

What the serratus posterior superior does

Raises the upper ribs during inspiration, assisting breathing in. It lies deep to the rhomboids and the shoulder blade.

  • Starts at: The lower nuchal ligament and the spinous processes of C7 through T3.
  • Attaches to: The upper borders and outer surfaces of ribs two through five.
  • Nerve supply: Intercostal nerves T2 to T5.

Symptoms people describe

  • A deep, nagging ache underneath the shoulder blade that you cannot reach to rub.
  • Pain that spreads down the back of the arm toward the little finger.
  • Discomfort that is worse with deep breathing.
  • Pain that persists after the rhomboids and traps have been treated without lasting effect.
  • Aching at night when lying on that side.
  • A sense of needing to dig under the shoulder blade for relief.

What makes it worse

Deep breaths or coughing · Desk, phone or screens · Reaching overhead · Lying on it at night. If two or more of those match your day, this muscle moves up the list.

Quick self-check

Education, not a diagnosis. The more of these that ring true, the more likely the serratus posterior superior is involved:

  • Is the ache deep underneath your shoulder blade, somewhere you cannot reach?
  • Has treating the muscles on top of the shoulder blade given only brief relief?
  • Does it spread down the back of your arm toward your little finger?
  • Is it worse with a deep breath?
  • Do you spend long hours at a screen with rounded shoulders?

Reach-around palpation. Reach the opposite hand across your chest to pull the shoulder blade outward, then have the area beneath its inner edge pressed. Suggests this muscle if: Deep tenderness under the blade reproduces the familiar ache.

Deep breath test. Take a slow maximal breath in and hold it briefly. Suggests this muscle if: The ache under the shoulder blade increases at the top of the breath.

What it gets mistaken for

  • Rhomboid or middle trapezius pain.
  • Rib joint irritation at the upper back.
  • Cervical disc problems referring into the shoulder blade.
  • Lung conditions, which must be excluded if there are breathing symptoms.
  • Gallbladder or cardiac referral to the upper back.

Common causes

  • Sustained rounded-shoulder posture at a screen.
  • Breathing patterns that use the upper chest rather than the diaphragm.
  • Prolonged coughing.
  • Carrying a heavy bag on one shoulder.
  • Sleeping on the same side with the arm forward.

Dr. Jared's 3-phase relief plan

Work through the phases in order and move on when the current one feels easy and pain-free. Every video is in the free MD Fit app.

Phase 1 · Calm it down

  1. Diaphragm breathing (video: 90/90 Breathing) · 5 minutes daily
    This muscle is a breathing accessory. If the diaphragm is not doing its job, this muscle never gets a rest.
  2. Shoulder blade glide (video: Serratus Stretch Breathing) · Ten slow breaths
    Lets the shoulder blade move away from the ribs so the deeper tissue can be reached.
  3. Side-lying rest position · Overnight
    A pillow hugged to the chest keeps the shoulder blade from collapsing forward in side-lying.

Phase 2 · Restore movement

  1. Thread the needle (video) · Ten each side, daily
    Opens the space between the shoulder blade and the ribs.
  2. Thoracic rotation (video: Quadruped T-Spine Rotation) · Ten each side
    Restores upper back rotation, which reduces the load on the rib-to-spine muscles.
  3. Chest opening (video: Baseball Cable Stretch) · Thirty seconds each side
    A tight chest holds the shoulder blade forward and keeps this muscle stretched and working.

Phase 3 · Strengthen

  1. Scapular retraction (video: Floor Slide Scapular Retract) · Two sets of ten, daily
    Builds the muscles that should be holding the shoulder blade position.
  2. Serratus anterior work (video: Scap Push Ups) · Two sets of ten
    Restores shoulder blade control against the rib cage.
  3. Row strength (video: Cable Row) · Three sets of ten, twice weekly
    Upper back strength to hold the posture the stretches create.

Suggested library videos for this muscle: Serratus Stretch Breathing, Thread The Needle, 90/90 Breathing.

Habits that help

  • Set up the screen so the shoulders are not rounded forward.
  • Breathe into the lower ribs rather than the upper chest.
  • Alternate the shoulder you carry a bag on, or use a backpack.
  • Take breathing breaks during long desk sessions.

What to expect

This muscle often responds slowly because it sits beneath the shoulder blade and is hard to reach. Most people notice change in two to four weeks once breathing and shoulder position are addressed.

When to see a professional

Book an evaluation if under-blade pain has not settled in three weeks. Any breathing symptom, chest pressure or fever needs a physician first. Get urgent care for any of these:

  • Upper back or shoulder blade pain with shortness of breath, which needs urgent assessment.
  • Pain with chest pressure, sweating or nausea, which is a 911 call.
  • Coughing up blood.
  • Fever with upper back pain.
  • Unexplained weight loss with persistent upper back pain.
