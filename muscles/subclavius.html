Where the subclavius sends pain

Under the collarbone and down the front of the arm and forearm into the thumb, index and middle fingers. People usually come in describing pain at the under the collarbone, thumb and index finger or front of the chest.

What the subclavius does

Pulls the collarbone down and forward and steadies it against the breastbone during arm movement.

  • Starts at: First rib and its cartilage.
  • Attaches to: Underside of the middle third of the collarbone.
  • Nerve supply: Nerve to subclavius (C5, C6).

Symptoms people describe

  • Ache just under the collarbone.
  • Pain running down the front of the arm and forearm into the thumb, index and middle fingers.
  • Arm heaviness or tingling when carrying bags on that shoulder.
  • Symptoms worse with the arm overhead or shoulders pulled down.
  • Tenderness pressing up under the collarbone.

What makes it worse

Deep breaths or coughing · Reaching overhead · Gripping, lifting or typing. If two or more of those match your day, this muscle moves up the list.

Quick self-check

Education, not a diagnosis. The more of these that ring true, the more likely the subclavius is involved:

  • Is there a tender spot just under the middle of your collarbone?
  • Does pain or tingling run down the front of the arm into the thumb and first two fingers?
  • Do heavy shoulder bags or backpack straps make it worse?
  • Does raising the arm overhead for a while bring on arm heaviness?
  • Do you breathe mostly into your upper chest?

Shoulder brace test. Pull the shoulders back and down firmly, as if standing at attention, and hold for 30 seconds. Suggests this muscle if: Pain under the collarbone or tingling down the arm that eases when you relax.

What it gets mistaken for

  • Thoracic outlet syndrome
  • Cervical radiculopathy (C6)
  • Carpal tunnel syndrome
  • Scalene trigger points
  • Pec minor syndrome

Common causes

  • Heavy shoulder bags and backpack straps.
  • Old collarbone fracture or shoulder injury.
  • Upper-chest breathing.
  • Rounded shoulders with the collarbone held down.
  • Repeated overhead work.

Dr. Jared's 3-phase relief plan

Work through the phases in order and move on when the current one feels easy and pain-free. Every video is in the free MD Fit app.

Phase 1 · Calm it down

  1. Under-collarbone release · 60 sec, twice daily
    Use two fingers to apply gentle pressure just under the middle of the collarbone, moving slightly outward while breathing slowly. Never press hard into the hollow.
  2. 90/90 Breathing (video) · 5 min, daily
    Breathe low so the first rib and collarbone can relax.

Phase 2 · Restore movement

  1. Baseball Cable Stretch (video) · 2 x 30 sec per side, daily
    Open the front of the shoulder gently; stop if tingling increases.
  2. Thoracic Open Book (video) · 2 x 8 per side, daily
    Rotate open to free the front of the chest.
  3. Standing Scalene Stretch (video) · 2 x 30 sec per side, daily
    Ease the neck muscles that share the space with the subclavius.

Phase 3 · Strengthen

  1. Floor Slide Scapular Retract (video) · 3 x 10, daily
    Hold the shoulder blades back without forcing them down.
  2. Scap Push Ups (video) · 3 x 10, every other day
    Build the serratus to support the shoulder girdle.

Suggested library videos for this muscle: Baseball Cable Stretch, 90/90 Breathing, Thoracic Open Book.

Habits that help

  • Use a backpack with both straps or a cross-body bag on alternating sides.
  • Avoid holding the shoulders forced down and back for long periods.
  • Practice low belly breathing during the day.

What to expect

Collarbone and arm symptoms often ease in 1 to 3 weeks once loading from bags and posture is reduced.

When to see a professional

Book an evaluation if tingling persists more than 2 weeks, or sooner if the hand becomes weak, swollen or discolored. Get urgent care for any of these:

  • Arm swelling, blue or pale color, or a cold hand (possible blood vessel compression).
  • Progressive hand weakness or muscle wasting.
  • Chest pain with shortness of breath: call 911.

Often, yes. Under the collarbone and down the front of the arm and forearm into the thumb, index and middle fingers. That referred pain is why it gets blamed on the joint or the spot that hurts instead of the muscle.

How long does subclavius pain take to settle?

Collarbone and arm symptoms often ease in 1 to 3 weeks once loading from bags and posture is reduced.

When should I see someone about it?

Book an evaluation if tingling persists more than 2 weeks, or sooner if the hand becomes weak, swollen or discolored.

What else can feel like subclavius pain?

Thoracic outlet syndrome, Cervical radiculopathy (C6), Carpal tunnel syndrome. An evaluation separates muscle pain from these, and anything that needs a physician is referred on.
