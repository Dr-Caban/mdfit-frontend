Where the pectoralis major upper head sends pain

Front of the shoulder over the front delt, often mistaken for a front-shoulder joint problem. People usually come in describing pain at the under the collarbone, front of the chest or front of the shoulder.

What the pectoralis major upper head does

Raises the arm forward and pulls it across the body; the 'upper chest' in incline pressing.

  • Starts at: Front of the inner half of the collarbone.
  • Attaches to: Front of the upper arm bone (lateral lip of the bicipital groove).
  • Nerve supply: Lateral pectoral nerve (C5–C7).

Symptoms people describe

  • Ache at the front of the shoulder over the front delt, often blamed on the shoulder joint.
  • Tight upper chest just below the collarbone.
  • Pain with incline pressing, push-ups or reaching across the body.
  • Rounded shoulders that feel hard to pull back.
  • Tender spots just under the collarbone toward the shoulder.

What makes it worse

Reaching overhead · Desk, phone or screens · Gripping, lifting or typing. If two or more of those match your day, this muscle moves up the list.

Quick self-check

Education, not a diagnosis. The more of these that ring true, the more likely the pectoralis major upper head is involved:

  • Is the pain at the front of the shoulder, but you can find tender spots in the upper chest just under the collarbone?
  • Does incline pressing or pushing up and forward bring it on?
  • Does pulling your shoulders back feel tight across the upper chest?
  • Do you spend hours with rounded shoulders at a desk or phone?
  • Does the pain stay above the chest without going to the fingers?

Doorway upper-chest test. Stand in a doorway with the forearm on the frame and the elbow above shoulder height, then step through gently. Suggests this muscle if: Tight pulling or your familiar front-shoulder ache in the upper chest.

What it gets mistaken for

  • Anterior deltoid strain
  • Biceps tendinopathy
  • AC joint pain
  • Shoulder impingement

Common causes

  • Lots of pressing with little pulling.
  • Rounded shoulders from desk and phone posture.
  • Sleeping on the side with the arm across the chest.
  • Carrying in front of the body for long periods.
  • Shallow chest breathing that keeps the chest muscles working.

Dr. Jared's 3-phase relief plan

Work through the phases in order and move on when the current one feels easy and pain-free. Every video is in the free MD Fit app.

Phase 1 · Calm it down

  1. Upper-chest ball release · 60–90 sec, twice daily
    Place a ball just below the collarbone toward the shoulder against a wall; hold gentle pressure and breathe.
  2. 90/90 Breathing (video) · 5 min, daily
    Breathe low into the belly and ribs so the chest muscles can relax.

Phase 2 · Restore movement

  1. Baseball Cable Stretch (video) · 2 x 30 sec per side, daily
    Arm above shoulder height to target the upper fibers; stretch, not pain.
  2. Thoracic Open Book (video) · 2 x 8 per side, daily
    Rotate open through the upper back while the chest lengthens.
  3. Floor Slide Scapular Retract (video) · 2 x 10, daily
    Draw the shoulder blades back and down as the arms slide.

Phase 3 · Strengthen

  1. Face Pull (video) · 3 x 12, every other day
    Strengthen the back of the shoulder to balance the chest.
  2. Eccentric Push Up (video) · 3 x 6, twice weekly
    Lower over 3–4 seconds with the shoulder blades gliding; no front-shoulder pinch.

Suggested library videos for this muscle: Baseball Cable Stretch, Thoracic Open Book, Wall Push Up.

Habits that help

  • Set screens at eye level and shoulders back.
  • Do two sets of pulling for every set of pressing.
  • Hug a pillow when side-sleeping so the arm does not fall across the chest.

What to expect

Front-shoulder ache from the upper pec usually improves in 1 to 3 weeks with stretching and posture changes.

When to see a professional

Book an evaluation if pain persists past 3 weeks or pressing remains painful. Any chest pain with other symptoms needs urgent medical care first. Get urgent care for any of these:

  • Chest pain with shortness of breath, sweating, nausea or pain into the jaw or left arm: call 911.
  • A visible gap or bruising in the front of the armpit after a heavy press (possible tear).
  • Swelling or a lump in the chest or armpit.

Front-shoulder ache from the upper pec usually improves in 1 to 3 weeks with stretching and posture changes.

What else can feel like pectoralis major upper head pain?
