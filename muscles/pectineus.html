Where the pectineus sends pain

Deep in the groin just below the crease, and into the upper inner thigh. A common missed cause of a groin that will not settle. People usually come in describing pain at the groin or inner thigh.

What the pectineus does

Adducts and flexes the hip. It sits highest of the adductor group, between psoas and adductor longus, and contributes to both bringing the leg in and lifting it forward.

  • Starts at: The pectineal line of the superior pubic ramus.
  • Attaches to: The pectineal line of the femur, running from the lesser trochanter down the back of the thigh bone.
  • Nerve supply: Femoral nerve, sometimes with a branch from the obturator nerve.

Symptoms people describe

  • Deep groin pain just below the crease, hard to pinpoint.
  • Pain in the upper inner thigh.
  • A groin strain that has not settled with usual adductor rehabilitation.
  • Discomfort lifting the knee toward the chest.
  • Pain with sudden changes of direction in sport.
  • A pulling sensation deep in the groin when sitting for long periods.

What makes it worse

Running or sports · Sitting · Stairs, squats or hills. If two or more of those match your day, this muscle moves up the list.

Quick self-check

Education, not a diagnosis. The more of these that ring true, the more likely the pectineus is involved:

  • Is your groin pain deep and just below the crease rather than further down the inner thigh?
  • Has a groin strain failed to settle with standard adductor exercises?
  • Does lifting your knee toward your chest provoke it?
  • Do you play a sport with sudden direction changes?
  • Is it uncomfortable after long periods of sitting?

High adductor palpation. Lie on your back with the knee bent and the leg turned outward. Press high in the groin, just below the crease and inside the femoral pulse, avoiding pressing on the pulse itself. Suggests this muscle if: Deep tenderness high in the groin reproduces the pain.

Resisted adduction in flexion. With the hip bent to about 45 degrees, squeeze the knees together against resistance. Suggests this muscle if: Pain high in the groin rather than further down the inner thigh.

What it gets mistaken for

  • Inguinal hernia, which must be excluded.
  • Hip joint problems including labral tear and osteoarthritis.
  • Osteitis pubis.
  • Iliopsoas-related groin pain.
  • Nerve entrapment in the groin.

Common causes

  • Sports with cutting, kicking and rapid direction change.
  • A sudden increase in training load.
  • Prolonged sitting, which keeps the hip flexed.
  • Horse riding or breaststroke swimming.
  • Compensating for a stiff or painful hip joint.

Dr. Jared's 3-phase relief plan

Work through the phases in order and move on when the current one feels easy and pain-free. Every video is in the free MD Fit app.

Phase 1 · Calm it down

  1. Relative rest from cutting sport · Two weeks
    Straight-line movement is usually fine; it is the direction changes that provoke it.
  2. Reduce sustained hip flexion (video: Walk Break Protocol Seated) · Every 30 minutes
    Long sitting keeps this muscle short.
  3. Gentle adductor lengthening (video: Adductor Lean) · Thirty seconds each side
    Easy range only while it is irritable.

Phase 2 · Restore movement

  1. Hip rotation work (video: 90/90 Hip Switch) · Ten each side, daily
    Restores rotation so the groin is not absorbing it.
  2. Lateral range (video: Cossack Squat) · Ten each side
    Loads the adductor group through range under control.
  3. Hip flexor lengthening (video: Couch Stretch) · Thirty seconds each side
    This muscle flexes the hip as well as adducting it.

Phase 3 · Strengthen

  1. Adductor loading (video: Standing Hip Abduction/Adduction) · Three sets of twelve each side
    Progressive adductor strength is the main protection against recurrence.
  2. Hip abductor balance (video: Standing Clamshell) · Two sets of fifteen each side
    Balance across the hip rather than only loading the inside.
  3. Return-to-sport loading (video: Cable Rotational Lunge) · Three sets of eight each side
    Reintroduces direction change under control.

Suggested library videos for this muscle: Adductor Lean, Cossack Squat, 90/90 Hip Switch.

Habits that help

  • Warm up adduction and rotation before cutting sports.
  • Build training volume gradually at the start of a season.
  • Break up long sitting.
  • Have any groin bulge assessed before returning to sport.

What to expect

Groin problems involving this muscle are often slower than other adductor strains, commonly four to six weeks, because it sits deep and is hard to load specifically.

When to see a professional

Groin pain needs a physician first if there is a bulge, fever, urinary or testicular symptoms, or if it follows a fall. Book an evaluation for groin pain that has not settled in three weeks. Get urgent care for any of these:

  • A bulge in the groin on straining or coughing, which suggests a hernia.
  • Groin pain with fever or urinary symptoms.
  • Testicular pain or swelling, which needs urgent assessment.
  • Groin pain after a fall, which may indicate a hip fracture, particularly in older adults.
  • Inability to bear weight.
