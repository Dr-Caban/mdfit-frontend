Where the gluteus minimus sends pain

Outer hip down the side of the thigh and leg to the ankle, or down the back of the thigh and calf. A common sciatica look-alike. People usually come in describing pain at the outside of the hip, outer calf, outer ankle, middle of the buttock, upper calf, outside of the thigh or back of the thigh. It is also a common hidden driver behind Sciatica-Type Pain.

What the gluteus minimus does

Abducts the hip (moves leg out) and internally rotates the thigh. A key stabilizer of the hip joint.

  • Starts at: Outer surface of the ilium (pelvic bone), below the gluteus medius.
  • Attaches to: Anterior aspect of the greater trochanter of the femur.
  • Nerve supply: Superior gluteal nerve (L4, L5, S1).

Symptoms people describe

  • Deep, aching pain in the buttock that is hard to pinpoint.
  • Pain that refers down the side of the thigh and leg, sometimes all the way to the ankle.
  • Pain that refers down the back of the thigh and calf, mimicking sciatica.
  • Intense pain when lying on the affected side, often worse than with gluteus medius.
  • Pain and a limp when walking, especially after getting up from a chair.

What makes it worse

Standing or walking · Lying on it at night · Sitting. If two or more of those match your day, this muscle moves up the list.

Quick self-check

Education, not a diagnosis. The more of these that ring true, the more likely the gluteus minimus is involved:

  • Does your pain travel down the outside of your leg, possibly reaching your ankle?
  • Does your pain sometimes travel down the back of your leg, making you think you have sciatica?
  • Is it nearly impossible to lie on the painful side at night?
  • Do you have a noticeable limp, especially for the first few steps after you stand up?
  • Is the pain a deep, constant ache in your buttock that you can't seem to touch directly?

Gluteus Minimus Palpation. Lie on your good side. Find the bony point of your hip (greater trochanter). The gluteus minimus trigger points are often found just in front of and above this bone. Press deeply to explore for exquisite tenderness. Suggests this muscle if: Finding a spot of extreme, sharp tenderness that reproduces your familiar buttock and leg pain.

What it gets mistaken for

  • Lumbar radiculopathy (true sciatica from a disc herniation)
  • Greater trochanteric pain syndrome (hip bursitis)
  • Hip joint osteoarthritis
  • Piriformis syndrome
  • Iliotibial (IT) band syndrome

Common causes

  • Sitting on a wallet in your back pocket.
  • A direct fall onto the side of the hip.
  • Prolonged sitting, especially in a chair that is too high or too hard.
  • Walking or running with poor pelvic stability (a 'hip drop' gait).
  • Wearing worn-out shoes that alter your walking mechanics.

Dr. Jared's 3-phase relief plan

Work through the phases in order and move on when the current one feels easy and pain-free. Every video is in the free MD Fit app.

Phase 1 · Calm it down

  1. Glute Minimus Release (Ball) · 2-3 minutes per side, daily
    Lie on your side with a lacrosse or tennis ball placed just in front of the bony point of your hip. Lean into it gently, breathing deeply. Avoid rolling directly on the bone.
  2. 90/90 Hip Switch (video) · 2 x 10 reps, daily
    This gentle mobility drill helps to improve internal and external rotation of the hip, which can help calm an irritated gluteus minimus.
  3. Side-Lying Pillow Squeeze · 3 x 10 sec holds, twice daily
    Lie on your side with a pillow between your knees. Gently squeeze the pillow and hold, engaging the inner thigh muscles, which can help the outer hip muscles relax.

Phase 2 · Restore movement

  1. Standing Clamshell (video) · 2 x 15 reps per side, every other day
    This exercise helps activate and strengthen the gluteus medius and minimus in a functional standing position.
  2. Standing Hip IR Liftoff (video) · 2 x 10 reps per side, every other day
    This specifically targets the internal rotation function of the gluteus minimus. Focus on slow, controlled movement without leaning your torso.
  3. Piriformis Stretch (Seated) · 2 x 30 sec per side, daily
    While seated, cross one ankle over the opposite knee. Gently lean forward with a flat back until you feel a stretch in the buttock. This can help stretch the deep hip rotators.

Phase 3 · Strengthen

  1. Glute Walk (video) · 2 x 10 steps each direction, every other day
    Use a resistance band around your ankles or knees. The side-stepping motion is excellent for building endurance in both the gluteus medius and minimus.
  2. Single Leg Glute Bridge (video) · 3 x 8 reps per side, every other day
    Drive through one heel to lift the hips level, without letting the pelvis drop on the free side. Pause 2 seconds at the top.
  3. Side Crab Walk (video) · 3 x 6 reps per side, every other day
    Stay low with a band around the knees or ankles and step sideways, keeping toes forward and the pelvis level.

Suggested library videos for this muscle: Standing Hip IR Liftoff, Standing Clamshell, 90/90 Hip Switch.

Habits that help

  • Never sit on your wallet. Keep it in your front pocket or jacket.
  • Get up and walk around at least once every hour.
  • Ensure your office chair provides good support and is at the correct height.
  • Invest in supportive footwear and replace running shoes regularly.

What to expect

Due to its common mimicry of sciatica, this muscle can be stubborn. Consistent release and activation can provide relief from the referred leg pain within 2-4 weeks. Building robust strength to correct underlying walking patterns may take 2-3 months.

When to see a professional

Book an appointment if you have numbness, tingling, or true weakness in your leg, or if the pain does not begin to improve after 3 weeks of dedicated effort. Get urgent care for any of these:

  • True weakness (foot drop) or loss of sensation in the leg or foot.
  • Loss of bowel or bladder control.
  • Pain that is not relieved by any position and is worsening.
  • History of cancer, fever, or unexplained weight loss.

Frequently asked questions

Can a tight gluteus minimus cause pain at the outside of the hip?

Often, yes. Outer hip down the side of the thigh and leg to the ankle, or down the back of the thigh and calf. A common sciatica look-alike. That referred pain is why it gets blamed on the joint or the spot that hurts instead of the muscle.

How long does gluteus minimus pain take to settle?

Due to its common mimicry of sciatica, this muscle can be stubborn. Consistent release and activation can provide relief from the referred leg pain within 2-4 weeks. Building robust strength to correct underlying walking patterns may take 2-3 months.

When should I see someone about it?

Book an appointment if you have numbness, tingling, or true weakness in your leg, or if the pain does not begin to improve after 3 weeks of dedicated effort.

What else can feel like gluteus minimus pain?

Lumbar radiculopathy (true sciatica from a disc herniation), Greater trochanteric pain syndrome (hip bursitis), Hip joint osteoarthritis. An evaluation separates muscle pain from these, and anything that needs a physician is referred on.
