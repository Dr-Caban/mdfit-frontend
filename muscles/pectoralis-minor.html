Where the pectoralis minor sends pain

Front of the shoulder and chest, down the inner arm to the last three fingers. Can pinch nerves to the hand. People usually come in describing pain at the ring and little finger, front of the chest, palm side of the wrist and hand or front of the shoulder.

What the pectoralis minor does

Pulls the shoulder blade forward and down. It's a key muscle in creating 'rounded shoulder' posture and can pinch nerves and blood vessels going to the arm.

  • Starts at: Ribs 3, 4, and 5.
  • Attaches to: A small hook-like bone on the front of the shoulder blade (coracoid process).
  • Nerve supply: Medial Pectoral Nerve (C8, T1)

Symptoms people describe

  • Sharp pain at the very front of the shoulder, under the collarbone.
  • Numbness, tingling, or weakness in the arm and hand, especially the last three fingers.
  • Pain that worsens when reaching overhead or backward.
  • A feeling of the arm 'going to sleep' easily, especially when sleeping or carrying a backpack.
  • A rounded or forward-slumped shoulder posture on one side.
  • Difficulty taking a full, deep breath into the upper chest.

What makes it worse

Desk, phone or screens · Deep breaths or coughing · Reaching overhead. If two or more of those match your day, this muscle moves up the list.

Quick self-check

Education, not a diagnosis. The more of these that ring true, the more likely the pectoralis minor is involved:

  • Do you get numbness or tingling down your arm and into your hand when you carry a backpack or heavy purse?
  • Is your pain located in a specific spot at the very front of your shoulder, just below the collarbone?
  • Does reaching straight up, like to a high shelf, cause a sharp, pinching pain in the front of your shoulder?
  • Do you often wake up with a 'dead arm' or pins and needles in your hand?
  • When you look in the mirror, does one shoulder seem to sit lower and more forward than the other?

Pec Minor Length Test. Lie on your back on the floor with your knees bent. Let your arms rest at your sides, palms up. Check if the back of your shoulder is touching the floor. Suggests this muscle if: The back of your affected shoulder cannot rest comfortably on the floor without you arching your back. This suggests the pec minor is pulling it forward.

What it gets mistaken for

  • Thoracic outlet syndrome (Pec Minor is a common cause of this)
  • Shoulder impingement syndrome
  • Biceps tendonitis
  • C8/T1 nerve root irritation (radiculopathy)
  • AC joint sprain

Common causes

  • Chronic slouching posture with rounded shoulders (desk work, phone use).
  • Repetitive overhead reaching, especially with poor mechanics.
  • Using a computer mouse that is too far away, causing a constant forward reach.
  • Heavy breathing from conditions like asthma or after intense cardio.
  • Sleeping in a curled-up fetal position.

Dr. Jared's 3-phase relief plan

Work through the phases in order and move on when the current one feels easy and pain-free. Every video is in the free MD Fit app.

Phase 1 · Calm it down

  1. Pec Minor Pressure Release · 2 minutes, once daily
    Place a lacrosse ball between your upper chest (just below the collarbone) and a wall or door frame. Gently apply pressure and make small movements to release this small, deep muscle.
  2. Thoracic Extension Over Chair (video) · 5-10 breaths, 3-5 times per day
    This gently opens the chest and reverses the slumped posture that shortens the pec minor. Focus on breathing into your upper chest.

Phase 2 · Restore movement

  1. Corner Pec Stretch · 2 x 30 sec, twice daily
    Stand in a corner, place forearms on each wall with elbows below shoulder height. Gently lean in until you feel a stretch in the front of your shoulders and chest. Avoid arching your back.
  2. Floor Slide Scapular Retract (video) · 2 x 10, once daily
    This activates the muscles that pull your shoulder blades back and down, directly opposing the pull of a tight pec minor.

Phase 3 · Strengthen

  1. Face Pull (video) · 3 x 15, every other day
    This is a key exercise to strengthen the upper back and external rotators of the shoulder, promoting better posture.
  2. Scap Push Ups (video) · 3 x 10, every other day
    This strengthens the serratus anterior muscle, which helps hold the shoulder blade against the rib cage and counteracts the pec minor's pull.

Suggested library videos for this muscle: Baseball Cable Stretch, Floor Slide Scapular Retract, Thoracic Extension Over Chair.

Habits that help

  • Position your mouse close to your keyboard to avoid reaching.
  • Use a headset for phone calls instead of cradling the phone.
  • When sitting, imagine a string pulling the top of your head towards the ceiling.
  • Perform a 'Seated Desk Posture Reset' every 30 minutes.

What to expect

Relief from nerve-like symptoms can occur within a week of consistent release and stretching. Lasting postural changes and strength balance will likely take 6-8 weeks of dedicated effort.

When to see a professional

Book an evaluation if you have persistent or worsening numbness, tingling, weakness, or color changes in your hand, or if your pain doesn't improve after 3 weeks. Get urgent care for any of these:

  • Persistent numbness, tingling, or weakness in the hand that doesn't go away with position changes.
  • A cool or pale hand compared to the other side, suggesting poor circulation.
  • A visible lump or deformity at the front of the shoulder.
  • Any chest pain that could be heart-related (see Pec Major red flags).

Frequently asked questions

Can a tight pectoralis minor cause pain at the ring and little finger?

Often, yes. Front of the shoulder and chest, down the inner arm to the last three fingers. Can pinch nerves to the hand. That referred pain is why it gets blamed on the joint or the spot that hurts instead of the muscle.

How long does pectoralis minor pain take to settle?

Relief from nerve-like symptoms can occur within a week of consistent release and stretching. Lasting postural changes and strength balance will likely take 6-8 weeks of dedicated effort.

When should I see someone about it?

Book an evaluation if you have persistent or worsening numbness, tingling, weakness, or color changes in your hand, or if your pain doesn't improve after 3 weeks.

What else can feel like pectoralis minor pain?

Thoracic outlet syndrome (Pec Minor is a common cause of this), Shoulder impingement syndrome, Biceps tendonitis. An evaluation separates muscle pain from these, and anything that needs a physician is referred on.
