Where the brachialis sends pain

Base of the thumb and the web of the thumb, and the front of the elbow. A frequently missed cause of thumb pain. People usually come in describing pain at the front of the elbow or thumb and index finger.

What the brachialis does

The main elbow flexor. Because it inserts on the ulna rather than the radius, it bends the elbow regardless of whether the palm is up or down, which the biceps cannot do.

  • Starts at: The lower half of the front surface of the humerus.
  • Attaches to: The coronoid process and tuberosity of the ulna.
  • Nerve supply: Musculocutaneous nerve, with a small contribution from the radial nerve.

Symptoms people describe

  • Pain at the base of the thumb and in the web between thumb and index finger.
  • Aching at the front of the elbow.
  • Thumb pain that no hand examination explains.
  • Discomfort carrying bags or lifting with the palm down.
  • Difficulty straightening the elbow fully after heavy lifting.
  • Weakness in elbow bending when the palm faces down.

What makes it worse

Gripping, lifting or typing · Bending or lifting. If two or more of those match your day, this muscle moves up the list.

Quick self-check

Education, not a diagnosis. The more of these that ring true, the more likely the brachialis is involved:

  • Do you have thumb pain that a hand examination has not explained?
  • Is the front of your elbow tender when you press underneath the biceps?
  • Is carrying bags with the palm down uncomfortable?
  • Is it hard to straighten your elbow fully after lifting?
  • Do you do a lot of gripping or curling at work or in the gym?

Brachialis palpation. Bend the elbow to 90 degrees and let the biceps go soft. Push the biceps tendon gently to one side and press into the muscle underneath, on the front of the upper arm bone. Suggests this muscle if: Deep tenderness reproduces the thumb or elbow pain.

Palm-down curl. With the palm facing down, bend the elbow against light resistance. Suggests this muscle if: Pain or weakness at the front of the elbow, since this position takes the biceps largely out of the movement.

What it gets mistaken for

  • De Quervain's tenosynovitis at the thumb side of the wrist.
  • Thumb base arthritis.
  • Biceps tendinopathy at the elbow.
  • Radial nerve irritation.
  • Cervical radiculopathy referring into the arm.

Common causes

  • Heavy or repeated lifting with the palm down.
  • Carrying bags, tools or a child on one arm.
  • A sudden increase in curling or pulling volume.
  • Working with the elbow held bent for long periods.
  • Sleeping with the elbow fully bent.

Dr. Jared's 3-phase relief plan

Work through the phases in order and move on when the current one feels easy and pain-free. Every video is in the free MD Fit app.

Phase 1 · Calm it down

  1. Change carrying position · Ongoing
    Carry with the palm facing in or use a bag over the shoulder instead of in the hand.
  2. Avoid full bent-elbow rest · Overnight
    Sleeping with the elbow fully bent keeps the muscle shortened all night.
  3. Gentle flexor lengthening (video: Forearm Flexor Stretch) · Thirty seconds, three times daily
    Straighten the elbow with the palm up, easing into a mild stretch only.

Phase 2 · Restore movement

  1. Elbow range restoration · Ten repetitions, several times daily
    Slowly straighten and bend the elbow through full available range, with no weight, to reclaim the last few degrees.
  2. Neutral-grip curls (video: Hammer Curl) · Two sets of ten, light
    A thumbs-up grip loads this muscle directly in a controlled way.
  3. Shoulder and neck check (video: Chin Glide Seated) · Two sets of ten
    Arm pain that does not settle is often driven from the neck.

Phase 3 · Strengthen

  1. Progressive curls (video: Bicep Curl) · Three sets of ten, twice weekly
    Build load gradually once pain-free through range.
  2. Loaded carries (video: Farmer Carry) · Three carries of 30 seconds
    Builds carrying capacity, which is the real-world demand.
  3. Grip and forearm work (video: Weighted Wrist Circles) · Two sets of ten each way
    Shares the load across the forearm rather than concentrating it.

Suggested library videos for this muscle: Bicep Curl, Hammer Curl, Forearm Flexor Stretch.

Habits that help

  • Vary your carrying arm and grip position.
  • Build curling and pulling volume gradually.
  • Avoid sleeping with the elbow fully bent.
  • Consider this muscle whenever thumb pain has no local explanation.

What to expect

Most brachialis pain settles within two to three weeks once carrying habits change and range is restored. Thumb symptoms often clear once the elbow is treated.

When to see a professional

Book an evaluation if elbow or thumb pain persists beyond three weeks, or sooner for sudden deformity, loss of elbow bending, or numbness and weakness in the hand. Get urgent care for any of these:

  • A sudden pop at the front of the elbow with visible deformity or bruising, which may be a tendon rupture.
  • Loss of the ability to bend the elbow against gravity.
  • Progressive numbness or weakness in the hand.
  • Elbow swelling with fever or redness.
  • Pain after a significant fall, which may indicate a fracture.
