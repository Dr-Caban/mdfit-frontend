Where the sternocleidomastoid sends pain

Forehead, around and behind the eye, cheek, ear and top of the head. Can cause dizziness or ear fullness. People usually come in describing pain at the forehead, ear area, front of the neck, behind or around the eye, top of the head, temple, side of the neck or under the collarbone. It is also a common hidden driver behind Jaw Pain & TMJ Dysfunction.

What the sternocleidomastoid does

When one side acts, it turns the head to the opposite side and bends it to the same side. Acting together, they bend the neck forward.

  • Starts at: Top of the sternum (breastbone) and inner part of the clavicle (collarbone).
  • Attaches to: Mastoid process of the temporal bone (the bony bump behind the ear).
  • Nerve supply: Spinal accessory nerve (CN XI) and cervical nerves (C2, C3).

Symptoms people describe

  • Pain in the forehead, often described as a 'headache over the eye'.
  • Pain around the eye, in the cheek, or in the jaw (TMJ) area.
  • Dizziness, vertigo, or a feeling of imbalance, especially with head turning.
  • Ear symptoms like ringing (tinnitus), fullness, or deep ear pain.
  • Pain at the top of the head or a sore throat feeling when swallowing.

What makes it worse

Turning your head or body · Desk, phone or screens · Deep breaths or coughing. If two or more of those match your day, this muscle moves up the list.

Quick self-check

Education, not a diagnosis. The more of these that ring true, the more likely the sternocleidomastoid is involved:

  • Do you experience pain or pressure in your forehead, above or around your eye?
  • Have you felt dizzy, off-balance, or had ringing in your ears that seems related to your neck pain?
  • Does the pain get worse when you turn your head to look over your shoulder, like when backing up a car?
  • Do you have pain in your cheek or jaw that has been mistaken for a sinus or dental problem?
  • Do you often sleep on your stomach or with your head turned sharply to one side?

SCM Palpation. Turn your head to one side and look slightly up; the SCM muscle will pop out. Gently pinch the muscle between your thumb and fingers and feel for tender, ropey spots from behind your ear down to your collarbone. Suggests this muscle if: Finding a very tender spot that reproduces your specific headache, face pain, or dizziness.

What it gets mistaken for

  • Migraine with aura
  • Trigeminal neuralgia
  • Sinusitis or sinus headache
  • TMJ (jaw joint) dysfunction
  • Inner ear problems (e.g., BPPV, vestibular neuritis)

Common causes

  • Whiplash injuries, even minor ones.
  • Forward head posture, which over-stretches and strains the SCM.
  • Sleeping on your stomach or with thick pillows.
  • Chronic coughing, or activities involving deep, forceful breathing (e.g., playing a wind instrument).
  • Turning your head to one side for long periods (e.g., watching TV, dual monitor setup).

Dr. Jared's 3-phase relief plan

Work through the phases in order and move on when the current one feels easy and pain-free. Every video is in the free MD Fit app.

Phase 1 · Calm it down

  1. SCM Release (video: SCM Release Seated) · 1-2 minutes per side, daily
    Gently pinch the muscle between your thumb and fingers as shown in the video. Hold tender spots with gentle pressure until they soften. Be careful not to press on the pulse in your neck (carotid artery).
  2. Gentle SCM Stretch · 2 x 20 seconds each side, daily
    Sit tall. Gently tilt your head back, then turn away from the side you want to stretch and tilt your ear to the opposite shoulder. You should feel a gentle pull along the front/side of your neck.
  3. Chin Glides (video: Chin Glide Seated) · 2 x 10 reps, several times daily
    This movement helps to reposition your head over your shoulders, taking the chronic strain off the SCM muscles.

Phase 2 · Restore movement

  1. Neck Neuroglide (video: Neck Neuroglide Seated) · 1 x 10 slow reps each side, daily
    This nerve mobilization can help calm down the nerve pathways that contribute to SCM tension and its unusual symptoms like dizziness.
  2. Deep Neck Flexor Activation (video: Deep Cervical Flexor Nods Seated) · 2 x 10 reps, daily
    Strengthening the small muscles at the front of your neck provides stability and helps the SCM to relax and do its job of moving, not just holding.

Phase 3 · Strengthen

  1. Isometric Neck Rotation · 3 x 10-second holds each side, every other day
    Place your hand on the side of your head. Gently try to turn your head into your hand, but don't let it move. This strengthens the SCM in a controlled way.
  2. Core Brace (video: Core Brace Intro Seated) · 5 x 10-second holds, several times daily
    A stable core provides a foundation for your head and neck. Learning to brace your core reduces strain on neck muscles during daily activities.
  3. Dumbbell Row (video) · 3 x 10-12 reps, twice a week
    A strong upper back helps maintain good posture, which is the key to unloading the SCM long-term. Use a light weight and focus on form.

Habits that help

  • Avoid sleeping on your stomach. Try sleeping on your back or side with a supportive pillow.
  • Position screens directly in front of you to avoid prolonged head turning.
  • When reading a book or on your phone, prop it up to eye level.
  • Practice diaphragmatic (belly) breathing to avoid using your neck muscles for respiration.

What to expect

Symptoms like dizziness and ear fullness can improve quickly, within a week of starting release work. The headache and facial pain patterns may take 2-4 weeks to resolve as you improve your posture and strength.

When to see a professional

If you experience severe, persistent dizziness, hearing loss, or any of the red flag symptoms, please book an appointment immediately for a comprehensive neurological and vestibular screen. Get urgent care for any of these:

  • Sudden onset of severe dizziness or vertigo, especially with difficulty speaking, walking, or seeing.
  • Pain or swelling of the lymph nodes along the muscle that doesn't resolve.
  • A pulsating sensation in the neck (do not press on this).
  • Headache accompanied by fever and a stiff neck that prevents you from touching your chin to your chest.
