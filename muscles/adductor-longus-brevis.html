Where the adductor longus & brevis sends pain

Deep groin, the inner thigh and down to the inner knee and top of the shin. People usually come in describing pain at the inner thigh, groin, inside of the knee or front of the thigh. It is also a common hidden driver behind Shin Splints and Lower-Leg Pain.

What the adductor longus & brevis does

Pulls the thigh inward and helps flex the hip; steadies the pelvis in cutting and kicking.

  • Starts at: Front of the pubic bone near the midline.
  • Attaches to: Middle third of the back of the thigh bone (linea aspera).
  • Nerve supply: Obturator nerve (L2–L4).

Symptoms people describe

  • Deep groin pain.
  • Ache along the inner thigh down to the inner knee and top of the shin.
  • Pain with kicking, sprinting, cutting or getting out of a car.
  • Tenderness near the pubic bone.

What makes it worse

Running or sports · Stairs, squats or hills. If two or more of those match your day, this muscle moves up the list.

Quick self-check

Education, not a diagnosis. The more of these that ring true, the more likely the adductor longus & brevis is involved:

  • Is the pain deep in the groin near the pubic bone?
  • Does squeezing your knees together against resistance hurt?
  • Does kicking, sprinting or changing direction bring it on?
  • Does the ache run down the inner thigh toward the knee?
  • Does getting out of a car hurt?

Squeeze test. Lie on your back with knees bent and squeeze a pillow or ball between the knees for 5 seconds. Suggests this muscle if: Pain in the groin or inner thigh.

What it gets mistaken for

  • Adductor strain or tendinopathy
  • Hip joint problems (FAI, labral tear)
  • Sports hernia (athletic pubalgia)
  • Inguinal hernia

Common causes

  • Soccer, hockey and sports with cutting and kicking.
  • Sudden increase in sprinting.
  • Weak core and hip strength.
  • Tight hips limiting rotation.

Dr. Jared's 3-phase relief plan

Work through the phases in order and move on when the current one feels easy and pain-free. Every video is in the free MD Fit app.

Phase 1 · Calm it down

  1. Inner thigh release · 60–90 sec, twice daily
    Lie face down with the leg out to the side and a ball under the inner thigh; ease weight onto tender spots.
  2. Gentle isometric squeeze · 5 x 10 sec at 50% effort, twice daily
    Squeeze a pillow between the knees gently; pain should stay under 3/10.

Phase 2 · Restore movement

  1. Standing Hip Abduction/Adduction (video) · 2 x 12 per side, daily
    Slow, controlled sweeps in a pain-free range.
  2. 90/90 Hip Switch (video) · 2 x 8, daily
    Restore hip rotation to unload the groin.

Phase 3 · Strengthen

  1. Copenhagen plank (short lever) · 3 x 15 sec per side, every other day
    Side plank with the top knee on a bench; lift the hips. Progress to the foot on the bench over weeks.
  2. Cossack Squat (video) · 3 x 6 per side, twice weekly (from week 4)
    Only partial depth at first; keep the heel down.

Suggested library videos for this muscle: Standing Hip Abduction/Adduction, Cossack Squat, Goblet Sumo Squat.

Habits that help

  • Warm up the groin before sport.
  • Build sprinting and cutting gradually.

What to expect

Muscle-level groin pain improves in 2 to 4 weeks; return to cutting sports in 4 to 8 weeks.

When to see a professional

Book an evaluation if the squeeze test stays painful after 3 weeks, or if there is a bulge, limp or night pain. Get urgent care for any of these:

  • A pop in the groin with bruising.
  • A bulge in the groin that increases with coughing (hernia).
  • Groin pain with fever or painful urination.
  • Hip pain that makes you limp or wakes you at night.

Frequently asked questions

Can a tight adductor longus & brevis cause pain at the inner thigh?

Often, yes. Deep groin, the inner thigh and down to the inner knee and top of the shin. That referred pain is why it gets blamed on the joint or the spot that hurts instead of the muscle.

How long does adductor longus & brevis pain take to settle?

Muscle-level groin pain improves in 2 to 4 weeks; return to cutting sports in 4 to 8 weeks.

When should I see someone about it?

Book an evaluation if the squeeze test stays painful after 3 weeks, or if there is a bulge, limp or night pain.

What else can feel like adductor longus & brevis pain?

Adductor strain or tendinopathy, Hip joint problems (FAI, labral tear), Sports hernia (athletic pubalgia). An evaluation separates muscle pain from these, and anything that needs a physician is referred on.
