Where the platysma sends pain

A prickling or burning across the front of the neck and upper chest, and sometimes over the jawline and chin. People usually come in describing pain at the front of the neck.

What the platysma does

Tenses the skin of the neck and draws the corners of the mouth downward and outward, as in a grimace. It is a muscle of facial expression rather than a mover of joints.

  • Starts at: The fascia covering the upper pectoralis major and deltoid, over the front of the upper chest.
  • Attaches to: The lower border of the mandible and the skin and muscles at the corner of the mouth.
  • Nerve supply: Facial nerve (cranial nerve VII), cervical branch.

Symptoms people describe

  • A prickling, burning or hot sensation over the front of the neck.
  • Discomfort across the upper chest below the collarbones.
  • A superficial, skin-level quality rather than a deep ache.
  • Visible vertical banding at the front of the neck when tense.
  • Symptoms that rise and fall with stress.

What makes it worse

Stress · Desk, phone or screens · Turning your head or body. If two or more of those match your day, this muscle moves up the list.

Quick self-check

Education, not a diagnosis. The more of these that ring true, the more likely the platysma is involved:

  • Is the sensation prickling or burning rather than a deep ache?
  • Is it right at the skin level across the front of your neck?
  • Does it come and go with stress?
  • Do visible cords or bands stand out at the front of your neck?
  • Do you hold tension in your jaw and face?

Platysma contraction. Pull the corners of your mouth down and back firmly in front of a mirror, as in a grimace, so the sheet stands out. Suggests this muscle if: The visible bands are tender, or contracting reproduces the prickling sensation.

Light skin-level palpation. Using very light pressure, move the skin of the front of the neck across the deeper tissue. Suggests this muscle if: Light contact, rather than deep pressure, reproduces the symptom.

What it gets mistaken for

  • Nerve irritation in the neck causing skin sensations.
  • Shingles before the rash appears.
  • Skin conditions.
  • Thyroid problems.
  • Anxiety-related sensations.

Common causes

  • Sustained stress and facial tension.
  • Forward head posture.
  • Scarring or surgery at the front of the neck or chest.
  • Habitual grimacing or straining expressions.
  • Prolonged cold exposure to the neck.

Dr. Jared's 3-phase relief plan

Work through the phases in order and move on when the current one feels easy and pain-free. Every video is in the free MD Fit app.

Phase 1 · Calm it down

  1. Very light touch only · Two minutes
    This is a thin sheet just beneath the skin. Deep pressure makes it worse; slow, feather-light skin gliding works better.
  2. Facial tension release (video: Box Breathing) · 5 minutes daily
    Slow breathing with a soft jaw and a relaxed face settles the expression muscles.
  3. Warmth · Ten minutes
    A warm, not hot, compress across the front of the neck.

Phase 2 · Restore movement

  1. Gentle neck lengthening (video: SCM Release Seated) · Two minutes per side
    Work the neighbouring front-of-neck muscles, which are often the real driver.
  2. Head position reset (video: Chin Glide Seated) · Two sets of ten, several times daily
    Reduces the stretch this sheet is held under all day.
  3. Upper chest opening (video: Baseball Cable Stretch) · Thirty seconds each side, daily
    The lower attachment is on the chest fascia, so chest tightness pulls on it.

Phase 3 · Strengthen

  1. Deep neck flexor work (video: Deep Cervical Flexor Nods Seated) · Ten second holds, five repetitions
    Restores the balance of the front of the neck.
  2. Postural endurance (video: Wall Posture Stand Up) · Daily
    Trains an upright position that does not stretch the front of the neck.
  3. Trunk stability (video: Dead Bug) · Three sets of ten each side
    A settled trunk reduces whole-body bracing.

Suggested library videos for this muscle: SCM Release Seated, Chin Glide Seated, Box Breathing.

Habits that help

  • Notice and release jaw and face tension through the day.
  • Keep screens at eye level.
  • Keep the neck warm in air conditioning.
  • Avoid deep massage directly over this area; it responds to light work.

What to expect

Symptoms here are usually closely tied to stress and head position, and often settle within one to three weeks once both are addressed.

When to see a professional

Book an evaluation if the sensation persists beyond three weeks. See a physician promptly for any rash, swelling, lump or facial weakness. Get urgent care for any of these:

  • A rash appearing in a band, which may be shingles.
  • Swelling or a lump in the neck.
  • Progressive numbness rather than prickling.
  • Difficulty swallowing or breathing.
  • Weakness of the face on one side.
