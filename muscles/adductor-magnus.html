Where the adductor magnus sends pain

Deep inside the pelvis and groin, and the inner thigh from the groin toward the knee. People usually come in describing pain at the inner thigh, groin or back of the thigh.

What the adductor magnus does

Pulls the thigh inward and helps extend the hip like a hamstring.

  • Starts at: Lower pubic bone and the sitting bone (ischial tuberosity).
  • Attaches to: Back of the thigh bone along its length and the adductor tubercle above the inner knee.
  • Nerve supply: Obturator nerve (L2–L4) and the tibial part of the sciatic nerve (L4).

Symptoms people describe

  • Deep pain inside the pelvis or groin, hard to pinpoint.
  • Ache along the inner thigh from the groin toward the knee.
  • Pain with deep squats, lunges or wide stances.
  • Pain sitting on the sitting bone.

What makes it worse

Running or sports · Stairs, squats or hills · Sitting. If two or more of those match your day, this muscle moves up the list.

Quick self-check

Education, not a diagnosis. The more of these that ring true, the more likely the adductor magnus is involved:

  • Is the pain deep in the groin or pelvis and hard to pinpoint?
  • Do deep squats or wide stances bring it on?
  • Is the inner thigh tender along its length toward the knee?
  • Does sitting on hard surfaces make it ache near the sitting bone?
  • Did it start after lunges, sumo lifts or horseback riding?

Wide-stance hinge. Stand with feet wide and toes out, hinge forward from the hips with a flat back. Suggests this muscle if: Pulling or your familiar ache in the inner thigh or deep groin.

What it gets mistaken for

  • Hamstring tendinopathy
  • Pelvic floor dysfunction
  • Hip joint problems
  • Obturator nerve irritation

Common causes

  • Deep squats, sumo deadlifts and lunges.
  • Horseback riding and skiing.
  • Tight hamstrings and adductors from sitting.
  • Sudden splits or slips.

Phase 3 · Strengthen

Habits that help

  • Warm up before deep squats or wide stances.
  • Use a cushion on hard seats.

What to expect

Usually improves within 3 to 4 weeks.

When to see a professional

Book an evaluation if deep pelvic pain persists, or comes with urinary, bowel or menstrual symptoms. Get urgent care for any of these:

  • Pelvic pain with fever, urinary or bowel symptoms.
  • A pop with bruising on the inner thigh.
  • Numbness on the inner thigh with weakness.
