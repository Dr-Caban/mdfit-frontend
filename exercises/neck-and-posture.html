Neck & Posture Exercises

★★★★★ 5.0 · 89 Google reviews

These are the 17 neck & posture exercises in Dr. Jared Caban's video library, the same ones he assigns as homework at MD Fit in Sarasota. They work on the neck, the base of the skull and the desk-posture muscles between the shoulder blades.

How to use this page

Exercises are grouped by type (desk & posture, lifestyle, mobility, activation, rehab, neuroglide and posture). Where Dr. Jared has written cues for an exercise in one of the muscle relief plans, they are shown here with the dose, and each cue names the muscle it is aimed at. 13 of the 17 exercises have written cues so far; the rest are listed with the muscles they are used for, and every one has a video in the free MD Fit app.

Pain usually comes from a muscle somewhere other than where it hurts, so the best exercise depends on the source. If you are not sure which muscle is involved, start from where it hurts or the muscle library, and follow the three-phase plan on that page: calm it down, restore movement, then strengthen.

Mobility exercises

90/90 Breathing (level 2 of 3, mobility, at home or in a gym).

  • For the Suboccipitals (calm-it-down phase): Lie on your back and focus on breathing into your belly, not your chest. This helps calm the nervous system and reduce overall muscle tension. Dose: 5 minutes, once or twice daily.
  • For the Scalenes (calm-it-down phase): Lie on your back with feet up on a chair. Focus on letting your belly rise as you inhale and fall as you exhale, keeping your chest and neck relaxed. Dose: 5 minutes, twice daily.
  • For the Infraspinatus (calm-it-down phase): Lie on your back with your feet on a chair or wall, knees and hips at 90 degrees. Focus on deep belly breaths to relax the nervous system and reduce muscle guarding. Dose: 5 minutes, once or twice daily.
  • For the Serratus anterior (calm-it-down phase): Lie on your back with your feet up on a chair. Focus on breathing deep into your belly, allowing your rib cage to expand gently without forcing it. Dose: 5 minutes, once or twice daily.

Also used in the relief plans for Intercostals, Brachioradialis, Psoas, Iliacus, Gluteus maximus, Tibialis posterior, Peroneals, Foot muscles, Pectoralis major, upper head, Pectoralis major, lower head and Subclavius.

Box Breathing (level 2 of 3, mobility, at home or in a gym).

  • For the Occipitalis (calm-it-down phase): Inhale for 4 seconds, hold for 4, exhale for 4, hold for 4. This technique helps lower stress and reduce unconscious muscle clenching. Dose: 5 minutes, once or twice daily.
  • For the Temporalis (calm-it-down phase): Inhale for 4 seconds, hold for 4, exhale for 4, hold for 4. This helps calm the nervous system and reduce clenching. Dose: 5 minutes, 1-2 times daily or when stressed.
  • For the Masseter (calm-it-down phase): Calming your nervous system is key to reducing clenching. Inhale for 4 seconds, hold for 4, exhale for 4, and hold for 4. Dose: 5 minutes, 1-2 times daily or when stressed.
  • For the Supinator (calm-it-down phase): Follow the video cues to inhale for 4 seconds, hold for 4, exhale for 4, and hold for 4. This helps calm the nervous system to reduce muscle tension. Dose: 5 minutes, 1-2 times daily.

Levator Scap Stretch Seated (level 2 of 3, mobility, at home or in a gym, done seated).

  • For the Levator scapulae (calm-it-down phase): Follow the video cues precisely: turn your head away from the painful side and look down towards your opposite armpit. Use gentle hand pressure. Dose: 2 x 30 seconds each side, twice daily.

Upper Trap Stretch Seated (level 2 of 3, mobility, at home or in a gym, done seated).

  • For the Upper trapezius (calm-it-down phase): Sit tall and gently tilt your ear toward your opposite shoulder. Use your hand for a light overpressure, but do not force it. Dose: 2 x 30 seconds each side, twice daily.

Activation exercises

Chin Glide Seated (level 2 of 3, activation, at home or in a gym, done seated).

  • For the Suboccipitals (restore-movement phase): Sit tall and gently glide your head backward over your shoulders, keeping your eyes level. This reverses the forward head posture that strains these muscles. Dose: 2 x 10 reps, twice daily.
  • For the Occipitalis (calm-it-down phase): Gently glide your head backward over your shoulders, keeping your eyes level. This reduces the tension on the back of the scalp. Dose: 2 x 10 reps, several times daily.
  • For the Sternocleidomastoid (calm-it-down phase): This movement helps to reposition your head over your shoulders, taking the chronic strain off the SCM muscles. Dose: 2 x 10 reps, several times daily.
  • For the Splenius capitis & cervicis (calm-it-down phase): Gently glide your chin straight back as if making a 'double chin'. Avoid tilting your head down; the motion is a straight glide. Dose: 2 x 10 reps, several times daily.

Also used in the relief plans for Temporalis and Masseter.

Core Brace Intro Seated (level 2 of 3, activation, at home or in a gym, done seated).

  • For the Sternocleidomastoid (strengthen phase): A stable core provides a foundation for your head and neck. Learning to brace your core reduces strain on neck muscles during daily activities. Dose: 5 x 10-second holds, several times daily.
  • For the Scalenes (strengthen phase): Engage your abdominal muscles as if you're about to be punched in the stomach. Breathe normally while holding this tension. Dose: 3 x 30 sec holds, daily.
  • For the Intercostals (strengthen phase): Gently tighten your abdominal muscles as if you are about to be poked in the stomach. Hold this gentle brace while breathing normally to help stabilize the rib cage. Dose: 3 x 10 second holds, several times a day.
  • For the Quadratus lumborum (restore-movement phase): Learning to gently engage your deep core muscles provides stability for the lumbar spine, allowing the overworked QL to relax. Dose: 2 x 10 reps of 10 sec holds, daily.

Also used in the relief plans for Multifidus.

Deep Cervical Flexor Nods Seated (level 2 of 3, activation, at home or in a gym, done seated).

  • For the Suboccipitals (calm-it-down phase): Gently nod your chin towards your throat as if making a double chin. This is a very small, subtle movement to activate the deep neck muscles. Dose: 2 x 10 slow reps, several times daily.
  • For the Occipitalis (strengthen phase): Strengthening the muscles at the front of your neck helps to balance the tension at the back, improving head posture. Dose: 3 x 10 reps, every other day.
  • For the Sternocleidomastoid (restore-movement phase): Strengthening the small muscles at the front of your neck provides stability and helps the SCM to relax and do its job of moving, not just holding. Dose: 2 x 10 reps, daily.
  • For the Scalenes (strengthen phase): Gently tuck your chin to create a 'double chin' and feel the deep muscles at the front of your neck engage. Don't use your large, outer neck muscles. Dose: 2 x 10 sec holds, 5 reps, daily.

Also used in the relief plans for Splenius capitis & cervicis, Temporalis and Masseter.

Rehab exercises

Eccentric Scalene Drop (level 2 of 3, rehab, at home or in a gym).

  • For the Scalenes (restore-movement phase): Use your hand to help lift your head from a side-tilted position, then slowly lower it back down over 3-5 seconds, controlling the movement. Dose: 2 x 8 reps per side, every other day.

SCM Release Seated (level 2 of 3, rehab, at home or in a gym, done seated).

  • For the Sternocleidomastoid (calm-it-down phase): Gently pinch the muscle between your thumb and fingers as shown in the video. Hold tender spots with gentle pressure until they soften. Be careful not to press on the pulse in your neck (carotid artery). Dose: 1-2 minutes per side, daily.

Standing Scalene Stretch (level 2 of 3, rehab, at home or in a gym).

  • For the Scalenes (calm-it-down phase): Gently tilt your ear away from the shoulder you're stretching. To deepen, you can gently hold onto a chair or place your other hand over your head. Dose: 2 x 30 sec per side, daily.
  • For the Subclavius (restore-movement phase): Ease the neck muscles that share the space with the subclavius. Dose: 2 x 30 sec per side, daily.
  • For the Rhomboid minor (restore-movement phase): Scalenes often refer here; ease them too. Dose: 2 x 30 sec per side, daily.

Towel Neck Stretch Eccentric (level 2 of 3, rehab, at home or in a gym).

  • For the Upper trapezius (restore-movement phase): Use a towel to gently resist as you slowly return your head from a side-bent position to neutral. This builds control and resilience in the muscle. Dose: 2 x 5 slow reps each side, daily.
  • For the Levator scapulae (restore-movement phase): After stretching, use a towel to gently resist as you bring your head back to neutral. This builds strength and control in the muscle's lengthened range. Dose: 2 x 5 slow reps in the levator stretch position, daily.
  • For the Splenius capitis & cervicis (restore-movement phase): Use a towel to gently pull your head forward into flexion, then use your neck muscles to slowly resist the towel as you return to the start position over 3-5 seconds. Dose: 2 x 8 reps, every other day.

Neuroglide exercises

Neck Neuroglide Seated (level 2 of 3, neuroglide, at home or in a gym, done seated).

  • For the Occipitalis (restore-movement phase): This gentle nerve mobilization helps reduce tension patterns that can affect the scalp and neck. Dose: 1 x 10 slow reps each side, daily.
  • For the Sternocleidomastoid (restore-movement phase): This nerve mobilization can help calm down the nerve pathways that contribute to SCM tension and its unusual symptoms like dizziness. Dose: 1 x 10 slow reps each side, daily.
  • For the Scalenes (restore-movement phase): Gently move through the prescribed range of motion to mobilize the nerves that pass through the neck and arm. Move slowly and without pain. Dose: 1 x 10 reps per side, daily.
  • For the Splenius capitis & cervicis (restore-movement phase): This helps ensure the nerves in the area are moving freely. Follow the video cues and move gently without forcing into pain. Dose: 1 x 10 reps per side, daily.

Also used in the relief plans for Brachioradialis and Supinator.

Posture exercises

Seated Desk Posture Reset (level 1 of 3, posture, at home or in a gym, done seated).

  • For the Suboccipitals (strengthen phase): Use this video to actively correct your posture throughout the day, strengthening the muscles that support a neutral head position. Dose: Hold for 30 seconds, every hour.
  • For the Occipitalis (restore-movement phase): Actively correcting your posture reduces the chronic strain on the muscles at the back of your head and neck. Dose: Hold for 30 seconds, every hour.
  • For the Upper trapezius (restore-movement phase): Regularly resetting your posture helps train your body to stop over-relying on the upper traps for support. Dose: Hold for 30 seconds, every hour.
  • For the Scalenes (restore-movement phase): Follow the video cues to reset your head, neck, and shoulder position to a more neutral alignment. Dose: Every 30 minutes at your desk.

Also used in the relief plans for Temporalis and Masseter.

Standing Posture Reset (level 1 of 3, posture, at home or in a gym). The video is in the free MD Fit app.

Stop and get checked if

  • An exercise causes sharp, shooting or steadily worsening pain, or pain that lasts into the next day.
  • You have numbness, tingling or weakness that is new or spreading.
  • Pain follows a fall, an accident or a blow.
  • You have chest pain, shortness of breath or sudden severe headache: call 911.
