Spine & Core Exercises

★★★★★ 5.0 · 89 Google reviews

These are the 24 spine & core exercises in Dr. Jared Caban's video library, the same ones he assigns as homework at MD Fit in Sarasota. They work on the low and mid back, the deep spinal muscles and the abdominal wall.

How to use this page

Exercises are grouped by type (power, strength, activation and mobility). Where Dr. Jared has written cues for an exercise in one of the muscle relief plans, they are shown here with the dose, and each cue names the muscle it is aimed at. 13 of the 24 exercises have written cues so far; the rest are listed with the muscles they are used for, and every one has a video in the free MD Fit app.

Pain usually comes from a muscle somewhere other than where it hurts, so the best exercise depends on the source. If you are not sure which muscle is involved, start from where it hurts or the muscle library, and follow the three-phase plan on that page: calm it down, restore movement, then strengthen.

Strength exercises

Dead Bug (level 1 of 3, strength, at home or in a gym).

  • For the Temporalis (strengthen phase): A strong core helps stabilize the entire spine, reducing compensatory tension in the neck and jaw. Dose: 3 x 10 reps per side, every other day.
  • For the Masseter (strengthen phase): A stable trunk provides a solid base for your head and neck, reducing the need for compensatory tension in the jaw. Dose: 3 x 10 reps per side, every other day.
  • For the Intercostals (strengthen phase): Lie on your back with knees bent at 90 degrees. Slowly lower one opposite arm and leg towards the floor while keeping your core braced and low back flat. Start with small movements. Dose: 3 x 8 reps per side, every other day.
  • For the Multifidus (restore-movement phase): This is a key exercise for multifidus retraining. Focus on keeping your low back absolutely still as you move your limbs. Pressing your back gently into the floor helps activate the deep stabilizers. Dose: 3 x 10 reps (5 per side), daily.

Also used in the relief plans for Psoas, Iliacus, Iliocostalis, Longissimus and External oblique.

Dynamic Plank (level 3 of 3, strength, at home).

  • For the Quadratus lumborum (strengthen phase): Lie on your side, propped on your elbow. Lift your hips off the floor, creating a straight line from head to feet. Start from your knees if a full plank is too difficult. Dose: 3 x 30-60 sec hold per side, every other day.

Activation exercises

Wall Posture Stand Up (level 1 of 3, activation, at home or in a gym). The video is in the free MD Fit app.

Farmer Carry (level 3 of 3, activation, in a gym).

  • For the Upper trapezius (strengthen phase): Carry a moderate weight in each hand. Keep your chest up and shoulders pulled down and back. This teaches the traps to stabilize the shoulder girdle correctly. Dose: 3 sets of 30-second walks, twice a week.
  • For the Levator scapulae (strengthen phase): Walking with weights teaches you to keep your shoulder blades 'down and back', strengthening the muscles that oppose the levator's shrugging action. Dose: 3 sets of 30-second walks, twice a week.
  • For the Scalenes (strengthen phase): Hold a moderate weight in each hand, stand tall with good posture, and walk. This builds postural endurance for the neck and shoulders. Dose: 3 x 40 yards, twice weekly.
  • For the Brachioradialis (strengthen phase): Hold a moderately heavy weight in each hand at your sides. Walk with tall posture, keeping your shoulders back and down, engaging your grip and core. Dose: 3 x 30 sec walks, every other day.

Also used in the relief plans for Quadratus lumborum, Lateral deltoid, Wrist extensors, Finger flexors and External oblique.

Bear Crawl (level 6 of 3, activation, at home). The video is in the free MD Fit app.

Mobility exercises

Baseball Cable Stretch (level 2 of 3, mobility, at home or in a gym).

  • For the Anterior deltoid (restore-movement phase): Open the front of the shoulder and chest gently; stop at a stretch, not pain. Dose: 2 x 30 sec per side, daily.
  • For the Pectoralis major, upper head (restore-movement phase): Arm above shoulder height to target the upper fibers; stretch, not pain. Dose: 2 x 30 sec per side, daily.
  • For the Pectoralis major, lower head (restore-movement phase): Arm at or just below shoulder height to target the sternal fibers. Dose: 2 x 30 sec per side, daily.
  • For the Subclavius (restore-movement phase): Open the front of the shoulder gently; stop if tingling increases. Dose: 2 x 30 sec per side, daily.

Also used in the relief plans for Coracobrachialis, Biceps, long head, Biceps, short head, Middle trapezius, Lower trapezius, Rhomboid major, External oblique and Pectoralis minor.

Cat Cow Desk Variation (level 2 of 3, mobility, at home or in a gym, done seated).

  • For the Intercostals (restore-movement phase): On your hands and knees (or seated), gently round your spine up towards the ceiling, then slowly arch it down. Move slowly and within a comfortable, pain-free range of motion. Dose: 2 x 10 cycles, once daily.
  • For the Multifidus (calm-it-down phase): Perform this movement very slowly and within a small, pain-free range. The goal is gentle movement to reduce stiffness, not a deep stretch. Dose: 2 x 10 cycles, daily.
  • For the Iliocostalis (calm-it-down phase): Smooth, pain-free spinal motion. Dose: 2 x 10, twice daily.
  • For the Longissimus (calm-it-down phase): Gentle spinal motion. Dose: 2 x 10, twice daily.

Lying Open Book (level 2 of 3, mobility, at home or in a gym).

  • For the Infraspinatus (restore-movement phase): Lie on your side with knees bent and arms straight out in front. Keeping your hips still, rotate your top arm and upper body open toward the ceiling to improve thoracic mobility. Dose: 2 x 8 reps per side, once daily.
  • For the Intercostals (restore-movement phase): Lie on your side with knees bent and arms extended in front of you. Keeping your hips still, rotate your top arm and torso open, as if opening a book. Only go as far as is comfortable. Dose: 2 x 8 reps per side, once daily.
  • For the Pectoralis major, lower head (restore-movement phase): Open the chest by rotating the upper back. Dose: 2 x 8 per side, daily.
  • For the External oblique (restore-movement phase): Rotate the upper back while the hips stay still. Dose: 2 x 8 per side, daily.

Pike Forward Fold (level 2 of 3, mobility, at home or in a gym).

  • For the Longissimus (restore-movement phase): Fold gently with soft knees, letting the back lengthen. Dose: 2 x 30 sec, daily.
  • For the Biceps femoris (restore-movement phase): Gentle fold with soft knees. Dose: 2 x 30 sec, daily.

Also used in the relief plans for Semitendinosus & semimembranosus.

Quadruped T-Spine Rotation (level 2 of 3, mobility, at home or in a gym).

  • For the Levator scapulae (restore-movement phase): Improving mobility in your mid-back reduces the rotational stress on your neck, giving the levator a break. Dose: 2 x 10 reps each side, daily.
  • For the Splenius capitis & cervicis (restore-movement phase): Improving mobility in your upper back reduces the strain on your neck. Focus on rotating through your rib cage. Dose: 2 x 10 reps per side, daily.
  • For the Quadratus lumborum (restore-movement phase): This mobilizes the mid-back, which can reduce the strain on the low back and QL. Follow the video cues for smooth, controlled rotation. Dose: 2 x 10 per side, daily.
  • For the External oblique (restore-movement phase): Restore upper-back rotation. Dose: 2 x 8 per side, daily.

Seated Mobility Flow Intermediate (level 2 of 3, mobility, at home or in a gym, done seated). The video is in the free MD Fit app.

Sit Turn Lean (level 2 of 3, mobility, at home or in a gym).

  • For the Quadratus lumborum (restore-movement phase): Sit tall in a chair. Reach one arm overhead and gently lean to the opposite side, feeling a stretch in your flank and low back. Keep both sit bones on the chair. Dose: 3 x 30 sec per side, daily.
  • For the Iliocostalis (restore-movement phase): Side-bend and rotate gently to lengthen the outer back. Dose: 2 x 6 per side, daily.

Thoracic Extension Over Chair (level 2 of 3, mobility, at home or in a gym).

  • For the Pectoralis minor (calm-it-down phase): This gently opens the chest and reverses the slumped posture that shortens the pec minor. Focus on breathing into your upper chest. Dose: 5-10 breaths, 3-5 times per day.
  • For the Lateral deltoid (restore-movement phase): Improve upper-back extension so the shoulder blade can rotate up as you lift. Dose: 2 x 8, daily.
  • For the Teres major (restore-movement phase): Extend the upper back so the arm can go overhead without the teres major pulling. Dose: 2 x 8, daily.
  • For the Pectoralis major, lower head (restore-movement phase): Extend the upper back so the chest is not held shortened. Dose: 2 x 8, daily.

Also used in the relief plans for Triceps, long head, Middle trapezius, Lower trapezius and Rhomboid major.

Thoracic Open Book (level 2 of 3, mobility, at home or in a gym).

  • For the Anterior deltoid (restore-movement phase): Rotate through the upper back so the shoulder can sit back without straining the front delt. Dose: 2 x 8 per side, daily.
  • For the Pectoralis major, upper head (restore-movement phase): Rotate open through the upper back while the chest lengthens. Dose: 2 x 8 per side, daily.
  • For the Subclavius (restore-movement phase): Rotate open to free the front of the chest. Dose: 2 x 8 per side, daily.

Thoracic Rotation Seated (level 2 of 3, mobility, at home or in a gym, done seated).

  • For the Middle trapezius (calm-it-down phase): Rotate gently through the upper back. Dose: 2 x 8 per side, daily.
  • For the Rhomboid minor (restore-movement phase): Smooth rotation through the upper back. Dose: 2 x 8 per side, daily.

Thread The Needle (level 2 of 3, mobility, at home or in a gym).

  • For the Serratus anterior (calm-it-down phase): Start on your hands and knees. Gently reach one arm under your body, rotating your torso slightly. Move slowly and only within a pain-free range. Dose: 2 x 8 reps per side, once daily.
  • For the Latissimus dorsi (restore-movement phase): On hands and knees, reach one arm under your body, then open it up toward the ceiling. Follow your hand with your eyes to encourage rotation through your mid-back. Dose: 2 x 10 reps per side, once daily.
  • For the Posterior deltoid (calm-it-down phase): Rotate gently through the upper back to ease the back of the shoulder. Dose: 2 x 8 per side, daily.
  • For the Teres major (restore-movement phase): Rotate and reach to lengthen the back of the shoulder. Dose: 2 x 8 per side, daily.

Also used in the relief plans for Triceps, long head, Rhomboid minor, Rhomboid major and External oblique.

Stop and get checked if

  • An exercise causes sharp, shooting or steadily worsening pain, or pain that lasts into the next day.
  • You have numbness, tingling or weakness that is new or spreading.
  • Pain follows a fall, an accident or a blow.
  • You have chest pain, shortness of breath or sudden severe headache: call 911.

Frequently asked questions

Yes, they are the same exercises Dr. Jared assigns as homework. Start with the level 1 versions, keep every repetition pain-free, and stop anything that causes sharp, shooting or worsening pain.
