Shoulder Exercises

★★★★★ 5.0 · 89 Google reviews

These are the 35 shoulder exercises in Dr. Jared Caban's video library, the same ones he assigns as homework at MD Fit in Sarasota. They work on the rotator cuff, the deltoids, the chest and the muscles that steer the shoulder blade.

How to use this page

Exercises are grouped by type (rehab, mobility, strength and activation). Where Dr. Jared has written cues for an exercise in one of the muscle relief plans, they are shown here with the dose, and each cue names the muscle it is aimed at. 18 of the 35 exercises have written cues so far; the rest are listed with the muscles they are used for, and every one has a video in the free MD Fit app.

Pain usually comes from a muscle somewhere other than where it hurts, so the best exercise depends on the source. If you are not sure which muscle is involved, start from where it hurts or the muscle library, and follow the three-phase plan on that page: calm it down, restore movement, then strengthen.

Rehab exercises

Abducted External Rotation (level 2 of 3, rehab, at home or in a gym).

  • For the Infraspinatus (strengthen phase): Raise your arm to the side, parallel to the floor, with your elbow bent at 90 degrees. Rotate your hand upward toward the ceiling, keeping your shoulder blade stable. Dose: 3 x 10 reps, every other day.
  • For the Lateral deltoid (strengthen phase): Arm out to the side at 90 degrees, rotate up slowly to build cuff endurance. Dose: 3 x 10, every other day.

Also used in the relief plans for Supraspinatus.

Abducted Internal Rotation (level 2 of 3, rehab, at home or in a gym).

  • For the Subscapularis (strengthen phase): Lie on your back with your arm abducted to 90 degrees, elbow bent. Slowly lower a very light weight toward the floor, then rotate it back up. This strengthens the muscle in a functional position. Dose: 3 x 10 reps, every other day.

Rotator Cuff External Rotation (level 2 of 3, rehab, at home or in a gym).

  • For the Supraspinatus (restore-movement phase): Keep your elbow pinned to your side at a 90-degree angle. Rotate your forearm outward against light band resistance. The movement should come from your shoulder, not your wrist. Dose: 2 x 15 reps, every other day.
  • For the Infraspinatus (strengthen phase): Use a light resistance band. Keep your elbow tucked into your side at a 90-degree angle and rotate your forearm outward against the band's resistance. Control the movement back. Dose: 3 x 15 reps, every other day.
  • For the Teres minor (strengthen phase): With a light band and elbow at your side, rotate your forearm outward. Focus on the small muscles at the back of your shoulder doing the work. Dose: 3 x 15 reps, every other day.
  • For the Anterior deltoid (strengthen phase): Build the cuff that keeps the ball centered so the front delt is not overworked. Dose: 3 x 15, every other day.

Also used in the relief plans for Posterior deltoid and Biceps, long head.

Rotator Cuff Internal Rotation (level 2 of 3, rehab, at home or in a gym).

  • For the Subscapularis (restore-movement phase): Anchor a light band. With your elbow at your side, pull the band across your body, rotating your arm inward. Control the movement back to the start. Dose: 2 x 15 reps, every other day.
  • For the Coracobrachialis (restore-movement phase): Gentle cuff work to share the load of centering the shoulder. Dose: 2 x 12 light, daily.

Shoulder Drop Hold (level 2 of 3, rehab, at home or in a gym).

  • For the Subscapularis (calm-it-down phase): Lie on your back and let your arm fall open to the side, supported by the floor. This provides a gentle, passive stretch to the front of the shoulder. Dose: 3 x 30-second holds, twice daily.
  • For the Anterior deltoid (calm-it-down phase): Let the shoulder settle down and back away from the ear and hold, keeping the neck relaxed. Dose: 3 x 20 sec, daily.
  • For the Lateral deltoid (calm-it-down phase): Relax the shoulder down away from the ear to unload the deltoid. Dose: 3 x 20 sec, daily.
  • For the Coracobrachialis (calm-it-down phase): Let the shoulder settle down and back. Dose: 3 x 20 sec, daily.

Also used in the relief plans for Biceps, long head.

Mobility exercises

Scapular CARs (level 2 of 3, mobility, at home or in a gym).

  • For the Upper trapezius (restore-movement phase): Controlled Articular Rotations help improve the movement and awareness of your shoulder blades, which can reduce the workload on the upper traps. Dose: 1 x 5 circles each direction, daily.
  • For the Levator scapulae (restore-movement phase): This exercise improves the mobility and control of your shoulder blade, which helps the levator scapulae to relax and function properly. Dose: 1 x 5 large, slow circles each direction, daily.
  • For the Supraspinatus (restore-movement phase): Move your shoulder blade through its full range of motion—up, back, down, and forward—while keeping your arm relatively still. This improves shoulder blade control. Dose: 1 x 5 circles each way, daily.
  • For the Teres minor (calm-it-down phase): Keeping your arms straight, gently move your shoulder blades through their full range of motion: up, forward, down, and back. This promotes gentle movement and awareness. Dose: 2 x 5 circles each way, twice daily.

Also used in the relief plans for Anterior deltoid, Lateral deltoid, Posterior deltoid and Lower trapezius.

Serratus Stretch Breathing (level 2 of 3, mobility, at home or in a gym).

  • For the Serratus anterior (calm-it-down phase): Lie on your non-painful side with a small pillow under your waist. Focus on breathing into your painful side, feeling the ribs expand with each slow inhale. Dose: 2 x 10 deep breaths, twice daily.
  • For the Intercostals (calm-it-down phase): Lie on your non-painful side with a pillow or rolled towel under your ribs. Let your body relax over the pillow to create a gentle opening stretch in the painful area as you breathe. Dose: 2 x 10 gentle breaths, once daily.

Strength exercises

Wall Push Up (level 1 of 3, strength, at home).

  • For the Serratus anterior (strengthen phase): Stand facing a wall and perform a push-up, focusing on feeling the muscles on the side of your ribs engage as you push away from the wall. Keep your core tight. Dose: 3 x 8-12 reps, every other day.
  • For the Anterior deltoid (strengthen phase): Slow 3-second lowering, shoulder blades gliding around the ribs, no front-shoulder pinch. Dose: 3 x 12, every other day.
  • For the Coracobrachialis (strengthen phase): Elbows at 45 degrees, not flared. Dose: 3 x 12, every other day.
  • For the Triceps, medial head (strengthen phase): Smooth pushing without slamming into lockout. Dose: 3 x 12, every other day.

Also used in the relief plans for Pectoralis major, upper head.

Strict Press (level 2 of 3, strength, in a gym).

  • For the Anterior deltoid (strengthen phase): Light dumbbells, ribs down, press in the slightly-forward 'scapular plane'. Progress only if pain-free next day. Dose: 3 x 8 light, twice weekly.

Eccentric Push Up (level 3 of 3, strength, at home).

  • For the Pectoralis major, upper head (strengthen phase): Lower over 3–4 seconds with the shoulder blades gliding; no front-shoulder pinch. Dose: 3 x 6, twice weekly.
  • For the Pectoralis major, lower head (strengthen phase): Slow lowering to rebuild pressing tolerance. Dose: 3 x 6, twice weekly.

Lat Pulldown (level 3 of 3, strength, in a gym).

  • For the Subscapularis (strengthen phase): This exercise strengthens the lats, which are partners in internal rotation, helping to balance the shoulder complex. Dose: 3 x 10-12 reps, every other day.
  • For the Latissimus dorsi (strengthen phase): Using a machine or band, pull the bar down to your upper chest. Focus on driving your elbows down and back, feeling the contraction in your back muscles. Dose: 3 x 8-12 reps, every other day.
  • For the Teres major (strengthen phase): Full overhead reach at the top, pull elbows to ribs, lower slowly. Dose: 3 x 10 light, twice weekly.

Lateral Raise (level 3 of 3, strength, in a gym).

  • For the Lateral deltoid (strengthen phase): Thumbs slightly up, lift to shoulder height only, lower over 3 seconds. Dose: 3 x 12 light, every other day.

Scap Push Ups (level 3 of 3, strength, at home).

  • For the Supraspinatus (strengthen phase): In a push-up or quadruped position, keep your elbows straight and just move your shoulder blades. Push the floor away to spread them apart, then let them sink together. This strengthens the serratus anterior, a key shoulder stabilizer. Dose: 3 x 10 reps, twice weekly.
  • For the Pectoralis minor (strengthen phase): This strengthens the serratus anterior muscle, which helps hold the shoulder blade against the rib cage and counteracts the pec minor's pull. Dose: 3 x 10, every other day.
  • For the Serratus anterior (restore-movement phase): In a plank or hands-and-knees position, keep your arms straight and pinch your shoulder blades together, then push them apart to round your upper back. The movement should only come from your shoulder blades. Dose: 2 x 10-15 reps, every other day.
  • For the Subclavius (strengthen phase): Build the serratus to support the shoulder girdle. Dose: 3 x 10, every other day.

Cable Fly (level 4 of 3, strength, in a gym). The video is in the free MD Fit app.

Dumbbell Row (level 4 of 3, strength, in a gym).

  • For the Occipitalis (strengthen phase): Building strength in your upper back creates a strong foundation for your neck and head, offloading the scalp muscles. Use a light weight. Dose: 3 x 10-12 reps, twice a week.
  • For the Sternocleidomastoid (strengthen phase): A strong upper back helps maintain good posture, which is the key to unloading the SCM long-term. Use a light weight and focus on form. Dose: 3 x 10-12 reps, twice a week.
  • For the Splenius capitis & cervicis (strengthen phase): Hinge at your hips with a flat back. Pull the weights up towards your chest, squeezing your shoulder blades together at the top. Dose: 3 x 10-12 reps, twice weekly.
  • For the Infraspinatus (strengthen phase): Support one knee and hand on a bench. Pull a dumbbell up toward your chest, squeezing your shoulder blade. This strengthens the back muscles that support the rotator cuff. Dose: 3 x 10-12 reps, every other day.

Also used in the relief plans for Teres minor, Latissimus dorsi, Posterior deltoid, Teres major, Pectoralis major, lower head, Middle trapezius and Rhomboid major.

Push Up (level 5 of 3, strength, at home).

  • For the Levator scapulae (strengthen phase): Proper push-ups (even on knees or a wall) strengthen the serratus anterior, a key muscle for scapular stability that helps offload the levator. Dose: 3 sets to near failure, twice a week.
  • For the Subscapularis (strengthen phase): Perform push-ups (on knees or toes). This integrates the subscapularis with other pressing muscles like the chest and serratus anterior. Dose: 3 x as many as possible with good form, every other day.

Activation exercises

Floor Slide Scapular Retract (level 2 of 3, activation, at home or in a gym).

  • For the Suboccipitals (strengthen phase): Strengthening your mid-back muscles provides a stable base for your neck and head, reducing the strain on the suboccipitals. Dose: 3 x 10 reps, every other day.
  • For the Upper trapezius (strengthen phase): This can also be done standing against a wall. It activates the lower trapezius and serratus anterior, muscles that help depress the shoulder blades and offload the upper traps. Dose: 3 x 10 reps, every other day.
  • For the Supraspinatus (calm-it-down phase): Perform this standing against a wall. Keep your forearms on the wall and slide them up and down, focusing on moving your shoulder blades without shrugging. Only go as high as you can without pain. Dose: 2 x 10 reps, daily.
  • For the Teres minor (restore-movement phase): Lie on your back with knees bent. Press your forearms into the floor and slide them overhead, focusing on keeping your shoulder blades down and back. Dose: 2 x 10 reps, once daily.

Also used in the relief plans for Pectoralis minor, Serratus anterior, Posterior deltoid, Teres major, Pectoralis major, upper head, Pectoralis major, lower head, Subclavius, Middle trapezius, Lower trapezius, Rhomboid minor and Rhomboid major.

Rotator Cuff 90 Band Vibration (level 2 of 3, activation, at home or in a gym).

  • For the Supraspinatus (strengthen phase): Hold your arm in the 'stop sign' position with a band providing tension. Perform small, quick vibrations to challenge the stability of the rotator cuff. Dose: 3 x 20 sec holds, twice weekly.
  • For the Lateral deltoid (restore-movement phase): Wake up the cuff so it centers the ball before the deltoid lifts. Dose: 3 x 20 sec, daily.

Scapular Clavicle Articulation Posterior (level 2 of 3, activation, at home or in a gym). The video is in the free MD Fit app.

Face Pull (level 5 of 3, activation, in a gym).

  • For the Upper trapezius (strengthen phase): This exercise strengthens the mid-back and external rotators, which helps to counterbalance the pull of tight upper traps and improve posture. Dose: 3 x 12-15 reps, twice a week.
  • For the Levator scapulae (strengthen phase): Pull a light band toward your face with elbows high, squeezing the shoulder blades back and down. Keep the neck long and relaxed. Dose: 3 x 12 reps, twice a week.
  • For the Splenius capitis & cervicis (strengthen phase): Using a band or cable, pull the rope towards your face, aiming your hands towards your ears. Squeeze your shoulder blades together and back. Dose: 3 x 15 reps, twice weekly.
  • For the Teres minor (strengthen phase): Using a cable or band at chest height, pull the handles toward your face, driving your elbows back and externally rotating your shoulders at the end of the movement. Dose: 3 x 12-15 reps, every other day.

Also used in the relief plans for Pectoralis minor, Anterior deltoid, Posterior deltoid, Pectoralis major, upper head, Coracobrachialis, Middle trapezius and Lower trapezius.

Stop and get checked if

  • An exercise causes sharp, shooting or steadily worsening pain, or pain that lasts into the next day.
  • You have numbness, tingling or weakness that is new or spreading.
  • Pain follows a fall, an accident or a blow.
  • You have chest pain, shortness of breath or sudden severe headache: call 911.

Frequently asked questions
