Leg, Knee & Ankle Exercises

These are the 15 leg, knee & ankle exercises in Dr. Jared Caban's video library, the same ones he assigns as homework at MD Fit in Sarasota. They work on the quads, hamstrings, calves, shins and the small muscles of the foot.

How to use this page

Exercises are grouped by type (mobility, rehab, strength, activation and power). Where Dr. Jared has written cues for an exercise in one of the muscle relief plans, they are shown here with the dose, and each cue names the muscle it is aimed at. 8 of the 15 exercises have written cues so far; the rest are listed with the muscles they are used for, and every one has a video in the free MD Fit app.

Pain usually comes from a muscle somewhere other than where it hurts, so the best exercise depends on the source. If you are not sure which muscle is involved, start from where it hurts or the muscle library, and follow the three-phase plan on that page: calm it down, restore movement, then strengthen.

Mobility exercises

Ankle Neuroglide (level 2 of 3, mobility, at home or in a gym).

  • For the Gastrocnemius (restore-movement phase): Follow the video to gently mobilize the nerves that run through the calf. This helps differentiate between muscle tightness and nerve tension. Dose: 2 x 15 reps per side, daily.
  • For the Soleus (restore-movement phase): Follow the video instructions for this gentle nerve mobilization exercise. This helps ensure the nerves in the lower leg are moving freely. Dose: 2 x 15 reps per side, daily.
  • For the Tibialis posterior (restore-movement phase): Follow the video to gently mobilize the nerves around the ankle. This can help reduce irritation and improve tissue health in the area. Dose: 2 x 10 reps, once daily.
  • For the Tibialis anterior (restore-movement phase): Follow the video to gently mobilize the nerves that run along the front of the ankle, which can become irritated with this condition. Dose: 2 x 10 reps, once daily.

Also used in the relief plans for Peroneals.

Knee Neuroglide (level 2 of 3, mobility, at home or in a gym).

  • For the Rectus femoris (restore-movement phase): This helps ensure the femoral nerve, which runs through the quads, is moving freely. Perform gently. Dose: 2 x 10 reps, daily.
  • For the Vastus medialis (calm-it-down phase): Gently perform this nerve glide to ensure the nerves supplying the quad are not restricted, which can inhibit muscle function. Dose: 2 x 10 reps, daily.
  • For the Popliteus (restore-movement phase): Follow the video to gently mobilize the nerves around the back of the knee. This is a gentle flossing motion, not an aggressive stretch. Dose: 2 x 10-15 reps, daily.
  • For the Vastus intermedius (restore-movement phase): Smooth knee and nerve motion. Dose: 2 x 10, daily.

Rehab exercises

Eccentric Calf Drops (level 2 of 3, rehab, at home or in a gym).

  • For the Gastrocnemius (strengthen phase): Stand on a step. Use both feet to rise up onto your toes, then lift the uninjured foot and *slowly* lower the heel of the affected foot below the step level. Use both feet to rise up again. Dose: 3 x 15 per side, every other day.
  • For the Soleus (strengthen phase): Perform as shown in the video, but maintain a bent knee throughout the movement to place more emphasis on the soleus muscle during the lowering phase. Dose: 3 x 15 per side, every other day.
  • For the Tibialis posterior (strengthen phase): Perform on a step as shown, focusing on a very slow (3-4 second) and controlled lowering of your heel. This builds strength and resilience in the tendon. Dose: 3 x 15 reps, every other day.
  • For the Tibialis anterior (strengthen phase): Strengthening the opposing calf muscles helps balance the forces around the ankle. Follow the video, focusing on the slow lowering phase. Dose: 3 x 15 reps, every other day.

Also used in the relief plans for Foot muscles.

Knee Circles (level 2 of 3, rehab, at home or in a gym).

  • For the Rectus femoris (calm-it-down phase): Stand and perform slow, controlled circles with your knees to gently mobilize the joint and improve fluid dynamics without load. Dose: 2 x 10 circles each way, twice daily.
  • For the Popliteus (calm-it-down phase): Sit on a high chair so your feet don't touch the floor. Gently swing your lower leg to make small circles, moving from the knee joint. Dose: 2 x 10 circles each way, twice daily.
  • For the Vastus intermedius (calm-it-down phase): Gentle knee motion. Dose: 2 x 10 each way, daily.

Also used in the relief plans for Vastus lateralis.

Strength exercises

Activation exercises

Heel Walk (level 1 of 3, activation, at home).

  • For the Tibialis anterior (strengthen phase): As shown in the video, walk on your heels while keeping the balls of your feet off the ground. This is the most direct way to strengthen the tibialis anterior. Dose: 3 x 20 steps, every other day.

Also used in the relief plans for Soleus.

Toe Walk (level 1 of 3, activation, at home).

  • For the Gastrocnemius (strengthen phase): Walk around on the balls of your feet, keeping your heels off the ground. This builds endurance in the calf muscles. Dose: 3 x 30 seconds, every other day.
  • For the Tibialis posterior (strengthen phase): Walk on your tiptoes as shown in the video. This directly strengthens the tibialis posterior and other calf muscles that support the arch. Dose: 3 x 20 steps, every other day.
  • For the Foot muscles (strengthen phase): Walking on your tiptoes as shown in the video strengthens the intrinsic muscles and the entire calf complex, which work together to support your foot. Dose: 3 x 20 steps, every other day.

Assault Bike (level 2 of 3, activation, in a gym). The video is in the free MD Fit app.

March Balance (level 2 of 3, activation, at home or in a gym).

  • For the Peroneals (strengthen phase): This adds a dynamic challenge to your balance, forcing the peroneals to react and stabilize the ankle as you move. Dose: 3 x 10 reps per side, every other day.

Single Leg Balance (level 2 of 3, activation, at home or in a gym).

  • For the Vastus medialis (restore-movement phase): This improves proprioception and stability around the knee joint, which is crucial for VMO function. Dose: 3 x 30 sec holds per side, daily.
  • For the Tibialis posterior (strengthen phase): Stand on one leg, focusing on keeping your arch lifted and your ankle stable. This retrains the muscle's crucial role in stabilizing your foot. Dose: 3 x 30-60 seconds, daily.
  • For the Peroneals (strengthen phase): This is the most important exercise to retrain the stabilizing function of the peroneals. As you get better, try it with your eyes closed or on an unstable surface like a pillow. Dose: 3 x 30-60 seconds, daily.
  • For the Foot muscles (strengthen phase): While balancing, focus on actively maintaining your arch and feeling the small muscles in your foot working to keep you stable. Dose: 3 x 30-60 seconds, daily.

Stop and get checked if

  • An exercise causes sharp, shooting or steadily worsening pain, or pain that lasts into the next day.
  • You have numbness, tingling or weakness that is new or spreading.
  • Pain follows a fall, an accident or a blow.
  • You have chest pain, shortness of breath or sudden severe headache: call 911.
