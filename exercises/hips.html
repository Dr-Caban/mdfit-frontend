Hip Exercises

★★★★★ 5.0 · 89 Google reviews

These are the 42 hip exercises in Dr. Jared Caban's video library, the same ones he assigns as homework at MD Fit in Sarasota. They work on the glutes, hip flexors, deep hip rotators and adductors.

How to use this page

Exercises are grouped by type (mobility, strength, activation and rehab). Where Dr. Jared has written cues for an exercise in one of the muscle relief plans, they are shown here with the dose, and each cue names the muscle it is aimed at. 22 of the 42 exercises have written cues so far; the rest are listed with the muscles they are used for, and every one has a video in the free MD Fit app.

Pain usually comes from a muscle somewhere other than where it hurts, so the best exercise depends on the source. If you are not sure which muscle is involved, start from where it hurts or the muscle library, and follow the three-phase plan on that page: calm it down, restore movement, then strengthen.

Mobility exercises

90/90 Hip Switch (level 2 of 3, mobility, at home or in a gym).

  • For the Gluteus medius (calm-it-down phase): Perform this movement slowly and gently to improve hip mobility without stressing the irritated muscle. Dose: 2 x 10 reps, daily.
  • For the Gluteus minimus (calm-it-down phase): This gentle mobility drill helps to improve internal and external rotation of the hip, which can help calm an irritated gluteus minimus. Dose: 2 x 10 reps, daily.
  • For the Piriformis (calm-it-down phase): This is an excellent gentle mobilizer for the deep hip rotators. Move slowly and within a pain-free range of motion. Dose: 2 x 10 reps, twice daily.
  • For the Tensor fasciae latae (calm-it-down phase): Sit on the floor and gently rotate your hips from side to side. This mobilizes the hip joint without stressing the TFL. Dose: 2 x 10 reps, daily.

Also used in the relief plans for Adductor longus & brevis, Adductor magnus and Psoas.

Couch Stretch (level 2 of 3, mobility, at home or in a gym).

  • For the Psoas (restore-movement phase): Kneel in a lunge position with your back shin against a wall or couch. Keep your torso upright and gently squeeze your glute to feel a stretch in the front of the hip. Dose: 2 x 30-60 sec per side, daily.
  • For the Iliacus (restore-movement phase): This stretch targets the entire iliopsoas complex. Focus on keeping your torso upright and squeezing the glute of the back leg to deepen the stretch. Dose: 2 x 30-60 sec per side, daily.
  • For the Tensor fasciae latae (restore-movement phase): Perform this stretch gently, focusing on squeezing the glute of the back leg to open the front of the hip. Do not force it. Dose: 2 x 30-45 sec per side, daily.
  • For the Rectus femoris (restore-movement phase): This is the key stretch for the rectus femoris as it extends the hip and flexes the knee. Squeeze the glute of the back leg to intensify the stretch. Dose: 2 x 30-45 sec per side, daily.

Also used in the relief plans for Vastus lateralis and Vastus intermedius.

Deep Squat Hold (level 2 of 3, mobility, at home or in a gym). The video is in the free MD Fit app.

Dynamic Childs Pose (level 2 of 3, mobility, at home or in a gym).

  • For the Latissimus dorsi (calm-it-down phase): From a kneeling position, fold forward and walk your hands out in front, then to one side to feel a stretch along the opposite side of your back. Breathe into the stretch. Dose: 2 x 30-60 seconds, once daily.
  • For the Latissimus dorsi (restore-movement phase): Sit back toward your heels with arms reaching forward, then walk the hands to one side to lengthen the opposite lat. Breathe into the side ribs. Dose: 2 x 12-15 reps, every other day.
  • For the Quadratus lumborum (calm-it-down phase): From hands and knees, sit your hips back towards your heels. Gently rock side to side to feel a stretch in your low back and lats. Breathe deeply into your back. Dose: 1 x 10 rocks, twice daily.
  • For the Iliacus (calm-it-down phase): From a child's pose position, gently rock your hips side to side to create a mild stretch and movement in the deep hip flexors and low back. Dose: 2 x 10 rocks, twice daily.

Also used in the relief plans for Gluteus maximus, Teres major, Iliocostalis, Longissimus and Adductor magnus.

Hip Neuroglide (level 2 of 3, mobility, at home or in a gym).

  • For the Psoas (restore-movement phase): Follow the video to gently mobilize the nerves that run through the front of the hip, improving their ability to slide and glide. Dose: 2 x 10 reps per side, daily.
  • For the Iliacus (restore-movement phase): Gently mobilize the femoral nerve, which passes near the iliacus, to ensure it can move freely and reduce irritation. Dose: 2 x 10 reps per side, daily.
  • For the Piriformis (restore-movement phase): This is crucial for piriformis issues. It helps the sciatic nerve slide more freely relative to the piriformis muscle, reducing irritation and referred symptoms. Dose: 2 x 10 reps per side, daily.
  • For the Gluteus maximus (restore-movement phase): Perform this gentle nerve mobilization to ensure the nerves around the hip are moving freely. Dose: 2 x 10 reps, daily.

Also used in the relief plans for Biceps femoris and Semitendinosus & semimembranosus.

Standing Clamshell (level 2 of 3, mobility, at home or in a gym).

  • For the Gluteus medius (restore-movement phase): This exercise activates the gluteus medius in a functional, weight-bearing position. Focus on keeping your pelvis level. Dose: 2 x 15 reps per side, every other day.
  • For the Gluteus minimus (restore-movement phase): This exercise helps activate and strengthen the gluteus medius and minimus in a functional standing position. Dose: 2 x 15 reps per side, every other day.
  • For the Piriformis (restore-movement phase): Lie on your side with knees bent and hips stacked. Lift your top knee without rocking your pelvis back. This strengthens other hip rotators to take the load off the piriformis. Dose: 2 x 15 reps per side, every other day.
  • For the Tensor fasciae latae (restore-movement phase): This exercise activates the gluteus medius, which helps reduce the workload on the TFL. Focus on rotating the hip, not just lifting the leg. Dose: 3 x 15 per side, every other day.

Standing Hip Abduction/Adduction (level 2 of 3, mobility, at home or in a gym).

  • For the Gluteus medius (strengthen phase): Use a cable or band to add resistance as you move your leg out to the side. Maintain a stable torso and pelvis throughout the movement. Dose: 3 x 12 reps per side, every other day.
  • For the Adductor longus & brevis (restore-movement phase): Slow, controlled sweeps in a pain-free range. Dose: 2 x 12 per side, daily.
  • For the Adductor magnus (restore-movement phase): Controlled sweeps in a pain-free range. Dose: 2 x 12 per side, daily.

Standing Hip CARs (level 2 of 3, mobility, at home or in a gym).

  • For the Tensor fasciae latae (restore-movement phase): Perform slow, controlled circles with your hip to improve joint mobility and control, reducing strain on the TFL. Dose: 2 x 5 circles each way, daily.

Standing Hip ER Liftoff (level 2 of 3, mobility, at home or in a gym).

  • For the Piriformis (strengthen phase): This directly strengthens the piriformis and other external rotators in a functional, standing position. Focus on control, not height. Dose: 3 x 10 reps per side, every other day.

Standing Hip IR Liftoff (level 2 of 3, mobility, at home or in a gym).

  • For the Gluteus minimus (restore-movement phase): This specifically targets the internal rotation function of the gluteus minimus. Focus on slow, controlled movement without leaning your torso. Dose: 2 x 10 reps per side, every other day.

Hip Thrust (level 3 of 3, mobility, at home).

  • For the Piriformis (strengthen phase): Shoulders on a bench, drive the hips up by squeezing the glutes, ribs down. Lower slowly; add load only when pain-free. Dose: 3 x 8 reps, every other day.
  • For the Gluteus maximus (strengthen phase): With your upper back on a bench and a weight across your hips, drive your hips up by squeezing your glutes. Keep your chin tucked. Dose: 3 x 10-12, twice weekly.
  • For the Longissimus (strengthen phase): Drive with the glutes, ribs down, no arching. Dose: 3 x 10, every other day.
  • For the Biceps femoris (strengthen phase): Strong glutes take load off the hamstrings. Dose: 3 x 10, twice weekly.

Strength exercises

Lunges (level 3 of 3, strength, at home).

  • For the Iliacus (strengthen phase): Lunges build single-leg stability and strength in the glutes and quads, which supports healthy hip function. Dose: 3 x 10 reps per side, every other day.
  • For the Vastus lateralis (strengthen phase): Take a controlled step forward, lowering your hips until both knees are bent at about a 90-degree angle. Push back to the starting position. Dose: 3 x 8-12 per leg, every other day.

Step Up (level 3 of 3, strength, in a gym).

  • For the Psoas (strengthen phase): Step onto a box or stair, driving through the heel of your front foot. Control the movement on the way down. This strengthens the glutes and quads. Dose: 3 x 10 reps per side, every other day.
  • For the Gluteus medius (strengthen phase): Focus on keeping your knee aligned over your foot and your pelvis level as you step up. This is a key functional strengthener for the glute medius. Dose: 3 x 10 reps per side, every other day.
  • For the Tensor fasciae latae (strengthen phase): Focus on driving through the heel of the front foot and keeping your pelvis level to build single-leg stability. Dose: 3 x 10 per side, twice weekly.
  • For the Rectus femoris (strengthen phase): Focus on a slow, controlled lowering (eccentric) phase, as this builds strength and resilience in the quad tendon and muscle. Dose: 3 x 10-12 per side, twice weekly.

Also used in the relief plans for Vastus medialis and Vastus intermedius.

Walking Lunge (level 3 of 3, strength, at home or in a gym). The video is in the free MD Fit app.

Bodyweight Squat (level 4 of 3, strength, at home).

  • For the Iliacus (strengthen phase): Practice squatting with good form, focusing on using your glutes to power the movement up. This retrains proper hip mechanics. Dose: 3 x 12 reps, every other day.
  • For the Rectus femoris (strengthen phase): Practice squatting with perfect form, ensuring your knees track over your feet and you're using your glutes to power the movement up. Dose: 3 x 15, twice weekly.
  • For the Vastus medialis (strengthen phase): Place your heels on a small plate or book. This allows for a more upright torso and can help you focus on quad activation, including the VMO. Dose: 3 x 12-15, twice weekly.
  • For the Vastus lateralis (strengthen phase): Focus on keeping your knees aligned over your second toe; don't let them collapse inward. Control the movement on the way down and up. Dose: 3 x 10-15, every other day.

Also used in the relief plans for Vastus intermedius.

Dowel Hip Hinge (level 4 of 3, strength, at home or in a gym).

  • For the Iliocostalis (strengthen phase): Hinge from the hips with a neutral spine so the glutes share the load. Dose: 3 x 10, every other day.
  • For the Biceps femoris (restore-movement phase): Learn to hinge so the hamstrings lengthen under control. Dose: 2 x 10, daily.
  • For the Semitendinosus & semimembranosus (restore-movement phase): Hinge pattern practice. Dose: 2 x 10, daily.

Rack Pull (level 5 of 3, strength, at home or in a gym).

  • For the Multifidus (strengthen phase): This is a limited range-of-motion deadlift. Set the bar on blocks so you only move it from just below the knees to standing. This strengthens the back in a safe, upright posture. Dose: 3 x 8-10, every other day.

Romanian Deadlift (level 5 of 3, strength, at home or in a gym).

  • For the Gluteus maximus (strengthen phase): Holding weights, hinge at your hips with a flat back, feeling a stretch in your hamstrings. Squeeze your glutes to return to standing. Dose: 3 x 8-10, twice weekly.
  • For the Adductor magnus (strengthen phase): Slow hinge with soft knees; loads the hamstring part of the magnus. Dose: 3 x 8 light, twice weekly.
  • For the Biceps femoris (strengthen phase): Slow lowering over 3 seconds. Dose: 3 x 8, twice weekly.
  • For the Semitendinosus & semimembranosus (strengthen phase): Slow lowering; progress load gradually. Dose: 3 x 8, twice weekly.

Activation exercises

Glute Walk (level 1 of 3, activation, at home or in a gym).

  • For the Gluteus medius (restore-movement phase): Place a resistance band around your ankles or knees. Take slow, controlled side steps, keeping your toes pointed forward and your torso upright. Dose: 2 x 10 steps each direction, every other day.
  • For the Gluteus minimus (strengthen phase): Use a resistance band around your ankles or knees. The side-stepping motion is excellent for building endurance in both the gluteus medius and minimus. Dose: 2 x 10 steps each direction, every other day.

Quadruped Glute Kick (level 1 of 3, activation, at home or in a gym).

  • For the Multifidus (restore-movement phase): From hands and knees, extend one leg back by squeezing your glute. The challenge is to keep your spine perfectly still, which requires the multifidus to work hard as a stabilizer. Dose: 3 x 15 per side, every other day.
  • For the Psoas (restore-movement phase): On all fours, engage your core and lift one leg back, squeezing the glute at the top. This activates the opposing muscle to help the psoas relax. Dose: 2 x 15 reps per side, every other day.
  • For the Gluteus maximus (restore-movement phase): On all fours, keep your core braced and extend one leg back, squeezing the glute at the top. Avoid arching your lower back. Dose: 3 x 15 per side, every other day.

Constant Glute Engagement (level 2 of 3, activation, at home or in a gym).

  • For the Gluteus maximus (strengthen phase): Practice this cue to gently engage your glutes with each step, improving your mind-muscle connection throughout the day. Dose: Incorporate into daily walking.

Single Leg Glute Bridge (level 4 of 3, activation, at home).

  • For the Quadratus lumborum (strengthen phase): Lie on your back with knees bent. Squeeze your glutes to lift your hips. Progressing to the single-leg version improves pelvic stability, reducing strain on the QL. Dose: 3 x 10-12 per side, every other day.
  • For the Multifidus (strengthen phase): Lifting your hips requires the multifidus to stabilize the spine. The single-leg version increases the stability demand significantly. Dose: 3 x 10-12 per side, every other day.
  • For the Psoas (strengthen phase): Lie on your back with one foot flat on the floor and the other leg extended. Drive through your heel to lift your hips, squeezing the glute. Dose: 3 x 12 reps per side, every other day.
  • For the Gluteus medius (strengthen phase): This variation challenges pelvic stability, forcing the gluteus medius to work harder to prevent the hip from dropping. Dose: 3 x 10 reps per side, every other day.

Also used in the relief plans for Gluteus minimus, Gluteus maximus, Tensor fasciae latae, Rectus femoris and Semitendinosus & semimembranosus.

Rehab exercises

Pillow Belt Reset (level 2 of 3, rehab, at home or in a gym).

  • For the Quadratus lumborum (calm-it-down phase): Follow the video instructions to gently reset the pelvis. This can provide immediate, temporary relief by relaxing deep muscles around the hips and low back. Dose: 1 x 5 reps of 5 sec holds, as needed.
  • For the Multifidus (calm-it-down phase): This gentle isometric exercise can help reset the relationship between the pelvis and sacrum, calming the deep muscles that attach there, including the multifidus. Dose: 1 x 5 reps of 5 sec holds, 1-2 times daily.
  • For the Tensor fasciae latae (calm-it-down phase): Use this technique to gently reset the pelvis and reduce compensatory tension in the hip flexors. Dose: 1 x 5 minutes, daily.

Table Eccentric Hip Drop (level 2 of 3, rehab, at home or in a gym). The video is in the free MD Fit app.

Stop and get checked if

  • An exercise causes sharp, shooting or steadily worsening pain, or pain that lasts into the next day.
  • You have numbness, tingling or weakness that is new or spreading.
  • Pain follows a fall, an accident or a blow.
  • You have chest pain, shortness of breath or sudden severe headache: call 911.

Frequently asked questions

Can I do these hip exercises on my own?

Yes, they are the same exercises Dr. Jared assigns as homework. Start with the level 1 versions, keep every repetition pain-free, and stop anything that causes sharp, shooting or worsening pain.
