How to use this page

Exercises are grouped by type (mobility, power, neuroglide, activation and strength). Where Dr. Jared has written cues for an exercise in one of the muscle relief plans, they are shown here with the dose, and each cue names the muscle it is aimed at. 2 of the 19 exercises have written cues so far; the rest are listed with the muscles they are used for, and every one has a video in the free MD Fit app.

Pain usually comes from a muscle somewhere other than where it hurts, so the best exercise depends on the source. If you are not sure which muscle is involved, start from where it hurts or the muscle library, and follow the three-phase plan on that page: calm it down, restore movement, then strengthen.

Mobility exercises

Front Crab Walk (level 4 of 3, mobility, at home). The video is in the free MD Fit app.

Side Crab Walk (level 5 of 3, mobility, at home).

  • For the Gluteus minimus (strengthen phase): Stay low with a band around the knees or ankles and step sideways, keeping toes forward and the pelvis level. Dose: 3 x 6 reps per side, every other day.
  • For the Tensor fasciae latae (strengthen phase): Place a band around your knees or ankles. Stay low and take slow, controlled steps sideways, keeping tension on the band to strengthen your gluteus medius. Dose: 3 x 10 steps each way, twice weekly.
  • For the Peroneals (strengthen phase): Place a band around your knees or ankles. Take side steps as shown to strengthen the hip muscles, which are crucial for controlling leg and ankle alignment. Dose: 3 x 10 steps each way, every other day.

Activation exercises

Row (level 3 of 3, activation, at home or in a gym).

  • For the Biceps, short head (strengthen phase): Smooth pulling to rebuild arm and back strength together. Dose: 3 x 10, twice weekly.
  • For the Rhomboid minor (strengthen phase): Start the pull with the shoulder blades. Dose: 3 x 10, twice weekly.

Stop and get checked if

  • An exercise causes sharp, shooting or steadily worsening pain, or pain that lasts into the next day.
  • You have numbness, tingling or weakness that is new or spreading.
  • Pain follows a fall, an accident or a blow.
  • You have chest pain, shortness of breath or sudden severe headache: call 911.

Frequently asked questions

Can I do these full-body exercises on my own?

Yes, they are the same exercises Dr. Jared assigns as homework. Start with the level 1 versions, keep every repetition pain-free, and stop anything that causes sharp, shooting or worsening pain. If you are not sure which muscle is behind your pain, start with the pain-location pages or book an evaluation.

Where are the videos?

Every exercise on this page has a video in the free MD Fit app, under Videos. Members also get them inside full programs with a week-by-week progression.

Follow the dose listed next to each cue. Where no dose is listed, the exercise is used inside a program or an in-clinic plan, where the dose depends on the person.
