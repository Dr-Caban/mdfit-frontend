Pickleball injuries we see most in Sarasota

By Dr. Jared Caban, MD, MSc, CEP · Published 2026-09-29

Why pickleball injuries are so common

Pickleball looks easy. It isn't. The quick starts and stops, the lateral shuffles, the overhead smashes and the paddle grip all stress the body in specific ways. Add that most new players are over 50 and haven't done athletic movement in years, and you have a predictable list of injuries.

Injury 1: Lateral epicondylitis (tennis elbow)

Pain on the outside of the paddle-side elbow, worse with gripping. Caused by the paddle hand doing too much work. Fix: lighter paddle grip, better technique, and a specific loading program for the wrist extensors. Treatment is usually 4–8 weeks.

Injury 2: Rotator cuff pain

Especially from overhead smashes and backhand drives in players who haven't done overhead work in years. Fix: scapular control first, cuff strength second, then gradually loading overhead patterns. Protocol is usually 6 weeks.

Injury 3: Calf strains

The classic 'pickleball calf.' You lunge for a drop shot, feel a pop in the calf, and you're done for the day. Fix: a graded return-to-court plan with calf raises, isometric holds and eccentric work. Most strains heal fully in 4–6 weeks with the right loading.

Injury 4: Low back pain

Especially from the kneeling-into-stand reach for low volleys and from twisting at the back instead of the hips. Fix: hip mobility, glute strength and teaching the player to rotate from the hips, not the lumbar spine.

Injury 5: Achilles tendon pain

The second-most-common lower-leg injury. Stiff, sore Achilles that is worse first thing in the morning. Fix: calf-strengthening program (slow heel raises off a step), court-time management while the tendon rebuilds, and often a shoe change.

How to stay on the court

Three keys. One: do a real warm-up (not just 'rally for a few minutes'). Our 10-minute pickleball warm-up is in the MD Fit app. Two: strength-train twice a week. Even light strength work is protective. Three: respect the first signal. The injuries above almost always start with a small ache a week or two before the day you can't play. Fix small things small.
