The 3 muscles behind most knee pain

By Dr. Jared Caban, MD, MSc, CEP · Published 2026-10-27

The knee is a messenger

The knee is a simple hinge sandwiched between two complex joints: the hip above and the ankle below. When either of those joints has a problem, the knee is the first to complain. That's why so many people have knee pain that doesn't improve from treating the knee itself.

Muscle 1: Vastus medialis (inner quad)

The vastus medialis is the teardrop-shaped quad muscle on the inside of your knee. It helps track the kneecap. When it's weak, the kneecap drifts outward and grinds against the groove it's supposed to glide in. That's a huge chunk of patellofemoral pain, the generic 'runner's knee.' Fixing it looks like Spanish squats, terminal knee extensions and single-leg wall sits.

Muscle 2: Gluteus medius (outer hip)

The glute medius lives on the outside of your pelvis. Its job is to keep your pelvis level when you stand on one leg. When it's weak, the opposite hip drops, your femur rotates inward, and the knee caves. Every time you take a step, you grind the knee a little. Side-lying clamshells, side planks and single-leg bridges rebuild it. Many people with IT band pain are actually glute-weak.

Muscle 3: Gastrocnemius and soleus (calves)

The calves control how your lower leg loads over your foot. Tight calves limit how far your knee can travel over your toes. That means every squat, every stair, every landing from a jog puts more rotational force into the knee. Simple calf stretching helps a little; loaded calf raises help a lot, especially the slow-eccentric kind.

Putting it together

At MD Fit, the knee evaluation always includes the hip and the ankle. We find which of the three muscles above are letting you down, release them if they're tight, and strengthen them if they're weak. Clients typically do a short daily routine in the app and come in for hands-on work and progression.

When to see your doctor

Red flags: knee swelling that gets worse over hours, inability to bear weight, a knee that locks or gives way, or a fever with a hot knee. Any of those are reasons to call your doctor the same day.
