Snowbird season in Sarasota: how to restart golf, pickleball and walking without flaring old injuries

By Dr. Jared Caban, MD, MSc, CEP · Published 2026-11-10

Every November the clinic calendar changes. The first cold snap up north sends our seasonal residents back to Sarasota, and within two weeks we start seeing the same injuries: a low back that went in the first round of golf, a calf that popped on the pickleball court, a knee that swelled after the first long beach walk. Almost all of it is avoidable.

Why the first two weeks back are the risky ones

Tissue adapts to what you ask of it. Six months of shorter days, indoor time and a long drive down I-75 leave tendons, discs and calf muscles tuned for a quieter life. Then the weather is perfect, the friends are here, and the calendar fills with tee times and court bookings that would have been normal in April.

The gap between what your body is ready for and what you ask of it is where injuries live. Muscles close that gap within a couple of weeks. Tendons, cartilage and the discs in your low back take longer, which is why the problem is rarely the first outing and usually the fourth or fifth. Many of our snowbirds are in their sixties and seventies, and the margin for a sudden jump in load narrows every decade. That is not a reason to do less; it is a reason to ramp up with intention.

A quick self-check before you book the first tee time

These are educational screens, not a diagnosis, but they tell you where to be careful.

  • Single-leg calf raise. Hold a wall and rise onto the ball of one foot twenty times. If one side fatigues or hurts well before the other, your gastrocnemius and soleus are not ready for pickleball lunges yet.
  • Thirty-second single-leg stand. Eyes open, no wall. Wobbling badly or needing to put the foot down early means balance and hip stability need a week of work before fast court movement.
  • Seated trunk rotation. Sit tall, arms crossed, and turn both ways. If one side stops noticeably short or pinches in the flank or low back, your golf swing will find that limit in the first nine holes.
  • Deep squat to a chair and back up. Ten slow reps. Knee pain on the way down or up is a signal to build before you load.

Any old injury that still talks to you during these screens is the one to protect first.

Golfers: the low back and the lead hip

The golf swing asks the hips and mid-back to rotate so the low back does not have to. After months off, hip rotation is usually what has quietly shrunk, and the low back picks up the slack. The result is the familiar one-sided ache that we cover in detail on our low back pain page.

Our suggested ramp: a week of hip and thoracic mobility work from the app before the first round, then a short-game and range session, then nine holes with a cart, then nine walking, then eighteen. Warm up with ten slow practice swings building speed gradually, not three fast ones on the first tee. Our golf performance program goes deeper on the swing itself.

Pickleball players: calves, Achilles and shoulders

Pickleball is the fastest-growing source of new patients in our clinic, and the injury list is predictable: calf strains from sudden lunges, Achilles irritation, and shoulder pain from overhead smashes in players who have not hit anything overhead since spring. Falls are the other big one, usually backpedalling for a lob.

Before the first game, spend a week on the calf and ankle videos in the app, including eccentric calf drops and ankle neuroglides. Then play doubles before singles and give yourself a day between the first several sessions. Decide now that you will not backpedal for lobs; turn and run instead. If you are already hurting, see our pickleball rehab page.

Walkers: the knee and the foot

Walking seems harmless, which is exactly why it catches people. Going from a few thousand steps a day in a northern winter to a three-mile beach walk on soft sand, in sandals, is a big jump for the knee and the plantar fascia. Knees with some arthritis are the most likely to swell; our knee osteoarthritis page covers what helps.

Start on firm, level ground in real shoes, add distance by about ten percent at a time, and save the soft-sand Siesta Key walk for week three. A sore knee the next morning that settles by lunch is normal adaptation. Swelling or pain that is worse two days later means you overshot.

What to do this week

  1. Pick the one old injury you most want to protect and spend ten minutes a day on its calm and restore videos in the free MD Fit app before you add sport.
  2. Walk daily first. Twenty to thirty minutes on firm ground gets the whole system moving before anything explosive.
  3. Schedule the ramp. Write down a two-week build for each sport instead of saying yes to every invitation.
  4. Warm up every single time. Five minutes of leg swings, calf raises, hip circles and gradual practice swings or soft dinks.
  5. Hydrate like it is Florida, because it is. Calf cramps and strains are far more common in dehydrated players.
  6. Book an assessment early if something hurts in the first two weeks. Our snowbird page has the details.

Muscles behind this pain

  • Gastrocnemius: Upper calf, back of the knee and the arch of the foot; deep cramp-like tightness.
  • Soleus: Heel and Achilles tendon, lower calf; can contribute to plantar fasciitis.
  • Quadratus lumborum: Low back and flank, over the SI joint and buttock, and the point of the hip.
  • Gluteus medius: Along the belt line, over the SI joint, the buttock and the top of the hip.
  • Supraspinatus: Deep ache at the top and side of the shoulder that can run down the outside of the arm to the elbow.

Red flags: see a physician, not a stretch

  • Sudden calf pain with swelling, warmth or redness, especially within two weeks of a long drive or flight. This can be a blood clot; seek urgent medical care the same day.
  • A pop in the calf, back of the knee or shoulder with immediate loss of function or inability to bear weight.
  • Chest pain, pressure, unusual breathlessness or light-headedness during exertion. Stop and call 911.
  • A knee or ankle that swells visibly within hours of an outing, locks, or gives way.
  • Back pain with numbness in the groin, leg weakness, or any change in bowel or bladder control.

Frequently asked questions

How long should I wait before playing pickleball after months off?

There is no fixed number, but a sensible pattern is a week of daily walking plus calf and hip work, then doubles with a rest day between sessions for the first two weeks. Players who skip that ramp are the ones we see for calf strains.

Is it safe to play golf with my old back problem?

Usually yes, with preparation. Restore hip and mid-back rotation before the first round, build from range to nine holes to eighteen, and warm up gradually each time. If the back is already flaring, get it assessed before you add rounds.

Should I stretch before golf or pickleball?

Move before you play rather than hold long static stretches. Leg swings, calf raises, hip circles and progressively faster practice swings or dinks prepare tissue better than a cold hamstring stretch. Save longer holds for after.
