Why your low back pain comes from your hips

By Dr. Jared Caban, MD, MSc, CEP · Published 2026-11-03

The myth: 'my back is weak'

Most people with chronic low back pain assume their core is weak or their spine is damaged. Sometimes that's true. More often, the back is working harder than it should because the hips above and below it are not doing their share. The spine ends up as a shock absorber for a joint that was designed to move freely.

What the hips are supposed to do

Hips are big ball-and-socket joints with three jobs: let your leg move in all directions, drive power when you push off, and keep the pelvis level while you walk and stand. When the hip flexors in the front stay short from sitting, when the glutes in the back get sleepy from lack of use, and when the deep rotators stiffen from inactivity, the hip can't do those three jobs. The low back picks up the slack.

Three signs your back pain is a hip problem

First, your pain is worse after a long drive, a long flight or a long day at a desk, and it eases when you walk around. Sitting shortens the hip flexors, and standing up stretches them again. Second, you can't get into a deep squat with your feet flat without the low back flexing or your heels lifting. The hips are supposed to let you squat; the back shouldn't have to bend to make up for them. Third, when you stand on one leg, your hip drops to the other side. That's a glute medius problem on the standing leg.

What we actually do about it

In the clinic, the fix usually looks like this. First, hands-on soft-tissue work to release the iliopsoas (the deep hip flexor that runs from your spine down to your femur) and the lateral hip. Second, specific strengthening of the glutes with movements that teach them to fire when you walk, stand up and climb stairs. Third, mobility drills that open the hips without stressing the back. Fourth, a short daily home routine, usually 10–15 minutes, in the MD Fit app.

What you can start today

Try a 30-second couch stretch each side, a slow squat holding onto a doorframe for ten reps, and a side-lying clamshell for ten reps each side. These three alone, done every day for two weeks, change how a lot of backs feel.

When back pain is not from the hips

Red flags that need medical care: numbness in the groin, bowel or bladder changes, saddle numbness, or progressive weakness in a leg. If any of these are present, call your doctor or go to the ER. Everyday mechanical low back pain is different and almost always responds to the right rehab.
