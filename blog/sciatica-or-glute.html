Sciatica or glute pain? How to tell

By Dr. Jared Caban, MD, MSc, CEP · Published 2026-09-15

Why they get confused

True sciatica is irritation of the sciatic nerve, usually from a disc or a tight muscle pressing on it. Glute pain is muscle pain in the buttock. Both can shoot pain into the back of the leg. Both are worse with sitting. Both can be miserable. The treatments are very different.

Question 1: How far does the pain go?

True sciatica typically shoots all the way past the knee, often into the calf or foot, and may follow a specific path along the back or outside of the leg. Glute pain is usually in the buttock and upper thigh, and doesn't travel below the knee.

Question 2: Are there numbness or tingling?

True sciatica often includes numbness, pins and needles, or weakness along the leg or foot. Glute pain is deep and achy but usually doesn't come with those neurological symptoms.

Question 3: What makes it better and worse?

Sciatica is often worse with certain back positions (bending forward or backward, depending on the cause) and may respond to specific nerve-glide exercises. Glute pain is often worse with pressure on the buttock (long drives, sitting on a hard chair) and responds to muscle release and strengthening.

Red flags that mean call your doctor now

Loss of bowel or bladder control. Numbness in the groin or saddle area. Rapidly progressive weakness in a leg. Any of these are emergency-care red flags, no matter which of the two conditions you think you have.

What we do

The evaluation separates the two in the first visit. Most true sciatica responds to rehabilitation without surgery when the right loading is prescribed. Most glute pain responds to targeted soft-tissue work and glute strengthening. Both get a daily home routine in the MD Fit app.
