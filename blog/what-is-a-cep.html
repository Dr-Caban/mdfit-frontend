What is a Clinical Exercise Physiologist (CEP)?

By Dr. Jared Caban, MD, MSc, CEP · Published 2026-09-01

Where CEPs come from

A Clinical Exercise Physiologist (CEP) is a credentialed exercise clinician who specializes in using exercise to prevent and manage chronic disease. The credential is issued through the American College of Sports Medicine (ACSM). The scope of practice includes cardiovascular, metabolic, pulmonary, neurological, orthopedic and some oncologic conditions.

What a CEP actually does

A CEP designs and delivers exercise programs for people whose medical history makes exercise more complex than 'go to the gym and figure it out.' That includes people with heart disease, high blood pressure, diabetes, obesity, osteoporosis, Parkinson's disease, multiple sclerosis, cancer recovery, long COVID, and more. CEPs can also prescribe exercise for healthy adults and athletes; the extra training just means they can safely handle the complex cases.

CEP vs. CCEP

CEP is the base credential. CCEP (Certified Clinical Exercise Physiologist, through ACSM) is the advanced credential, with higher training requirements and a longer board exam. Dr. Jared holds both.

CEP vs. Physical Therapist

Physical therapists work after an injury or surgery, usually in insurance-reimbursed care, focused on recovering function. CEPs often work before or alongside PT, focused on exercise prescription for people with medical conditions, and can continue past the point PT ends to build long-term strength and conditioning. The two professions overlap, and the best outcomes often come from both working together.

CEP vs. Personal Trainer

A personal trainer is a fitness professional, no medical scope. A CEP is a healthcare clinician. If you have a diagnosis of any kind, especially cardiovascular or metabolic, a CEP is the right starting point. If you are healthy and want to get fitter, both can help.
