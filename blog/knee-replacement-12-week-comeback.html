After knee replacement: a safe 12-week comeback

By Dr. Jared Caban, MD, MSc, CEP · Published 2026-10-06

Weeks 1–2: Control the swelling, protect the range

The first two weeks are about swelling, pain and range of motion. Elevate the leg above your heart several times a day. Ice for 15 minutes every 2–3 hours. Walk short distances every hour. Follow the home-exercise plan your surgeon's team gave you. The goal is zero degrees of extension (knee fully straight) and about 90 degrees of flexion. These numbers matter: losing extension in the first weeks is hard to recover.

Weeks 3–4: Build the walking base

Transition off the walker to a cane, then to no device as your gait improves. Target 10–15 minutes of continuous walking. Start closed-chain strength: wall sits, mini squats to a chair, step-ups on a 4-inch step. Keep working on flexion. If you aren't at 110 degrees of flexion by week 4, we push harder.

Weeks 5–8: Add real strength

This is when the clinic work really matters. We add step-ups, leg press, single-leg balance, and gentle hip work. Walking distance moves to 30 minutes. You should be able to climb a flight of stairs normally. Many clients return to driving around week 5 or 6 depending on which leg was operated on and their surgeon's clearance.

Weeks 9–12: Return to activity

By week 9, most clients are walking normally, climbing stairs without a railing, and doing resistance training. Golfers restart short putting and chipping around week 8, work up to 9 holes by week 10, and full 18 by weeks 11–12. Pickleball players are usually back to light rallying around week 10, full matches by week 12 or 14. Swimming returns around week 6 once incisions are fully healed.

The goal at 12 weeks

Zero to 120+ degrees of knee flexion. Normal walking gait with no limp. Ability to climb a flight of stairs normally. Single-leg balance for 30 seconds. Ten controlled step-ups. Return to the recreational activities you did before, within reason.

Common mistakes

Trying to walk too far too soon, which flares swelling and sets you back. Skipping the home program on the days you aren't in the clinic. Chasing flexion without protecting extension. Returning to high-impact activity before the strength is there. We help you avoid all of these.
