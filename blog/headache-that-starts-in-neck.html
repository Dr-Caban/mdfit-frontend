The headache that starts in your neck: upper trapezius, suboccipitals and your phone

By Dr. Jared Caban, MD, MSc, CEP · Published 2026-11-10

If your headache starts at the base of your skull or on top of your shoulder and creeps forward to your temple or behind one eye, it may not be starting in your head at all. In our Sarasota clinic the two most common culprits for desk and phone users are the upper trapezius and the suboccipitals.

Two muscles, two different headache maps

The upper trapezius is the thick muscle on top of your shoulder. When it is irritated, it refers pain up the side of the neck, behind the ear and into the temple in a question-mark shape. People describe a heavy, burning ache on the shoulder that becomes a headache by late afternoon.

The suboccipitals are four small muscles between the top two vertebrae and the base of the skull. They make the fine adjustments that keep your eyes level. Their headache feels like a tight band from the back of the head wrapping forward to behind the eye or into the forehead. It is deep, persistent and often mistaken for migraine.

Both patterns fall under cervicogenic headache or tension-type headache. The label matters less than the mechanism: pain generated in the neck and felt in the head.

Why phones and desks feed both of them

Every inch your head drifts forward of your shoulders adds load to the muscles holding it up. Looking down at a phone or leaning into a monitor puts the suboccipitals on a long, low-grade contraction to keep your eyes on the screen, and asks the upper traps to hold the shoulder girdle up against the weight of the arms.

Add stress, which makes most people shrug without noticing, a bag on one shoulder, a phone cradled against the ear, or a pillow stack that bends the neck all night, and you have the recipe we see in desk neck pain week after week. Jaw clenching and eye strain feed the suboccipitals too.

Self-check: which muscle is talking?

These screens come from our free app. They are educational, not a diagnosis.

Upper trapezius leans likely if:

  • The pain starts on top of the shoulder and travels up the side of the neck to the temple.
  • Carrying a heavy bag on that shoulder makes it clearly worse.
  • Pinching the thick muscle between neck and shoulder point finds a tender band that sends pain up toward the head.
  • You catch yourself shrugging when concentrating or cold.

Suboccipitals lean likely if:

  • The headache feels like a band from the back of the head to behind the eye.
  • It is worse after a long stretch looking down at a screen or book.
  • Tucking your chin to make a double chin feels stiff or reproduces the pull at the base of the skull.
  • Gentle pressure into the hollows just under the skull brings on the familiar headache feeling.

Many people have both; the plan below treats both.

What to do this week

  1. Release. For the upper trap, lean a lacrosse ball between the muscle and a wall for two to three minutes per side. For the suboccipitals, lie on your back with two balls or a peanut under the base of the skull and gently nod yes and shake no for two minutes. Once or twice daily.
  2. Stretch and nod. Use the Upper Trap Stretch Seated video, two holds of thirty seconds per side, and the Deep Cervical Flexor Nods Seated video, two sets of ten slow chin tucks.
  3. Reset every hour. The Seated Desk Posture Reset takes thirty seconds. Set a timer.
  4. Breathe. Five minutes of 90/90 Breathing in the evening lowers the baseline tension that keeps the shoulders up.
  5. Strengthen once the headache frequency drops. Face pulls, floor slides with scapular retraction and farmer carries teach the lower traps and mid-back to carry the shoulder girdle so the upper traps can stop. The posterior neck guide has the full progression.

Fix the setup, not just the muscle

  • Raise the monitor so the top third is at eye level, and bring the phone up to your face instead of your face down to the phone.
  • Set the chair and keyboard so elbows rest near ninety degrees and shoulders hang.
  • Swap a single-strap bag for a backpack, and use earbuds for calls.
  • Use one supportive pillow that keeps the neck in line with the spine.
  • When you notice stress, exhale and let the shoulders drop. It is the single most repeated cue in our clinic.

When it is not a muscle

Neck muscles are a common and fixable headache source, but not the only one. Migraine, occipital neuralgia, upper neck joint irritation and cervical disc problems can overlap with this picture, and some headaches are emergencies. If the self-checks do not fit, if your headaches are changing, or if a few weeks of consistent work does not reduce them, book an assessment. Muscles behind this pain

  • Upper trapezius: Up the side of the neck, behind the ear and into the temple (a question-mark shape).
  • Suboccipitals: A band of headache from the base of the skull wrapping to behind the eye and the forehead.
  • Levator scapulae: The angle where the neck meets the shoulder, down the inner edge of the shoulder blade.
  • Splenius capitis & cervicis: Pain on the top of the head, and deep ache inside the head behind the eye.
  • Lower trapezius: Up the back of the neck to the base of the skull, the top of the shoulder, and along the inner shoulder blade.

Red flags: see a physician, not a stretch

  • A sudden, severe headache that peaks within seconds or minutes, or the worst headache of your life. Call 911.
  • Headache with fever and a stiff neck you cannot bend to touch chin to chest, with confusion, drowsiness or a rash. Call 911.
  • Headache with weakness, numbness, facial droop, slurred speech, vision loss or trouble walking. Call 911.
  • A headache that begins after a fall, car accident or blow to the head, especially with vomiting or loss of consciousness.
  • A new type of headache after age 50, one that wakes you from sleep, or a steady change in your usual pattern.
  • Headache with numbness, tingling or weakness running down an arm.

Frequently asked questions

Can neck muscles really cause a headache behind my eye?

Yes. The suboccipitals at the base of the skull commonly refer a band of pain forward to behind the eye and into the forehead, and the upper trapezius refers to the temple. The pain is felt in the head but generated in the neck.

How do I tell a neck headache from a migraine?

Neck-driven headaches usually start at the base of the skull or shoulder, worsen with posture and screen time, and can be reproduced by pressing on the muscle. Migraine more often brings nausea, light sensitivity and throbbing. They can overlap, so ask if unsure.

Is a lacrosse ball safe to use on my neck?

Used gently at the base of the skull and on the thick upper trap muscle, yes for most people. Avoid the front and sides of the neck, and stop if you feel dizziness, numbness or a sharp increase in headache.

How long until the headaches ease?

Many people notice fewer and milder headaches within a couple of weeks of daily release and posture resets. Lasting change needs several weeks of strengthening and habit change.
