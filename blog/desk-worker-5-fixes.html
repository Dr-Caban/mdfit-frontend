Sitting all day? 5 fixes a desk worker can start today

By Dr. Jared Caban, MD, MSc, CEP · Published 2026-10-13

The problem isn't just posture

The problem with desk work isn't that you sit badly. It's that you sit for too long, in the same position, with your head craned forward and your hip flexors shortened. The fix isn't a fancier chair. The fix is interrupting the pattern.

Fix 1: The 30-minute rule

Set a timer. Every 30 minutes, stand up, take ten steps, roll your shoulders back, and sit down. That one minute is enough to restart circulation in your legs, decompress your spine and remind your muscles they exist. If you do nothing else from this list, do this one.

Fix 2: The chin tuck

Three times a day, sit tall, pull your chin straight back (not up or down) toward your throat as if making a small double-chin, hold for five seconds, release. Ten reps. This counters the forward-head posture that drives tension headaches and neck pain.

Fix 3: The couch stretch

Pick a time (right before dinner, or during a show) and do a 30-second couch stretch on each side. Kneel in front of a couch or wall, put the top of your foot behind you against the couch, squeeze your glute and tuck your pelvis under. Eight hours of sitting shortens the hip flexors. One minute a day keeps them honest.

Fix 4: The doorway pec stretch

Stand in a doorway, hands on the frame at shoulder height, step one foot through, feel the stretch across the front of your chest and shoulders. 30 seconds. Rounded shoulders at a desk get tight pecs. This re-opens the chest and lets the shoulder blades sit flat.

Fix 5: Walk after dinner

A 15-minute walk after dinner is one of the highest-return things you can do for a desk-worker body. Blood sugar, hips, calves, mood, sleep: it helps all of them. If this feels obvious, it's because it's the one that works and no one does it.
