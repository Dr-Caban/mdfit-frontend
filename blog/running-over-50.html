Running without pain over 50

By Dr. Jared Caban, MD, MSc, CEP · Published 2026-09-22

The body does change

Three things change for runners over 50. Tendons stiffen and need longer to adapt to new loads. Recovery takes longer, especially between hard sessions. Muscle mass drops about 1% a year starting around 40 unless you actively fight it. None of these means you have to stop running. All of them mean the plan has to change.

Change 1: Add strength training

If you are not strength training by age 50, your running career has a timer on it. Two sessions a week of basic lower-body strength (squats, hinges, split squats, calf raises) protects your tendons, muscles and bones. Clients who add strength training usually say their running feels easier within 4–6 weeks.

Change 2: Protect sleep and recovery

Most runners over 50 can handle three to four quality running days a week. Four to five hard sessions a week usually leads to injury within a few months. Make the easy days easy, the hard days hard, and give yourself the sleep to adapt.

Change 3: Think in weeks, not days

Younger runners can push a hard session the day after a hard session. Over 50, think about load across the whole week. The question isn't 'how do I feel today?' It's 'how is this week landing on top of last week?'

The specific injuries we see

Achilles tendon pain, plantar fasciitis, hamstring tendon pain near the sit bone, and knee pain are the big four. All of them respond well to targeted loading programs that use the real research on tendons: specific, progressive, and more loading, not less. If you have one of these and have been resting for weeks, call us. The fix is probably not more rest.
