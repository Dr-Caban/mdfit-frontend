The three directions that matter

The hip is a ball and socket joint that should move in every direction. Modern life mostly uses one.

  • Flexion — knee toward chest. Plentiful in most people because sitting is flexion, though it may be limited by the socket.
  • Extension — leg behind the body. Routinely limited by hours of sitting, and the loss shows up as the low back arching to compensate during walking and lifting.
  • Rotation — turning in and out. The most neglected of the three and the one whose loss most reliably shows up as groin, buttock or low back trouble.

A routine that only stretches the hamstrings and calls it hip mobility is working on the direction that is usually least restricted.

The routine

  1. Half-kneeling hip flexor stretch, 45 seconds each side. Squeeze the back glute and tuck the pelvis before leaning in. Without that, the stretch is felt in the low back instead of the psoas.
  2. 90/90 switches, 8 each direction. Seated, both knees bent at right angles, rotating side to side under control. Directly trains rotation.
  3. 90/90 lift-offs, 5 each side. From the same position, lift the back knee off the floor. Active rotation, which is the part that makes range usable.
  4. Deep squat hold, 30 to 60 seconds. Heels down if possible, holding onto something. Elevate heels if needed.
  5. Glute bridge, 10 slow. Builds the extension you just stretched into, which is what makes it stick.
  6. Side-lying hip abduction, 10 each side. For the gluteus medius, which controls the hip in walking and single-leg work.

Stretch, then load. Stretching alone changes how it feels; adding the strength work is what keeps the range.

Tight hip or structural limit

Worth knowing which you have, because they need opposite responses.

A muscular restriction feels like a stretch — a pulling sensation, usually in the front or back of the thigh, that eases as you warm up and improves over weeks. A structural limit feels like a block: a firm, bony stop, often with pinching at the front of the hip, that does not change with warming up or with months of stretching.

Hip socket depth and orientation vary considerably between people. If your hip stops hard and pinches at the front, pushing deeper is likely to irritate rather than improve it. See why squat depth differs and hip labral tear if pinching is accompanied by catching or clicking.

What stiff hips cause elsewhere

The hip is the biggest joint between ground and spine, and what it does not do, something else does. Limited extension usually means the low back extends instead, which is a common thread in people whose backs ache after walking or standing.

Limited rotation shows up in golf and pickleball particularly — if the hip will not turn, the lumbar spine takes the rotation, and the lumbar spine is not built to rotate much. See low back pain and SI joint pain.

Dose and expectations

Five to ten minutes most days beats forty minutes on a Sunday. Expect three to four weeks before a meaningful change, and expect the change to hold only if you keep loading the range.

If one hip is markedly stiffer than the other, or if stretching reliably produces groin pain or pinching, get it assessed rather than persisting — see functional mobility.

Muscles behind this pain

  • Psoas: Vertical band along the low back, plus the front of the thigh and groin.
  • Iliacus: Groin, front of the thigh, and the back of the pelvis high in the glute.
  • Gluteus medius: Along the belt line, over the SI joint, the buttock and the top of the hip.
  • Gluteus maximus: Buttock and the tailbone; aches with long sitting and walking uphill.
  • Tensor fasciae latae: Front-outer hip down the outside of the thigh, usually stopping at the knee.
  • Piriformis: Mid-buttock and back of the hip, down the back-outside of the thigh toward the calf.

When to get medical care first

  • Sharp groin pain, catching, clicking or locking during hip movement.
  • Pain that travels down the leg, or numbness, tingling or weakness.
  • Night pain in the hip that wakes you and is not related to position.
  • A sudden loss of hip range with significant pain, particularly after a fall.
  • Hip pain with fever or feeling generally unwell, which needs prompt medical assessment.

Frequently asked questions

How long until my hips feel looser?

Most people notice something within two to three weeks of daily work. Lasting change takes longer and depends on loading the new range, not just stretching into it.

Why does the hip flexor stretch hurt my back?

Usually because the pelvis is not tucked and the glute is not squeezed, so the stretch is taken up by the lumbar spine instead of the hip flexors.

Can I stretch out a structural limit?

No. If the end point is a firm bony block with pinching at the front, stretching harder tends to irritate the joint rather than improve it.

Should I stretch a hip that has arthritis?

Gentle regular movement is usually helpful and often reduces stiffness. Forcing painful end range is not, and a programme for an arthritic hip is worth having tailored.
