Why pain spikes in the first weeks back

The problem is not Florida. It is the jump. Up north, winter tends to mean less walking, less sun and a lot more sitting. Then comes a long drive or flight, which is more sitting, followed by a sudden burst of golf, pickleball, tennis, beach walking, boating and yard work, often all in the same week. Tendons and joints adapt to load slowly, over weeks, and a body that has done very little for two months does not have the capacity it had in April. The muscles that were already tight from sitting, the hip flexors, the chest, the upper traps, the calves, are asked to perform immediately, and they object.

Walking on sand and shell at Siesta or Lido adds its own load to the calves and feet.

What seasonal residents come in for

  • Low back pain after the drive down and the first rounds of golf. See low back pain and our golf page.
  • Hip and outer-hip pain from a sudden increase in walking, especially on sand. See hip pain.
  • Knee pain in people with knee osteoarthritis who were doing fine until the activity tripled.
  • Shoulder pain after returning to pickleball or tennis overhead play. See shoulder pain and the pickleball page.
  • Plantar fascia and Achilles pain from flip-flops, sandals and barefoot beach walking after months in supportive winter boots. See plantar fasciitis and Achilles tendon pain.
  • Neck stiffness and headaches after the travel and the new bed.

The muscles behind the seasonal pattern

  • Psoas: the hip flexor that shortens with a long drive and refers to the low back and front of the hip. It is the most common reason the first week in Sarasota starts with a stiff back.
  • Gluteus medius: the hip stabilizer that is weak after a sedentary winter and overworked by sudden walking. Refers along the belt line and the outer hip. See the lateral hip guide.
  • Quadratus lumborum: the deep low-back muscle that side-bends and gets overloaded by a golf swing it has not done since fall.
  • Soleus and foot intrinsics: the calf and sole muscles that face sand, flat sandals and long walks all at once. Behind most heel and Achilles complaints we see in December. See the calf guide.
  • Upper trapezius: tight from the drive, the new pillow and the first overhead serves.

How a season at MD Fit usually goes

The first visit is an assessment: how you move, what is tight, what is weak, what you want to be able to do by spring. Hands-on work releases the tissues that are guarding, corrective movements restore the ranges the winter took, and the homework goes into the app. Over the season the plan shifts toward strength, so that you leave in April more capable than you arrived in November, not just less sore.

Your first two weeks in Sarasota

  1. Ramp up, do not jump: one round of golf or one session of pickleball, then a day off, for the first two weeks.
  2. After the drive, walk before you unpack. Ten minutes, then a half-kneeling hip flexor stretch for a minute each side.
  3. Alternate beach walking with walking in supportive shoes on firm ground; let the calves and feet catch up gradually.
  4. Start a short daily routine now, before anything hurts: calf raises, sit-to-stands, a doorway chest stretch and a gentle spine rotation on the floor.

When to come in

Soreness after the first round is expected. Pain that has not settled after two weeks of sensible activity, pain that is changing how you walk or swing, pain into a limb, or anything that is stopping you from doing what you came to Sarasota to do is worth an assessment.

Muscles behind this pain

  • Psoas: Vertical band along the low back, plus the front of the thigh and groin.
  • Gluteus medius: Along the belt line, over the SI joint, the buttock and the top of the hip.
  • Quadratus lumborum: Low back and flank, over the SI joint and buttock, and the point of the hip.
  • Soleus: Heel and Achilles tendon, lower calf; can contribute to plantar fasciitis.
  • Foot muscles: Heel and sole of the foot; first-step-in-the-morning pain like plantar fasciitis.
  • Upper trapezius: Up the side of the neck, behind the ear and into the temple (a question-mark shape).

How MD Fit works with you

  1. Assess. A hands-on evaluation of how you move and which muscles reproduce your pain.
  2. Release. Targeted soft-tissue work on the muscle at the source.
  3. Correct. Mobility and movement fixes so the problem stops coming back.
  4. Assign homework. Short daily video routines in the free MD Fit app.
  5. Strengthen. Progressive strength work so you stay pain-free and keep doing what you love.

When to get medical care first

  • Calf pain with swelling, warmth or redness after a long drive or flight, which can indicate a blood clot and needs urgent medical care.
  • Chest pain, breathlessness or a racing heart during activity in the heat: call 911.
  • Back pain with numbness in the groin or a change in bowel or bladder control.
  • A fall, especially one with a blow to the head or in someone on blood thinners.
  • Sudden inability to bear weight on a leg or lift an arm after an awkward swing or step.

Frequently asked questions

Can I be seen if I am only in Sarasota for a few months?

Yes. Most seasonal residents are here for four to six months, which is plenty of time to assess, treat and build a strength base. The app keeps the plan going after you leave.

Will Dr. Jared coordinate with my doctor at home?

With your permission, yes. He will share his findings and the plan so that your northern providers know what was done and can continue it.

Why did my knees do fine all summer and hurt the first week here?

Usually because activity multiplied overnight. Arthritic knees tolerate load they are used to; a sudden tripling of walking and sport exceeds that tolerance. A gradual ramp and stronger hips and quads usually restore it.
