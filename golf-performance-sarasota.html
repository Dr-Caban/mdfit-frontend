Why the body, not the swing, is usually the limiter

A golf swing asks the hips to rotate while the trunk stays stable, then asks the trunk to rotate while the hips brake. When a hip cannot turn, the low back turns instead. When the mid-back is stiff, the shoulders and elbows make up the difference. Your pro can see the compensation on video; giving the hip rotation back is the clinic's job.

The muscles that most often limit Sarasota golfers are the psoas (tight from cart seats and desks, blocks hip extension through impact), the gluteus medius (lets the lead hip slide instead of post), the lats (pull the trail shoulder down and shorten the backswing) and the obliques, which fatigue and refer pain to the side of the trunk late in a round.

Injuries we treat in golfers

  • Low back pain that shows up on the back nine or the morning after. Often a quadratus lumborum or oblique problem driven by a hip that does not rotate.
  • Golfer's elbow at the inside of the trail elbow from gripping too hard and casting the club. We release the wrist flexors and rebuild grip strength with eccentric loading.
  • Lead-hip pain, outside or front of the hip, from the lead side taking the braking force of every swing.
  • Trail shoulder and neck tightness from a shortened backswing.
  • Knee pain in the lead knee when the hip stops rotating and the knee twists to finish the turn.

In each case the first visit includes hands-on release of the muscles reproducing your pain, then the specific mobility and strength work that stops the pattern from reloading them.

The 6-Week Beginner Golf Protocol

Three workouts a week of 20 to 30 minutes, mobility first. Week one establishes posture and movement control with the 90/90 Hip Switch, Couch Stretch with Glute Engagement, Golf Address Posture Hold, Seated Thoracic Rotation and Open Book Rotation. Weeks two and three build range and rotational stability with the Golfer Switch Row, Split-Stance Torso Rotation and Assisted Cossack Squat. The final weeks connect it to the swing itself with the Light Cable Swing (high to low), Step-Through Golf Swing, Controlled Backswing Pause Drill and Controlled Three-Quarter Swing. Every exercise is a video in the free MD Fit app, so you can do it at home before a tee time at Bobby Jones.

Intermediate and advanced programs

The 6-Week Intermediate Golf Program (three 30 to 35 minute sessions) moves from Rotational Strength Foundations through Unilateral Strength, Rotational Power, Ground Force and Speed Integration to Performance and Repeatability. Expect the Pallof Press, Lateral Ball Throw, Medicine Ball Rotational Scoop, Jump Switch Lunge, Trap Bar Deadlift and Golfer Snatch, progressing from moderate load to maximum-speed Step-Through Golf Swings in week six.

The 6-Week Advanced Golf Program is four sessions a week of 45 to 60 minutes for players who already train. It layers explosive work such as the Lateral Bound to Stick, Rotational Medicine Ball Throw into a wall, Cable Woodchop and heavy Suitcase Carry with March, and finishes with On-Course Power and Endurance, meaning repeatable power under fatigue.

What an evaluation covers for golfers

Bring your club. Dr. Jared watches your address posture and a few slow swings, then tests the components: hip internal and external rotation on each side, thoracic rotation in a golf posture, single-leg balance, and grip and forearm strength. Hands-on testing of the muscles above checks which ones reproduce your familiar pain. You leave with the release work done, the first two or three videos assigned and a clear picture of which side of the swing your body is compensating for.

Playing while you rehab

Most golfers do not need to stop. Typical adjustments in the first weeks are a shorter backswing, a lighter grip pressure, fewer range balls after the round, a hip-rotation warm-up before the first tee and a cool-down release routine in the app afterward. As rotation returns we take the restrictions off. If a particular movement needs to pause, you will be told exactly what and for how long.

Muscles behind this pain

  • Psoas: Vertical band along the low back, plus the front of the thigh and groin.
  • Gluteus medius: Along the belt line, over the SI joint, the buttock and the top of the hip.
  • Gluteus maximus: Buttock and the tailbone; aches with long sitting and walking uphill.
  • Latissimus dorsi: Bottom tip of the shoulder blade and down the back of the arm to the ring and little fingers.
  • External oblique: Abdominal wall, the side of the belly and down into the groin; can feel like heartburn or a side stitch.
  • Wrist flexors: Inside of the elbow (golfer's elbow) and the palm side of the wrist.

What to expect

Hip and thoracic mobility usually changes within the first two to three weeks of daily work; most golfers notice a longer, easier backswing before distance returns. The three programs run six weeks each, and players often move from one to the next across a season. Pain that has built up over years can take longer, and Dr. Jared will give you a realistic range at the evaluation.

When to get medical care first

  • Back pain with numbness, tingling or weakness running down a leg should be evaluated by a physician before you return to the course.
  • A sudden sharp pain in the ribs or side during a swing, especially with pain on breathing, may be a rib stress injury and needs imaging.
  • Loss of bladder or bowel control or numbness in the groin is an emergency.
  • Elbow pain with swelling, locking or an inability to straighten the arm should be checked before loading it.

Frequently asked questions

Will this change my swing?

It changes what your body can do. Your coach handles the swing; we handle how your body loads it. Many golfers find their pro's cues finally stick once the hip or mid-back can actually get there.

Which program should I start with?

If you have pain or have not trained in a while, the beginner protocol, regardless of handicap. Dr. Jared confirms placement at the evaluation and moves you up when the movement is ready.

Do I need a gym for the programs?

The beginner protocol needs very little equipment and is designed for home. The intermediate and advanced programs use cables, medicine balls and a trap bar, so a gym or a well-equipped home setup helps.

Can I keep playing during treatment?

Usually yes, with adjustments such as a lighter grip, a shorter swing and a warm-up and cool-down routine. Your plan spells out what to keep, modify or pause.
