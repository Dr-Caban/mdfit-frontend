Pain at the palm side of the wrist and hand: What's Causing It

★★★★★ 5.0 · 89 Google reviews

Pain on the palm side of the wrist and hand usually feels like a deep ache in the heel of the hand or the inner wrist, sometimes with a cramping palm or a finger that catches when it straightens. People notice it after a long drive gripping the wheel, carrying heavy bags, using hand tools, or sleeping with the wrist curled under. The wrist flexors, the muscles on the underside of the forearm that bend the wrist and anchor the grip, and the finger flexors, which curl the fingers, are the most frequent sources. The pectoralis minor and latissimus can add ache down the inner arm into the hand.

Is it this, or something next to it?

A physician should first rule out carpal tunnel syndrome and other nerve problems when there is numbness, tingling or night waking, and a fracture, ligament injury or arthritis with swelling. Muscle pain here is tender in the underside of the forearm and worse gripping hard, with normal sensation. Back-of-wrist pain is on top with wrist extension, thumb and index finger pain hugs the thumb edge, and ring and little finger pain follows the outer edge of the hand.

Typical story: a Sarasota resident drives north and back for a family visit, gripping the wheel for many hours, then unloads suitcases and shops with heavy bags by the handles. A deep ache sets into the heel of the hand and inner wrist that cramps when wringing a towel. The fingers do not tingle, and pressing the underside of the forearm reproduces the hand ache.

Muscles that commonly refer pain here

Ranked the way the MD Fit pain map ranks them, most common first:

  1. Wrist flexors. Inside of the elbow (golfer's elbow) and the palm side of the wrist. Worse with: gripping, lifting or typing.
  2. Finger flexors. Palm and the front of the fingers, sometimes a 'trigger finger' catching feeling. Worse with: gripping, lifting or typing.
  3. Pectoralis minor. Front of the shoulder and chest, down the inner arm to the last three fingers.
  4. Latissimus dorsi. Bottom tip of the shoulder blade and down the back of the arm to the ring and little fingers.

What people with this pain describe

Taken from Dr. Jared's notes on the wrist flexors and the finger flexors, the two most common sources here:

  • Pain at the inside of the elbow (golfer's elbow).
  • Ache on the palm side of the forearm and wrist.
  • Pain gripping, lifting or throwing.
  • Weaker grip.
  • Ache in the palm and the front of the fingers.
  • A catching or locking finger when you open the hand.
  • Forearm fatigue and ache after gripping.

Quick self-check

Education, not a diagnosis. The more of these that ring true, the more likely the top muscles are involved:

  • Is the pain at or just below the bony bump on the inside of the elbow?
  • Does bending the wrist forward against resistance hurt?
  • Does gripping, golf, throwing or lifting with the palm up bring it on?
  • Do you feel ache in the palm or along the front of the fingers when gripping?
  • Does a finger catch or click when you open your hand?

How it usually starts

  • Golf, throwing and racquet sports.
  • Heavy gripping and lifting with the palms up.
  • Climbing and pull-ups.
  • Sudden increase in manual work.
  • Long gripping: tools, phone, steering, climbing.
  • Heavy carrying with a narrow handle.

What it gets mistaken for

Medial epicondylitis; Ulnar nerve irritation (cubital tunnel); Pronator teres syndrome; Trigger finger (stenosing tenosynovitis); Carpal tunnel syndrome. An evaluation separates muscle pain from these, and anything that needs a physician is referred on.

What makes it worse, and what that tells you

  • Gripping, lifting or typing. Points toward the wrist flexors, finger flexors, latissimus dorsi.

Warning signs: get medical care, not a stretch

  • Pain after a fall or impact with deformity, or you cannot bear weight or use the limb: get an X-ray.
  • A joint that is hot, red, swollen and painful, especially with fever: urgent care.
  • Numbness, pins and needles or weakness that is spreading or constant: see a physician.
  • Pain that is constant, worse at night and not changed by movement or position: see a physician.

Frequently asked questions

Why does it hurt at the palm side of the wrist and hand if nothing is wrong there?

Muscles refer pain: a tight band in one muscle sends a predictable pattern of pain somewhere else. For pain at the palm side of the wrist and hand, the usual sources are the wrist flexors, finger flexors, pectoralis minor.

How long does wrist flexors pain usually take to settle?

Tendon-related inner-elbow pain usually needs 6 to 12 weeks of consistent loading.

What makes it more likely to be the wrist flexors?

It is worse with gripping, lifting or typing, and pressing into the muscle reproduces the pain you know. Inside of the elbow (golfer's elbow) and the palm side of the wrist.

When should I book instead of working on it myself?

Book an evaluation if pain persists past 4 weeks or finger numbness appears.
