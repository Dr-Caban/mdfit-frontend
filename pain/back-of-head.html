Pain at the back of the head: What's Causing It

★★★★★ 5.0 · 89 Google reviews

Pain at the back of the head is often felt as a spreading ache over the rounded part of the skull, with a tender scalp that makes brushing the hair or resting the head on a pillow uncomfortable. It is commonly noticed on waking after sleeping with the head propped too high, or after hours of concentrating with the neck held still. The occipitalis, the thin muscle that covers the back of the skull and moves the scalp, is the usual local source. The suboccipitals and the splenius muscles, which run up the back of the neck to turn the head, frequently feed an ache into the same area.

Is it this, or something next to it?

A physician should first rule out head emergencies: a sudden worst-ever headache, headache after a head injury, headache with fever, confusion or weakness, or a new persistent headache in later life all need urgent evaluation. Muscle-related pain here comes with a tender scalp and tight neck muscles and changes with neck position. Base-of-skull pain sits lower and deeper, top-of-head pain is on the crown, and nerve irritation usually produces a sharp, electric or tingling quality.

Typical story: a Sarasota resident on a long road trip north spends ten hours a day with the head pressed back against a headrest and the neck held rigid. A dull ache spreads over the back of the head with a scalp that feels bruised to the touch. It eases after a few days of normal movement and returns on the drive home.

Muscles that commonly refer pain here

Ranked the way the MD Fit pain map ranks them, most common first:

  1. Occipitalis. Ache at the back of the head that can shoot through the skull to behind the eye, with scalp tenderness. Worse with: stress, desk, phone or screens.
  2. Suboccipitals. A band of headache from the base of the skull wrapping to behind the eye and the forehead.
  3. Splenius capitis & cervicis. Pain on the top of the head, and deep ache inside the head behind the eye.

What people with this pain describe

Taken from Dr. Jared's notes on the occipitalis, the most common source here:

  • Aching pain at the very back of the head.
  • Pain that feels like it shoots through the skull to behind one eye.
  • Tenderness of the scalp, making it uncomfortable to brush hair or wear a hat.
  • A feeling of pressure or a 'skull cap' of pain.

Quick self-check

Education, not a diagnosis. The more of these that ring true, the more likely the top muscles are involved:

  • Is your scalp at the back of your head very tender to the touch?
  • Does the pain sometimes feel like it's shooting from the back of your head straight through to your eye?
  • Do you notice the pain more when you are stressed or clenching your jaw?

How it usually starts

  • Chronic forward head posture, which puts tension on the entire back line of the body.
  • Emotional stress leading to unconscious scalp muscle tension.
  • Clenching the jaw or grinding teeth (bruxism).
  • Prolonged desk work or screen time.

What it gets mistaken for

Occipital neuralgia; Tension-type headache; Suboccipital muscle pain. An evaluation separates muscle pain from these, and anything that needs a physician is referred on.

What makes it worse, and what that tells you

  • Stress. Points toward the occipitalis, suboccipitals.
  • Desk, phone or screens. Points toward the occipitalis, suboccipitals, splenius capitis & cervicis.

Warning signs: get medical care, not a stretch

  • A sudden, severe headache unlike any you have had, or the worst headache of your life: call 911.
  • Headache with fever and a stiff neck, confusion, slurred speech, weakness or numbness on one side, or loss of vision: call 911.
  • Head pain after a fall or blow to the head, especially with vomiting or drowsiness: emergency care.
  • Jaw or face pain with chest pressure, shortness of breath or sweating can be a heart attack: call 911.

Frequently asked questions

Why does it hurt at the back of the head if nothing is wrong there?

Muscles refer pain: a tight band in one muscle sends a predictable pattern of pain somewhere else. For pain at the back of the head, the usual sources are the occipitalis, suboccipitals, splenius capitis & cervicis.

How long does occipitalis pain usually take to settle?

Scalp tenderness and referred pain often improve within 1-2 weeks with consistent release work and postural correction. Full resolution depends on managing underlying stress and habits.

What makes it more likely to be the occipitalis?

It is worse with stress, desk, phone or screens, and pressing into the muscle reproduces the pain you know. Ache at the back of the head that can shoot through the skull to behind the eye, with scalp tenderness.

When should I book instead of working on it myself?
