Pain at the inner edge of the shoulder blade: What's Causing It

★★★★★ 5.0 · 89 Google reviews

Pain along the inner edge of the shoulder blade usually feels like a sharp, nagging ache in a narrow strip right where the blade meets the upper back, often with a stiff neck on the same side and an urge to dig a ball into the spot. People notice it on waking with the head turned, after a long phone call, or when reversing the car. The levator scapulae, the muscle that runs from the neck to the top inner corner of the blade and lifts the shoulder, and the rhomboid minor, which pins the upper edge of the blade to the spine, are the main sources.

Is it this, or something next to it?

A physician should first rule out heart and lung causes when this pain comes with chest pressure or shortness of breath, and a neck nerve problem if it radiates into the arm with numbness or weakness. Muscle pain here is tender along the edge of the blade and worsens when turning the head toward the painful side. Between-the-blades pain sits lower and more central, bottom-tip pain is down near the ribs, and neck-joint pain is higher on the spine itself.

Typical story: a Sarasota resident holds a phone against one shoulder through a week of long calls, then sleeps on the stomach with the head turned the same direction. A sharp ache develops along the inner edge of one shoulder blade with a neck that will not turn fully. The arm is strong and sensation is normal, and the ache eases when the shoulder is propped up.

Muscles that commonly refer pain here

Ranked the way the MD Fit pain map ranks them, most common first:

  1. Levator scapulae. The angle where the neck meets the shoulder, down the inner edge of the shoulder blade.
  2. Rhomboid minor. Along the inner edge of the upper shoulder blade, next to the spine. Worse with: desk, phone or screens, gripping, lifting or typing.
  3. Lower trapezius. Up the back of the neck to the base of the skull, the top of the shoulder, and along the inner shoulder blade.
  4. Middle trapezius. Burning ache between the shoulder blade and the spine, and the top of the shoulder.
  5. Scalenes. Chest, front and back of the upper arm, inner shoulder blade and down the forearm to the thumb and index finger.

What people with this pain describe

Taken from Dr. Jared's notes on the rhomboid minor, the most common source here:

  • Ache along the inner edge of the upper shoulder blade, next to the spine.
  • A 'knot' high between the shoulder blades near the base of the neck.
  • Ache with long desk work or carrying a bag on one shoulder.
  • Feels better briefly after cracking or stretching.

Quick self-check

Education, not a diagnosis. The more of these that ring true, the more likely the top muscles are involved:

  • Is the pain high, at the level of the base of the neck, right beside the spine?
  • Is there a knot along the inner edge of the top of the shoulder blade?
  • Do you carry a bag on one shoulder or sit for hours?

How it usually starts

  • Rounded shoulders stretching the rhomboids all day.
  • Carrying bags on one shoulder.
  • Long periods reaching forward (keyboard, steering).
  • Scalene or neck tension referring into the area.

What it gets mistaken for

Levator scapulae trigger points; Scalene referral; Cervical radiculopathy (C5). An evaluation separates muscle pain from these, and anything that needs a physician is referred on.

What makes it worse, and what that tells you

  • Desk, phone or screens. Points toward the levator scapulae, rhomboid minor, lower trapezius.
  • Gripping, lifting or typing. Points toward the rhomboid minor, middle trapezius.

Warning signs: get medical care, not a stretch

  • Pain after a fall or impact with deformity, or you cannot bear weight or use the limb: get an X-ray.
  • A joint that is hot, red, swollen and painful, especially with fever: urgent care.
  • Numbness, pins and needles or weakness that is spreading or constant: see a physician.
  • Pain that is constant, worse at night and not changed by movement or position: see a physician.

Frequently asked questions

Why does it hurt at the inner edge of the shoulder blade if nothing is wrong there?

Muscles refer pain: a tight band in one muscle sends a predictable pattern of pain somewhere else. For pain at the inner edge of the shoulder blade, the usual sources are the levator scapulae, rhomboid minor, lower trapezius.

How long does rhomboid minor pain usually take to settle?

Usually eases in 1 to 2 weeks.

What makes it more likely to be the rhomboid minor?

It is worse with desk, phone or screens, gripping, lifting or typing, and pressing into the muscle reproduces the pain you know. Along the inner edge of the upper shoulder blade, next to the spine.

When should I book instead of working on it myself?

Book an evaluation if the knot keeps returning after 3 weeks or there are arm symptoms.
