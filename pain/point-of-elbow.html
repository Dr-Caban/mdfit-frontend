Pain at the point of the elbow: What's Causing It

★★★★★ 5.0 · 89 Google reviews

Pain at the point of the elbow often feels like a deep ache or a sore, bruised feeling right on the bony tip, sometimes spreading to the inner elbow and the ring and little fingers. People notice it leaning on the elbow at a desk, pushing up from a chair, or after overhead work. The medial head of the triceps, the deep part of the arm-straightening muscle that attaches at the point, is the most frequent source. The long head of the triceps, which runs from the shoulder blade down the back of the arm, adds a wider ache down the back of the arm and forearm.

Is it this, or something next to it?

A physician should first rule out a fracture or bursitis if the tip is swollen, hot or follows a fall, and an ulnar nerve problem when there is persistent numbness in the ring and little fingers. Muscle pain here is tender in the triceps just above the point and worse straightening the arm against resistance. Front-of-elbow pain is in the soft crease, forearm pain runs toward the wrist, and golfer's elbow is tender on the inner bony knob.

Typical story: a Sarasota resident drives long hours with the elbow planted on the console, then spends evenings at a laptop leaning on the same arm. A bruised-feeling ache develops at the point of the elbow that nags when pushing up out of a chair. There is no swelling or redness, the fingers feel normal, and pressing the triceps just above the tip reproduces the ache.

Muscles that commonly refer pain here

Ranked the way the MD Fit pain map ranks them, most common first:

  1. Triceps medial head. The point of the elbow and the inner elbow, a golfer's-elbow look-alike, sometimes the ring and little fingers. Worse with: gripping, lifting or typing.
  2. Triceps long head. Back of the shoulder and arm, up toward the base of the neck, and down the back of the forearm.

Quick self-check

Education, not a diagnosis. The more of these that ring true, the more likely the top muscles are involved:

  • Is the pain right at the point of the elbow or just above it?
  • Does fully straightening the elbow against resistance hurt?
  • Does leaning on the elbow aggravate it?

How it usually starts

  • Lockout in presses and push-ups.
  • Leaning on elbows at a desk.
  • Throwing and racquet sports.
  • Using crutches.

What it gets mistaken for

Medial epicondylitis (golfer's elbow); Olecranon bursitis; Ulnar nerve irritation (cubital tunnel). An evaluation separates muscle pain from these, and anything that needs a physician is referred on.

What makes it worse, and what that tells you

  • Gripping, lifting or typing. Points toward the triceps medial head, triceps long head.

Warning signs: get medical care, not a stretch

  • Pain after a fall or impact with deformity, or you cannot bear weight or use the limb: get an X-ray.
  • A joint that is hot, red, swollen and painful, especially with fever: urgent care.
  • Numbness, pins and needles or weakness that is spreading or constant: see a physician.
  • Pain that is constant, worse at night and not changed by movement or position: see a physician.

Frequently asked questions

Why does it hurt at the point of the elbow if nothing is wrong there?

Muscles refer pain: a tight band in one muscle sends a predictable pattern of pain somewhere else. For pain at the point of the elbow, the usual sources are the triceps medial head, triceps long head.

How long does triceps medial head pain usually take to settle?

Usually settles in 2 to 3 weeks.

What makes it more likely to be the triceps medial head?

It is worse with gripping, lifting or typing, and pressing into the muscle reproduces the pain you know. The point of the elbow and the inner elbow, a golfer's-elbow look-alike, sometimes the ring and little fingers.

When should I book instead of working on it myself?

Book an evaluation if there is swelling at the elbow tip, finger numbness or no improvement in 3 weeks.
