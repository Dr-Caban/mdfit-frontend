Pain at the outside of the hip: What's Causing It

★★★★★ 5.0 · 89 Google reviews

Pain on the outside of the hip feels like a deep ache or sharp tenderness over the bony point of the hip that makes lying on that side nearly impossible, sometimes spreading down the outer thigh. People notice it when climbing stairs, getting up from a low chair, standing for long periods, or crossing the legs. The gluteus minimus, the small deep hip muscle that steadies the pelvis with each step, and the tensor fasciae latae, the muscle at the front-outer hip that tenses the long band down the thigh, are the most common sources. The gluteus medius and quadratus lumborum add ache from above and behind.

Is it this, or something next to it?

A physician should first rule out hip joint arthritis, a stress fracture of the femoral neck in runners, and a lumbar nerve problem with numbness or weakness. Muscle-referred outer hip pain is tender to press on the soft tissue above and behind the hip bone and worse lying on that side, while hip joint disease usually hurts in the groin and limits rotation. Middle-of-buttock pain is behind in the cheek, upper-buttock pain runs along the belt line.

Typical story: a Sarasota resident stands for long shifts on hard floors, starts a daily walking program on the sidewalk, and sleeps on the same side every night. A deep ache develops over the outside of one hip that wakes them whenever they roll onto it and aches on stairs. Groin motion is free and painless, and the soft tissue just above the hip bone is tender.

Muscles that commonly refer pain here

Ranked the way the MD Fit pain map ranks them, most common first:

  1. Gluteus minimus. Outer hip down the side of the thigh and leg to the ankle, or down the back of the thigh and calf. A common sciatica look-alike. Worse with: standing or walking, lying on it at night, sitting.
  2. Tensor fasciae latae. Front-outer hip down the outside of the thigh, usually stopping at the knee.
  3. Gluteus medius. Along the belt line, over the SI joint, the buttock and the top of the hip.
  4. Quadratus lumborum. Low back and flank, over the SI joint and buttock, and the point of the hip.

What people with this pain describe

Taken from Dr. Jared's notes on the gluteus minimus, the most common source here:

  • Deep, aching pain in the buttock that is hard to pinpoint.
  • Pain that refers down the side of the thigh and leg, sometimes all the way to the ankle.
  • Pain that refers down the back of the thigh and calf, mimicking sciatica.
  • Intense pain when lying on the affected side, often worse than with gluteus medius.

Quick self-check

Education, not a diagnosis. The more of these that ring true, the more likely the top muscles are involved:

  • Does your pain travel down the outside of your leg, possibly reaching your ankle?
  • Does your pain sometimes travel down the back of your leg, making you think you have sciatica?
  • Is it nearly impossible to lie on the painful side at night?

How it usually starts

  • Sitting on a wallet in your back pocket.
  • A direct fall onto the side of the hip.
  • Prolonged sitting, especially in a chair that is too high or too hard.
  • Walking or running with poor pelvic stability (a 'hip drop' gait).

What it gets mistaken for

Lumbar radiculopathy (true sciatica from a disc herniation); Greater trochanteric pain syndrome (hip bursitis); Hip joint osteoarthritis. An evaluation separates muscle pain from these, and anything that needs a physician is referred on.

What makes it worse, and what that tells you

  • Standing or walking. Points toward the gluteus minimus, gluteus medius, quadratus lumborum.
  • Lying on it at night. Points toward the gluteus minimus, tensor fasciae latae, gluteus medius.
  • Sitting. Points toward the gluteus minimus, tensor fasciae latae, quadratus lumborum.

Warning signs: get medical care, not a stretch

  • Pain after a fall or impact with deformity, or you cannot bear weight or use the limb: get an X-ray.
  • A joint that is hot, red, swollen and painful, especially with fever: urgent care.
  • Numbness, pins and needles or weakness that is spreading or constant: see a physician.
  • Pain that is constant, worse at night and not changed by movement or position: see a physician.

Frequently asked questions

Why does it hurt at the outside of the hip if nothing is wrong there?

Muscles refer pain: a tight band in one muscle sends a predictable pattern of pain somewhere else. For pain at the outside of the hip, the usual sources are the gluteus minimus, tensor fasciae latae, gluteus medius.

How long does gluteus minimus pain usually take to settle?

Due to its common mimicry of sciatica, this muscle can be stubborn. Consistent release and activation can provide relief from the referred leg pain within 2-4 weeks. Building robust strength to correct underlying walking patterns may take 2-3 months.

What makes it more likely to be the gluteus minimus?

It is worse with standing or walking, lying on it at night, sitting, and pressing into the muscle reproduces the pain you know. Outer hip down the side of the thigh and leg to the ankle, or down the back of the thigh and calf. A common sciatica look-alike.

When should I book instead of working on it myself?

Book an appointment if you have numbness, tingling, or true weakness in your leg, or if the pain does not begin to improve after 3 weeks of dedicated effort.
