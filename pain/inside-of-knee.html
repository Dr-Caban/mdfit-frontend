Pain at the inside of the knee: What's Causing It

★★★★★ 5.0 · 89 Google reviews

Pain on the inside of the knee usually feels like a deep ache or a tender line along the inner joint, sometimes with a sense that the knee wants to buckle on stairs, flaring after pivoting or long walks on uneven ground. People notice it after pickleball, golf, kneeling in the garden, or a new lunge program. The vastus medialis, the teardrop quadriceps muscle just above the inner knee that locks the knee straight and steadies the kneecap, is the most common source. The adductors from the inner thigh and the inner hamstrings, which attach near the inner knee, add ache along the same side.

Is it this, or something next to it?

A physician should first rule out a medial meniscus tear or MCL sprain with locking, swelling or giving way after a twist, arthritis with swelling and warmth, and pes anserine bursitis with a tender puffy spot below the joint. Muscle pain here is tender in the inner thigh muscles above the joint and reproduced by squeezing the knees together, with no swelling. Kneecap pain is front and center, outside-of-knee pain follows the outer band from the hip.

Typical story: a Sarasota golfer plays several rounds in a week on soft uneven fairways and adds a long day of kneeling to repot plants. A deep ache develops along the inside of one knee that makes the leg feel wobbly going downstairs. The joint is not swollen or locking, and pressing the teardrop muscle above the inner knee and the inner thigh reproduces the ache.

Muscles that commonly refer pain here

Ranked the way the MD Fit pain map ranks them, most common first:

  1. Vastus medialis. Inner knee and inside of the kneecap; can make the knee feel like it buckles. Worse with: stairs, squats or hills, running or sports.
  2. Adductor longus & brevis. Deep groin, the inner thigh and down to the inner knee and top of the shin.
  3. Semitendinosus & semimembranosus. The buttock crease, back of the thigh and the inner back of the knee, sometimes the inner calf.
  4. Semitendinosus. The buttock crease and the back of the thigh, into the inner back of the knee and sometimes the upper inner calf.
  5. Gracilis. A hot, superficial, stinging line down the inner thigh, sometimes continuing to the inner knee.
  6. Sartorius. A sharp or tingling line down the front of the thigh along the muscle itself, and into the inner knee.

What people with this pain describe

Taken from Dr. Jared's notes on the vastus medialis, the most common source here:

  • Aching or sharp pain on the inner side of the knee and kneecap.
  • A feeling that the knee might 'give way' or buckle, especially on stairs.
  • Swelling or puffiness on the inside of the knee, just above the kneecap.
  • A 'crunching' or 'grinding' sound (crepitus) under the kneecap with movement.

Quick self-check

Education, not a diagnosis. The more of these that ring true, the more likely the top muscles are involved:

  • Is your pain located specifically on the inner side of your knee or kneecap?
  • Does your knee ever feel like it's going to buckle or give out, especially on stairs?
  • Do you see or feel a 'teardrop' shaped muscle on the inside of your thigh just above the knee, and is it tender to press on?

How it usually starts

  • An imbalance between the vastus medialis and the vastus lateralis (outer quad), causing the kneecap to pull sideways.
  • Weakness or poor activation of the VMO, often following a knee injury or surgery.
  • Flat feet (overpronation), which can cause the lower leg to rotate inward and affect kneecap tracking.
  • Weak hip and gluteal muscles, leading to poor leg alignment.

What it gets mistaken for

Medial meniscus tear.; Medial collateral ligament (MCL) sprain.; Patellofemoral pain syndrome.. An evaluation separates muscle pain from these, and anything that needs a physician is referred on.

What makes it worse, and what that tells you

  • Stairs, squats or hills. Points toward the vastus medialis, adductor longus & brevis, semitendinosus.
  • Running or sports. Points toward the vastus medialis, adductor longus & brevis, semitendinosus & semimembranosus.

Warning signs: get medical care, not a stretch

  • Pain after a fall or impact with deformity, or you cannot bear weight or use the limb: get an X-ray.
  • A joint that is hot, red, swollen and painful, especially with fever: urgent care.
  • Numbness, pins and needles or weakness that is spreading or constant: see a physician.
  • Pain that is constant, worse at night and not changed by movement or position: see a physician.

Frequently asked questions

Why does it hurt at the inside of the knee if nothing is wrong there?

Muscles refer pain: a tight band in one muscle sends a predictable pattern of pain somewhere else. For pain at the inside of the knee, the usual sources are the vastus medialis, adductor longus & brevis, semitendinosus & semimembranosus.

How long does vastus medialis pain usually take to settle?

A reduction in buckling sensations and daily pain can often be felt in 2-4 weeks. Rebuilding the VMO's strength and coordination to prevent recurrence during sports can take 8-12 weeks.

What makes it more likely to be the vastus medialis?

It is worse with stairs, squats or hills, running or sports, and pressing into the muscle reproduces the pain you know. Inner knee and inside of the kneecap; can make the knee feel like it buckles.

When should I book instead of working on it myself?

Book an appointment if your knee is locking, if it swells up significantly after activity, or if the feeling of instability persists despite 3-4 weeks of consistent exercise.
