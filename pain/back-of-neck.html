Pain at the back of the neck: What's Causing It

★★★★★ 5.0 · 89 Google reviews

Pain in the back of the neck usually feels like a stiff, aching column running from the hairline down to the shoulders, often with a heavy head and slow, grinding turns. People notice it on waking after a tall pillow, after a day looking down at a phone, or at the end of a drive. The splenius muscles, the long straps up the back of the neck that extend and turn the head, and the suboccipitals, the small muscles that fine-tune head position, are the most frequent sources. The levator scapulae and upper trapezius add ache lower down toward the shoulders.

Is it this, or something next to it?

A physician should first rule out neck emergencies, including neck pain with fever and stiffness, pain after a car accident or fall, sudden severe headache, or arm weakness, numbness or trouble with balance. Muscle pain here is tender along the back of the neck and eases with gentle chin tucks and warmth. Side-of-neck pain runs down toward the collarbone, front-of-neck pain sits over the windpipe, and neck-meets-shoulder pain is lower and out at the angle of the shoulder blade.

Typical story: a Sarasota resident spends evenings reading on a tablet in bed with the head propped forward on two pillows, then drives to work with the seat reclined and the chin jutting forward. A stiff column of ache settles into the back of the neck every morning and slowly loosens by midday. Arms feel normal, and gentle chin tucks ease the ache.

Muscles that commonly refer pain here

Ranked the way the MD Fit pain map ranks them, most common first:

  1. Suboccipitals. A band of headache from the base of the skull wrapping to behind the eye and the forehead.
  2. Splenius capitis & cervicis. Pain on the top of the head, and deep ache inside the head behind the eye.
  3. Levator scapulae. The angle where the neck meets the shoulder, down the inner edge of the shoulder blade.
  4. Upper trapezius. Up the side of the neck, behind the ear and into the temple (a question-mark shape).
  5. Lower trapezius. Up the back of the neck to the base of the skull, the top of the shoulder, and along the inner shoulder blade. A hidden driver of neck tension. Worse with: desk, phone or screens, reaching overhead.

What people with this pain describe

Taken from Dr. Jared's notes on the lower trapezius, the most common source here:

  • Ache up the back of the neck toward the base of the skull and behind the ear.
  • Ache across the top of the shoulder.
  • Ache along the inner edge of the shoulder blade.
  • Neck tension that keeps coming back even after neck stretches.

Quick self-check

Education, not a diagnosis. The more of these that ring true, the more likely the top muscles are involved:

  • Do you get neck or base-of-skull tension that never fully goes away with neck stretches?
  • Is there a tender spot low on the inner edge of the shoulder blade?
  • Do your shoulders creep up toward your ears when you reach overhead?

How it usually starts

  • Slumped sitting that stretches and weakens the lower trap.
  • Shoulders shrugging during overhead work.
  • Tight chest and lats pulling the shoulder blade forward.
  • Little or no pulling exercise.

What it gets mistaken for

Upper trapezius or levator trigger points; Cervicogenic headache; Thoracic facet irritation. An evaluation separates muscle pain from these, and anything that needs a physician is referred on.

What makes it worse, and what that tells you

  • Desk, phone or screens. Points toward the suboccipitals, splenius capitis & cervicis, levator scapulae.
  • Reaching overhead. Points toward the lower trapezius.

Warning signs: get medical care, not a stretch

  • Neck pain with fever and a stiff neck, or with confusion: call 911.
  • Neck pain after a car crash or fall, or with weakness, numbness or clumsiness in the arms or legs: get checked urgently.
  • Neck or arm pain with chest pressure, shortness of breath, sweating or nausea can be a heart attack: call 911.
  • Pain that is constant, worse at night, with unexplained weight loss: see a physician.

Frequently asked questions

Why does it hurt at the back of the neck if nothing is wrong there?

Muscles refer pain: a tight band in one muscle sends a predictable pattern of pain somewhere else. For pain at the back of the neck, the usual sources are the suboccipitals, splenius capitis & cervicis, levator scapulae.

How long does lower trapezius pain usually take to settle?

Neck tension often eases within 2 weeks; lower-trap strength takes 4 to 6 weeks to build.

What makes it more likely to be the lower trapezius?

It is worse with desk, phone or screens, reaching overhead, and pressing into the muscle reproduces the pain you know. Up the back of the neck to the base of the skull, the top of the shoulder, and along the inner shoulder blade. A hidden driver of neck tension.
