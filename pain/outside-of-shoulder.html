Pain at the outside of the shoulder or upper arm: What's Causing It

★★★★★ 5.0 · 89 Google reviews

Pain on the outside of the shoulder or upper arm typically feels like a deep ache over the rounded cap of the shoulder that runs down the side of the arm toward the elbow, often disturbing sleep. People notice it lifting the arm sideways, reaching into the back seat, or carrying a bag. The supraspinatus, the rotator cuff muscle that starts the arm lifting sideways, is the most common deep source. The lateral deltoid, the muscle that forms the cap of the shoulder and lifts the arm out, and the infraspinatus behind it both refer pain down the outer arm.

Is it this, or something next to it?

A physician should first rule out a rotator cuff tear, fracture or dislocation when pain follows a fall or the arm cannot be lifted. Muscle pain on the outer shoulder is tender to press on the deltoid and usually keeps full strength. Front-of-shoulder pain hurts reaching behind, under-collarbone pain is inner and higher, and deep joint pain feels inside with catching, while a neck nerve problem adds numbness.

Typical story: a Sarasota pickleball player ramps up to daily games, hitting hundreds of overhead and side-arm shots, and starts carrying a heavy paddle bag on the same side. A deep ache sets into the outside of the shoulder that wakes them when rolling onto it at night and aches when lifting a coffee cup out to the side. Strength is fine and the arm moves fully.

Muscles that commonly refer pain here

Ranked the way the MD Fit pain map ranks them, most common first:

  1. Lateral deltoid. Outside of the shoulder cap and the side of the upper arm toward the deltoid's insertion. Worse with: reaching overhead, gripping, lifting or typing, lying on it at night.
  2. Supraspinatus. Deep ache at the top and side of the shoulder that can run down the outside of the arm to the elbow.
  3. Infraspinatus. Deep in the front of the shoulder joint, down the front and outside of the arm.
  4. Scalenes. Chest, front and back of the upper arm, inner shoulder blade and down the forearm to the thumb and index finger.

What people with this pain describe

Taken from Dr. Jared's notes on the lateral deltoid, the most common source here:

  • Ache over the outside of the shoulder cap and down the side of the upper arm.
  • Pain lifting the arm out to the side or holding it at shoulder height.
  • Soreness lying on that shoulder.
  • Fatigue and ache after carrying bags or working with the arms raised.

Quick self-check

Education, not a diagnosis. The more of these that ring true, the more likely the top muscles are involved:

  • Is the pain on the outside of the shoulder cap, not the front or back?
  • Does lifting your arm out to the side hurt, especially holding it at shoulder height?
  • Does it ache after carrying heavy bags or working with your arms up?

How it usually starts

  • Repeated lateral raises or overhead work without rest.
  • Carrying heavy bags with the arm held away from the body.
  • Sleeping on one side every night.
  • A weak supraspinatus forcing the lateral delt to start every lift.

What it gets mistaken for

Supraspinatus tendinopathy; Subacromial bursitis; Shoulder impingement. An evaluation separates muscle pain from these, and anything that needs a physician is referred on.

What makes it worse, and what that tells you

  • Reaching overhead. Points toward the lateral deltoid, supraspinatus, infraspinatus.
  • Gripping, lifting or typing. Points toward the lateral deltoid.
  • Lying on it at night. Points toward the lateral deltoid, supraspinatus, infraspinatus.

Warning signs: get medical care, not a stretch

  • Pain after a fall or impact with deformity, or you cannot bear weight or use the limb: get an X-ray.
  • A joint that is hot, red, swollen and painful, especially with fever: urgent care.
  • Numbness, pins and needles or weakness that is spreading or constant: see a physician.
  • Pain that is constant, worse at night and not changed by movement or position: see a physician.

Frequently asked questions

Why does it hurt at the outside of the shoulder or upper arm if nothing is wrong there?

Muscles refer pain: a tight band in one muscle sends a predictable pattern of pain somewhere else. For pain at the outside of the shoulder or upper arm, the usual sources are the lateral deltoid, supraspinatus, infraspinatus.

How long does lateral deltoid pain usually take to settle?

Most people feel easier side-lifting within 1 to 2 weeks; endurance for carrying and overhead work builds over 4 to 6 weeks.

What makes it more likely to be the lateral deltoid?

It is worse with reaching overhead, gripping, lifting or typing, lying on it at night, and pressing into the muscle reproduces the pain you know. Outside of the shoulder cap and the side of the upper arm toward the deltoid's insertion.

When should I book instead of working on it myself?

Book an evaluation if lifting the arm stays painful after 3 weeks, there is a painful arc between 60 and 120 degrees, or weakness is getting worse.
