Pain at the back of the hand or wrist: What's Causing It

★★★★★ 5.0 · 89 Google reviews

Pain on the back of the hand or wrist feels like a dull ache or sharp twinge bending the wrist back, pushing up from a chair, or doing a plank, sometimes with stiff, puffy fingers in the morning. People notice it after long days typing, a weekend of yard work, or a new upper-body program. The wrist extensors, the muscles on top of the forearm that pull the hand back and steady the wrist when gripping, and the finger extensors, which lift the fingers, are the most common sources and refer pain down into the back of the hand. The scalenes and subscapularis can add ache from higher up.

Is it this, or something next to it?

A physician should first rule out a fracture or ligament injury after a fall onto the hand, a ganglion cyst, arthritis, or a nerve problem with numbness. Muscle-referred pain here is reproduced by pressing the top of the forearm and by resisted wrist extension, while the wrist itself is not swollen. Palm-side pain is on the underside with gripping, thumb and index finger pain sits on the thumb edge, and ring and little finger pain is on the opposite edge.

Typical story: a Sarasota resident starts a home workout plan with daily planks and push-ups, then spends a weekend weeding and raking. An ache develops across the back of the wrist that twinges when pushing up from the floor or a low chair. There is no swelling or history of a fall, and pressing the top of the forearm reproduces the wrist ache.

Muscles that commonly refer pain here

Ranked the way the MD Fit pain map ranks them, most common first:

  1. Wrist extensors. Outside of the elbow (tennis elbow) and the back of the wrist and hand on the thumb side. Worse with: gripping, lifting or typing, desk, phone or screens.
  2. Finger extensors. Back of the forearm and back of the hand into the middle or ring finger.
  3. Scalenes. Chest, front and back of the upper arm, inner shoulder blade and down the forearm to the thumb and index finger.
  4. Subscapularis. Back of the shoulder, down the back of the arm, sometimes a band around the wrist.
  5. Coracobrachialis. Front of the shoulder, the back of the upper arm and forearm, and the back of the hand toward the middle finger.

What people with this pain describe

Taken from Dr. Jared's notes on the wrist extensors, the most common source here:

  • Pain at the outside of the elbow (tennis elbow).
  • Ache down the back of the forearm to the back of the hand near the thumb side.
  • Pain gripping, shaking hands, lifting a cup or turning a key.
  • Weaker grip.

Quick self-check

Education, not a diagnosis. The more of these that ring true, the more likely the top muscles are involved:

  • Does gripping or lifting a cup with the palm down hurt at the outside of the elbow?
  • Is there a tender spot just below the bony bump on the outside of the elbow?
  • Does lifting your wrist back against resistance hurt?

How it usually starts

  • Repetitive gripping, mouse use and typing with the wrist bent back.
  • Racquet sports and tool use.
  • Sudden increase in lifting or yard work.
  • Neck and shoulder tension adding load.

What it gets mistaken for

Lateral epicondylitis (tendinopathy); Radial tunnel syndrome; Cervical radiculopathy (C6–C7). An evaluation separates muscle pain from these, and anything that needs a physician is referred on.

What makes it worse, and what that tells you

  • Gripping, lifting or typing. Points toward the wrist extensors, finger extensors.
  • Desk, phone or screens. Points toward the wrist extensors, finger extensors, scalenes.

Warning signs: get medical care, not a stretch

  • Pain after a fall or impact with deformity, or you cannot bear weight or use the limb: get an X-ray.
  • A joint that is hot, red, swollen and painful, especially with fever: urgent care.
  • Numbness, pins and needles or weakness that is spreading or constant: see a physician.
  • Pain that is constant, worse at night and not changed by movement or position: see a physician.

Frequently asked questions

Why does it hurt at the back of the hand or wrist if nothing is wrong there?

Muscles refer pain: a tight band in one muscle sends a predictable pattern of pain somewhere else. For pain at the back of the hand or wrist, the usual sources are the wrist extensors, finger extensors, scalenes.

How long does wrist extensors pain usually take to settle?

Tendon-driven elbow pain takes 6 to 12 weeks of consistent loading; muscle tenderness eases sooner.

What makes it more likely to be the wrist extensors?

It is worse with gripping, lifting or typing, desk, phone or screens, and pressing into the muscle reproduces the pain you know. Outside of the elbow (tennis elbow) and the back of the wrist and hand on the thumb side.

When should I book instead of working on it myself?

Book an evaluation if pain persists past 4 weeks, wakes you at night, or there is any weakness or numbness.
