Pain at the buttock crease: What's Causing It

★★★★★ 5.0 · 89 Google reviews

Pain in the buttock crease feels like a deep ache or bruised sensation right at the sit bone where the buttock meets the thigh, worst sitting on a hard surface, driving, or stretching the leg forward. People notice it after a long ride, hours on a hard chair or boat bench, deep lunges or deadlifts, or an aggressive hamstring stretch. The inner hamstrings, the semitendinosus and semimembranosus that attach to the sit bone and bend the knee and extend the hip, are the most common source. The gluteus maximus, which attaches nearby, adds a broader ache with long sitting.

Is it this, or something next to it?

A physician should first rule out a hamstring tendon tear or bony avulsion after a sudden pop, ischial bursitis with swelling, a lumbar nerve problem with numbness or weakness. Muscle pain here is tender exactly on the sit bone and worse sitting and stretching the hamstring. Back-of-thigh pain sits lower in the muscle belly, back-of-knee pain is well below behind the joint, and tailbone pain is central at the base of the spine.

Typical story: a Sarasota cyclist increases weekend mileage on a new bike with a hard saddle, then spends weekdays on a wooden dining chair working from home. A deep bruised ache develops right at the sit bone of one buttock that is sharpest sitting down and stretching the leg forward. There has been no sudden pop or bruising, and pressing the sit bone reproduces the spot.

Muscles that commonly refer pain here

Ranked the way the MD Fit pain map ranks them, most common first:

  1. Semitendinosus & semimembranosus. The buttock crease, back of the thigh and the inner back of the knee, sometimes the inner calf. Worse with: sitting, running or sports.
  2. Gluteus maximus. Buttock and the tailbone.
  3. Semitendinosus. The buttock crease and the back of the thigh, into the inner back of the knee and sometimes the upper inner calf. Worse with: sitting, running or sports, stairs, squats or hills.
  4. Semimembranosus. The buttock crease and the back of the thigh, concentrating deep in the inner back of the knee.
  5. Quadratus femoris. Deep in the buttock low down near the sit bone, and into the back of the hip.

What people with this pain describe

Taken from Dr. Jared's notes on the semitendinosus & semimembranosus and the semitendinosus, the two most common sources here:

  • Ache at the buttock crease and sitting bone.
  • Pain down the back of the thigh.
  • Ache at the inner back of the knee, sometimes into the inner calf.
  • Pain sitting on hard chairs.
  • Pain at the buttock crease, right on the sit bone.
  • Aching down the back of the thigh toward the inner knee.
  • A cord-like tender band on the inner side behind the knee.

Quick self-check

Education, not a diagnosis. The more of these that ring true, the more likely the top muscles are involved:

  • Is the pain at the inner back of the knee or the buttock crease?
  • Does sitting on a hard chair hurt at the sitting bone?
  • Does bending forward with straight legs pull at the inner back of the thigh?
  • Is your pain right at the sit bone where you sit down?
  • Can you feel a tender rope-like tendon on the inner side behind your knee?

How it usually starts

  • Long sitting on hard surfaces.
  • Hill running and sprinting.
  • Heavy deadlifts without progression.
  • Weak glutes.
  • Sprinting and acceleration, particularly without a proper warm-up.
  • A sudden increase in hill running.

What it gets mistaken for

Proximal hamstring tendinopathy; Pes anserine bursitis; Baker's cyst; Proximal hamstring tendinopathy.; Sciatic nerve irritation.. An evaluation separates muscle pain from these, and anything that needs a physician is referred on.

What makes it worse, and what that tells you

  • Sitting. Points toward the semitendinosus & semimembranosus, gluteus maximus, semitendinosus.
  • Running or sports. Points toward the semitendinosus & semimembranosus, semitendinosus, semimembranosus.
  • Stairs, squats or hills. Points toward the gluteus maximus, semitendinosus, semimembranosus.

Warning signs: get medical care, not a stretch

  • Pain after a fall or impact with deformity, or you cannot bear weight or use the limb: get an X-ray.
  • A joint that is hot, red, swollen and painful, especially with fever: urgent care.
  • Numbness, pins and needles or weakness that is spreading or constant: see a physician.
  • Pain that is constant, worse at night and not changed by movement or position: see a physician.

Frequently asked questions

Why does it hurt at the buttock crease if nothing is wrong there?

Muscles refer pain: a tight band in one muscle sends a predictable pattern of pain somewhere else. For pain at the buttock crease, the usual sources are the semitendinosus & semimembranosus, gluteus maximus, semitendinosus.

How long does semitendinosus & semimembranosus pain usually take to settle?

Usually improves in 2 to 4 weeks; tendon pain at the sitting bone can take 8 to 12 weeks.

What makes it more likely to be the semitendinosus & semimembranosus?

It is worse with sitting, running or sports, and pressing into the muscle reproduces the pain you know. The buttock crease, back of the thigh and the inner back of the knee, sometimes the inner calf.

When should I book instead of working on it myself?

Book an evaluation if sitting pain persists past 3 weeks, or after any pop with bruising.
