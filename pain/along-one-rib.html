Pain along one rib: What's Causing It

★★★★★ 5.0 · 89 Google reviews

Pain along one rib usually feels like a thin line of sharp or burning ache that follows the curve of a single rib from the back toward the front, often catching with a deep breath, a cough, a sneeze or a twist. People notice it after a coughing illness, a heavy twisting lift, or a fall. The intercostals, the small muscles between each pair of ribs that expand and compress the chest with every breath, are almost always the source. The serratus anterior, the muscle that hugs the side of the rib cage, can add a wider ache.

Is it this, or something next to it?

A physician must first rule out heart, lung and other emergencies: rib pain with shortness of breath, fever, a cough bringing up blood, chest pressure, or pain after a significant fall needs urgent care, and a blistering rash along one rib suggests shingles. Muscle pain here is tender to press along the rib and reproduced by a deep breath and twist. Side-of-rib pain is broader under the arm, front-of-chest pain is central, and abdominal pain sits below the cage.

Typical story: a Sarasota gardener spends a weekend hauling mulch bags and twisting to dump them, then catches a cold with a hard cough. A sharp line of pain develops along one lower rib that catches with every sneeze and when reaching up into a cabinet. Breathing is otherwise normal, there is no rash, and pressing along the rib finds a tender streak.

Muscles that commonly refer pain here

Ranked the way the MD Fit pain map ranks them, most common first:

  1. Intercostals. Sharp or aching pain along the rib, worse with deep breaths, coughing or twisting. Worse with: deep breaths or coughing, turning your head or body.
  2. Serratus anterior. Side of the rib cage and the bottom tip of the shoulder blade.

What people with this pain describe

Taken from Dr. Jared's notes on the intercostals, the most common source here:

  • A sharp, stabbing, or aching pain located directly between or along a single rib.
  • Pain that gets significantly worse with a deep breath, cough, sneeze, or laugh.
  • Tenderness to the touch in the space between two ribs.
  • A feeling of tightness or restriction around your chest.

Quick self-check

Education, not a diagnosis. The more of these that ring true, the more likely the top muscles are involved:

  • Is your pain sharp and located in a specific spot on your rib cage that you can point to with one finger?
  • Does taking a deep breath, coughing, or sneezing make the pain dramatically worse?
  • Can you press on a spot between your ribs and reproduce the exact pain?

How it usually starts

  • Forceful and repetitive coughing or sneezing.
  • A sudden, sharp twisting of the torso, such as in golf, tennis, or reaching into the back seat of a car.
  • A direct blow or impact to the chest or ribs.
  • Over-stretching the torso, such as reaching for something far to the side.

What it gets mistaken for

Rib fracture; Serratus anterior muscle strain; Pleurisy or other lung conditions. An evaluation separates muscle pain from these, and anything that needs a physician is referred on.

What makes it worse, and what that tells you

  • Deep breaths or coughing. Points toward the intercostals, serratus anterior.
  • Turning your head or body. Points toward the intercostals.

Warning signs: get medical care, not a stretch

  • Chest, upper-back or arm pain with pressure, shortness of breath, sweating, nausea or pain spreading to the jaw or arm can be a heart attack: call 911 now, do not stretch it out.
  • Sudden sharp chest pain with trouble breathing can be a lung problem: call 911.
  • Chest-wall pain after a fall or impact with trouble breathing: emergency care.
  • MD Fit treats chest-wall and rib muscle pain only after a physician has ruled out the heart and lungs.

Frequently asked questions

Why does it hurt along one rib if nothing is wrong there?

Muscles refer pain: a tight band in one muscle sends a predictable pattern of pain somewhere else. For pain along one rib, the usual sources are the intercostals, serratus anterior.

How long does intercostals pain usually take to settle?

Intercostal strains can be very painful. Expect the sharpest pain to subside in 1-3 weeks, but a dull ache may linger for 4-6 weeks or more. It is important to avoid re-injury during this time.

What makes it more likely to be the intercostals?

It is worse with deep breaths or coughing, turning your head or body, and pressing into the muscle reproduces the pain you know. Sharp or aching pain along the rib, worse with deep breaths, coughing or twisting.

When should I book instead of working on it myself?

See a professional if the pain is severe and unmanageable, if you have any red flags, or if there is no improvement after 2 weeks of gentle care.
