Pain at the middle of the buttock: What's Causing It

★★★★★ 5.0 · 89 Google reviews

Pain in the middle of the buttock often feels like a deep, hot ache in the center of one cheek, sometimes shooting down the back of the thigh toward the calf, and it is commonly called sciatica. It is noticed after long sitting on a wallet or hard seat, a long drive, or a sudden increase in walking or running. The piriformis, the small deep muscle that rotates the leg outward and runs right over the sciatic nerve, is a frequent source. The gluteus minimus, the small side hip muscle that steadies the pelvis, is a classic sciatica look-alike, referring pain down the leg to the ankle.

Is it this, or something next to it?

A physician should first rule out a true lumbar disc or nerve root problem, especially with numbness, progressive leg weakness, bladder or bowel change, or pain worse with coughing and sneezing, and hip joint disease. Muscle-referred buttock pain is reproduced by pressing deep in the cheek, with a normal nerve exam. Upper-buttock pain is along the belt line, outside-of-hip pain is lateral over the hip bone, and a hip joint problem usually hurts in the groin with rotation.

Typical story: a Sarasota resident drives eight hours with a thick wallet in the back pocket, then sits through a week of long meetings. A deep ache settles in the middle of one buttock that occasionally sends a line down the back of the thigh after sitting. Reflexes and strength are normal, coughing does not worsen it, and pressing deep into the buttock reproduces the leg ache.

Muscles that commonly refer pain here

Ranked the way the MD Fit pain map ranks them, most common first:

  1. Piriformis. Mid-buttock and back of the hip, down the back-outside of the thigh toward the calf. Worse with: sitting, running or sports, stairs, squats or hills.
  2. Gluteus minimus. Outer hip down the side of the thigh and leg to the ankle, or down the back of the thigh and calf.
  3. Gluteus medius. Along the belt line, over the SI joint, the buttock and the top of the hip.
  4. Longissimus. Low in the buttock and over the SI joint, often well below where the muscle actually is.

What people with this pain describe

Taken from Dr. Jared's notes on the piriformis, the most common source here:

  • A deep, aching pain in the center of the buttock.
  • Pain that refers down the back or back-outside of the thigh, often stopping at the knee.
  • Numbness or tingling down the back of the leg if the muscle is compressing the sciatic nerve.
  • Pain that is significantly worse with sitting, especially on hard surfaces or while driving.

Quick self-check

Education, not a diagnosis. The more of these that ring true, the more likely the top muscles are involved:

  • Is your pain focused in the dead center of one buttock?
  • Does sitting for more than 15-20 minutes dramatically increase your buttock pain?
  • Does the pain travel down the back of your thigh, but usually not past your knee?

How it usually starts

  • Prolonged sitting, which compresses and shortens the muscle.
  • Sitting on a wallet in a back pocket ('wallet neuritis').
  • Overuse from activities like running or cycling without proper glute strength.
  • Anatomical variation where the sciatic nerve passes through the piriformis muscle.

What it gets mistaken for

Lumbar radiculopathy (true sciatica from a disc herniation); Sacroiliac (SI) joint dysfunction; Hamstring tendinopathy. An evaluation separates muscle pain from these, and anything that needs a physician is referred on.

What makes it worse, and what that tells you

  • Sitting. Points toward the piriformis, gluteus minimus, longissimus.
  • Running or sports. Points toward the piriformis, gluteus medius.
  • Stairs, squats or hills. Points toward the piriformis, gluteus medius.

Warning signs: get medical care, not a stretch

  • Loss of bladder or bowel control, or numbness in the groin or inner thighs: call 911.
  • Leg weakness that is getting worse, or numbness spreading down both legs: emergency care.
  • Back pain with fever, chills or unexplained weight loss, or that is constant and not eased by any position: see a physician.
  • Back pain after a fall or crash, or if you have osteoporosis or take steroids: get checked for a fracture.

Frequently asked questions

Why does it hurt at the middle of the buttock if nothing is wrong there?

Muscles refer pain: a tight band in one muscle sends a predictable pattern of pain somewhere else. For pain at the middle of the buttock, the usual sources are the piriformis, gluteus minimus, gluteus medius.

How long does piriformis pain usually take to settle?

Relief from sitting-related pain can often be felt within the first 1-2 weeks. Reducing nerve irritation and building enough glute strength to prevent recurrence is a longer process, typically taking 6-12 weeks of consistent work.

What makes it more likely to be the piriformis?

It is worse with sitting, running or sports, stairs, squats or hills, and pressing into the muscle reproduces the pain you know. Mid-buttock and back of the hip, down the back-outside of the thigh toward the calf.

When should I book instead of working on it myself?

Schedule an in-person evaluation if you experience true numbness, tingling, or weakness that doesn't improve with nerve glides, or if your pain is not significantly better after 4 weeks.
