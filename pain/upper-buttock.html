Pain at the upper buttock at the belt line: What's Causing It

★★★★★ 5.0 · 89 Google reviews

Pain in the upper buttock at the belt line feels like a broad, dull ache just below the waistband along the rim of the pelvis, sharp when standing on one leg, stepping up a curb, or getting out of bed. People notice it after a long walk on soft sand, a jump in running mileage, or weeks of standing with weight on one hip. The gluteus medius, the side hip muscle that keeps the pelvis level on every step, is the most common source. The quadratus lumborum, the deep side muscle above it, and the iliacus inside the pelvis also refer pain here.

Is it this, or something next to it?

A physician should first rule out serious spinal causes with leg weakness, numbness or bladder change, inflammatory SI joint arthritis, and kidney problems with urinary symptoms or fever. Muscle pain here is tender along the rim of the pelvis and worse standing on that leg. Middle-of-buttock pain sits lower in the cheek and may shoot down the leg, outside-of-hip pain is over the hip bone and hurts lying on it, and SI pain is a focused spot near the dimples.

Typical story: a Sarasota resident restarts running after a summer off and runs several miles each morning on soft sand. Within two weeks a broad ache sets in along the belt line of one buttock that sharpens stepping up a curb or standing on one leg to put on shoes. Legs are strong, and pressing the rim of the pelvis finds the tender band.

Muscles that commonly refer pain here

Ranked the way the MD Fit pain map ranks them, most common first:

  1. Gluteus medius. Along the belt line, over the SI joint, the buttock and the top of the hip. Worse with: standing or walking, lying on it at night, running or sports, stairs, squats or hills.
  2. Quadratus lumborum. Low back and flank, over the SI joint and buttock, and the point of the hip.
  3. Iliacus. Groin, front of the thigh, and the back of the pelvis high in the glute.
  4. Iliocostalis. Low back and buttock from the lumbar part.

What people with this pain describe

Taken from Dr. Jared's notes on the gluteus medius, the most common source here:

  • A persistent, nagging ache along the belt line or top of the buttock.
  • Pain that can wrap around to the bony point on the side of the hip.
  • Pain that refers over the sacroiliac (SI) joint, often mistaken for an SI joint problem.
  • Difficulty and pain when lying on the affected side at night.

Quick self-check

Education, not a diagnosis. The more of these that ring true, the more likely the top muscles are involved:

  • Is your pain located high on your buttock, right along your belt line?
  • Does it hurt to lie on that side at night, forcing you to switch positions?
  • Do you feel pain or a sense of your hip 'giving way' when you stand on one leg to put on pants?

How it usually starts

  • Sitting for long periods with legs crossed.
  • Standing with all your weight shifted onto one hip ('hip-sitting').
  • Running on a banked surface (like the edge of a road) or always running the same direction on a track.
  • Weakness from inactivity, leading to overuse when activity is resumed.

What it gets mistaken for

Sacroiliac (SI) joint dysfunction; Lumbar facet joint syndrome; Greater trochanteric pain syndrome (hip bursitis). An evaluation separates muscle pain from these, and anything that needs a physician is referred on.

What makes it worse, and what that tells you

  • Standing or walking. Points toward the gluteus medius, quadratus lumborum, iliocostalis.
  • Lying on it at night. Points toward the gluteus medius.
  • Running or sports. Points toward the gluteus medius, iliacus.
  • Stairs, squats or hills. Points toward the gluteus medius.

Warning signs: get medical care, not a stretch

  • Loss of bladder or bowel control, or numbness in the groin or inner thighs: call 911.
  • Leg weakness that is getting worse, or numbness spreading down both legs: emergency care.
  • Back pain with fever, chills or unexplained weight loss, or that is constant and not eased by any position: see a physician.
  • Back pain after a fall or crash, or if you have osteoporosis or take steroids: get checked for a fracture.

Frequently asked questions

Why does it hurt at the upper buttock at the belt line if nothing is wrong there?

Muscles refer pain: a tight band in one muscle sends a predictable pattern of pain somewhere else. For pain at the upper buttock at the belt line, the usual sources are the gluteus medius, quadratus lumborum, iliacus.

How long does gluteus medius pain usually take to settle?

You should notice less pain at night and with standing within 2-3 weeks. Consistent strengthening for 6-8 weeks is often needed to build the endurance to prevent the pain from returning.

What makes it more likely to be the gluteus medius?

It is worse with standing or walking, lying on it at night, running or sports, stairs, squats or hills, and pressing into the muscle reproduces the pain you know. Along the belt line, over the SI joint, the buttock and the top of the hip.

When should I book instead of working on it myself?

Book an evaluation if you have a 'snapping' sensation in your hip, if pain persists beyond 3-4 weeks, or if you have significant weakness that makes you feel unstable.
