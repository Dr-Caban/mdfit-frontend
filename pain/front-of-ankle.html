Pain at the front of the ankle or big toe: What's Causing It

★★★★★ 5.0 · 89 Google reviews

Pain at the front of the ankle or big toe usually feels like a dull ache or a tight, pulling sensation across the front of the ankle crease and along the top of the foot toward the big toe, sometimes with a sense of a weak or slapping foot when walking. People notice it after a sudden increase in walking or running, hiking downhill, walking in soft sand, or driving long distances with the foot held on the pedal. The tibialis anterior, the muscle along the front of the shin that lifts the foot and toes with every step and controls the foot as it lands, is the primary source and refers pain down to the front of the ankle and the big toe.

Is it this, or something next to it?

A physician should first rule out a stress fracture of the shin or foot, gout or arthritis in the big toe joint with redness, swelling and heat, a nerve problem with numbness or true foot drop, and anterior compartment syndrome with hard swelling after exertion. Muscle pain here is tender along the front of the shin and reproduced by lifting the foot against resistance. Inner-ankle pain is on the inside below the bone, outer-ankle pain is on the outside, and sole pain is underneath the heel.

Typical story: a Sarasota resident drives a long distance south with the right foot flexed on the pedal for hours, then begins daily walks in deep soft sand. A dull pulling ache develops across the front of the ankle and toward the big toe, worse after long walks and on waking. The toe joint is not red or swollen, and pressing along the front of the shin reproduces the ankle ache.

Muscles that commonly refer pain here

Ranked the way the MD Fit pain map ranks them, most common first:

  1. Tibialis anterior. Front of the shin, front of the ankle and the big toe. Worse with: running or sports, stairs, squats or hills.

What people with this pain describe

Taken from Dr. Jared's notes on the tibialis anterior, the most common source here:

  • Aching or sharp pain on the front of the shin, just to the outside of the shin bone.
  • Pain at the front of the ankle, where the foot meets the leg.
  • Pain that sometimes travels down to the top of the big toe.
  • A feeling of weakness when trying to lift your foot or toes.

Quick self-check

Education, not a diagnosis. The more of these that ring true, the more likely the top muscles are involved:

  • Is your pain located in the muscle on the front and outer side of your shin bone?
  • Does the pain sometimes refer to the front of your ankle or the top of your big toe?
  • Is the pain worse when you go down stairs or walk/run downhill?

How it usually starts

  • Overuse from activities like running (especially downhill), hiking, or kicking sports.
  • Tight calf muscles, which force the tibialis anterior to work harder to lift the foot.
  • Switching to minimalist shoes or a different running style too quickly.
  • Lacing shoes, boots, or skates too tightly across the front of the ankle.

What it gets mistaken for

Shin splints (medial tibial stress syndrome); Tibial stress fracture; Chronic exertional compartment syndrome. An evaluation separates muscle pain from these, and anything that needs a physician is referred on.

What makes it worse, and what that tells you

  • Running or sports. Points toward the tibialis anterior.
  • Stairs, squats or hills. Points toward the tibialis anterior.

Warning signs: get medical care, not a stretch

  • Pain after a fall or impact with deformity, or you cannot bear weight or use the limb: get an X-ray.
  • A joint that is hot, red, swollen and painful, especially with fever: urgent care.
  • Numbness, pins and needles or weakness that is spreading or constant: see a physician.
  • Pain that is constant, worse at night and not changed by movement or position: see a physician.

Frequently asked questions

Why does it hurt at the front of the ankle or big toe if nothing is wrong there?

Muscles refer pain: a tight band in one muscle sends a predictable pattern of pain somewhere else. For pain at the front of the ankle or big toe, the usual sources are the tibialis anterior.

How long does tibialis anterior pain usually take to settle?

Mild strains often feel significantly better within 1-3 weeks. More persistent pain from chronic overuse can take 4-8 weeks of consistent rehab to resolve.

What makes it more likely to be the tibialis anterior?

It is worse with running or sports, stairs, squats or hills, and pressing into the muscle reproduces the pain you know. Front of the shin, front of the ankle and the big toe.

When should I book instead of working on it myself?

Book an appointment if you experience 'foot drop' (inability to lift your foot), numbness on top of the foot, or pain that is severe and doesn't improve with rest.
