Pain at the forehead: What's Causing It

★★★★★ 5.0 · 89 Google reviews

Pain across the forehead often feels like a tight band or a dull pressure sitting just above the eyebrows, and many people assume it is a sinus problem or eye strain. It commonly builds through the afternoon and is noticeable in the evening after a long day of looking at screens. The muscles most often responsible are the sternocleidomastoid, the thick strap on the front of the neck that turns the head and holds it up in a forward position, and the suboccipitals, the small muscles at the base of the skull that fine-tune where the eyes are pointing. Both can send a steady ache forward into the forehead.

Is it this, or something next to it?

A physician should first rule out head emergencies: a sudden worst-ever headache, headache with fever and stiff neck, confusion, vision loss or weakness means 911, not stretching. Forehead pain from muscle usually sits in a flat band and eases when the neck is gently moved. Temple pain tends to be one-sided and pulsing; behind-the-eye pain feels deeper and sharper; ear-area pain sits further back and often arrives with jaw clenching. Sinus pain usually comes with congestion and worsens bending forward.

Typical story: a remote worker in Sarasota spends the day on a laptop at the kitchen counter, head drifting forward toward the screen, then scrolls a phone on the couch in the evening. By dinner there is a tight band across the forehead most days of the week, with no congestion and no vision changes, and it fades on weekends spent outdoors.

Muscles that commonly refer pain here

Ranked the way the MD Fit pain map ranks them, most common first:

  1. Sternocleidomastoid. Forehead, around and behind the eye, cheek, ear and top of the head. Can cause dizziness or ear fullness. Worse with: turning your head or body, desk, phone or screens, deep breaths or coughing.
  2. Suboccipitals. A band of headache from the base of the skull wrapping to behind the eye and the forehead.
  3. Occipitalis. Ache at the back of the head that can shoot through the skull to behind the eye, with scalp tenderness.

What people with this pain describe

Taken from Dr. Jared's notes on the sternocleidomastoid, the most common source here:

  • Pain in the forehead, often described as a 'headache over the eye'.
  • Pain around the eye, in the cheek, or in the jaw (TMJ) area.
  • Dizziness, vertigo, or a feeling of imbalance, especially with head turning.
  • Ear symptoms like ringing (tinnitus), fullness, or deep ear pain.

Quick self-check

Education, not a diagnosis. The more of these that ring true, the more likely the top muscles are involved:

  • Do you experience pain or pressure in your forehead, above or around your eye?
  • Have you felt dizzy, off-balance, or had ringing in your ears that seems related to your neck pain?
  • Does the pain get worse when you turn your head to look over your shoulder, like when backing up a car?

How it usually starts

  • Whiplash injuries, even minor ones.
  • Forward head posture, which over-stretches and strains the SCM.
  • Sleeping on your stomach or with thick pillows.
  • Chronic coughing, or activities involving deep, forceful breathing (e.g., playing a wind instrument).

What it gets mistaken for

Migraine with aura; Trigeminal neuralgia; Sinusitis or sinus headache. An evaluation separates muscle pain from these, and anything that needs a physician is referred on.

What makes it worse, and what that tells you

  • Turning your head or body. Points toward the sternocleidomastoid, suboccipitals.
  • Desk, phone or screens. Points toward the sternocleidomastoid, suboccipitals, occipitalis.
  • Deep breaths or coughing. Points toward the sternocleidomastoid.

Warning signs: get medical care, not a stretch

  • A sudden, severe headache unlike any you have had, or the worst headache of your life: call 911.
  • Headache with fever and a stiff neck, confusion, slurred speech, weakness or numbness on one side, or loss of vision: call 911.
  • Head pain after a fall or blow to the head, especially with vomiting or drowsiness: emergency care.
  • Jaw or face pain with chest pressure, shortness of breath or sweating can be a heart attack: call 911.

Frequently asked questions

Why does it hurt at the forehead if nothing is wrong there?

Muscles refer pain: a tight band in one muscle sends a predictable pattern of pain somewhere else. For pain at the forehead, the usual sources are the sternocleidomastoid, suboccipitals, occipitalis.

How long does sternocleidomastoid pain usually take to settle?

Symptoms like dizziness and ear fullness can improve quickly, within a week of starting release work. The headache and facial pain patterns may take 2-4 weeks to resolve as you improve your posture and strength.

What makes it more likely to be the sternocleidomastoid?

It is worse with turning your head or body, desk, phone or screens, deep breaths or coughing, and pressing into the muscle reproduces the pain you know. Forehead, around and behind the eye, cheek, ear and top of the head. Can cause dizziness or ear fullness.
