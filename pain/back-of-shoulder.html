Pain at the back of the shoulder: What's Causing It

★★★★★ 5.0 · 89 Google reviews

Pain in the back of the shoulder often feels like a focused, deep ache just behind the shoulder joint, as if a thumb is pressing into it, worse reaching across the body, pulling a door open, or sleeping with the arm forward. People notice it after rowing, swimming, backhands, or a long drive with hands high on the wheel. The posterior deltoid, the back part of the shoulder cap that pulls the arm backward, and the teres minor, the small rotator cuff muscle behind the joint that rotates the arm outward, are the most common sources. The subscapularis and teres major can add a wider ache down the arm.

Is it this, or something next to it?

A physician should first rule out a rotator cuff tear, labral injury or fracture after a fall, and a neck nerve problem if pain comes with numbness or weakness. Muscle pain here is tender to press directly behind the joint and reproduced by reaching across the body. Back-of-upper-arm pain travels further down toward the elbow, shoulder-blade pain sits on the flat bone closer to the spine, and deep joint pain feels inside with overhead motion and locking.

Typical story: a Sarasota tennis player works on a two-handed backhand for several weeks and adds rowing at the gym, then drives a long distance with the arms held up on the wheel. A pinpoint deep ache develops at the back of one shoulder that catches when reaching across to the passenger seat. The shoulder moves fully and the sore spot can be found with one thumb.

Muscles that commonly refer pain here

Ranked the way the MD Fit pain map ranks them, most common first:

  1. Posterior deltoid. Back of the shoulder and the back of the upper arm. Worse with: reaching behind your back, gripping, lifting or typing.
  2. Teres minor. A localized deep ache at the back of the shoulder. Worse with: reaching overhead, lying on it at night.
  3. Subscapularis. Back of the shoulder, down the back of the arm, sometimes a band around the wrist.
  4. Teres major. Back of the shoulder and the back of the upper arm, sometimes the back of the forearm.
  5. Infraspinatus. Deep in the front of the shoulder joint, down the front and outside of the arm.
  6. Triceps long head. Back of the shoulder and arm, up toward the base of the neck, and down the back of the forearm.

What people with this pain describe

Taken from Dr. Jared's notes on the posterior deltoid and the teres minor, the two most common sources here:

  • Ache at the back of the shoulder and the back of the upper arm.
  • Pain reaching behind you, such as into a back pocket or the back seat.
  • Soreness after rowing, pulling or throwing.
  • Tender spots on the back of the shoulder cap.
  • A distinct, localized, deep ache at the back of the shoulder.
  • Pain is often described as a 'silver dollar' sized spot of tenderness.
  • Pain is sharp when reaching overhead or across the body.

Quick self-check

Education, not a diagnosis. The more of these that ring true, the more likely the top muscles are involved:

  • Is the pain at the back of the shoulder, behind the shoulder cap?
  • Does reaching behind you (back pocket, seat belt, back seat) bring it on?
  • Did it start after rowing, pulling or throwing?
  • Do you feel a very specific, deep ache at the back of your shoulder, about the size of a coin?
  • Does pressing on the fleshy part just below the bony point of your shoulder reproduce your exact pain?

How it usually starts

  • High-volume rowing, pulling or throwing.
  • Sudden backward reach, such as grabbing something from the back seat.
  • Weak rotator cuff (infraspinatus, teres minor) leaving the rear delt to stabilize.
  • Rounded upper back keeping the rear delt on constant stretch.
  • Overuse in throwing sports or racquet sports.
  • Working with arms overhead for long periods (e.g., electrician, painter).

What it gets mistaken for

Infraspinatus or teres minor trigger points; Posterior rotator cuff tendinopathy; Posterior shoulder capsule tightness; Infraspinatus strain (often occur together); Posterior shoulder impingement. An evaluation separates muscle pain from these, and anything that needs a physician is referred on.

What makes it worse, and what that tells you

  • Reaching behind your back. Points toward the posterior deltoid, subscapularis, infraspinatus.
  • Gripping, lifting or typing. Points toward the posterior deltoid, teres major, triceps long head.
  • Reaching overhead. Points toward the teres minor, subscapularis, teres major.
  • Lying on it at night. Points toward the teres minor, infraspinatus.

Warning signs: get medical care, not a stretch

  • Pain after a fall or impact with deformity, or you cannot bear weight or use the limb: get an X-ray.
  • A joint that is hot, red, swollen and painful, especially with fever: urgent care.
  • Numbness, pins and needles or weakness that is spreading or constant: see a physician.
  • Pain that is constant, worse at night and not changed by movement or position: see a physician.

Frequently asked questions

Why does it hurt at the back of the shoulder if nothing is wrong there?

Muscles refer pain: a tight band in one muscle sends a predictable pattern of pain somewhere else. For pain at the back of the shoulder, the usual sources are the posterior deltoid, teres minor, subscapularis.

How long does posterior deltoid pain usually take to settle?

Back-of-shoulder ache from the rear delt usually settles in 1 to 3 weeks; pulling strength returns over 4 to 6 weeks.

What makes it more likely to be the posterior deltoid?

It is worse with reaching behind your back, gripping, lifting or typing, and pressing into the muscle reproduces the pain you know. Back of the shoulder and the back of the upper arm.

When should I book instead of working on it myself?

Book an evaluation if reaching behind stays painful past 3 weeks or the shoulder feels unstable or clicks painfully.
