Pain between the shoulder blades: What's Causing It

★★★★★ 5.0 · 89 Google reviews

Pain between the shoulder blades often feels like a burning, aching knot right alongside the spine, like a hot coin held against the back, and it builds through a day of sitting. People notice it after long desk sessions or drives with rounded shoulders, and it eases when the shoulders are pulled back. The rhomboid major, the muscle that connects the inner edge of the blade to the spine and squeezes the blades together, and the middle trapezius, the muscle that lies over it and does the same job, are the main sources. The scalenes in the neck often refer a deeper ache into the same area.

Is it this, or something next to it?

A physician should first rule out heart, lung, aortic and gallbladder causes: pain between the blades with chest pressure, shortness of breath, sweating, a tearing sensation, fever or pain after meals needs urgent care. Muscle pain here is tender to press beside the spine and eases with a shoulder squeeze. Inner-edge-of-blade pain sits a little higher and out on the bone edge, bottom-tip pain is lower near the ribs.

Typical story: a Sarasota remote worker relocates the office to a dining table and works ten-hour days on a laptop with no external monitor. By mid-afternoon a burning ache sits between the shoulder blades, relieved briefly by squeezing them together. The physician's check is clear, breathing is fine, and the spot is tender when pressed beside the spine.

Muscles that commonly refer pain here

Ranked the way the MD Fit pain map ranks them, most common first:

  1. Scalenes. Chest, front and back of the upper arm, inner shoulder blade and down the forearm to the thumb and index finger.
  2. Rhomboid major. Between the shoulder blade and the spine. Often strained because tight chest muscles pull the blades apart. Worse with: desk, phone or screens, gripping, lifting or typing.
  3. Middle trapezius. Burning ache between the shoulder blade and the spine, and the top of the shoulder.
  4. Levator scapulae. The angle where the neck meets the shoulder, down the inner edge of the shoulder blade.
  5. Rhomboid minor. Along the inner edge of the upper shoulder blade, next to the spine.
  6. Iliocostalis. Low back and buttock from the lumbar part.
  7. Spinalis. A narrow strip of pain close to the spine in the mid and upper back, between and just below the shoulder blades. Worse with: sitting, standing or walking, desk, phone or screens, bending or lifting.
  8. Serratus posterior superior. Deep ache underneath the shoulder blade that you cannot reach, spreading down the back of the arm to the little finger side. Worse with: deep breaths or coughing, desk, phone or screens, reaching overhead, lying on it at night.

What people with this pain describe

Taken from Dr. Jared's notes on the rhomboid major and the spinalis, the two most common sources here:

  • Ache between the shoulder blade and the spine, lower down.
  • A superficial nagging ache that is hard to reach.
  • Worse after desk work, driving or rowing.
  • Often appears alongside tight chest muscles.
  • A narrow strip of aching right beside the spine in the mid back.
  • Pain that builds through a day of sitting rather than arriving suddenly.
  • Discomfort between and just below the shoulder blades.

Quick self-check

Education, not a diagnosis. The more of these that ring true, the more likely the top muscles are involved:

  • Is the ache between the spine and the lower half of the shoulder blade?
  • Is it a nagging, superficial ache rather than sharp?
  • Are your chest muscles tight and shoulders rounded?
  • Is your pain in a narrow strip right beside your spine rather than out to the side?
  • Does it build through a day of sitting?

How it usually starts

  • Tight chest muscles pulling the shoulder blades apart.
  • Long sitting with the arms forward.
  • Heavy rowing without good form.
  • Weak middle and lower traps.
  • Prolonged unsupported sitting.
  • A rounded upper back from screen work.

What it gets mistaken for

Middle trapezius trigger points; Scalene referral; Rib or thoracic facet irritation; Thoracic facet or rib joint irritation.; Vertebral compression fracture, particularly in older adults.. An evaluation separates muscle pain from these, and anything that needs a physician is referred on.

What makes it worse, and what that tells you

  • Desk, phone or screens. Points toward the scalenes, rhomboid major, middle trapezius.
  • Gripping, lifting or typing. Points toward the rhomboid major, middle trapezius, rhomboid minor.
  • Sitting. Points toward the iliocostalis, spinalis.
  • Standing or walking. Points toward the iliocostalis, spinalis.
  • Bending or lifting. Points toward the iliocostalis, spinalis.
  • Deep breaths or coughing. Points toward the scalenes, serratus posterior superior.

Warning signs: get medical care, not a stretch

  • Chest, upper-back or arm pain with pressure, shortness of breath, sweating, nausea or pain spreading to the jaw or arm can be a heart attack: call 911 now, do not stretch it out.
  • Sudden sharp chest pain with trouble breathing can be a lung problem: call 911.
  • Chest-wall pain after a fall or impact with trouble breathing: emergency care.
  • MD Fit treats chest-wall and rib muscle pain only after a physician has ruled out the heart and lungs.

Frequently asked questions

Why does it hurt between the shoulder blades if nothing is wrong there?

Muscles refer pain: a tight band in one muscle sends a predictable pattern of pain somewhere else. For pain between the shoulder blades, the usual sources are the scalenes, rhomboid major, middle trapezius.

How long does rhomboid major pain usually take to settle?

Usually eases within 1 to 3 weeks once the chest is released and the back strengthened.

What makes it more likely to be the rhomboid major?

It is worse with desk, phone or screens, gripping, lifting or typing, and pressing into the muscle reproduces the pain you know. Between the shoulder blade and the spine. Often strained because tight chest muscles pull the blades apart.

When should I book instead of working on it myself?

Book an evaluation if pain persists past 3 weeks or keeps returning.
