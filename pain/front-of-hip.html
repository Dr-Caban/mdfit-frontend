Pain at the front of the hip bone: What's Causing It

★★★★★ 5.0 · 89 Google reviews

Pain at the front of the hip bone feels like a sharp or aching tenderness on the bony point at the front of the pelvis, with tightness standing tall after sitting or a pinch lifting the knee. People notice it after long drives, desk hours, a jump in cycling or running, or a new program of sit-ups and leg raises. The rectus femoris, the long quadriceps muscle that runs from this bone down to the kneecap and both lifts the thigh and straightens the knee, is the most common source. The iliacus and TFL, the hip flexors beside it, add ache along the front of the hip.

Is it this, or something next to it?

A physician should first rule out hip joint problems such as a labral tear, impingement or arthritis, a stress fracture or avulsion in young athletes, and a hernia or abdominal cause with swelling or fever. Muscle pain here is tender right on the bony point and worse lifting the knee against resistance, while joint pain pinches deep in the groin with bending and rotation. Groin pain sits lower and more inward in the crease toward the inner thigh.

Typical story: a Sarasota resident adds a daily spin class and a set of hanging leg raises to an already desk-bound week. A sharp tenderness develops on the bony point at the front of one hip that pinches when lifting the knee to climb stairs and feels tight standing up from a long sit. Hip rotation is free, and pressing the bone itself reproduces the spot.

Muscles that commonly refer pain here

Ranked the way the MD Fit pain map ranks them, most common first:

  1. Rectus femoris. A deep ache around and behind the kneecap, and the front of the hip bone. Worse on stairs or after sitting. Worse with: stairs, squats or hills, sitting, running or sports.
  2. Iliacus. Groin, front of the thigh, and the back of the pelvis high in the glute.
  3. Tensor fasciae latae. Front-outer hip down the outside of the thigh, usually stopping at the knee.

What people with this pain describe

Taken from Dr. Jared's notes on the rectus femoris, the most common source here:

  • A deep, nagging ache felt around or behind the kneecap (patella).
  • Pain at the front of the hip, near the hip bone, especially when lifting the leg.
  • Stiffness and pain in the front of the knee after sitting for a long time ('movie-goer's sign').
  • Pain that is worse when going down stairs or down a hill.

Quick self-check

Education, not a diagnosis. The more of these that ring true, the more likely the top muscles are involved:

  • Is your main pain a deep ache around or under your kneecap?
  • Does the pain get worse after you've been sitting for a while with your knees bent?
  • Do you also sometimes feel a separate pain point at the very front of your hip bone?

How it usually starts

  • Prolonged sitting, which keeps the muscle in a shortened state at the hip and lengthened at the knee.
  • Overuse from activities like kicking, sprinting, or cycling.
  • Weak gluteal muscles, causing the rectus femoris to overwork as a hip flexor.
  • Direct trauma, like a blow to the front of the thigh.

What it gets mistaken for

Patellofemoral pain syndrome (runner's knee).; Patellar tendonitis (jumper's knee).; Chondromalacia patellae (cartilage wear).. An evaluation separates muscle pain from these, and anything that needs a physician is referred on.

What makes it worse, and what that tells you

  • Stairs, squats or hills. Points toward the rectus femoris.
  • Sitting. Points toward the rectus femoris, iliacus, tensor fasciae latae.
  • Running or sports. Points toward the rectus femoris, iliacus, tensor fasciae latae.

Warning signs: get medical care, not a stretch

  • Pain after a fall or impact with deformity, or you cannot bear weight or use the limb: get an X-ray.
  • A joint that is hot, red, swollen and painful, especially with fever: urgent care.
  • Numbness, pins and needles or weakness that is spreading or constant: see a physician.
  • Pain that is constant, worse at night and not changed by movement or position: see a physician.

Frequently asked questions

Why does it hurt at the front of the hip bone if nothing is wrong there?

Muscles refer pain: a tight band in one muscle sends a predictable pattern of pain somewhere else. For pain at the front of the hip bone, the usual sources are the rectus femoris, iliacus, tensor fasciae latae.

How long does rectus femoris pain usually take to settle?

Pain from sitting should decrease within a week or two. Building the strength to handle activities like running and stairs without pain typically takes 4-8 weeks of consistent effort.

What makes it more likely to be the rectus femoris?

It is worse with stairs, squats or hills, sitting, running or sports, and pressing into the muscle reproduces the pain you know. A deep ache around and behind the kneecap, and the front of the hip bone. Worse on stairs or after sitting.

When should I book instead of working on it myself?
