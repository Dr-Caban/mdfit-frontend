Pain at the base of the skull: What's Causing It

★★★★★ 5.0 · 89 Google reviews

Pain at the base of the skull typically feels like a deep, gripping ache right where the neck meets the head, often with a sense that the head is too heavy and a tight rim of tension spreading up over the back of the skull. It is usually worst after long periods of looking at a screen or steering wheel and when turning to look over a shoulder. The suboccipitals, the small muscles that nod and rotate the head on the top vertebra, are nearly always involved. The upper trapezius, the shoulder-shrugging muscle that also hangs the head from the shoulders, and the lower trapezius frequently add to the ache.

Is it this, or something next to it?

A physician should first rule out head and neck emergencies, including a sudden severe headache at the back of the head, headache with fever or stiff neck, or weakness and numbness after a fall or car accident. Muscle pain here is tender to press directly under the skull and eases with gentle chin tucks. Back-of-head pain sits higher on the bone, top-of-head pain is felt on the crown, and neck-joint pain is usually sharper and one-sided with turning.

Typical story: a Sarasota golfer plays three rounds a week, drives a cart with the head jutting forward, then watches television with a thick pillow pushing the head up. A heavy ache sets in at the base of the skull by evening and eases only when lying flat. There is no dizziness or vision change, just a sense that the head is hard to hold up.

Muscles that commonly refer pain here

Ranked the way the MD Fit pain map ranks them, most common first:

  1. Suboccipitals. A band of headache from the base of the skull wrapping to behind the eye and the forehead.
  2. Upper trapezius. Up the side of the neck, behind the ear and into the temple (a question-mark shape). Very common tension headache source. Worse with: stress, desk, phone or screens, gripping, lifting or typing.
  3. Splenius capitis & cervicis. Pain on the top of the head, and deep ache inside the head behind the eye.
  4. Lower trapezius. Up the back of the neck to the base of the skull, the top of the shoulder, and along the inner shoulder blade.
  5. Semispinalis capitis. A band of pain wrapping from the base of the skull over the head toward the forehead and temple.

What people with this pain describe

Taken from Dr. Jared's notes on the upper trapezius, the most common source here:

  • Pain that wraps up the side of the neck to the temple, like a question mark.
  • A very common source of 'tension headaches'.
  • A heavy, aching, or burning feeling on top of the shoulder.
  • Stiffness in the neck, especially when tilting your head to the side.

Quick self-check

Education, not a diagnosis. The more of these that ring true, the more likely the top muscles are involved:

  • Does your pain start on top of your shoulder and travel up the side of your neck to your temple?
  • Do you feel a constant, heavy weight or ache on your shoulders, especially when stressed?
  • Does carrying a heavy bag or purse on your shoulder make the pain significantly worse?

How it usually starts

  • Chronic stress, which leads to unconscious shoulder shrugging.
  • Poor posture, especially slouching with a forward head.
  • Carrying heavy bags, backpacks, or children on one side.
  • Repetitive arm movements with poor shoulder stability (e.g., typing with a high keyboard).

What it gets mistaken for

Cervical disc issues (radiculopathy); Neck joint (facet) arthritis; Thoracic Outlet Syndrome. An evaluation separates muscle pain from these, and anything that needs a physician is referred on.

What makes it worse, and what that tells you

  • Stress. Points toward the suboccipitals, upper trapezius, semispinalis capitis.
  • Desk, phone or screens. Points toward the suboccipitals, upper trapezius, splenius capitis & cervicis.
  • Gripping, lifting or typing. Points toward the upper trapezius.

Warning signs: get medical care, not a stretch

  • A sudden, severe headache unlike any you have had, or the worst headache of your life: call 911.
  • Headache with fever and a stiff neck, confusion, slurred speech, weakness or numbness on one side, or loss of vision: call 911.
  • Head pain after a fall or blow to the head, especially with vomiting or drowsiness: emergency care.
  • Jaw or face pain with chest pressure, shortness of breath or sweating can be a heart attack: call 911.

Frequently asked questions

Why does it hurt at the base of the skull if nothing is wrong there?

Muscles refer pain: a tight band in one muscle sends a predictable pattern of pain somewhere else. For pain at the base of the skull, the usual sources are the suboccipitals, upper trapezius, splenius capitis & cervicis.

How long does upper trapezius pain usually take to settle?

You can expect significant relief from pain and headaches within 1-2 weeks of consistent stretching and release work. Lasting relief comes from building strength and changing habits over 4-6 weeks.

What makes it more likely to be the upper trapezius?

It is worse with stress, desk, phone or screens, gripping, lifting or typing, and pressing into the muscle reproduces the pain you know. Up the side of the neck, behind the ear and into the temple (a question-mark shape). Very common tension headache source.
