Pain at the top of the head: What's Causing It

★★★★★ 5.0 · 89 Google reviews

Pain on the top of the head is often described as pressure on the crown, like a cap that is too tight, or a sore spot that feels as if the skull itself hurts when touched. People notice it late in the day after hours of looking down at a phone, or after sleeping with the head bent to one side. The splenius capitis and cervicis, the long muscles up the back of the neck that rotate and extend the head, most often refer pain to the crown. The sternocleidomastoid at the front of the neck can add a deeper ache to the top of the head.

Is it this, or something next to it?

A physician should first rule out head emergencies, including a sudden severe headache, headache with fever, neck stiffness, vision change or weakness, and any new headache after a blow to the head. Muscle-referred crown pain usually comes with a stiff upper back of the neck and is reproduced by pressing there. Base-of-skull pain is low under the bone, back-of-head pain is on the curve of the skull, and scalp conditions usually show changes in the skin itself.

Typical story: a Sarasota office worker spends the day switching between a monitor set too low and a phone held in the lap, head tilted down for hours. By late afternoon there is a tight pressure on the crown of the head and the back of the neck feels stiff when turning to check a blind spot on the drive home.

What people with this pain describe

Taken from Dr. Jared's notes on the splenius capitis & cervicis, the most common source here:

Quick self-check

Education, not a diagnosis. The more of these that ring true, the more likely the top muscles are involved:

  • Does your headache feel like it's on the very top of your skull?
  • Do you have a persistent, deep ache that feels like it's behind your eye?
  • Is your neck stiff and painful when you try to tuck your chin to your chest?

How it usually starts

  • Forward head posture from computer or phone use.
  • Whiplash injury, which overstretches these muscles.
  • Sleeping with too many pillows, forcing the neck into flexion.
  • Activities that require looking up for long periods (e.g., rock climbing, painting).

What it gets mistaken for

Cervicogenic headache (headache originating from the neck joints).; Migraine or tension-type headache.; Occipital neuralgia.. An evaluation separates muscle pain from these, and anything that needs a physician is referred on.

Warning signs: get medical care, not a stretch

  • A sudden, severe headache unlike any you have had, or the worst headache of your life: call 911.
  • Headache with fever and a stiff neck, confusion, slurred speech, weakness or numbness on one side, or loss of vision: call 911.
  • Head pain after a fall or blow to the head, especially with vomiting or drowsiness: emergency care.
  • Jaw or face pain with chest pressure, shortness of breath or sweating can be a heart attack: call 911.

Frequently asked questions

Why does it hurt at the top of the head if nothing is wrong there?

Muscles refer pain: a tight band in one muscle sends a predictable pattern of pain somewhere else. For pain at the top of the head, the usual sources are the splenius capitis & cervicis, sternocleidomastoid.

How long does splenius capitis & cervicis pain usually take to settle?

Headaches often decrease in frequency and intensity within the first 1-2 weeks. Neck stiffness may take 3-6 weeks to fully resolve with consistent exercises.

What makes it more likely to be the splenius capitis & cervicis?

It is worse with desk, phone or screens, turning your head or body, and pressing into the muscle reproduces the pain you know. Pain on the top of the head, and deep ache inside the head behind the eye.

When should I book instead of working on it myself?

See a professional if your headache is severe, doesn't respond to self-care, or is accompanied by any of the red flag symptoms.
