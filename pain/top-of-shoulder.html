Pain at the top of the shoulder: What's Causing It

★★★★★ 5.0 · 89 Google reviews

Pain on the top of the shoulder typically feels like a heavy, aching ridge running from the base of the neck out toward the point of the shoulder, often described as carrying a backpack that never comes off. It is noticed after stressful work with the shoulders held up, after carrying groceries or a child, or when reaching overhead. The upper trapezius, the broad muscle that shrugs the shoulder and holds the head up, is the main source of the ache along the ridge. The supraspinatus, the rotator cuff muscle under the top of the blade that starts the arm lifting sideways, adds a deeper ache toward the point.

Is it this, or something next to it?

A physician should first rule out heart and lung causes when shoulder-top pain comes with chest pressure, shortness of breath, sweating or exertion, and gallbladder or diaphragm problems when it follows meals. Muscle pain here is tender to pinch along the ridge and eases when the shoulder is supported. Neck-origin pain usually worsens with turning the head, while a rotator cuff or joint problem hurts more with lifting the arm out to the side and may be weak.

Typical story: a Sarasota boater spends weekends hauling lines and coolers, then returns to a desk where the shoulders creep up toward the ears during a busy week. A heavy ache settles along the top of one shoulder, with a hard knot that is tender to the thumb. It is worst in the evening and better after a warm shower and a few shoulder rolls.

Muscles that commonly refer pain here

Ranked the way the MD Fit pain map ranks them, most common first:

  1. Upper trapezius. Up the side of the neck, behind the ear and into the temple (a question-mark shape).
  2. Supraspinatus. Deep ache at the top and side of the shoulder that can run down the outside of the arm to the elbow. Worse reaching overhead. Worse with: reaching overhead, lying on it at night.
  3. Levator scapulae. The angle where the neck meets the shoulder, down the inner edge of the shoulder blade.
  4. Lateral deltoid. Outside of the shoulder cap and the side of the upper arm toward the deltoid's insertion.
  5. Lower trapezius. Up the back of the neck to the base of the skull, the top of the shoulder, and along the inner shoulder blade.
  6. Middle trapezius. Burning ache between the shoulder blade and the spine, and the top of the shoulder.

What people with this pain describe

Taken from Dr. Jared's notes on the supraspinatus, the most common source here:

  • A deep, nagging ache felt on the top or outer part of your shoulder.
  • Pain that refers down the side of your arm, often stopping around the mid-arm or elbow.
  • A sharp pinch or pain when you try to lift your arm overhead, like reaching into a high cabinet.
  • Weakness when trying to lift your arm out to the side.

Quick self-check

Education, not a diagnosis. The more of these that ring true, the more likely the top muscles are involved:

  • Do you feel a sharp pain in your shoulder when you start to lift your arm out to the side, especially between 60 and 120 degrees (a 'painful arc')?
  • Is the pain a deep ache on the outside of your shoulder that sometimes travels down your arm?
  • Does it hurt to lie on that shoulder at night?

How it usually starts

  • Repetitive overhead activities (e.g., painting, swimming, throwing).
  • Lifting an object that is too heavy or with poor technique.
  • A fall onto an outstretched arm or directly onto the shoulder.
  • Poor posture (rounded shoulders) which narrows the space for the tendon.

What it gets mistaken for

Subacromial bursitis (often occurs alongside supraspinatus issues).; Biceps tendonitis.; Cervical radiculopathy (a pinched nerve in the neck referring pain to the shoulder).. An evaluation separates muscle pain from these, and anything that needs a physician is referred on.

What makes it worse, and what that tells you

  • Reaching overhead. Points toward the supraspinatus, lateral deltoid, lower trapezius.
  • Lying on it at night. Points toward the supraspinatus, levator scapulae, lateral deltoid.

Warning signs: get medical care, not a stretch

  • Pain after a fall or impact with deformity, or you cannot bear weight or use the limb: get an X-ray.
  • A joint that is hot, red, swollen and painful, especially with fever: urgent care.
  • Numbness, pins and needles or weakness that is spreading or constant: see a physician.
  • Pain that is constant, worse at night and not changed by movement or position: see a physician.

Frequently asked questions

Why does it hurt at the top of the shoulder if nothing is wrong there?

Muscles refer pain: a tight band in one muscle sends a predictable pattern of pain somewhere else. For pain at the top of the shoulder, the usual sources are the upper trapezius, supraspinatus, levator scapulae.

How long does supraspinatus pain usually take to settle?

Minor strains can feel better in 2-4 weeks. More significant tendinopathy or irritation can take 6-12 weeks of consistent, progressive rehabilitation to see significant improvement in strength and function.

What makes it more likely to be the supraspinatus?

It is worse with reaching overhead, lying on it at night, and pressing into the muscle reproduces the pain you know. Deep ache at the top and side of the shoulder that can run down the outside of the arm to the elbow. Worse reaching overhead.

When should I book instead of working on it myself?

Book an evaluation if you have significant weakness (e.g., a positive Drop Arm Test), if the pain resulted from a specific trauma, or if there is no improvement after 3-4 weeks of dedicated rehab.
