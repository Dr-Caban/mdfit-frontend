Pain on the shoulder blade: What's Causing It

★★★★★ 5.0 · 89 Google reviews

Pain on the shoulder blade commonly feels like a deep ache or burning spot on the flat of the bone itself, that begs to be pressed against a doorframe. People notice it after long mouse or phone use, carrying a heavy bag, or sleeping on one side. The infraspinatus, the rotator cuff muscle that covers most of the blade and rotates the arm outward, is the most common source and is often tender over the bone. The middle and lower trapezius, the muscles that pull the blades back and down toward the spine, add a burning ache along the inner edge and top.

Is it this, or something next to it?

A physician should first rule out heart, lung and gallbladder problems when blade pain comes with chest pressure, shortness of breath, sweating, fever or pain after fatty meals, and a neck nerve problem if pain brings tingling into the arm. Muscle pain here is tender to press over the blade and changes with arm position. Back-of-shoulder pain sits further out behind the joint, back-of-upper-arm pain runs down the arm, and between-the-blades pain is on the spine side of the bone.

Typical story: a Sarasota real estate agent spends days driving between showings with one hand on the wheel and the other on a phone, then catches up on emails at night. A burning ache settles into the flat of one shoulder blade that begs to be pressed against a doorframe. The arm works normally, and pressing the blade reproduces the exact spot.

Muscles that commonly refer pain here

Ranked the way the MD Fit pain map ranks them, most common first:

  1. Infraspinatus. Deep in the front of the shoulder joint, down the front and outside of the arm.
  2. Middle trapezius. Burning ache between the shoulder blade and the spine, and the top of the shoulder. Worse with: desk, phone or screens, gripping, lifting or typing.
  3. Supraspinatus. Deep ache at the top and side of the shoulder that can run down the outside of the arm to the elbow.
  4. Lower trapezius. Up the back of the neck to the base of the skull, the top of the shoulder, and along the inner shoulder blade.

Quick self-check

Education, not a diagnosis. The more of these that ring true, the more likely the top muscles are involved:

  • Is the pain a burning ache between the spine and the inner edge of the shoulder blade?
  • Does it build up the longer you sit at a desk or on your phone?
  • Does squeezing the shoulder blades together feel weak or bring it on?

How it usually starts

  • Rounded shoulders with tight chest muscles stretching the middle trap.
  • Long desk and phone sessions.
  • Carrying a heavy backpack.
  • Weakness from little pulling exercise.

What it gets mistaken for

Rhomboid trigger points; Thoracic facet joint irritation; Scalene referral. An evaluation separates muscle pain from these, and anything that needs a physician is referred on.

What makes it worse, and what that tells you

  • Desk, phone or screens. Points toward the middle trapezius, lower trapezius.
  • Gripping, lifting or typing. Points toward the middle trapezius.

Warning signs: get medical care, not a stretch

  • Pain after a fall or impact with deformity, or you cannot bear weight or use the limb: get an X-ray.
  • A joint that is hot, red, swollen and painful, especially with fever: urgent care.
  • Numbness, pins and needles or weakness that is spreading or constant: see a physician.
  • Pain that is constant, worse at night and not changed by movement or position: see a physician.

Frequently asked questions

Why does it hurt on the shoulder blade if nothing is wrong there?

Muscles refer pain: a tight band in one muscle sends a predictable pattern of pain somewhere else. For pain on the shoulder blade, the usual sources are the infraspinatus, middle trapezius, supraspinatus.

How long does middle trapezius pain usually take to settle?

Burning between the shoulder blades often eases in 1 to 2 weeks; endurance builds over 4 to 6 weeks.

What makes it more likely to be the middle trapezius?

It is worse with desk, phone or screens, gripping, lifting or typing, and pressing into the muscle reproduces the pain you know. Burning ache between the shoulder blade and the spine, and the top of the shoulder.

When should I book instead of working on it myself?

Book an evaluation if pain persists past 3 weeks, wakes you at night, or comes with chest symptoms (seek urgent care first).
