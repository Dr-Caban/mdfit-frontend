Pain at the back of the thigh: What's Causing It

★★★★★ 5.0 · 89 Google reviews

Pain in the back of the thigh often feels like a deep ache or a tight, cramping pull along the hamstring, running from under the buttock toward the back of the knee, noticed when sitting on a hard chair, bending to tie shoes, striding out, or standing up after a long drive. The hamstrings, the long muscles that bend the knee and pull the leg back with every stride, are the most common local source. The piriformis and gluteus minimus, deep hip muscles that sit over or near the sciatic nerve, refer a line of ache down the back of the thigh that is often mistaken for sciatica.

Is it this, or something next to it?

A physician should first rule out a lumbar nerve root problem with numbness, progressive weakness or bladder change, a hamstring tear with bruising after a sprint, and a deep vein clot with swelling and warmth in the leg. Muscle pain here is tender to press in the hamstring or deep buttock and reproduced by bending the knee against resistance. Buttock-crease pain is focused high at the sit bone, and back-of-knee pain is low behind the joint rather than mid-thigh.

Typical story: a Sarasota resident drives for hours to visit family, then jumps straight into a long beach walk and bends repeatedly to collect shells. A deep tight ache develops along the back of one thigh that pulls when sitting on a hard bench and when leaning forward to put on sandals. There is no bruising or swelling, and pressing the hamstring finds tender bands.

Muscles that commonly refer pain here

Ranked the way the MD Fit pain map ranks them, most common first:

  1. Biceps femoris. Back of the knee on the outside and up the back of the thigh. Worse with: sitting, running or sports.
  2. Semitendinosus & semimembranosus. The buttock crease, back of the thigh and the inner back of the knee, sometimes the inner calf.
  3. Piriformis. Mid-buttock and back of the hip, down the back-outside of the thigh toward the calf.
  4. Gluteus minimus. Outer hip down the side of the thigh and leg to the ankle, or down the back of the thigh and calf.
  5. Adductor magnus. Deep inside the pelvis and groin, and the inner thigh from the groin toward the knee.

What people with this pain describe

  • Ache at the back of the knee on the outside.

Quick self-check

Education, not a diagnosis. The more of these that ring true, the more likely the top muscles are involved:

  • Is the pain on the outer back of the knee or the outer back of the thigh?
  • Does sprinting or long striding bring it on?
  • Is there a tender cord on the outside of the back of the knee?

How it usually starts

  • Sprinting and kicking.
  • Long sitting with knees bent.
  • Weak glutes making the hamstrings overwork.
  • Running without warm-up.

What it gets mistaken for

Hamstring strain; Lateral knee ligament injury; Fibular nerve irritation. An evaluation separates muscle pain from these, and anything that needs a physician is referred on.

What makes it worse, and what that tells you

  • Sitting. Points toward the biceps femoris, semitendinosus & semimembranosus, piriformis.
  • Running or sports. Points toward the biceps femoris, semitendinosus & semimembranosus, piriformis.

Warning signs: get medical care, not a stretch

  • A swollen, warm, red or tender calf, especially after travel, surgery or bed rest, can be a blood clot: call 911 or go to the ER.
  • Sudden severe calf pain with a pop, and you cannot push off the foot: urgent care.
  • Leg pain with numbness in the groin or loss of bladder or bowel control: call 911.
  • Coldness, paleness or no pulse in the foot: emergency care.

Frequently asked questions

Why does it hurt at the back of the thigh if nothing is wrong there?

Muscles refer pain: a tight band in one muscle sends a predictable pattern of pain somewhere else. For pain at the back of the thigh, the usual sources are the biceps femoris, semitendinosus & semimembranosus, piriformis.

How long does biceps femoris pain usually take to settle?

Mild cases improve in 2 to 3 weeks; return to sprinting in 4 to 6 weeks.

What makes it more likely to be the biceps femoris?

It is worse with sitting, running or sports, and pressing into the muscle reproduces the pain you know. Back of the knee on the outside and up the back of the thigh.

When should I book instead of working on it myself?

Book an evaluation after any pop or bruising, or if pain persists past 3 weeks.
