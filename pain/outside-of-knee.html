Pain at the outside of the knee: What's Causing It

★★★★★ 5.0 · 89 Google reviews

Pain on the outside of the knee feels like a sharp, burning ache or tight band on the outer edge of the joint that comes on partway through a run or walk, worst going downhill or downstairs. People notice it after more running mileage, cycling with a low saddle, or long beach walks on a slope. The vastus lateralis, the large outer quadriceps muscle that straightens the knee, is the most common local source. The tensor fasciae latae at the hip, which tightens the long outer band of the thigh, and the outer hamstring that attaches at the outer knee add ache along the same line.

Is it this, or something next to it?

A physician should first rule out a lateral meniscus tear or LCL sprain with locking or giving way after a twist, arthritis with swelling and warmth, and a stress fracture of the outer shin bone in runners. Muscle-referred pain here is tender along the outer thigh and hip rather than on the joint line itself, and eases with rest. Kneecap pain is central and worse after sitting, inside-of-knee pain is on the opposite side and may feel like buckling.

Typical story: a Sarasota runner training for a winter race increases mileage quickly and runs the sloped shoreline every morning facing the same way. A sharp burning ache appears on the outside of one knee a couple of miles in, worse running or walking downhill. The knee is not swollen or locking, and pressing the outer thigh and front-outer hip finds tender bands.

Muscles that commonly refer pain here

Ranked the way the MD Fit pain map ranks them, most common first:

  1. Vastus lateralis. Down the outer thigh to the outside of the knee.
  2. Tensor fasciae latae. Front-outer hip down the outside of the thigh, usually stopping at the knee. Worse with: sitting, running or sports, lying on it at night.
  3. Biceps femoris. Back of the knee on the outside and up the back of the thigh.

What people with this pain describe

Taken from Dr. Jared's notes on the tensor fasciae latae, the most common source here:

  • A sharp or aching pain at the front and/or outer part of the hip.
  • Pain that refers down the outside of the thigh, often mistaken for IT band syndrome.
  • Discomfort that worsens when lying on the affected side at night.
  • Pain when standing up after prolonged sitting.

Quick self-check

Education, not a diagnosis. The more of these that ring true, the more likely the top muscles are involved:

  • Is your pain located at the very front and side of your hip, right where a front pocket would be?
  • Does the pain travel down the outside of your thigh, but usually stops before reaching your knee?
  • Is it painful to lie directly on the affected hip at night?

How it usually starts

  • Prolonged sitting with hips flexed, which shortens the muscle.
  • Running or cycling with poor mechanics, causing the TFL to overwork.
  • Weak gluteus medius muscles, forcing the TFL to compensate for pelvic stability.
  • Sleeping in the fetal position, which keeps the muscle in a shortened state.

What it gets mistaken for

Trochanteric bursitis (hip bursitis).; IT band syndrome (though TFL is often the root cause).; Hip labral tear or femoroacetabular impingement (FAI).. An evaluation separates muscle pain from these, and anything that needs a physician is referred on.

What makes it worse, and what that tells you

  • Sitting. Points toward the tensor fasciae latae, biceps femoris.
  • Running or sports. Points toward the vastus lateralis, tensor fasciae latae, biceps femoris.
  • Lying on it at night. Points toward the vastus lateralis, tensor fasciae latae.

Warning signs: get medical care, not a stretch

  • Pain after a fall or impact with deformity, or you cannot bear weight or use the limb: get an X-ray.
  • A joint that is hot, red, swollen and painful, especially with fever: urgent care.
  • Numbness, pins and needles or weakness that is spreading or constant: see a physician.
  • Pain that is constant, worse at night and not changed by movement or position: see a physician.

Frequently asked questions

Why does it hurt at the outside of the knee if nothing is wrong there?

Muscles refer pain: a tight band in one muscle sends a predictable pattern of pain somewhere else. For pain at the outside of the knee, the usual sources are the vastus lateralis, tensor fasciae latae, biceps femoris.

How long does tensor fasciae latae pain usually take to settle?

You should notice less pain with daily activities within 1-2 weeks. Full resolution, especially for athletes, may take 4-6 weeks as you retrain movement patterns and build glute strength.

What makes it more likely to be the tensor fasciae latae?

It is worse with sitting, running or sports, lying on it at night, and pressing into the muscle reproduces the pain you know. Front-outer hip down the outside of the thigh, usually stopping at the knee.

When should I book instead of working on it myself?

Schedule a visit if you have a painful clicking in the hip, if the pain doesn't improve with 2 weeks of care, or if it significantly impacts your ability to walk or sleep.
