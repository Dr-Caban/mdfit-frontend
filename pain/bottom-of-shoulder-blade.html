Pain at the bottom tip of the shoulder blade: What's Causing It

★★★★★ 5.0 · 89 Google reviews

Pain at the bottom tip of the shoulder blade often feels like a deep, hard-to-reach ache right at the lowest point of the blade, sometimes with a sharp stitch when breathing deeply or reaching overhead. People notice it after pull-ups, swimming, paddling, or carrying heavy items at the side, and after a long coughing illness. The latissimus dorsi, the broad back muscle that pulls the arm down and back, attaches near this tip and commonly refers pain there. The serratus anterior, the muscle that wraps the side ribs and pulls the blade forward, and the iliocostalis along the outer back also send an ache to the lower blade.

Is it this, or something next to it?

A physician should first rule out heart, lung and gallbladder causes: lower-blade pain with chest pressure, shortness of breath, fever, or pain after fatty meals, particularly on the right, needs urgent attention. Muscle pain here is tender to press at the tip of the blade and reproduced by pulling the arm down against resistance or a deep breath. Between-the-blades pain is higher and beside the spine, inner-edge pain is along the upper blade, and rib-joint pain is sharp with twisting.

Typical story: a Sarasota kayaker increases weekend mileage and adds lat pulldowns at the gym, then carries heavy groceries in from the car. A deep ache lodges at the bottom tip of one shoulder blade that catches with a deep breath and when reaching into the back seat. Digestion is normal, breathing is fine, and the exact spot is tender when pressed.

Muscles that commonly refer pain here

Ranked the way the MD Fit pain map ranks them, most common first:

  1. Latissimus dorsi. Bottom tip of the shoulder blade and down the back of the arm to the ring and little fingers. Worse with: reaching overhead, gripping, lifting or typing.
  2. Serratus anterior. Side of the rib cage and the bottom tip of the shoulder blade.
  3. Iliocostalis. Low back and buttock from the lumbar part.

What people with this pain describe

Taken from Dr. Jared's notes on the latissimus dorsi, the most common source here:

  • A persistent, deep ache at the bottom angle of the shoulder blade.
  • Pain that travels down the back of the arm and forearm, sometimes reaching the ring and little fingers.
  • A feeling of stiffness or restriction when reaching overhead.
  • A dull ache in the mid-back, especially after pulling activities.

Quick self-check

Education, not a diagnosis. The more of these that ring true, the more likely the top muscles are involved:

  • Does the pain feel like a deep ache under your shoulder blade that refers down the back of your arm?
  • Do you feel a 'catch' or stiffness when you try to reach for something on a high shelf?
  • Have you noticed numbness or tingling in your ring and little finger along with this shoulder and back pain?

How it usually starts

  • Overuse from pulling exercises like pull-ups, rowing, or swimming.
  • Carrying heavy bags or backpacks consistently on one shoulder.
  • Prolonged sitting with poor posture, especially reaching forward for a computer mouse.
  • Vigorous activities like chopping wood or playing tennis.

What it gets mistaken for

Rotator cuff tear or tendinopathy; Cervical radiculopathy (pinched nerve in the neck); Thoracic outlet syndrome. An evaluation separates muscle pain from these, and anything that needs a physician is referred on.

What makes it worse, and what that tells you

  • Reaching overhead. Points toward the latissimus dorsi.
  • Gripping, lifting or typing. Points toward the latissimus dorsi.

Warning signs: get medical care, not a stretch

  • Pain after a fall or impact with deformity, or you cannot bear weight or use the limb: get an X-ray.
  • A joint that is hot, red, swollen and painful, especially with fever: urgent care.
  • Numbness, pins and needles or weakness that is spreading or constant: see a physician.
  • Pain that is constant, worse at night and not changed by movement or position: see a physician.

Frequently asked questions

Why does it hurt at the bottom tip of the shoulder blade if nothing is wrong there?

Muscles refer pain: a tight band in one muscle sends a predictable pattern of pain somewhere else. For pain at the bottom tip of the shoulder blade, the usual sources are the latissimus dorsi, serratus anterior, iliocostalis.

How long does latissimus dorsi pain usually take to settle?

You can expect relief from the sharpest pain within 1-2 weeks of stretching and release work. Building strength and resilience can take 4-8 weeks of consistent exercise.

What makes it more likely to be the latissimus dorsi?

It is worse with reaching overhead, gripping, lifting or typing, and pressing into the muscle reproduces the pain you know. Bottom tip of the shoulder blade and down the back of the arm to the ring and little fingers.

When should I book instead of working on it myself?

Schedule a visit if your overhead reach doesn't improve, if you have persistent numbness or tingling in your fingers, or if the pain significantly limits your daily activities after 2 weeks.
