Pain at the front of the elbow: What's Causing It

★★★★★ 5.0 · 89 Google reviews

Pain at the front of the elbow usually feels like a deep ache or a tight pulling sensation in the crease, with weakness when carrying a bag, curling a weight, or lifting a child. People notice it after heavy lifting, a day pulling weeds or hauling lines, or sleeping with the elbow bent. The biceps, the two-headed muscle that bends the elbow and turns the palm up, is the most common source, and tight bands in it refer ache down to the crease. The brachioradialis, the forearm muscle that lifts the hand when the thumb points up, adds ache at the outer front of the crease.

Is it this, or something next to it?

A physician should first rule out a biceps tendon tear, especially after a sudden pop with bruising and a change in arm shape, and a nerve problem if pain comes with tingling into the hand. Muscle pain here is tender to pinch in the biceps and reproduced by curling against resistance. Point-of-elbow pain is at the back, forearm pain runs down toward the wrist, and golfer's elbow sits on the bony inner knob rather than in the soft crease.

Typical story: a Sarasota boater spends a weekend hauling anchor and dock lines and lifting coolers, then starts a new arm day at the gym. A deep ache settles into the front of the elbow crease that pulls when carrying groceries or turning a stiff doorknob. There is no bruising or bulge in the arm, and pinching the biceps finds a sore band.

Muscles that commonly refer pain here

Ranked the way the MD Fit pain map ranks them, most common first:

  1. Biceps long head. Front of the shoulder over the biceps groove and down the front of the arm toward the elbow crease. Worse with: reaching overhead, gripping, lifting or typing.
  2. Biceps short head. Front of the shoulder near the collarbone tip, the inner front of the arm and the elbow crease. Worse with: gripping, lifting or typing.
  3. Brachioradialis. Outside of the elbow and the web of the thumb.

What people with this pain describe

Taken from Dr. Jared's notes on the biceps long head and the biceps short head, the two most common sources here:

  • Ache at the front of the shoulder over the biceps groove.
  • Pain down the front of the arm toward the elbow crease.
  • Pain lifting with the palm up, or reaching overhead.
  • A tender cord you can roll under your fingers at the front of the shoulder.
  • Ache at the front of the shoulder, closer to the chest than the groove.
  • Pain on the inner front of the upper arm.
  • Pain at the elbow crease when lifting or curling.

Quick self-check

Education, not a diagnosis. The more of these that ring true, the more likely the top muscles are involved:

  • Is there a tender spot in the groove at the front of the shoulder that moves when you rotate the arm?
  • Does lifting or carrying with the palm up hurt at the front of the shoulder?
  • Does the pain run down the front of the upper arm?
  • Is the tender spot on the inner half of the biceps, toward the body?
  • Does curling or carrying with the palm up hurt at the elbow crease?

How it usually starts

  • Repeated overhead work and throwing.
  • Heavy curls and carrying with the palms up.
  • Rotator cuff weakness so the biceps works as a stabilizer.
  • Rounded shoulders that pinch the tendon.
  • Heavy curls and chin-ups.
  • Carrying boxes with the palms up.

What it gets mistaken for

Biceps tendinopathy or SLAP tear; Infraspinatus trigger points; Anterior deltoid strain; Distal biceps tendinopathy; Coracobrachialis trigger points. An evaluation separates muscle pain from these, and anything that needs a physician is referred on.

What makes it worse, and what that tells you

  • Reaching overhead. Points toward the biceps long head.
  • Gripping, lifting or typing. Points toward the biceps long head, biceps short head, brachioradialis.

Warning signs: get medical care, not a stretch

  • Pain after a fall or impact with deformity, or you cannot bear weight or use the limb: get an X-ray.
  • A joint that is hot, red, swollen and painful, especially with fever: urgent care.
  • Numbness, pins and needles or weakness that is spreading or constant: see a physician.
  • Pain that is constant, worse at night and not changed by movement or position: see a physician.

Frequently asked questions

Why does it hurt at the front of the elbow if nothing is wrong there?

Muscles refer pain: a tight band in one muscle sends a predictable pattern of pain somewhere else. For pain at the front of the elbow, the usual sources are the biceps long head, biceps short head, brachioradialis.

How long does biceps long head pain usually take to settle?

Tendon-region pain usually eases over 2 to 4 weeks; full overhead and lifting tolerance in 6 to 8 weeks.

What makes it more likely to be the biceps long head?

It is worse with reaching overhead, gripping, lifting or typing, and pressing into the muscle reproduces the pain you know. Front of the shoulder over the biceps groove and down the front of the arm toward the elbow crease.

When should I book instead of working on it myself?

Book an evaluation if there is clicking or catching, a bulge in the arm, or no improvement in 3 weeks.
