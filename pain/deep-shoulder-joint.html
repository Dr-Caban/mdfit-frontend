Pain at the deep inside the shoulder joint: What's Causing It

★★★★★ 5.0 · 89 Google reviews

Pain deep inside the shoulder joint is usually described as an ache that cannot be pointed to, as if it sits in the middle of the ball and socket, sometimes with a sense of stiffness or a vague catch when reaching overhead or behind the back. People notice it reaching into a high cabinet, washing hair, or sleeping on that side. The infraspinatus and supraspinatus, the two rotator cuff muscles on the back of the shoulder blade that steer the ball in the socket, refer deep aching into the joint. The subscapularis, the rotator cuff muscle on the front of the blade, adds the stiff, frozen-shoulder quality.

Is it this, or something next to it?

A physician should first rule out a rotator cuff tear, labral injury, frozen shoulder, arthritis or infection, especially when the joint is hot, swollen or badly restricted after injury. Muscle-referred deep pain usually improves when the shoulder blade is supported and the tender spots on the blade are pressed. Front-of-shoulder pain is more surface and pinchy, outside-of-shoulder pain is over the deltoid, and under-collarbone pain sits high and inner. Joint problems often click, lock or lose motion.

Typical story: a Sarasota golfer with a new swing coach increases range time sharply, then adds overhead fishing casts on the weekend. A deep ache settles inside the shoulder joint that is hard to localize, worst reaching behind to tuck in a shirt and when lying on that side. The shoulder still moves fully, and pressing the tender back of the shoulder blade echoes the deep ache.

Muscles that commonly refer pain here

Ranked the way the MD Fit pain map ranks them, most common first:

  1. Infraspinatus. Deep in the front of the shoulder joint, down the front and outside of the arm.
  2. Supraspinatus. Deep ache at the top and side of the shoulder that can run down the outside of the arm to the elbow.
  3. Subscapularis. Back of the shoulder, down the back of the arm, sometimes a band around the wrist. Can feel like a frozen shoulder. Worse with: reaching overhead, reaching behind your back.
  4. Biceps long head. Front of the shoulder over the biceps groove and down the front of the arm toward the elbow crease.

What people with this pain describe

Taken from Dr. Jared's notes on the subscapularis, the most common source here:

  • Aching pain felt at the back of the shoulder and down the back of the upper arm.
  • A strange, band-like sensation or pain around the wrist, like a tight watchband.
  • Profound stiffness and inability to lift the arm, mimicking a 'frozen shoulder'.
  • Pain when reaching across the body or behind the back.

Quick self-check

Education, not a diagnosis. The more of these that ring true, the more likely the top muscles are involved:

  • Do you feel a strange band of pain or tightness around your wrist, even though the main problem is your shoulder?
  • Does your shoulder feel incredibly stiff, as if it's 'frozen', making it hard to lift your arm to the side?
  • Is the pain primarily at the back of your shoulder and down the back of your arm?

How it usually starts

  • Repetitive forceful internal rotation (e.g., swimming, throwing).
  • Falling on an outstretched arm.
  • Immobilization of the shoulder, such as after a fracture or surgery.
  • Chronic poor posture, which shortens the muscle.

What it gets mistaken for

Adhesive capsulitis (Frozen Shoulder); Cervical radiculopathy (C7/C8 nerve root); Long head of biceps tendon pathology. An evaluation separates muscle pain from these, and anything that needs a physician is referred on.

What makes it worse, and what that tells you

  • Reaching overhead. Points toward the infraspinatus, supraspinatus, subscapularis.
  • Reaching behind your back. Points toward the infraspinatus, subscapularis.

Warning signs: get medical care, not a stretch

  • Pain after a fall or impact with deformity, or you cannot bear weight or use the limb: get an X-ray.
  • A joint that is hot, red, swollen and painful, especially with fever: urgent care.
  • Numbness, pins and needles or weakness that is spreading or constant: see a physician.
  • Pain that is constant, worse at night and not changed by movement or position: see a physician.

Frequently asked questions

Why does it hurt at the deep inside the shoulder joint if nothing is wrong there?

Muscles refer pain: a tight band in one muscle sends a predictable pattern of pain somewhere else. For pain at the deep inside the shoulder joint, the usual sources are the infraspinatus, supraspinatus, subscapularis.

How long does subscapularis pain usually take to settle?

Due to its tendency to cause significant stiffness, subscapularis issues can be stubborn. Expect gradual improvement in mobility over 3-4 weeks, with strength returning over 6-12 weeks. Consistency is key.

What makes it more likely to be the subscapularis?

It is worse with reaching overhead, reaching behind your back, and pressing into the muscle reproduces the pain you know. Back of the shoulder, down the back of the arm, sometimes a band around the wrist. Can feel like a frozen shoulder.

When should I book instead of working on it myself?

If you have symptoms of a 'frozen shoulder' (severe, progressive loss of motion) or if you fail the 'Lift-Off Test', it's crucial to get a professional diagnosis to rule out a full tear or adhesive capsulitis.
