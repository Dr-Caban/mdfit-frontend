Pain at the side of the neck: What's Causing It

★★★★★ 5.0 · 89 Google reviews

Pain on the side of the neck usually feels like a tight cord or a deep ache running from under the jaw toward the collarbone, often with a sense that turning the head one way is stiff or catchy. It is noticed on waking, checking a blind spot while driving, or after a long day on the phone. The scalenes, the slender muscles from the side of the neck to the top ribs that lift the chest with each breath, are the most frequent source. The levator scapulae, the muscle that hoists the shoulder blade up toward the ear, and the strap-like SCM at the front also contribute.

Is it this, or something next to it?

A physician should first rule out serious causes: neck pain with fever, swollen glands that persist, difficulty swallowing, or pain spreading into the jaw and arm with chest pressure or shortness of breath needs urgent attention. Side-of-neck muscle pain is tender to press and often sends tingling toward the thumb side of the hand. Front-of-neck pain sits over the windpipe, back-of-neck pain is along the spine, and neck-meets-shoulder pain is lower at the angle of the shoulder.

Typical story: a Sarasota resident who spends hours on calls holding the phone against one shoulder, breathing shallowly through a stressful season, develops a steady ache down the side of the neck with occasional tingling into the hand. Turning the head feels stiff in the morning and loosens through the day, and the ache returns with long stretches of talking.

Muscles that commonly refer pain here

Ranked the way the MD Fit pain map ranks them, most common first:

  1. Scalenes. Chest, front and back of the upper arm, inner shoulder blade and down the forearm to the thumb and index finger. Can add tingling in the hand. Worse with: deep breaths or coughing, desk, phone or screens, stress, lying on it at night.
  2. Levator scapulae. The angle where the neck meets the shoulder, down the inner edge of the shoulder blade.
  3. Sternocleidomastoid. Forehead, around and behind the eye, cheek, ear and top of the head.
  4. Upper trapezius. Up the side of the neck, behind the ear and into the temple (a question-mark shape).

What people with this pain describe

Taken from Dr. Jared's notes on the scalenes, the most common source here:

  • Aching pain in the chest, sometimes mistaken for a heart issue.
  • Pain, numbness, or tingling that travels from the neck down the arm to the thumb and index finger.
  • A feeling of a 'dead arm' or waking up with a numb hand.
  • Aching pain between the shoulder blades.

Quick self-check

Education, not a diagnosis. The more of these that ring true, the more likely the top muscles are involved:

  • Do you feel pain or tingling that runs from your neck down your arm to your thumb and index finger?
  • Does your pain get worse when you carry something heavy, like a bag of groceries?
  • Do you ever feel a strange, non-cardiac pain in your chest, especially after a lot of coughing?

How it usually starts

  • Shallow, chest-breathing patterns, especially during stress or intense exercise.
  • Forward head posture from prolonged desk work or phone use ('tech neck').
  • Whiplash injury from a car accident or fall.
  • Chronic coughing from asthma, bronchitis, or allergies.

What it gets mistaken for

Cervical disc herniation (pinched nerve in the neck).; Thoracic Outlet Syndrome (TOS).; Rotator cuff injury.. An evaluation separates muscle pain from these, and anything that needs a physician is referred on.

What makes it worse, and what that tells you

  • Deep breaths or coughing. Points toward the scalenes, sternocleidomastoid.
  • Desk, phone or screens. Points toward the scalenes, levator scapulae, sternocleidomastoid.
  • Stress. Points toward the scalenes, levator scapulae, upper trapezius.
  • Lying on it at night. Points toward the scalenes, levator scapulae.

Warning signs: get medical care, not a stretch

  • Neck pain with fever and a stiff neck, or with confusion: call 911.
  • Neck pain after a car crash or fall, or with weakness, numbness or clumsiness in the arms or legs: get checked urgently.
  • Neck or arm pain with chest pressure, shortness of breath, sweating or nausea can be a heart attack: call 911.
  • Pain that is constant, worse at night, with unexplained weight loss: see a physician.

Frequently asked questions

Why does it hurt at the side of the neck if nothing is wrong there?

Muscles refer pain: a tight band in one muscle sends a predictable pattern of pain somewhere else. For pain at the side of the neck, the usual sources are the scalenes, levator scapulae, sternocleidomastoid.

How long does scalenes pain usually take to settle?

With consistent application of these steps, most people feel a noticeable improvement in 2-4 weeks. Full resolution may take 6-8 weeks.

What makes it more likely to be the scalenes?

It is worse with deep breaths or coughing, desk, phone or screens, stress, lying on it at night, and pressing into the muscle reproduces the pain you know. Chest, front and back of the upper arm, inner shoulder blade and down the forearm to the thumb and index finger. Can add tingling in the hand.

When should I book instead of working on it myself?

Book an evaluation if your symptoms include significant weakness, numbness that doesn't go away, or if the pain is not improving after 2 weeks of consistent self-care.
