Pain at the sole of the foot: What's Causing It

★★★★★ 5.0 · 89 Google reviews

Pain in the sole of the foot feels like a deep bruise or sharp stab under the heel or through the arch, worst with the first steps out of bed and again after sitting, easing as the foot warms up. People notice it after a jump in standing or walking, switching to flat sandals, or returning to exercise. The small intrinsic foot muscles, the layers of muscle under the arch that grip the ground and spring the foot forward, are the usual local source. The calf muscles, which pull on the heel through the Achilles, and the tibialis posterior refer ache into the arch and heel too.

Is it this, or something next to it?

A physician should first rule out a heel stress fracture, a true plantar fascia tear with sudden pain and bruising, tarsal tunnel syndrome with burning or tingling, gout or arthritis with a hot swollen joint,. Muscle-referred sole pain is tender deep in the arch and calf and eases as the foot warms up. Inner-ankle pain is up along the inside of the ankle, outer-ankle pain is on the outside bone, and front-of-ankle pain is on top near the big toe.

Typical story: a Sarasota resident starts a new job standing all day on tile, lives in flat flip-flops, and adds evening walks on the hard sidewalk. A deep bruised stab develops under one heel with the first steps every morning that eases after a few minutes, then returns after sitting through lunch. There is no swelling or heat, and pressing the arch and calf finds tender spots.

Muscles that commonly refer pain here

Ranked the way the MD Fit pain map ranks them, most common first:

  1. Foot muscles. Heel and sole of the foot; first-step-in-the-morning pain like plantar fasciitis. Worse with: standing or walking, running or sports.
  2. Gastrocnemius. Upper calf, back of the knee and the arch of the foot.
  3. Soleus. Heel and Achilles tendon, lower calf.
  4. Tibialis posterior. Inner ankle and arch, along the inner shin.

What people with this pain describe

Taken from Dr. Jared's notes on the foot muscles, the most common source here:

  • Sharp, stabbing pain in the heel or arch, especially with the first few steps in the morning.
  • Aching, cramping, or burning pain in the sole of the foot.
  • Pain that feels like you are walking on a pebble or a bruise.
  • General foot fatigue or aching after standing or walking for a while.

Quick self-check

Education, not a diagnosis. The more of these that ring true, the more likely the top muscles are involved:

  • Is your pain worst with the very first steps you take after getting out of bed or after a long period of sitting?
  • Does your pain feel like a deep ache or cramp in the arch of your foot?
  • Does walking barefoot on tile or hardwood floors make your foot pain significantly worse?

How it usually starts

  • Weakness in the small muscles of the foot, leading to poor arch support.
  • Consistently wearing unsupportive footwear like flip-flops, flat sandals, or worn-out shoes.
  • A sudden increase in weight-bearing activity like running, walking, or standing for a new job.
  • Tight calf muscles, which increases the strain on the bottom of the foot.

What it gets mistaken for

Plantar fasciitis (these two conditions are very closely related and often occur together); Tarsal tunnel syndrome (nerve entrapment); Heel pad atrophy (thinning of the fat pad under the heel). An evaluation separates muscle pain from these, and anything that needs a physician is referred on.

What makes it worse, and what that tells you

  • Standing or walking. Points toward the foot muscles, gastrocnemius, soleus.
  • Running or sports. Points toward the foot muscles, gastrocnemius, soleus.

Warning signs: get medical care, not a stretch

  • Pain after a fall or impact with deformity, or you cannot bear weight or use the limb: get an X-ray.
  • A joint that is hot, red, swollen and painful, especially with fever: urgent care.
  • Numbness, pins and needles or weakness that is spreading or constant: see a physician.
  • Pain that is constant, worse at night and not changed by movement or position: see a physician.

Frequently asked questions

Why does it hurt at the sole of the foot if nothing is wrong there?

Muscles refer pain: a tight band in one muscle sends a predictable pattern of pain somewhere else. For pain at the sole of the foot, the usual sources are the foot muscles, gastrocnemius, soleus.

How long does foot muscles pain usually take to settle?

Heel and arch pain can be persistent. With consistent daily exercises and better footwear choices, you should notice a gradual improvement in 4-6 weeks. Full recovery can often take 3-6 months.

What makes it more likely to be the foot muscles?

It is worse with standing or walking, running or sports, and pressing into the muscle reproduces the pain you know. Heel and sole of the foot; first-step-in-the-morning pain like plantar fasciitis.

When should I book instead of working on it myself?

Book an evaluation if your pain is severe, you have constant numbness or tingling, or if you see no improvement after 4 weeks of consistent self-care.
