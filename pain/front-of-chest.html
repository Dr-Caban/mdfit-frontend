Pain at the front of the chest: What's Causing It

★★★★★ 5.0 · 89 Google reviews

Pain in the front of the chest from muscle usually feels like a dull, aching soreness or tightness over the breastbone or upper pec, sometimes running down the inner arm, worse when pressing on it, reaching across the body or stretching. People notice it after push-ups or heavy lifting, hours hunched over a keyboard, or sleeping curled on one side. The pectoralis major, the fan-shaped chest muscle that pulls the arm forward and across, and the pectoralis minor, the small muscle beneath it that tips the shoulder blade forward, are the most common sources. The scalenes in the neck can add a deeper ache.

Is it this, or something next to it?

A physician must first rule out heart, lung and other chest emergencies: chest pain with pressure or squeezing, shortness of breath, sweating, nausea, pain spreading to the jaw or arm, or pain brought on by exertion is a 911 call, not a muscle problem. Muscle chest pain is reproduced by pressing on the chest wall and by stretching the arm back. Rib-side pain is lateral and breath-related, along-one-rib pain follows one rib, and abdominal-wall pain sits below the cage.

Typical story: a Sarasota resident restarts a gym routine after the summer away, does a heavy chest day, then spends the week driving and typing with rounded shoulders. A deep aching tightness settles over the front of the chest and reaches down the inner arm. A physician's check is clear, the chest wall is tender to press, and stretching the arm back reproduces the familiar ache.

Muscles that commonly refer pain here

Ranked the way the MD Fit pain map ranks them, most common first:

  1. Pectoralis major lower head. Front of the chest, down the inner arm to the inner elbow and the ring and little fingers. Chest pain must be checked by a doctor first. Worse with: desk, phone or screens, deep breaths or coughing, gripping, lifting or typing.
  2. Pectoralis major upper head. Front of the shoulder over the front delt, often mistaken for a front-shoulder joint problem. Worse with: reaching overhead, desk, phone or screens, gripping, lifting or typing.
  3. Pectoralis minor. Front of the shoulder and chest, down the inner arm to the last three fingers.
  4. Scalenes. Chest, front and back of the upper arm, inner shoulder blade and down the forearm to the thumb and index finger.
  5. Subclavius. Under the collarbone and down the front of the arm and forearm into the thumb, index and middle fingers.

What people with this pain describe

Taken from Dr. Jared's notes on the pectoralis major lower head and the pectoralis major upper head, the two most common sources here:

  • Ache across the front of the chest that can feel alarming.
  • Pain running down the inner arm to the inner elbow and sometimes the ring and little fingers.
  • Chest tightness that changes when you move the arm or press on the muscle.
  • Pain with bench pressing, push-ups or hugging motions.
  • Ache at the front of the shoulder over the front delt, often blamed on the shoulder joint.
  • Tight upper chest just below the collarbone.
  • Pain with incline pressing, push-ups or reaching across the body.

Quick self-check

Education, not a diagnosis. The more of these that ring true, the more likely the top muscles are involved:

  • Does pressing on the chest muscle reproduce the chest pain?
  • Does the pain change when you move your arm or press, rather than with walking or effort?
  • Does pain travel down the inner arm toward the ring and little fingers?
  • Is the pain at the front of the shoulder, but you can find tender spots in the upper chest just under the collarbone?
  • Does incline pressing or pushing up and forward bring it on?

How it usually starts

  • Heavy or high-volume pressing.
  • Rounded posture and long desk hours.
  • Shallow chest breathing and stress.
  • Carrying children, groceries or boxes in front of the chest.
  • Lots of pressing with little pulling.
  • Rounded shoulders from desk and phone posture.

What it gets mistaken for

Heart pain (angina): must be ruled out first; Costochondritis; Thoracic outlet syndrome; Anterior deltoid strain; Biceps tendinopathy. An evaluation separates muscle pain from these, and anything that needs a physician is referred on.

What makes it worse, and what that tells you

  • Desk, phone or screens. Points toward the pectoralis major lower head, pectoralis major upper head, pectoralis minor.
  • Deep breaths or coughing. Points toward the pectoralis major lower head, pectoralis minor, scalenes.
  • Gripping, lifting or typing. Points toward the pectoralis major lower head, pectoralis major upper head, subclavius.
  • Reaching overhead. Points toward the pectoralis major upper head, pectoralis minor, subclavius.

Warning signs: get medical care, not a stretch

  • Chest, upper-back or arm pain with pressure, shortness of breath, sweating, nausea or pain spreading to the jaw or arm can be a heart attack: call 911 now, do not stretch it out.
  • Sudden sharp chest pain with trouble breathing can be a lung problem: call 911.
  • Chest-wall pain after a fall or impact with trouble breathing: emergency care.
  • MD Fit treats chest-wall and rib muscle pain only after a physician has ruled out the heart and lungs.

Frequently asked questions

Why does it hurt at the front of the chest if nothing is wrong there?

Muscles refer pain: a tight band in one muscle sends a predictable pattern of pain somewhere else. For pain at the front of the chest, the usual sources are the pectoralis major lower head, pectoralis major upper head, pectoralis minor.

How long does pectoralis major lower head pain usually take to settle?

Muscle-driven chest pain usually eases within 1 to 2 weeks once the muscle is released and posture improves.

What makes it more likely to be the pectoralis major lower head?

It is worse with desk, phone or screens, deep breaths or coughing, gripping, lifting or typing, and pressing into the muscle reproduces the pain you know. Front of the chest, down the inner arm to the inner elbow and the ring and little fingers. Chest pain must be checked by a doctor first.

When should I book instead of working on it myself?

Any chest pain should be checked by a doctor first. Book with Dr. Jared once heart causes are ruled out, or if arm and hand symptoms persist.
