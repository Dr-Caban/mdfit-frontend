Pain under the collarbone: What's Causing It

★★★★★ 5.0 · 89 Google reviews

Pain under the collarbone usually feels like a deep, tender ache just below the bone, sometimes with a sense of fullness or tingling running down the front of the arm into the thumb and index finger. People notice it after a heavy bag strap, carrying a sleeping child, or hours with the shoulders rolled forward. The subclavius, a small muscle hidden under the collarbone that anchors it to the first rib, is the most common local source. The upper part of the pectoralis major, the chest muscle that pulls the arm forward, and the scalenes in the neck can add aching and tingling along the same path.

Is it this, or something next to it?

A physician should first rule out heart and lung problems: pain under the collarbone with chest pressure, shortness of breath, sweating or exertion needs urgent care, and swelling or color change in the arm needs prompt assessment. Muscle pain here is tender to press right under the bone and eases when the shoulder is pulled back. Front-of-shoulder pain is further out over the joint, outside-of-shoulder pain is over the deltoid, and deep joint pain feels inside with overhead reaching.

Typical story: a Sarasota parent carries a toddler on one hip and a heavy diaper bag on the opposite shoulder for months. A deep ache develops under the collarbone on the bag side, with occasional tingling into the thumb and index finger by evening. Pressing just beneath the collarbone reproduces the ache, and sleeping on that side makes the morning worse.

Muscles that commonly refer pain here

Ranked the way the MD Fit pain map ranks them, most common first:

  1. Subclavius. Under the collarbone and down the front of the arm and forearm into the thumb, index and middle fingers. Worse with: deep breaths or coughing, reaching overhead, gripping, lifting or typing.
  2. Pectoralis major upper head. Front of the shoulder over the front delt, often mistaken for a front-shoulder joint problem.
  3. Scalenes. Chest, front and back of the upper arm, inner shoulder blade and down the forearm to the thumb and index finger.
  4. Sternocleidomastoid. Forehead, around and behind the eye, cheek, ear and top of the head.

Quick self-check

Education, not a diagnosis. The more of these that ring true, the more likely the top muscles are involved:

  • Is there a tender spot just under the middle of your collarbone?
  • Does pain or tingling run down the front of the arm into the thumb and first two fingers?
  • Do heavy shoulder bags or backpack straps make it worse?

How it usually starts

  • Heavy shoulder bags and backpack straps.
  • Old collarbone fracture or shoulder injury.
  • Upper-chest breathing.
  • Rounded shoulders with the collarbone held down.

What it gets mistaken for

Thoracic outlet syndrome; Cervical radiculopathy (C6); Carpal tunnel syndrome. An evaluation separates muscle pain from these, and anything that needs a physician is referred on.

What makes it worse, and what that tells you

  • Deep breaths or coughing. Points toward the subclavius, scalenes, sternocleidomastoid.
  • Reaching overhead. Points toward the subclavius, pectoralis major upper head.
  • Gripping, lifting or typing. Points toward the subclavius, pectoralis major upper head.

Warning signs: get medical care, not a stretch

  • Chest, upper-back or arm pain with pressure, shortness of breath, sweating, nausea or pain spreading to the jaw or arm can be a heart attack: call 911 now, do not stretch it out.
  • Sudden sharp chest pain with trouble breathing can be a lung problem: call 911.
  • Chest-wall pain after a fall or impact with trouble breathing: emergency care.
  • MD Fit treats chest-wall and rib muscle pain only after a physician has ruled out the heart and lungs.

Frequently asked questions

Why does it hurt under the collarbone if nothing is wrong there?

Muscles refer pain: a tight band in one muscle sends a predictable pattern of pain somewhere else. For pain under the collarbone, the usual sources are the subclavius, pectoralis major upper head, scalenes.

How long does subclavius pain usually take to settle?

Collarbone and arm symptoms often ease in 1 to 3 weeks once loading from bags and posture is reduced.

What makes it more likely to be the subclavius?

It is worse with deep breaths or coughing, reaching overhead, gripping, lifting or typing, and pressing into the muscle reproduces the pain you know. Under the collarbone and down the front of the arm and forearm into the thumb, index and middle fingers.

When should I book instead of working on it myself?

Book an evaluation if tingling persists more than 2 weeks, or sooner if the hand becomes weak, swollen or discolored.
