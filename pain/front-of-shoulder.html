Pain at the front of the shoulder: What's Causing It

★★★★★ 5.0 · 89 Google reviews

Pain in the front of the shoulder commonly feels like a deep ache or a sharp pinch at the front of the joint, worse when reaching behind the back, putting on a jacket, or lying on that side at night. People notice it after an overhead project, a new lifting program, or sleeping with the arm under the pillow. The infraspinatus, the rotator cuff muscle on the back of the shoulder blade that rotates the arm outward, is a surprisingly frequent source of front-shoulder pain. The anterior deltoid and the long head of the biceps, which lift the arm forward and bend the elbow, add a more local ache.

Is it this, or something next to it?

A physician should first rule out a labral tear, fracture or dislocation after a fall, and heart or lung causes when pain comes with chest pressure or shortness of breath. Muscle-referred front-shoulder pain is often reproduced by pressing the back of the shoulder blade, while a biceps tendon problem is tender right in the groove. Under-collarbone pain sits higher and medial, outside-of-shoulder pain is over the deltoid, and deep joint pain feels inside with a catching or locking quality.

Typical story: a Sarasota resident repaints a lanai ceiling over a long weekend, arms overhead for hours, then sleeps on that side. A deep ache settles into the front of the shoulder that stabs when reaching back to grab a seatbelt or fasten a bra. Pressing on the back of the shoulder blade reproduces the front ache, and the shoulder is otherwise stable.

Muscles that commonly refer pain here

Ranked the way the MD Fit pain map ranks them, most common first:

  1. Infraspinatus. Deep in the front of the shoulder joint, down the front and outside of the arm. Hard to reach behind your back or sleep on that side. Worse with: reaching overhead, lying on it at night, reaching behind your back.
  2. Anterior deltoid. Front of the shoulder and down the front of the upper arm, close to the muscle itself. Often blamed on the joint. Worse with: reaching overhead, gripping, lifting or typing.
  3. Biceps long head. Front of the shoulder over the biceps groove and down the front of the arm toward the elbow crease.
  4. Pectoralis major upper head. Front of the shoulder over the front delt, often mistaken for a front-shoulder joint problem.
  5. Coracobrachialis. Front of the shoulder, the back of the upper arm and forearm, and the back of the hand toward the middle finger. Worse with: reaching overhead, reaching behind your back.
  6. Pectoralis minor. Front of the shoulder and chest, down the inner arm to the last three fingers.
  7. Biceps short head. Front of the shoulder near the collarbone tip, the inner front of the arm and the elbow crease.
  8. Scalenes. Chest, front and back of the upper arm, inner shoulder blade and down the forearm to the thumb and index finger.

What people with this pain describe

Taken from Dr. Jared's notes on the infraspinatus and the anterior deltoid, the two most common sources here:

  • A deep, aching pain felt in the front of the shoulder.
  • Pain that travels down the front and outside of the arm, sometimes to the hand.
  • Difficulty reaching behind your back, like to fasten a bra or grab a wallet.
  • Inability to sleep on the affected shoulder due to pain.
  • Ache at the front of the shoulder, right over the muscle, that people often assume is the joint.
  • Pain lifting the arm forward, pressing overhead or pushing a door open.
  • Tenderness you can press on just below the front of the collarbone tip.

Quick self-check

Education, not a diagnosis. The more of these that ring true, the more likely the top muscles are involved:

  • Is it painful to sleep on your affected shoulder?
  • Do you feel a deep ache in the FRONT of your shoulder, even though the muscle is on the back?
  • Is it difficult to reach behind your back, like tucking in a shirt or fastening a bra?
  • Is the pain at the very front of the shoulder, just below the bony tip of the collarbone?
  • Does lifting your straight arm forward to shoulder height bring it on?

How it usually starts

  • Repetitive overhead activities (e.g., throwing, swimming, painting).
  • Sudden trauma, like a fall onto an outstretched arm.
  • Poor posture, with rounded shoulders and a forward head position.
  • Sleeping on the same side every night, compressing the shoulder.
  • Heavy or frequent pressing (bench, push-ups, overhead press) without enough pulling work.
  • Rounded shoulders and a forward head posture.

What it gets mistaken for

Shoulder bursitis; Biceps tendonitis; Cervical radiculopathy (pinched nerve in the neck); Biceps tendinopathy (long head); Rotator cuff tendinopathy or tear. An evaluation separates muscle pain from these, and anything that needs a physician is referred on.

What makes it worse, and what that tells you

  • Reaching overhead. Points toward the infraspinatus, anterior deltoid, biceps long head.
  • Lying on it at night. Points toward the infraspinatus, scalenes.
  • Reaching behind your back. Points toward the infraspinatus, coracobrachialis.
  • Gripping, lifting or typing. Points toward the anterior deltoid, biceps long head, pectoralis major upper head.

Warning signs: get medical care, not a stretch

  • Pain after a fall or impact with deformity, or you cannot bear weight or use the limb: get an X-ray.
  • A joint that is hot, red, swollen and painful, especially with fever: urgent care.
  • Numbness, pins and needles or weakness that is spreading or constant: see a physician.
  • Pain that is constant, worse at night and not changed by movement or position: see a physician.

Frequently asked questions

Why does it hurt at the front of the shoulder if nothing is wrong there?

Muscles refer pain: a tight band in one muscle sends a predictable pattern of pain somewhere else. For pain at the front of the shoulder, the usual sources are the infraspinatus, anterior deltoid, biceps long head.

How long does infraspinatus pain usually take to settle?

With consistent adherence to the protocol, most people feel a significant reduction in pain within 2-4 weeks. Full strength and function may take 6-8 weeks to restore.

What makes it more likely to be the infraspinatus?

It is worse with reaching overhead, lying on it at night, reaching behind your back, and pressing into the muscle reproduces the pain you know. Deep in the front of the shoulder joint, down the front and outside of the arm. Hard to reach behind your back or sleep on that side.

When should I book instead of working on it myself?

If pain is severe, doesn't improve after 2 weeks of self-care, or if you suspect a tear from an injury, book an appointment for a full diagnostic evaluation.
