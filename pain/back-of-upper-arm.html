Pain at the back of the upper arm: What's Causing It

★★★★★ 5.0 · 89 Google reviews

Pain in the back of the upper arm usually feels like a deep, dull ache running from the back of the shoulder down toward the elbow, sometimes with a sense of heaviness or weakness when pushing up from a chair or reaching overhead. People notice it after push-ups, dips, a day of pruning, or hours leaning on an elbow at a desk. The triceps, the three-headed muscle that straightens the elbow when pushing, is the most frequent local source. The teres major and subscapularis, muscles on the shoulder blade that pull the arm down and rotate it inward, refer an ache down the same line.

Is it this, or something next to it?

A physician should first rule out a nerve problem when pain comes with numbness, tingling or weakness, a fracture or tendon rupture after a fall or heavy lift, and heart causes if arm pain arrives with chest pressure or shortness of breath. Muscle pain here is tender to pinch in the triceps and worse pushing against resistance. Back-of-shoulder pain stays up near the joint, shoulder-blade pain is on the flat bone itself, and neck-origin pain changes with head position.

Typical story: a Sarasota resident spends a weekend trimming palms with loppers, arms held up and pushing repeatedly, then leans on an elbow at a laptop all week. A deep ache develops down the back of the upper arm that burns when pushing out of a low beach chair or reaching to the top shelf. The hand has full feeling, and pinching the triceps finds a sore band.

Muscles that commonly refer pain here

Ranked the way the MD Fit pain map ranks them, most common first:

  1. Triceps long head. Back of the shoulder and arm, up toward the base of the neck, and down the back of the forearm. Worse with: reaching overhead, gripping, lifting or typing.
  2. Triceps lateral head. Back of the upper arm and the outside of the elbow, a common tennis-elbow look-alike, and the back of the forearm. Worse with: gripping, lifting or typing.
  3. Teres major. Back of the shoulder and the back of the upper arm, sometimes the back of the forearm. Hurts reaching up and forward. Worse with: reaching overhead, gripping, lifting or typing.
  4. Subscapularis. Back of the shoulder, down the back of the arm, sometimes a band around the wrist.
  5. Latissimus dorsi. Bottom tip of the shoulder blade and down the back of the arm to the ring and little fingers.
  6. Scalenes. Chest, front and back of the upper arm, inner shoulder blade and down the forearm to the thumb and index finger.
  7. Coracobrachialis. Front of the shoulder, the back of the upper arm and forearm, and the back of the hand toward the middle finger.

What people with this pain describe

Taken from Dr. Jared's notes on the triceps long head and the triceps lateral head, the two most common sources here:

  • Ache at the back of the shoulder and the back of the upper arm.
  • Pain sometimes spreading up toward the top of the shoulder and base of the neck.
  • Ache down the back of the forearm.
  • Pain straightening the arm overhead, such as reaching up or pressing overhead.
  • Ache in the back of the upper arm on the outer side.
  • Pain at the outside of the elbow that can be mistaken for tennis elbow.

Quick self-check

Education, not a diagnosis. The more of these that ring true, the more likely the top muscles are involved:

  • Does the pain run along the back of the upper arm from the armpit toward the elbow?
  • Does reaching overhead with the elbow bent (like scratching your upper back) hurt?
  • Does straightening your arm against resistance bring it on?
  • Is the tender spot on the outer back of the upper arm, a hand-width above the elbow?
  • Do you have outer-elbow pain but gripping is not the main trigger?

How it usually starts

  • Dips, push-ups, skull-crushers and overhead pressing.
  • Repetitive hammering or racquet sports.
  • Using crutches or a walker.
  • Long periods leaning on the elbows.
  • Push-ups, dips and bench pressing.
  • Hammering and tool work.

What it gets mistaken for

Teres major or minor trigger points; Posterior shoulder impingement; Cervical radiculopathy (C7); Lateral epicondylitis (tennis elbow); Radial tunnel syndrome. An evaluation separates muscle pain from these, and anything that needs a physician is referred on.

What makes it worse, and what that tells you

  • Reaching overhead. Points toward the triceps long head, teres major, subscapularis.
  • Gripping, lifting or typing. Points toward the triceps long head, triceps lateral head, teres major.

Warning signs: get medical care, not a stretch

  • Pain after a fall or impact with deformity, or you cannot bear weight or use the limb: get an X-ray.
  • A joint that is hot, red, swollen and painful, especially with fever: urgent care.
  • Numbness, pins and needles or weakness that is spreading or constant: see a physician.
  • Pain that is constant, worse at night and not changed by movement or position: see a physician.

Frequently asked questions

Why does it hurt at the back of the upper arm if nothing is wrong there?

Muscles refer pain: a tight band in one muscle sends a predictable pattern of pain somewhere else. For pain at the back of the upper arm, the usual sources are the triceps long head, triceps lateral head, teres major.

How long does triceps long head pain usually take to settle?

Usually improves within 2 to 3 weeks; overhead pressing tolerance over 4 to 6 weeks.

What makes it more likely to be the triceps long head?

It is worse with reaching overhead, gripping, lifting or typing, and pressing into the muscle reproduces the pain you know. Back of the shoulder and arm, up toward the base of the neck, and down the back of the forearm.

When should I book instead of working on it myself?

Book an evaluation if hand numbness, wrist weakness or elbow swelling appear, or pain persists past 3 weeks.
