Pain at the side of the ribs: What's Causing It

★★★★★ 5.0 · 89 Google reviews

Pain on the side of the ribs often feels like a persistent stitch or a sharp catch along the rib cage under the armpit, worse when breathing deeply, coughing, laughing or twisting to look behind. People notice it after a run, a coughing bout, a long paddle, or repeated reaching. The serratus anterior, the saw-toothed muscle that wraps the side of the ribs and pulls the shoulder blade forward with each reach or punch, is the most common source. The intercostals, the thin muscles between the ribs that work with every breath, and the latissimus dorsi along the side of the back can add to the ache.

Is it this, or something next to it?

A physician must first rule out heart, lung and abdominal emergencies: side-of-rib pain with shortness of breath, fever, cough with blood, a sudden sharp pain when breathing, or pain after a fall needs urgent assessment. Muscle pain here is tender to press between or over the ribs and reproduced by a deep breath plus a twist. Front-of-chest pain sits over the breastbone, along-one-rib pain follows a single rib line, and abdominal pain is below the cage and tied to digestion.

Typical story: a Sarasota paddleboarder or kayaker heads out for a long session after months off, twisting and reaching with every stroke. Over the next two days a stitch-like ache sets in along the side of the ribs that catches with every deep breath and when rolling over in bed. Breathing itself is fine, and pressing between the ribs finds a tender spot.

Muscles that commonly refer pain here

Ranked the way the MD Fit pain map ranks them, most common first:

  1. Serratus anterior. Side of the rib cage and the bottom tip of the shoulder blade; a side "stitch" that hurts with deep breaths. Worse with: deep breaths or coughing, running or sports.
  2. Intercostals. Sharp or aching pain along the rib, worse with deep breaths, coughing or twisting.
  3. Latissimus dorsi. Bottom tip of the shoulder blade and down the back of the arm to the ring and little fingers.

What people with this pain describe

Taken from Dr. Jared's notes on the serratus anterior, the most common source here:

  • A sharp, nagging ache on the side of your chest or ribs, often described as a 'side stitch' that won't go away.
  • Pain that feels like it's under the bottom tip of your shoulder blade.
  • Difficulty taking a full, deep breath without a sharp pain or 'catch'.
  • A feeling of weakness or instability when pushing a door or performing a push-up.

Quick self-check

Education, not a diagnosis. The more of these that ring true, the more likely the top muscles are involved:

  • Do you feel a sharp 'stitch' in your side when you try to take a deep breath or run?
  • Does the pain feel like it's located under the bottom tip of your shoulder blade?
  • Do you feel a 'catch' or sharp pain when you reach forward or push something, like a heavy door?

How it usually starts

  • A sudden increase in strenuous exercise, especially running, swimming, or weightlifting.
  • Persistent, hard coughing from asthma, bronchitis, or allergies.
  • Repetitive pushing movements, like in push-ups or bench press with poor form.
  • Poor posture with shoulders rounded forward for long periods.

What it gets mistaken for

Intercostal muscle strain; Rib fracture or stress fracture; Pleurisy (inflammation of the lung lining). An evaluation separates muscle pain from these, and anything that needs a physician is referred on.

What makes it worse, and what that tells you

  • Deep breaths or coughing. Points toward the serratus anterior, intercostals.
  • Running or sports. Points toward the serratus anterior.

Warning signs: get medical care, not a stretch

  • Chest, upper-back or arm pain with pressure, shortness of breath, sweating, nausea or pain spreading to the jaw or arm can be a heart attack: call 911 now, do not stretch it out.
  • Sudden sharp chest pain with trouble breathing can be a lung problem: call 911.
  • Chest-wall pain after a fall or impact with trouble breathing: emergency care.
  • MD Fit treats chest-wall and rib muscle pain only after a physician has ruled out the heart and lungs.

Frequently asked questions

Why does it hurt at the side of the ribs if nothing is wrong there?

Muscles refer pain: a tight band in one muscle sends a predictable pattern of pain somewhere else. For pain at the side of the ribs, the usual sources are the serratus anterior, intercostals, latissimus dorsi.

How long does serratus anterior pain usually take to settle?

With consistent gentle movement and strengthening, you should notice a significant decrease in pain within 2-3 weeks. Full recovery and return to strenuous activity may take 4-6 weeks.

What makes it more likely to be the serratus anterior?

It is worse with deep breaths or coughing, running or sports, and pressing into the muscle reproduces the pain you know. Side of the rib cage and the bottom tip of the shoulder blade; a side "stitch" that hurts with deep breaths.

When should I book instead of working on it myself?

Book an appointment if the pain is severe, doesn't improve after 1-2 weeks of self-care, or if you have any of the 'Red Flag' symptoms.
