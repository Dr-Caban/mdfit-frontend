Pain at the behind or around the eye: What's Causing It

★★★★★ 5.0 · 89 Google reviews

Pain behind or around the eye commonly feels like a deep, boring ache inside the socket or a pressure pushing the eye forward, and it is often labelled a migraine or sinus infection. It tends to appear after long periods of reading, driving or looking slightly upward at a monitor, and often starts at the base of the skull before travelling forward. The suboccipitals, the small muscles that steer the head on the top of the neck, are the usual source; the splenius muscles along the back of the neck and the SCM strap at the front also refer a deep ache into this spot.

Is it this, or something next to it?

A physician should first rule out head and eye emergencies: a sudden worst-ever headache, a red painful eye with blurred vision or halos, double vision, or pain with fever and stiff neck all need urgent care. Muscle-referred eye pain is usually reproduced by pressing at the base of the skull and eases with gentle neck movement. Forehead pain is broader and more superficial, temple pain is pulsing and lateral, and ear-area pain sits behind rather than in front.

Typical story: a Sarasota resident finishes a long book or binge-reads on a tablet propped low in the lap, chin tucked for hours. Later that evening a deep ache settles behind one eye along with a stiff upper neck. Eyes test fine, there is no congestion, and the pain fades after a night of good sleep but returns with the next long reading session.

Muscles that commonly refer pain here

Ranked the way the MD Fit pain map ranks them, most common first:

  1. Suboccipitals. A band of headache from the base of the skull wrapping to behind the eye and the forehead. Often mistaken for migraine. Worse with: desk, phone or screens, stress, turning your head or body.
  2. Sternocleidomastoid. Forehead, around and behind the eye, cheek, ear and top of the head.
  3. Splenius capitis & cervicis. Pain on the top of the head, and deep ache inside the head behind the eye.
  4. Occipitalis. Ache at the back of the head that can shoot through the skull to behind the eye, with scalp tenderness.

What people with this pain describe

Taken from Dr. Jared's notes on the suboccipitals, the most common source here:

  • A headache that feels like a tight band starting at the base of your skull.
  • Pain that wraps forward to behind your eye or into your forehead.
  • The headache can feel deep, achy, and persistent, sometimes mistaken for a migraine.
  • Stiffness and reduced ability to nod or tuck your chin.

Quick self-check

Education, not a diagnosis. The more of these that ring true, the more likely the top muscles are involved:

  • Does your headache feel like a tight band wrapping from the back of your head to behind your eye?
  • Is the pain worse when you spend a long time looking down at a screen or book?
  • Do you feel stiffness mostly when trying to tuck your chin to your chest?

How it usually starts

  • Forward head posture from prolonged screen time ('tech neck').
  • Sleeping with too many pillows or on your stomach.
  • Whiplash injury from a car accident or fall.
  • Clenching your jaw or grinding your teeth (bruxism).

What it gets mistaken for

Migraine headache; Tension-type headache; Cervicogenic headache (from upper neck joints). An evaluation separates muscle pain from these, and anything that needs a physician is referred on.

What makes it worse, and what that tells you

  • Desk, phone or screens. Points toward the suboccipitals, sternocleidomastoid, splenius capitis & cervicis.
  • Stress. Points toward the suboccipitals, occipitalis.
  • Turning your head or body. Points toward the suboccipitals, sternocleidomastoid, splenius capitis & cervicis.

Warning signs: get medical care, not a stretch

  • A sudden, severe headache unlike any you have had, or the worst headache of your life: call 911.
  • Headache with fever and a stiff neck, confusion, slurred speech, weakness or numbness on one side, or loss of vision: call 911.
  • Head pain after a fall or blow to the head, especially with vomiting or drowsiness: emergency care.
  • Jaw or face pain with chest pressure, shortness of breath or sweating can be a heart attack: call 911.

Frequently asked questions

Why does it hurt at the behind or around the eye if nothing is wrong there?

Muscles refer pain: a tight band in one muscle sends a predictable pattern of pain somewhere else. For pain at the behind or around the eye, the usual sources are the suboccipitals, sternocleidomastoid, splenius capitis & cervicis.

How long does suboccipitals pain usually take to settle?

With consistent exercises and habit changes, most people feel a noticeable reduction in headache frequency and intensity within 2-4 weeks.

What makes it more likely to be the suboccipitals?

It is worse with desk, phone or screens, stress, turning your head or body, and pressing into the muscle reproduces the pain you know. A band of headache from the base of the skull wrapping to behind the eye and the forehead. Often mistaken for migraine.

If your headaches are severe, worsening, or accompanied by dizziness, numbness, or weakness, please book an appointment for a full evaluation.
