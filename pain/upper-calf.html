Pain at the upper calf: What's Causing It

★★★★★ 5.0 · 89 Google reviews

Pain in the upper calf feels like a deep, cramp-like tightness in the thick muscle below the back of the knee, sometimes with night cramps or a sense that the calf will seize when pointing the toes. People notice it on the first steps in the morning, pushing off on a brisk walk, or after a day in flat sandals or heels. The gastrocnemius, the large calf muscle that crosses the back of the knee and powers every push-off, is the main source and refers ache into the back of the knee and arch. The gluteus minimus at the hip can refer ache down the calf.

Is it this, or something next to it?

A physician should first rule out a deep vein clot, especially with calf swelling, warmth, redness or recent travel, a muscle tear with bruising after a sudden pop, and arterial problems causing cramping that starts after a set walking distance. Muscle upper-calf pain is tender to pinch in the muscle belly and reproduced by rising onto the toes with a straight knee. Lower-calf pain sits down near the Achilles, and outer-calf pain runs along the outside of the shin.

Typical story: a Sarasota resident swaps supportive shoes for flat flip-flops for the whole season and starts long walks along the bay. A deep cramping tightness sets into the upper calf that wakes them with a night cramp and tightens with the first steps each morning. There is no swelling, warmth or recent long travel, and pinching the calf muscle finds a tender band.

Muscles that commonly refer pain here

Ranked the way the MD Fit pain map ranks them, most common first:

  1. Gastrocnemius. Upper calf, back of the knee and the arch of the foot.
  2. Gluteus minimus. Outer hip down the side of the thigh and leg to the ankle, or down the back of the thigh and calf.
  3. Plantaris. The back of the knee and the upper inner calf; a sudden sharp pain here is sometimes called tennis leg. Worse with: running or sports, stairs, squats or hills.

What people with this pain describe

Taken from Dr. Jared's notes on the plantaris, the most common source here:

  • A sudden sharp pain at the back of the knee or upper inner calf during activity, often described as feeling like being kicked.
  • Aching at the back of the knee.
  • Bruising appearing in the calf over the following days.
  • Pain on pushing off or rising onto the toes.

Quick self-check

Education, not a diagnosis. The more of these that ring true, the more likely the top muscles are involved:

  • Did you feel a sudden sharp pain in your calf, like being kicked or struck?
  • Did bruising appear in your calf over the following days?
  • Is the pain at the back of the knee or the upper inner calf?

How it usually starts

  • A sudden push-off or lunge in racquet sports, which is why it is called tennis leg.
  • Sprinting without adequate warm-up.
  • A sudden increase in hill running.
  • Landing awkwardly from a jump.

What it gets mistaken for

Medial gastrocnemius tear, which is more common and presents similarly.; Deep vein thrombosis, which must be excluded.; Baker's cyst, which can rupture and cause calf pain.. An evaluation separates muscle pain from these, and anything that needs a physician is referred on.

What makes it worse, and what that tells you

  • Running or sports. Points toward the gastrocnemius, plantaris.
  • Stairs, squats or hills. Points toward the gastrocnemius, plantaris.

Warning signs: get medical care, not a stretch

  • A swollen, warm, red or tender calf, especially after travel, surgery or bed rest, can be a blood clot: call 911 or go to the ER.
  • Sudden severe calf pain with a pop, and you cannot push off the foot: urgent care.
  • Leg pain with numbness in the groin or loss of bladder or bowel control: call 911.
  • Coldness, paleness or no pulse in the foot: emergency care.

Frequently asked questions

Why does it hurt at the upper calf if nothing is wrong there?

Muscles refer pain: a tight band in one muscle sends a predictable pattern of pain somewhere else. For pain at the upper calf, the usual sources are the gastrocnemius, gluteus minimus, plantaris.

How long does plantaris pain usually take to settle?

Calf tears involving this muscle usually settle in four to six weeks with graded loading. Returning to sprinting too early is the most common reason for recurrence.

What makes it more likely to be the plantaris?

It is worse with running or sports, stairs, squats or hills, and pressing into the muscle reproduces the pain you know. The back of the knee and the upper inner calf; a sudden sharp pain here is sometimes called tennis leg.

When should I book instead of working on it myself?

Calf swelling with warmth, redness or tenderness needs urgent assessment to exclude a clot. Inability to rise onto the toes needs same-day assessment for an Achilles rupture.
