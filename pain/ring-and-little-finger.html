Pain at the ring and little finger: What's Causing It

★★★★★ 5.0 · 89 Google reviews

Pain in the ring and little finger typically feels like an ache, tingling or numb heaviness along the outer edge of the hand, sometimes running up the inner arm, and it often appears after sitting with rounded shoulders or sleeping with the arm overhead. People notice it after long typing sessions, leaning on the elbows, or cycling. The pectoralis minor, the small chest muscle that tips the shoulder forward and sits over the nerves and vessels to the arm, and the latissimus dorsi, the broad back muscle under the armpit, refer pain to these two fingers. The subscapularis and the lower pectoralis major also send ache down the inner arm.

Is it this, or something next to it?

A physician should first rule out ulnar nerve entrapment at the elbow or wrist, a neck nerve problem, and heart causes when inner arm and finger pain comes with chest pressure, shortness of breath or sweating. Muscle-referred pain here shifts with shoulder posture and is reproduced by pressing under the collarbone or in the armpit. Thumb and index finger pain is on the opposite edge, palm-side pain is in the heel of the hand, back-of-wrist pain is on top.

Typical story: a Sarasota resident spends weeks hunched over a laptop at a cafe with the shoulders rolled forward, then sleeps with the arm tucked overhead. The ring and little finger ache and tingle by late afternoon and on waking, easing when the chest is stretched. Sensation tests normal, and pressing the small chest muscle under the collarbone echoes the fingers.

Muscles that commonly refer pain here

Ranked the way the MD Fit pain map ranks them, most common first:

  1. Pectoralis minor. Front of the shoulder and chest, down the inner arm to the last three fingers. Can pinch nerves to the hand. Worse with: desk, phone or screens, deep breaths or coughing, reaching overhead.
  2. Pectoralis major lower head. Front of the chest, down the inner arm to the inner elbow and the ring and little fingers.
  3. Latissimus dorsi. Bottom tip of the shoulder blade and down the back of the arm to the ring and little fingers.
  4. Subscapularis. Back of the shoulder, down the back of the arm, sometimes a band around the wrist.
  5. Triceps medial head. The point of the elbow and the inner elbow, a golfer's-elbow look-alike, sometimes the ring and little fingers.

What people with this pain describe

Taken from Dr. Jared's notes on the pectoralis minor, the most common source here:

  • Sharp pain at the very front of the shoulder, under the collarbone.
  • Numbness, tingling, or weakness in the arm and hand, especially the last three fingers.
  • Pain that worsens when reaching overhead or backward.
  • A feeling of the arm 'going to sleep' easily, especially when sleeping or carrying a backpack.

Quick self-check

Education, not a diagnosis. The more of these that ring true, the more likely the top muscles are involved:

  • Do you get numbness or tingling down your arm and into your hand when you carry a backpack or heavy purse?
  • Is your pain located in a specific spot at the very front of your shoulder, just below the collarbone?
  • Does reaching straight up, like to a high shelf, cause a sharp, pinching pain in the front of your shoulder?

How it usually starts

  • Chronic slouching posture with rounded shoulders (desk work, phone use).
  • Repetitive overhead reaching, especially with poor mechanics.
  • Using a computer mouse that is too far away, causing a constant forward reach.
  • Heavy breathing from conditions like asthma or after intense cardio.

What it gets mistaken for

Thoracic outlet syndrome (Pec Minor is a common cause of this); Shoulder impingement syndrome; Biceps tendonitis. An evaluation separates muscle pain from these, and anything that needs a physician is referred on.

What makes it worse, and what that tells you

  • Desk, phone or screens. Points toward the pectoralis minor, pectoralis major lower head.
  • Deep breaths or coughing. Points toward the pectoralis minor, pectoralis major lower head.
  • Reaching overhead. Points toward the pectoralis minor, latissimus dorsi, subscapularis.

Warning signs: get medical care, not a stretch

  • Numbness or weakness in the hand that is spreading or constant, or dropping things: see a physician for nerve testing.
  • A cold, pale or blue hand, or sudden severe pain: emergency care.
  • Hand pain with chest pressure, shortness of breath or sweating can be a heart attack: call 911.
  • Swelling, redness and fever in a joint: urgent care.

Frequently asked questions

Why does it hurt at the ring and little finger if nothing is wrong there?

Muscles refer pain: a tight band in one muscle sends a predictable pattern of pain somewhere else. For pain at the ring and little finger, the usual sources are the pectoralis minor, pectoralis major lower head, latissimus dorsi.

How long does pectoralis minor pain usually take to settle?

Relief from nerve-like symptoms can occur within a week of consistent release and stretching. Lasting postural changes and strength balance will likely take 6-8 weeks of dedicated effort.

What makes it more likely to be the pectoralis minor?

It is worse with desk, phone or screens, deep breaths or coughing, reaching overhead, and pressing into the muscle reproduces the pain you know. Front of the shoulder and chest, down the inner arm to the last three fingers. Can pinch nerves to the hand.

When should I book instead of working on it myself?

Book an evaluation if you have persistent or worsening numbness, tingling, weakness, or color changes in your hand, or if your pain doesn't improve after 3 weeks.
