Pain at the outer ankle: What's Causing It

★★★★★ 5.0 · 89 Google reviews

Pain at the outer ankle feels like a dull ache or sharp twinge around and below the outer ankle bone, sometimes with a sense that the ankle will give way on uneven ground, noticed stepping off a curb, walking on soft sand or gravel, or after long standing. The peroneals, the muscles along the outer lower leg whose tendons wrap behind the ankle bone and stop the foot rolling in, are the main source and often stay tight and tender long after a sprain has healed. The gluteus minimus at the hip can refer ache all the way down to the ankle.

Is it this, or something next to it?

A physician should first rule out a fracture or ligament tear after a sprain, especially with swelling, bruising or inability to bear weight, peroneal tendon tears or dislocation with snapping, arthritis, and a lumbar nerve problem with numbness. Muscle outer-ankle pain is tender along the outer calf muscles above the bone and reproduced by turning the foot outward against resistance. Inner-ankle pain is on the opposite side, front-of-ankle pain is on top, and sole pain is underneath.

Typical story: a Sarasota resident sprains an ankle on a shell-covered beach path, rests for a week, then goes straight back to long walks and standing on a rocking boat. A lingering ache sits around the outer ankle bone with a feeling that it could roll again. Swelling has resolved and weight bearing is fine, and pressing the muscles along the outer shin reproduces the ankle ache.

Muscles that commonly refer pain here

Ranked the way the MD Fit pain map ranks them, most common first:

  1. Peroneals. Outer ankle and outside of the foot; common after ankle sprains. Worse with: running or sports, standing or walking.
  2. Gluteus minimus. Outer hip down the side of the thigh and leg to the ankle, or down the back of the thigh and calf.

What people with this pain describe

Taken from Dr. Jared's notes on the peroneals, the most common source here:

  • Aching or sharp pain located behind or below the outer ankle bone.
  • Pain that runs along the outside edge of the foot toward the little toe.
  • A feeling of ankle instability, weakness, or that it might 'give way'.
  • Swelling and tenderness on the outside of the ankle.

Quick self-check

Education, not a diagnosis. The more of these that ring true, the more likely the top muscles are involved:

  • Is your pain mainly located behind or just below your outer ankle bone?
  • Do you have a history of 'rolling' your ankle (ankle sprains) on this side?
  • Does your ankle feel unstable, like it could easily give out from under you?

How it usually starts

  • Acute ankle sprains, which stretch or damage the peroneal tendons.
  • Chronic ankle instability from repeated sprains.
  • Overuse from running on banked surfaces (like the edge of a road) or uneven trails.
  • Having high-arched feet, which places more stress on the outside of the foot.

What it gets mistaken for

Lateral ankle ligament sprain; Fracture of the 5th metatarsal (base of the little toe); Sural nerve irritation. An evaluation separates muscle pain from these, and anything that needs a physician is referred on.

What makes it worse, and what that tells you

  • Running or sports. Points toward the peroneals.
  • Standing or walking. Points toward the peroneals, gluteus minimus.

Warning signs: get medical care, not a stretch

  • Pain after a fall or impact with deformity, or you cannot bear weight or use the limb: get an X-ray.
  • A joint that is hot, red, swollen and painful, especially with fever: urgent care.
  • Numbness, pins and needles or weakness that is spreading or constant: see a physician.
  • Pain that is constant, worse at night and not changed by movement or position: see a physician.

Frequently asked questions

Why does it hurt at the outer ankle if nothing is wrong there?

Muscles refer pain: a tight band in one muscle sends a predictable pattern of pain somewhere else. For pain at the outer ankle, the usual sources are the peroneals, gluteus minimus.

How long does peroneals pain usually take to settle?

Following an acute injury, it can take 4-8 weeks to regain stability and strength. For chronic tendon pain, consistent rehab for 6-12 weeks is often needed for significant improvement.

What makes it more likely to be the peroneals?

It is worse with running or sports, standing or walking, and pressing into the muscle reproduces the pain you know. Outer ankle and outside of the foot; common after ankle sprains.

When should I book instead of working on it myself?

Book an evaluation if you have persistent ankle instability, a painful 'snapping' tendon, or if you are unable to return to your activities without pain after 4-6 weeks of self-care.
