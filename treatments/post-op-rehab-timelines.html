How to read these timelines

The ranges below are typical, not promises. Your surgeon's protocol always comes first, and the size of the repair, your age, your health before surgery and how much work you can do between visits all move the dates. Dr. Jared uses the timeline as a map: it tells us which phase you are in and what has to be true before you move to the next one. Progression is earned by meeting criteria such as range of motion, swelling control and strength relative to the other side, not by the calendar alone.

Knee replacement (TKA)

Weeks 0–2: pain control, range of motion, swelling management. Weeks 2–6: walking distance, stair training, early strength. Weeks 6–12: full strength, balance, return to activity. With a consistent plan, walking groups, golf and pickleball are often realistic goals by month three. More on knee replacement rehab.

Hip replacement (THA)

Weeks 0–4: safe mobility and transfers, early glute activation. Weeks 4–8: strength, gait, stairs. Weeks 8–16: return to activity. Most clients are back to full daily life and recreation by around month four. More on hip replacement rehab.

Rotator cuff repair

Phase 1 (weeks 0–6): sling, passive range, posture resets. Phase 2 (weeks 6–12): active range, scapular control. Phase 3 (months 3–6): progressive strengthening. Phase 4 (months 6+): return to overhead work and sport. Timelines depend on the size of the repair. More on rotator cuff repair rehab.

ACL reconstruction

Months 0–3: range, early strength, light cardio. Months 3–6: strength base, running progression, change of direction. Months 6–9: sport-specific training. Return-to-sport testing before full competition. More on ACL reconstruction recovery.

Spine procedures (laminectomy, fusion, microdiscectomy)

Weeks 0–6: walking program and gentle mobility, strict adherence to surgeon precautions. Weeks 6–12: core and hip strength, gradual loading. Months 3–6: return to lifting, work and recreation.

Before surgery, if there is time

A visit or two before a planned joint replacement or ACL reconstruction often makes the first weeks afterward easier. You learn the early exercises while you are still comfortable, build some strength in the surrounding muscles such as the vastus medialis for a knee or the gluteus medius for a hip.

What a rehab visit looks like

  1. Review. Pain, swelling, sleep and how the home exercises went, so the session matches where you actually are this week.
  2. Measure. Range of motion and, later, strength and balance compared with the other side.
  3. Hands-on. Soft-tissue work on the muscles that are guarding, and gentle joint work within your surgeon's precautions.
  4. Exercise. The day's progression, practiced until you can do it correctly without supervision.
  5. Update the app. Your routine in the free MD Fit app is adjusted so the homework always reflects the current phase.

Between visits

Home work is where most of the recovery happens. Early on it is often short and frequent: a few minutes of range-of-motion work several times a day. Later it becomes a proper strength session two or three times a week. The app videos show the exact variation you were given.

When to call your surgeon instead

A calf that becomes swollen, warm or painful; a fever; redness or drainage at the incision; sudden loss of motion or a joint that gives way; chest pain or shortness of breath. These are not rehab questions. Call your surgeon's office or seek urgent care, and let us know so your plan can pause until you are cleared.

Frequently asked questions

Do you take insurance?

No. MD Fit is self-pay, so there is no referral, no limit on visits set by a plan, and every session is one-on-one with Dr. Jared for the full time. You receive an itemized receipt at each visit; some clients submit it to their plan or HSA/FSA themselves. Check with your plan.

When do I start?

As soon as your surgeon clears you. Bring your post-op instructions to the first visit.

How often will I come?

Usually two visits a week early on, tapering to once a week as you progress, with home work between visits.

Do you communicate with my surgeon?

Yes, with your permission. Many surgeons in Sarasota and Bradenton know Dr. Jared and his approach.

I had surgery months ago and still feel behind. Is it too late?

No. Stiffness, weakness and limping that linger after a formal rehab course ends are common reasons people come to MD Fit. The evaluation measures where you are now and the plan picks up from there.

Can I do this alongside physical therapy?

Yes, with your PT and surgeon aware. Some clients use MD Fit for additional hands-on work or to continue strengthening once their insurance-covered visits run out.
