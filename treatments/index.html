How the services fit together

Most people come to MD Fit with a symptom, not a service in mind: a back that will not settle, a knee three months after surgery, a shoulder that stops the golf swing short. The treatments listed above are not separate programs you have to choose between. They are phases and tools that Dr. Jared combines depending on what the evaluation shows, usually in roughly this order.

  1. Find the source. A 90-minute evaluation ($300) that tests how you move and which muscles reproduce your pain.
  2. Calm it down. Hands-on soft-tissue release, the same technique used in medical massage, on the muscles found.
  3. Restore motion. Functional mobility work so the joint can move through the range daily life asks of it.
  4. Practice at home. Short video routines in the free MD Fit app between follow-up visits ($150).
  5. Build capacity. Strength and performance training so the problem has less reason to return.

Where to start

If you are not sure which page describes you, the free phone consult is the simplest first step; Dr. Jared will tell you whether an evaluation, a massage or a call to your physician makes the most sense. A few shortcuts:

  • You know which muscles are tight and want hands-on work without a full workup: book medical massage directly ($250 for 60 minutes, $300 for 90).
  • You have surgery scheduled or recently behind you: read the post-op timelines, then book an evaluation with your surgeon's protocol in hand.
  • Pain has lasted more than three months and has outlived several other treatments: the chronic pain program is built for that history.
  • A heart, metabolic, lung or neurological condition makes exercise feel risky: clinical exercise physiology starts conservatively and coordinates with your physician.
  • You feel fine but move stiffly, or you want to train seriously: functional mobility and strength and performance can be booked as stand-alone coaching.
