What medical massage is, and what it is not

Medical massage is clinical soft-tissue work aimed at a problem. Dr. Jared is looking for the specific muscles and connective tissue that reproduce your pain or limit your motion, then releasing them with sustained pressure, friction and movement-assisted technique. It is not a relaxation massage, and it is not full-body by default. If your neck and the top of your shoulders are the problem, that is where most of the hour goes. You will often be asked to breathe, turn your head or lift an arm while he works so the tissue lets go in the position you actually use.

It is also not a diagnosis. Finding a tender, tight upper trapezius or quadratus lumborum tells us where the tissue is unhappy; it does not tell us why your pain started. When the why matters, Dr. Jared will say so and point you toward an evaluation or your physician.

Who books it

  • Desk workers with a familiar band of tension from the base of the skull to the top of the shoulder.
  • Runners, lifters and tennis or pickleball players whose calves, hips or forearms stay tight between hard weeks.
  • People with a long-standing stiff spot in the low back or outside of the hip that stretching never quite reaches.
  • Clients already in a rehab plan who want extra hands-on time between follow-up visits.
  • Anyone with muscle guarding after an injury or surgery, once the surgeon has cleared soft-tissue work.

How a session runs

  1. Intake. You complete a short form when you book: where it hurts, what makes it worse, and any surgeries, medications or medical conditions we should know about.
  2. A few minutes of testing. Dr. Jared watches you move and presses on the likely muscles to find the ones that reproduce your symptoms, so the work goes where it counts.
  3. Hands-on work. Deep, specific pressure on those tissues, often while you move the joint through its range. You stay in control of the pressure at every point.
  4. Resets. Toward the end you repeat one or two movements that were restricted so your nervous system registers the new range.
  5. Homework. One to three short videos are added to your free MD Fit app so you can keep the tissue moving at home.

What to expect afterward

Most people feel looser and move more freely when they get off the table. Some soreness the next day, similar to the feeling after a new workout, is common and usually fades within a day or two. Drinking water, walking and doing your app homework the same evening often help the change hold. If an area feels sharper rather than simply tender, let us know; that is useful information for the next visit.

How it fits with the rest of MD Fit

Massage on its own can quiet a muscle; it rarely changes the habit that overloaded it. That is why every session ends with homework, and why Dr. Jared may suggest pairing massage with functional mobility work or a strength plan when the same spot keeps coming back. If your pain is spreading, includes numbness or weakness, follows a fall, or has not changed after a few sessions, he will recommend a full evaluation ($300, about 90 minutes) or a visit with your physician rather than more massage.

Frequently asked questions

Do you take insurance?

No. MD Fit is self-pay, which is what lets every session be a full hour or more with Dr. Jared himself and with no visit caps or pre-approvals. You receive an itemized receipt; some clients submit it to their plan or HSA/FSA themselves. Check with your plan.

Can I book without an evaluation?

Yes. The intake form captures what we need to make the session safe and useful.

How often should I come?

Many people start weekly or every two weeks for the first month, then taper to once a month for maintenance.

What should I wear?

Comfortable clothing you can move in. Dr. Jared works through clothing or with draping, whichever lets him reach the tissue and keeps you comfortable. Shorts and a tank top make hips, legs and shoulders easier to treat.

Is deep pressure required?

No. The goal is a muscle that releases, not a session you have to brace through. Pressure is adjusted to what your tissue responds to, and you can ask for more or less at any point.
