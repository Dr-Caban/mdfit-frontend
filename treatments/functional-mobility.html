Mobility vs. flexibility

Flexibility is how far a joint can be moved by someone else, or by gravity in a stretch. Mobility is how far you can move it yourself, with control, and ideally under some load. Clients who can touch their toes but cannot squat below parallel have flexibility without mobility. So does the golfer who can be pulled into a big backswing on the table but cannot get there on the tee. The MD Fit approach builds range you can actually use.

Three things that usually limit motion

Dr. Jared sorts a restriction into one of three buckets before treating it, because each needs a different fix.

  • Muscle and fascia. Tissue that has shortened or developed trigger points: a tight psoas limiting hip extension, or a stiff soleus blocking the ankle in a squat. Hands-on release plus loaded stretching works well here.
  • Joint capsule. The joint itself feels blocked at end range, often after injury, surgery or years of disuse, as in a frozen shoulder or a stiff post-op knee. This responds to patient, repeated work at end range, not force.
  • Motor control. The range exists but your body does not trust it, so you stop short. Common after a back episode or an ankle sprain. The fix is graded practice that teaches the nervous system the position is safe.

What you take home

Every mobility client leaves with a short daily routine in the free MD Fit app, usually 10 to 15 minutes. The routines use household items (a doorway, a chair, a towel, a step) and are filmed by Dr. Jared so the cue you hear at home matches the one you heard in clinic. Each video is tagged to the muscle it targets, so if your plan includes the gluteus medius or the pec minor, you can read why that muscle matters in the muscle library. As your range improves, the routine is swapped for the next progression rather than simply added to.

What progress usually looks like

Hands-on work often produces a noticeable change in range within a session; the question is whether it holds. In the first two or three weeks the gain usually fades between visits and comes back faster each time. After that, people often notice the daily tasks first: putting on socks, reaching the top shelf, checking the blind spot while driving. Lasting change comes from strength through the new range, which is why the program shifts toward loaded movement as the restriction resolves.

Who it's for

Desk workers who feel stiff by mid-afternoon. Older adults who want to keep squatting, reaching and balancing well. Golfers, swimmers and lifters who have hit a technical ceiling that turns out to be a range-of-motion ceiling. Anyone recovering from an injury or surgery that left one side tighter than the other. If you cannot put on socks without pain, this is a reasonable place to start.

When mobility work is not the answer

Stiffness with swelling, warmth or a joint that locks or gives way needs a look from your physician before anyone stretches it. Loss of motion with numbness or weakness, or neck stiffness with fever or a severe headache, are reasons to seek medical care first. If Dr. Jared finds something during the evaluation that does not fit a soft-tissue or motor-control picture, he will tell you and help you get to the right provider.

Frequently asked questions

Do you take insurance?

No. MD Fit is self-pay. The trade-off is long one-on-one visits with the same clinician and no cap on how many sessions your plan allows. You receive an itemized receipt; some clients submit it to their plan or HSA/FSA themselves. Check with your plan.

Do I need to be flexible to start?

No. We meet you where you are and progress gradually.

Will stretching alone work?

Stretching helps temporarily. Lasting mobility needs strength through the new range, which is what the home program builds.

How long until I see change?

Many people feel more range in two to three weeks with consistent daily work.

Is this the same as physical therapy?

There is overlap, but MD Fit is a cash-pay clinic run by a physician and clinical exercise physiologist, not a PT practice. Visits are one-on-one for the full session and the plan continues into strength work rather than ending at discharge.

Can I combine it with medical massage?

Yes. Many clients book a medical massage between mobility follow-ups when a region is particularly stubborn. Dr. Jared will tell you if he thinks it would speed things up.
