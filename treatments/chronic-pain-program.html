Why chronic pain is different

After months of pain, two things tend to happen. The nervous system becomes more sensitive, so the same input hurts more than it used to. And the body learns to protect the painful area: you stop rotating through your spine, you carry the shoulder a little high, you shorten your stride. Those protective habits keep certain muscles working overtime, and they become tender and tight in their own right. The result is often a loop in which the original injury has long since healed but the muscles guarding it still reproduce the pain. The fix is not only finding the sore muscle; it is rebuilding confidence that movement is safe so the guarding can switch off.

The first visit

The evaluation takes about 90 minutes and costs $300. Expect a long conversation first: the history of the pain, what has been tried, sleep, stress, past medical events and what you have stopped doing because of it. Then a movement screen and gentle hands-on testing to find the muscles that reproduce your symptoms. With long-standing low back pain that is often the quadratus lumborum, the psoas or the gluteus medius; with chronic neck pain and tension headaches it is frequently the suboccipitals and levator scapulae. We usually leave the first visit with one or two things that reproduce your pain and one or two things that already feel better. That becomes the plan.

What the program includes

  • Hands-on release of the muscles that still reproduce your pain, so the day-to-day volume comes down enough to move.
  • Graded movement. Starting with motions you can do comfortably and expanding the range and load in small, planned steps rather than testing the limit.
  • Breathing and down-regulation. Simple breathing work that reduces muscle guarding and often improves sleep, which in turn lowers pain sensitivity.
  • Education. A clear, plain-English explanation of what is and is not happening in your body, because fear of damage is one of the strongest drivers of guarding.
  • Strength. Eventually, a loading program, because muscles that are strong enough for daily life stop needing to guard.

The weekly structure

Most clients come once or twice a week for the first month at $150 per follow-up, then taper as the home program carries more of the load. Home work lives in the free MD Fit app and is kept short and realistic, often ten minutes, because a routine you actually do beats a thorough one you skip. The app's videos were filmed by Dr. Jared, so the cues match what you heard in clinic, and you can note flare-ups so the next session starts with what changed.

MD Fit is a self-pay practice. We don't bill insurance and you don't need a referral; you pay at the visit and receive an itemized receipt. Evaluation $300 (about 90 minutes), follow-ups $150, medical massage $250 for 60 minutes or $300 for 90.

How we measure progress

Pain scores are one measure, but a noisy one; chronic pain rises and falls with sleep, stress and weather. So we also track what you can do: how long you can sit, walk or stand, how you slept, whether you got back to the garden or the pickleball court. Flare-ups are expected and are treated as information, not failure. People often notice that flares become shorter and less frightening before they become less frequent.

When to refer out

If anything suggests the pain needs imaging, medication review or a different specialist, Dr. Jared tells you and refers out. Pain with unexplained weight loss, night pain that does not ease with position, new numbness or weakness, or changes in bladder or bowel control are reasons to see your physician promptly. MD Fit works alongside your medical team, not instead of it, and medication decisions always stay with your prescribing doctor.

Frequently asked questions

Do you take insurance?

No. MD Fit is a self-pay practice. For long-standing pain that usually works in your favor: one clinician who knows the whole history, long sessions and no visit caps. You get an itemized receipt; some clients submit it to their plan or HSA/FSA themselves, so check with your plan.

Will I ever be pain-free?

Many chronic-pain clients become pain-free or close to it. Some reduce pain enough to live well again. Your evaluation sets realistic expectations.

Is this for fibromyalgia?

Many fibromyalgia clients benefit from gentle loading and nervous-system work. We go at the pace you can handle.

Do I need to try medications first?

No. Medication decisions stay with your physician. MD Fit focuses on movement and soft-tissue care.

What if hands-on work has flared me up before?

Tell us. Pressure, duration and the order of the session are adjusted for a sensitive nervous system, and the first visit is deliberately gentle so you can see how your body responds before anything deeper is attempted.

Do I have to stop the activities I love?

Usually the opposite. Part of the plan is finding a version of the activity you can tolerate now, such as a shorter walk or a lighter set, and building from there rather than resting until the pain is gone.
