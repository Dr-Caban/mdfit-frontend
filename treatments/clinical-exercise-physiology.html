What a Clinical Exercise Physiologist does

CEPs prescribe exercise for people with cardiovascular, metabolic, pulmonary, neurological and musculoskeletal conditions. The training is about dosing: how much, how hard, how often, and what to watch for in someone whose heart, lungs, blood sugar or balance adds a layer of risk. Dr. Jared holds the CEP credential and the CCEP, and he is also a physician, so the medical history and the exercise plan are read by the same person. This is not a replacement for your cardiologist, endocrinologist or neurologist. It is the part of your care that turns their advice to "stay active" into a specific, safe weekly plan.

Conditions we commonly work with

  • High blood pressure, type 2 diabetes and prediabetes, high cholesterol and obesity, where regular exercise is part of the treatment but starting feels uncertain.
  • Stable, physician-cleared coronary artery disease, heart failure or atrial fibrillation, after formal cardiac rehab ends or when it was never offered.
  • Osteoporosis and osteopenia, where loading bone safely matters and some movements should be avoided.
  • Parkinson's disease, multiple sclerosis and recovery after stroke, where balance, strength and fatigue management are the focus.
  • COPD, long COVID and other causes of exercise intolerance, where pacing is as important as effort.
  • Chronic joint problems such as knee osteoarthritis, hip osteoarthritis or spinal stenosis in someone who also has one of the conditions above.

The first visit

The evaluation takes about 90 minutes and costs $300. It covers a full history, your medication list, resting heart rate and blood pressure, a movement screen and a conservative first dose of exercise so Dr. Jared can see how you respond rather than guess. You leave with a written starting plan and notes your other providers can use. If anything in your history or your vitals suggests you should be cleared or tested by your physician first, he will tell you that day and help you get there.

How the program progresses

Follow-up visits are $150 and usually run weekly at first. Each one adjusts the dose based on how you handled the previous week: how your blood pressure behaved, how long fatigue lasted, whether your joints objected. Progress is deliberately gradual. Between visits you follow the plan in the free MD Fit app, where the videos show exactly the version of each exercise you practiced in clinic, and you can log how it felt. Many clients eventually move on to the strength and performance side of the practice once the medical picture is stable and the habit is set.

What to expect

People often expect to be pushed hard and are surprised at how modest the first few weeks are. That is on purpose. The early goal is a routine your body tolerates well enough to repeat, because consistency is what moves blood pressure, blood sugar, strength and balance over months. Soreness should stay mild. Chest pain, unusual shortness of breath, dizziness or palpitations during exercise are reasons to stop and call your physician, and we go over those warning signs with you at the first visit.

Communication with your care team

With your permission, we send a short summary to your doctor after the evaluation and whenever anything meaningful changes, so your care is coordinated and your physician knows what you are doing and how you are tolerating it.

Who should see their physician first

Unstable or recently diagnosed heart disease, a cardiac event or surgery in the past few weeks without clearance, uncontrolled blood pressure or blood sugar, or new neurological symptoms all belong with your physician before an exercise program starts. If that describes you, the free phone consult is still a good first call: Dr. Jared can tell you what clearance to ask for so you are ready when it is time.

Frequently asked questions

Do I need a doctor's referral?

Not required to book, but welcome. Bring any recent cardiac testing, labs or imaging that your doctor has shared with you.

Do you take insurance?

No. MD Fit is a self-pay practice. That means no referral, no visit limits set by a plan, and the full session with one clinician who knows your history. You get an itemized receipt at each visit; some clients submit it to their plan or HSA/FSA themselves, so check with your plan.

Can I bring my spouse to the first visit?

Absolutely. Many clients bring a partner for the first visit, especially if balance or memory is a concern.

How is this different from a personal trainer?

A trainer is generally certified to work with healthy adults. A Clinical Exercise Physiologist is trained and credentialed to prescribe exercise for people with diagnosed medical conditions, to monitor the response, and to know when to stop and refer back to the physician.

Will I need to exercise every day?

Usually not at first. Most plans start with two or three short sessions a week and build toward daily walking plus two or three strength days as your tolerance improves.
