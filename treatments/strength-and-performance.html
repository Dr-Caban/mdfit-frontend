What makes it clinical

Every program starts with a movement and strength assessment: squat, hinge, press, pull, carry and single-leg work, plus whatever your sport asks of you. Dr. Jared is watching for the limits that will cap your progress or get you hurt: an ankle that will not bend enough for a clean squat, a shoulder that compensates on the press, a hip that drifts on single-leg work. Loads are then prescribed from what you showed that day rather than from a template. Because he is a physician and a Certified Clinical Exercise Physiologist, blood pressure, medications, joint replacements and old injuries are part of the plan from the start, not things you have to mention apologetically.

Who we train

First-time lifters who want to learn the main movements correctly from the beginning. Adults returning to the gym after years off or after an injury. Golfers, pickleball players, runners and tennis players who want strength that shows up in their sport. Experienced lifters and CrossFit athletes working around a cranky shoulder or back. People in their 60s, 70s and 80s who want to stay strong enough to travel, garden and get up off the floor without thinking about it.

A typical session

  1. Check-in. How did the last session and the week of homework feel? Anything sore, tight or different?
  2. Prep. Five to ten minutes of targeted mobility for the joints that session will load, often the hips and thoracic spine.
  3. Main lifts. Two or three primary movements with coaching on every set, loads adjusted as you go.
  4. Accessory work. The single-joint and single-leg work that protects the weak links found in your assessment.
  5. Finisher and plan. A short conditioning piece if it suits your goal, then a written plan for your other training days.

Sessions run 45 to 60 minutes. The first visit is the full 90-minute evaluation ($300); coaching sessions after that are $150.

Training between sessions

Most clients see Dr. Jared once or twice a week and train on their own the rest of the time. The plan for those days lives in the free MD Fit app: exercise videos filmed by Dr. Jared, sets and reps, and the cues he used with you in the room. It is written for the equipment you actually have, whether that is a commercial gym, a pair of dumbbells at home or a hotel fitness room. When something feels off between visits, you note it in the app and the next session starts there.

Where strength fits in the bigger picture

At MD Fit, strength training is usually the last phase of a rehab plan rather than a separate business. Someone who came in for low back pain or shoulder impingement often finishes with a loading program, because muscle that can handle more load tends to complain less about daily life. The reverse is true too: if a lift keeps reproducing pain despite good technique, the session turns into an assessment and hands-on work for the muscle involved, and the loading plan is adjusted rather than pushed through.

What to expect

Early sessions usually feel easier than you expect; the point is to learn positions and find your starting loads, not to leave exhausted. Strength gains in the first weeks come mostly from coordination, and the bigger changes in how you look and feel typically follow over a few months of consistent work. If a medical condition makes you unsure whether lifting is safe, clinical exercise physiology is the same clinician with a more conservative starting point, and the two blend as you progress.

Frequently asked questions

Do you take insurance?

No. MD Fit is a self-pay practice, and coaching is paid at the visit with an itemized receipt. The benefit is that your plan is set by you and Dr. Jared, not by visit caps or approvals. Some clients submit receipts to their plan or HSA/FSA themselves; check with your plan.

How often should I train?

Two to four sessions a week works for most people. Many clients do one or two with Dr. Jared and the rest on their own with the written plan.

Do you program for home workouts?

Yes. Many clients train once a week in-clinic and follow a home or commercial-gym plan the rest of the week.

Can I combine this with rehab?

That's what MD Fit is built for. The strength plan picks up where rehab leaves off, in the same clinic with the same clinician.

Do I need to be in shape before I start?

No. The assessment sets your starting point wherever it is, and the first weeks are built around learning movements at easy loads.

What if I have a joint replacement or an old injury?

Bring the details to the evaluation. Dr. Jared works within surgeons' precautions and chooses exercise variations that load the muscle without irritating the joint, and he will tell you if something needs a physician's clearance first.
